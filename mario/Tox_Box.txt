'''Tox Boxes''' are rare enemies in the ''[[Mario (franchise)|Mario]]'' franchise. They first appear in ''[[Super Mario 64]]''.

==Profile==
===Physical description===
Tox Boxes resemble metal or stone boxes with multiple faces. Tox Boxes have faces on all but one side; the last side is only a square hole.

Originally, in ''Super Mario 64'', Tox Boxes appear as red industrial metal boxes with spray-painted faces in ''Super Mario 64'' and ''[[Super Mario 64 DS]]''

Since ''[[Super Mario Galaxy]]'', instead of having the faces spray-painted on, Tox Boxes are carved in two subtly different ways which match on opposite ends, both resembling a Thwomp's face and appearing as a different face depending on which side is upward. These Tox Boxes also lack a face on the side opposite the hole. The hole itself has also been expanded quite a bit, and is colored in red to resemble a mouth. They currently appear as a relative of [[Thwomp]] designed after the Japanese {{wp|Oni (folklore)|oni}}, and their Japanese name was changed to reflect the new appearance.

===Behavior===
Tox Boxes flip endlessly along set paths, which are usually similar to their width.

==Background==
[[Shigeru Miyamoto]] regards the Tox Box as one of his best designs and a "masterpiece," stating Tox Boxes embody his game design philosophy of making gameplay mechanics clear and quickly understood at a first glance.<ref>"Miyamoto sees the Tox Box, a cube that is constantly moving to crush Mario but has a hole on one side he can safely hide in, as one of his best designs. ''When the hole is on your side, Mario can just get into the cube and you’re safe, but if he doesn’t, you get squashed. And it’s easy to see. It’s very clear and understandable. It’s also easy to predict,'' he says, smiling. ''But once you actually start thinking about it, then it becomes complicated. Once you start trying to put that into action, it becomes complicated. I feel like that is probably one of my masterpieces.''" [https://www.ign.com/articles/2017/06/27/e3-2017-shigeru-miyamoto-and-the-legacy-of-mario Interview with IGN]. Retrieved June 28, 2017</ref>

==History==
===''Super Mario'' series===
====''Super Mario 64''====
[[File:Toxboxsides.png|thumb|left|Tox Boxes roll around to crush [[Mario]] in ''[[Super Mario 64]]''.]]
In ''Super Mario 64'', Tox Boxes appear in the eighth course, [[Shifting Sand Land]]. Five of them are seen traveling along the central maze of tiles north east of the course above a pit of muddy [[quicksand]].

Tox Boxes attempt to flatten Mario by rolling over him. If Mario gets squished by a Tox Box's side, he loses three wedges of [[Health Meter|health]], and if he is without his [[Mario's Cap|cap]], he loses four units of damage instead. Mario can avoid taking damage by standing in the square hole of a Tox Box as it passes over the tile he is standing on. However, the edges of the holed side still crush Mario if he stands under them. Tox Boxes cannot be defeated.

[[Long Jump|Long-jump]]ing into the side of a Tox Box's face that is above quicksand, off the side of the track, causes Mario to lose a life instantly, playing the animation as if he has just fallen into the quicksand.<ref>[https://www.suppermariobroth.com/post/184881267815/in-super-mario-64-jumping-toward-a-tox-box-in Supper Mario Broth], May 15, 2019.</ref>

===''Super Mario 64 DS''===
[[File:SM64DS Tox Box Sand.png|thumb|A Tox Box stuck on the sand in ''Super Mario 64 DS'']]
In ''[[Super Mario 64 DS]]'', Tox Boxes retain much of the same role as before, except they now can be destroyed by [[Wario]]. If Wario [[jump]]s while inside of a Tox Box's hollow side, the Tox Box flies into the air and smashes upon hitting the ground. Alternatively, if Wario [[punch]]es or [[kick]]s while inside of a Tox Box, it is flung off; if the Tox Box lands in the quicksand, it slowly sinks, but if the Tox Box lands on the regular sand, it stays in one spot until Wario exits the course.

Tox Boxes are also seen in the minigame [[Tox Box Shuffle]], where one of them traps a [[Green Yoshi]] and shuffles with two other Tox Boxes. The player has to guess which Tox Box is holding Yoshi. If the player guesses correctly, they win, but they only have two chances to, otherwise it is a [[Game Over]]. As the levels increase, Tox Boxes move faster, and they also hide a [[Cyan Yoshi]], in which case the player must select the Tox Box containing the correct Yoshi displayed on the top screen.
{{br|left}}

====''Super Mario Galaxy''====
[[File:Chain Planets.png|thumb|left|Tox Boxes in ''[[Super Mario Galaxy]]'']]
Tox Boxes reappear in ''[[Super Mario Galaxy]]''. These redesigned boxes usually appear alongside [[Thwomp]]s. They appear only in the [[Beach Bowl Galaxy]] and the [[Toy Time Galaxy]]. In both galaxies, they appear on small pathways, much as they did in Shifting Sand Land. Tox Boxes behave similarly, except they are slightly slower than before. This time, if Mario or Luigi get caught underneath the solid side of a Tox Box, they lose a life instantly. Just as before, Mario or Luigi has to land under the safe, hollow side to avoid being crushed by an incoming Tox Box, but they still must avoid the teeth bordering the hollow side as they can still crush them.

When either galaxy is under the effects of the [[Fast Foe Comet]], Tox Boxes move a lot faster, like other enemies, obstacles, and platforming, forcing a new method of timing when approaching a Tox Box.
{{br}}
====''Super Mario Galaxy 2''====
[[File:SMG2 Stone Cyclone Tox Boxes.png|thumb|Tox Boxes in ''[[Super Mario Galaxy 2]]'']]
In ''[[Super Mario Galaxy 2]]'', Tox Boxes reappear in the [[Stone Cyclone Galaxy]], which is a remake of the Cyclone Stone from the [[Beach Bowl Galaxy]] in ''Super Mario Galaxy''. Tox Boxes move faster than before, flipping once roughly every second. Tox Boxes still cannot be defeated, although they and other obstacles can be drastically slowed using the various [[Lever Switch|blue switches]] in the Beach Bowl Galaxy. Tox Boxes move even faster during the [[mission]] [[Fast Foes on the Cyclone Stone]].

===''Mario Party: Island Tour''===
[[File:Tox Box Game.png|thumb|left]]
Two Tox Boxes appear in the minigame [[Hide 'n' Splat]] in ''[[Mario Party: Island Tour]]''. They retain their ''Super Mario Galaxy'' design but are noticeably smaller. Tox Boxes retain the same behavior as before, and the characters must either avoid the Tox Boxes or hide underneath their hollow side to avoid being crushed. If a character is crushed by a Tox Box, they are eliminated from the minigame. Over time, the Tox Boxes move faster, and the characters have to avoid a third Tox Box. Ultimately, whoever is the last character remaining or anyone who outlasts the Tox Boxes once time is up wins. When the minigame ends, the Tox Boxes disappear.
{{br}}

===''Super Mario-kun''===
[[File:ToxBoxSuperMarioKun.png|thumb|x200px|The Tox Box in ''Super Mario-kun'']]
A Tox Box makes an appearance in volume 16 of ''[[Super Mario-kun]]''. It is an obstacle against Mario's path to retrieve his lost hat from [[Klepto]]. Hatless, Mario tries using a [[! Block]] to combat against the Tox Box; he still ends up getting smashed. In the manga, the Tox Box is able to smash Mario, even on the safe side.
