The '''Tobī''' is an enemy from ''[[Super Mario Bros. Wonder]]''. They are found in haunted locales. They are also known as Moving Doors and Noknoks.

== Profile ==
=== Physical description ===
As implied from the file names they were given, MovingDoor and ChaseDoor, Tobīs are mimic-type enemies that resemble doors. Their face cover is a purple "door" that has an orange "knob," and their true form, which only appears in their aggroed state depicts them with a bluish-green "face" that has a mouth with sharp teeth and no eyes or nose in the "doorway" and yellow feet on the bottom side of the "doorframe."

=== Behavior ===
In their calm state, Tobīs mimic regular [[Door]]s, being indistinguishable from them. However, when it is approached, the Tobī goes into its "aggroed state," charging at the potential prey, [[Mario (character)|Mario]] included. Mario can stomp on the Tobīs to stun them, but none of his other attacks work on the enemy. Mario can safely approach a stunned Tobī and attempt to enter them. Given enough time being stunned, the Tobīs will eventually revert back to their "aggroed state" unless Mario leaves their territory. However, if the Tobīs are interacted with, they are either destroyed (in which case they drop a golden [[Coin]]), or they become a regular [[Door]] and stay that way until the level ends and Mario goes back in.

== In ''Super Mario Bros. Wonder'' ==
Tobīs are only seen in the levels Light-Switch Mansion and KO Arena Fungi Funk in [[Fungi Mines]] and [[The Final Battle! Bowser's Rage Stage]] in [[Castle Bowser]].

== Trivia ==
*Tobīs bear a resemblance to the Mimic from the ''Backrooms''.

== Behind the scenes ==
=== Name ===
Tobīs were named after their resemblance to real doors in Japanese, with the name coming from ''Tobira'', Japanese for "door" and written トビー.

== Nomenclature ==
The Japanese name of the Tobī matches the English name. The files refer to them as MovingDoor and ChaseDoor.

The Chinese dub calls the enemy ''Yá Mén'', literally "tooth door" and written 牙門. Korean calls them ''muni'' (written 무니), from ''mun'' ("door"; written 문) with the noun-forming suffix -''i'' (written 이)
