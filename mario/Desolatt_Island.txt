'''Desolatt Island''' is a major location from ''[[Mario & Luigi: Brothership]]''. Found drifting around in the [[Color-Full Sea]]'s northern part, it is the fifth major island to be linked to [[Shipshape Island]]'s [[Uni-Tree]]. Desolatt Island is one of two desert-themed locations in the game, the other being [[Allsand Island]], and both islands seem to have [[Merrygo Island]] as their neighbour, due to the latter's outskirts being covered in sand. Unlike the first major islands of the other seas, Desolatt Island is not available immediately when the gang arrives in the Color-Full Sea, with its accessibility requiring the early discovery of the Color-Full Sea's [[Great Lighthouse Islands|Great Lighthouse Island]]. Its connection to the Uni-Tree leads to Merrygo Island's unlocking.

== History ==
Upon reaching the island in their journey to reunite [[Concordia]], [[Mario (character)|Mario]] and [[Luigi]] find the island's residents being kidnapped by [[Snaptor|birds]]. They meet four kids named Ireen, Dulles, Loog, and Ellow, who refer to themselves as [[IDLE]], claim to be the island's protectors, and know how to reach the island's [[Lighthouse]]. The IDLE gang tests the Bros. by playing hide-and-seek with them. Upon having their hiding spots discovered, the IDLE kids open the way to the lighthouse. Upon reaching the lighthouse, Mario and Luigi encounter the [[Extension Corps]], who were first seen at the [[Lushgreen Sea]]'s [[Great Lighthouse Islands|Great Lighthouse Island]]. The trio's members reveal their names to be Ecks (the flying pink spade connector arm being), Ten (the purple lanky member with the headphone jack design), and Shun (the green DVI connector ruffian). Their boss, [[Zokket]], also appears, voicing his contempt for Mario and Luigi's meddling and ordering Ten to dispose of them. As Zokket leaves to continue his plan, Ten attacks the Bros., only to be defeated and drop his [[Battle Plug|Battle Plugs]].

== Behind the Scenes ==
Desolatt Island's name comes from the word "desolate" (which means "to deprive of inhabitants" despite the place being uninhabited, although it could also refer to how the [[Snaptor|Snaptors]] carried the island's residents off during their attack), from the Latin verb ''dēsōlāre'' ("to make lonely"). The replacement of the final E with T could come from "watt," which is a unit of power, perhaps alluding to [[Concordia]]'s general electric theme and Desolatt's plug-shaped houses.

== Nomenclature ==
In Japanese, Desolatt Island is called ''Sabiretta'' (written サビレッタ), which comes from a duplication of the T in ''sabireta'' ("desolated"; written 寂れた). Chinese translates the island's name as ''Jìjìng'' ("quiet"; written 寂靜) In Spanish, it has the name ''Desolita'', which comes from either the present infinitive or past participle of ''desolar'' ("to desolate") and ''solito'' ("alone"). Its Portuguese name, ''Exília'', comes from a feminisation of ''exílio'' ("exile"). Italian refers to it as ''Desola'', which means "he desolates." Its French name, ''Troglodia'', comes from "troglodyte." In German, the island's name is ''Desolütje'', from "desolate" and the diminutive/affectionate suffix "''-tje''." Dutch translates its name as ''Vehlaate'', from ''verlaten'' ("to abandon"). In Korean, it is called ''Soerakeu'' (written 쇠라크), from ''soerak'' ("deterioration"; written 쇠락).
