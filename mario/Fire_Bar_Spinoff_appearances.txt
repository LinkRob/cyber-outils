==''Mario'' game appearances==
===''Mario Kart'' series===
====''Mario Kart: Double Dash!!''====
[[File:Waluigi Stadium straight path MKDD.png|thumb|left|200px|Fire Bars in Waluigi Stadium]]
[[File:Fire BarMK8.png|thumb|A Podoboo Cog in {{classic|DS|Wario Stadium}} in ''Mario Kart 8'']]
In ''[[Mario Kart: Double Dash!!]]'', Fire Bars (referred to as <b>Podoboo Cogs</b>) appear on the [[Waluigi Stadium]] and [[Bowser's Castle (Mario Kart: Double Dash!!)|Bowser's Castle]] courses. The former has a [[Podoboo]] face on it and can be crashed into with a [[Star]] until it slows down back to its normal speed. They burn racers on contact, briefly causing them to spin out. The game also introduces a similar type of obstacle, [[Ring of Fire|Rings of Fire]], which are obviously rings of fireballs.

====''Mario Kart DS''====
[[File:Podoboo Cog Stadium DS.png|thumb|left]]
Podoboo Cogs later return in ''[[Mario Kart DS]]'', in the [[Wario Stadium (Mario Kart DS)|Wario Stadium]] course, although it lacks a Podoboo face and only has two fireballs on each three sides with the one in the center.

====''Mario Kart Wii''====
In ''[[Mario Kart Wii]]'', they are known as '''Podoboo Cogs'''.<ref>Hodgson, David S. J. ''Mario Kart Wii'' Premiere Edition. Pages 92 and 128.</ref> They appear in the game's [[Bowser's Castle (Mario Kart Wii)|Bowser's Castle]] and one retro course, {{classic course|GCN|Waluigi Stadium}}. Podoboo Cogs are designed differently between courses: In Bowser's Castle, they have three fireballs on each side, while in {{classic course|GCN|Waluigi Stadium}}, they retain the Podoboo face at the center and also have the number of fireballs increased to four on each side.

Fire Bars are also objects in two [[K'NEX]] building sets based on ''Mario Kart Wii'', where they are referred to as '''Fire Cogs''' in some regions, most regions, however, used <b>Podoboo Cog</b> as their name.

====''Mario Kart 8''====
[[File:Podoboo Cog Blazing.png|thumb|200px|A Fire Chain in ''Mario Kart 8'']]
In ''[[Mario Kart 8]]'', Fire Bars (referred to as '''Fire Chains''' in the Prima Games guide) appear in the game's unique [[Bowser's Castle (Mario Kart 8)|Bowser's Castle]] and return in the retro course {{classic course|DS|Wario Stadium}}. In the latter course, several additional Fire Chains have been added to the [[anti-gravity]] section, where it also shown that they can spin through the mesh tracks.

====''Mario Kart Live: Home Circuit''====
In ''[[Mario Kart Live: Home Circuit]]'', they appear in Bowser's Castle as gate obstacles with their appearance from the ''Super Mario Maker'' games.
{{br}}

===''Paper Mario'' series===
====''Paper Mario''====
[[File:Firebar PM.png|thumb|A Fire Bar as seen in Mt. Lavalava]]
In ''[[Paper Mario]]'', the Fire Bars reappear in [[Koopa Bros. Fortress]] and [[Mt. Lavalava]]; this time they swing sideways. They also swing either clockwise or counter-clockwise. If Mario touches one, he loses some [[HP]], but if Mario's partner touches one, nothing happens. Mario can [[jump]] over a Fire Bar, which causes it to swing faster. If Mario dodges a Fire Bar ten times, it vanishes and drops a number of [[coin]]s. The game marks the first instance in which Fire Bars can be destroyed.
{{br}}

====''Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door''====
[[File:PMTTYD POS Firebar.png|thumb|left|A fire bar in the Palace of Shadow]]
In ''[[Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door]]'', Fire Bars first appear in Bowser's third platform level, [[World 3 (Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door)|World 3]]. They function identically as in the ''Super Mario'' series. Bowser can destroy Fire Bars using his [[Fire Breath|fire breath]]. However, if Bowser touches the fireballs, they either decrease his size or defeat him (if he is small). 

Mario only ever encounters Fire Bars in the [[Palace of Shadow]]. Here, Fire Bars utilize the same behavior from ''Paper Mario''.
{{br}}
====''Super Paper Mario''====
[[File:SPM-Firebar.png|thumb|A Fire Bar in Yold Ruins]]
[[File:Firebar SPM.png|thumb|left|A Fire Bar sprite in ''Super Paper Mario'']]
Fire Bars reappear in ''[[Super Paper Mario]]''. In the game, they act in a similar fashion as in ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]'' and appear in Chapter 1-4 and 8-2. Mario can flip into 3-D mode to avoid the Fire Bars.
{{br}}
===''Mario Party'' series===
====''Mario Party 4''====
[[File:Mp4 fb.png|thumb|left|Fire Bars in ''Mario Party 4'']]
Fire Bars also appear in ''[[Mario Party 4]]'' in [[The Final Battle! (minigame)|The Final Battle!]] They take away a [[heart]] from the player when touched.

====''Mario Party: Island Tour''====
[[File:Great Bars of Fire.png|thumb|Fire Bars in ''Mario Party: Island Tour'']]
In ''[[Mario Party: Island Tour]]'', Fire Bars appear in the minigame [[Great Bars of Fire]], where they are featured in sets of four: two of which are higher and two are lower. The players need to jump over or duck under the Fire Bars, and if a character hits a Fire Bar three times, they are out, and the last player standing wins.

====''Mario Party 10''====
[[File:Bowser's Fire Bar Fury.jpg|thumb|left|Fire Bars in Bowser's Fire Bar Fury]]
Fire Bars are featured in the Bowser Minigame [[Bowser's Fire Bar Fury]]. Here, [[Bowser]] places his opponents in a small machine with two Fire Bars. Players must move around or jump around to avoid being scorched by the Fire Bars. If a player is hit, they lose a heart, and if a player loses all their hearts, they are eliminated.
{{br}}

====''Mario Party: The Top 100''====
In ''[[Mario Party: The Top 100]]'', Fire Bars reappear in the minigame [[The Final Battle! (minigame)|The Final Battle!]] from ''[[Mario Party 4]]'' in the same roles.
{{br}}
===''Super Princess Peach''===
[[File:Fire Bar SPP sprite.png|frame|left]]
In ''[[Super Princess Peach]]'', Fire Bars make their first appearances in [[Bowser's Villa]] as obstacles, damaging Peach when she comes into contact with them. Within the area, they appear in sections requiring [[Subrella]], mirroring how they appeared in an underwater section of [[World 8-4 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 8-4]] in ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]'' Fire Bars also appear in unlock-able levels [[Ladida Plains 1-9]] and [[Hoo's Wood 2-9]]; in the former, only one appears in the Subrella section while in the latter, there are ten Fire Bars in total: five in the starting section and five in the Subrella section. When Peach is near a Fire Bar, it emits a swinging sound effect.

Horizontally-spinning Fire Bars with a different appearance appear in the minigame [[Toad Tote]].

===''Mario & Luigi: Paper Jam''===
[[File:Fire Bar M&LPJ.png|thumb|left|Fire Bars in ''Mario & Luigi: Paper Jam'']]
In ''[[Mario & Luigi: Paper Jam]]'', Fire Bars appear in the [[Trio Whirligig]] minigame in the [[Arcade (Mario & Luigi: Paper Jam)|Arcade]] as obstacles. They are made up of two to seven bars. If Mario or Luigi touch a Fire Bar, they lose a point, and if they are hit three times, the minigame ends. Purple Fire Bars also appear in the mission "[[Bye-Bye, Boo]]."
{{br}}

===''Mario Golf: Super Rush''===
[[File:Firebar MarioGolfSuperRush.png|thumb|A Fire Bar in ''Mario Golf: Super Rush'']]
Fire Bars make their debut in the ''[[Mario Golf (series)|Mario Golf]]'' series in ''[[Mario Golf: Super Rush]]'' exclusive to the [[Bowser Highlands]] course. These Fire Bars are not connected to a block.

==In other media==
===''The Super Mario Bros. Movie''===
In ''[[The Super Mario Bros. Movie]]'', mechanical representations of Fire Bars appear in [[Princess Peach]]'s training course, these Firebars however are made of a solid linoleum rather than actual fire.
