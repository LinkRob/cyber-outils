The '''''Classic NES Series''''', titled '''''NES Classics''''' in Europe and Australia, is a series of games for the [[Game Boy Advance]] consisting of [[reissue|ports]] of [[Nintendo Entertainment System]] games. It is the international counterpart of Japan's '''''Famicom Mini''''' series, which features ports of both [[Family Computer]] and [[Family Computer Disk System]] games. The ''Classic NES Series'' began in 2004 and ended the same year, or early 2005 in Europe and Australia. Some [[e-Reader]] cards similarly included a Nintendo Entertainment System game on it. The ''Classic NES Series'' only has twelve games within two series whereas the ''Famicom Mini'' series has thirty games within three series.

Every ''Classic NES Series'' title is mostly the same as their respective original release, but have squished graphics due to the Game Boy Advance's lower screen resolution. Ports of games with a two-player mode made use of multiplayer via a Game Boy Advance Game Link Cable or Wireless Adapter. Every ''Classic NES Series'' and ''Famicom Mini'' game comes with a unique Pause menu, accessed by pressing {{button|gba|L}} or {{button|gba|R}}, and some games allow the player to save their high scores. Every ''Classic NES Series'' game is contained within a light gray cartridge to resemble the default color of NES cartridges. The color of a ''Famicom Mini'' cartridge usually matches the color of the original Family Computer cartridge or Family Computer Disk System floppy disk.

==''Mario''-related games==
In total, the ''Classic NES Series'' has only three ''[[Mario (franchise)|Mario]]''-related installments. The ''Famicom Mini'' series has six ''Mario''-related games, three of which were not released in English-speaking countries as part of the ''Classic NES Series'' line-up.

*'''''[[Donkey Kong (game)|Donkey Kong]]''''' &ndash; Released on February 14, 2004 in Japan, on June 7, 2004 in North America, and on July 9, 2004 in Europe. Notably, this game does not include [[50m]], a level exclusive to [[Donkey Kong (Game Boy)|the Game Boy version]] and the arcade version of the game.
*'''''[[Dr. Mario (game)|Dr. Mario]]''''' &ndash; Released on May 21, 2004 in Japan,  on October 25, 2004 in North America, and on January 7, 2005 in Europe.
*'''''[[Super Mario Bros.]]''''' &ndash; Released on February 14, 2004 in Japan, on June 7, 2004 in North America, and on July 9, 2004 in Europe. On September 13, 2005, it was re-released in Japan on the occasion of the game's 20th anniversary.
''Mario'' games released in Japan only:
*'''''[[Mario Bros. (game)|Mario Bros.]]''''' &ndash; Released on May 21, 2004.
*'''''[[Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels|Super Mario Bros. 2]]''''' &ndash; Released on August 10, 2004. Known as ''Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels'' in the West. Being a port of a [[Family Computer Disk System]] game, ''Super Mario Bros. 2'' also includes the classic "Please Set Disk Card" screen that can be triggered by holding the A button while the Game Boy Advance logo is shown.
*'''''[[Wrecking Crew]]''''' &ndash; Released on May 21, 2004.

There is a canceled game from [[iQue]] titled ''[[Famicom Mini Collection]]'', which features eleven built-in Famicom Mini titles, including every ''Mario''-related one.
