'''''Mario & Sonic''''' is a crossover video game series starring characters from the ''[[Mario (franchise)|Mario]]'' and [[w:c:sonic:Sonic the Hedgehog (series)|''Sonic the Hedgehog'']] franchises. These games are made by [[SEGA]] and are exclusive to the [[Nintendo DS]], [[Nintendo 3DS]], [[Wii]], [[Wii U]], [[Nintendo Switch]], and arcade systems.

The first game, ''[[Mario & Sonic at the Olympic Games]]'', is an official game for the [[wikipedia:2008 Summer Olympics|Beijing 2008 Olympics]], and it features venues similar to real ones in Beijing. The game would go on to receive five sequels: one to honor the [[wikipedia:2010 Winter Olympics|Vancouver 2010 Winter Olympics]], one to honor the [[wikipedia:2012 Summer Olympics|London 2012 Summer Olympics]], one to honor the [[wikipedia:2014 Winter Olympics|Sochi 2014 Winter Olympics]], one to honor the [[wikipedia:2016 Summer Olympics|Rio 2016 Summer Olympics]], and one to honor the [[wikipedia:2020 Summer Olympics|Tokyo 2020 Summer Olympics]].

==History==
[[File:Mario and Sonic Race.png|left|200px|thumb|[[Mario]] and [[Sonic]] competing.]]
SEGA received the rights to make video games based on the 2008 Beijing Olympics. The International Olympic Committee (IOC) desired that SEGA make the Olympics appealing to a younger audience, so naturally they knew to use their ''Sonic the Hedgehog'' characters. Due to the atmosphere of competitive sportsmanship the Olympics had to offer, combined with the continued desire to interest younger audiences, SEGA received approval from Nintendo to include [[Mario]] in the game with [[Sonic]].<ref>http://www.gamespot.com/wii/sports/mariosonicattheolympicgames/news.html?sid=6168338</ref>

This crossover was big news to the international gaming community because of the long business rivalry between the two mascots throughout the majority of the 1990s. After the first game, ''[[Mario & Sonic at the Olympic Games]]'' was very successful and received much praise, and [[SEGA]] made a sequel, ''[[Mario & Sonic at the Olympic Winter Games]]''. The third installment titled ''[[Mario & Sonic at the London 2012 Olympic Games]]'' was announced on April 21, 2011.<ref>http://www.sega.com/games/mario-and-sonic-at-the-london-2012-olympic-games/</ref> ''[[Super Smash Bros. Brawl]]'' is a Nintendo game that has both Mario and Sonic as playable characters, however it is not officially part of the ''Mario & Sonic'' series.

Many fans thought that it was quite odd due to the rivalry of Mario and Sonic in the 90's.

==Attributes==
So far, all games in this series have been based on the Olympics and are officially licensed by the IOC through exclusive licensee International Sports Multimedia. The gameplay primarily revolves around the Wii's motion controls or the [[Nintendo DS|DS]] touch pad control system. Though the [[Wii]] and DS versions share the same titles, the graphics and gameplay are completely different in each version.

==Trivia==
*''[[Super Smash Bros. Brawl]]'', ''[[Super Smash Bros. for Nintendo 3DS]]/[[Super Smash Bros. for Wii U]], ''and'' [[Super Smash Bros. Ultimate]]'' are not officially part of the ''Mario & Sonic'' Series, even though both characters are playable in the games.
*Sumo Digital considered making Mario a guest star in ''Sonic & Sega All-Stars Racing'' exclusively on the Wii version, but SEGA rejected the idea, not wanting to cross contaminate with the ''Mario & Sonic'' series.
*All of the female characters (excluding guest characters such as Rouge the Bat) in each of the games have a different outfit, but the male characters (including Metal Sonic) keep the same outfit that they have in regular games.<ref>http://seganerds.thekartel.com/blog/2010/02/22/exclusive_qa_with_sonic_and_sega_all-star_racings_producers</ref> In ''Mario & Sonic at the Olympic Games Tokyo 2020'', many of the male characters have special outfits as well.
*According to series' executive producer Lee Cocker, there will be no more installments in the ''Mario & Sonic'' series and that ''Mario & Sonic at the Olympic Games Tokyo 2020'' is the final installment of the series. This is because the [[International Olympic Committee|IOC]] wanted to focus on other partners, including [[Non-fungible token|non-fungible tokens]] and [[esports]].
*Since the start of the series, neither the [[2018 Winter Olympics]] in Pyeongchang, South Korea, nor the [[2022 Winter Olympics]] in Beijing, China, have had ''Mario & Sonic'' games to coincide with their events. In the former's case, [[Ubisoft]] acquired the rights to make the official video games for the 2018 Olympic Winter Games before SEGA could.
