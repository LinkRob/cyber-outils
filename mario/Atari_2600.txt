The '''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_2600 Atari 2600]''' (originally known as the '''Atari Video Computer System''', '''Atari VCS''' or simply "Atari") is an 8-bit [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ROM_cartridge cartridge]-based game console produced by the former American gaming company [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari,_Inc. Atari, Inc.] and is its first home system. It was released in 1977, the same year when [[Shigeru Miyamoto]] joined [[Nintendo]] as well as the Japan-only [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_TV-Game Color TV-Game] series (its very first console). One of the earliest and most important consoles of all-time, the Atari 2600 is often believed to be the very first home system ever created, but it is not actually such, as the [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnavox_Odyssey Magnavox Odyssey] predates it by five years in 1972. However, this is the first of its kind to be a majorly successful console, setting the foundation for every future system in decades to come, including the [[Nintendo Entertainment System]]. Although the Atari 2600 as a whole is [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_2600_hardware primitive by today's standards], as it is older than the NES and even the ''[[Donkey Kong (video game)|Donkey Kong]]'' arcade game, it was revolutionary for its time.  

Part of a [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_generation_of_video_game_consoles generation before the 8-bit era], the Atari VCS primarily competed with the [[Intellivision]] and, later, [[ColecoVision]], and is by far the most popular console of its market in spite of being the least technologically advanced. The 2600 would eventually retire on January 1, 1992, lasting over 14 years and making itself one of the longest-lasting home consoles. As the [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_game_consoles best-selling system of its day], it ultimately sold 30 million units worldwide. The Atari 2600 was succeeded by the [[Atari 5200]] and then the [[Atari 7800]] (which both were not quite as successful). 

Some of the Atari 2600's best-known games include ''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Invaders Space Invaders]'' (its [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killer_application killer application]), ''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroids_(video_game) Asteroids]'', ''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_Command Missile Command]'', ''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yars%27_Revenge Yars' Revenge]'', ''[https://atari.fandom.com/wiki/Adventure_(2600) Adventure]'', ''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pole_Position Pole Position]'', ''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitfall! Pitfall!]'', ''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berzerk_(video_game) Berzerk]'', and more. 

The Atari VCS played a major role for the eventual widespread success of the NES. In 1983, the video game industry in the United States suffered an [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_crash_of_1983 infamous crash], hurting video game sales dramatically, mainly due to this system's two most notorious games - ''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pac-Man_(Atari_2600_video_game) Pac-Man]'' (ironically its [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_Atari_2600_video_games best-seller] with over 8 million sold) and ''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.T._the_Extra-Terrestrial_(video_game) E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial]'', for being poorly created and receiving high criticism as among the [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_video_games_notable_for_negative_reception worst games ever made]. By the time Nintendo's breakthrough console was released in 1985, mainly thanks to ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]'', the gaming world was soon revived and the multi-billiondollar company avoided any missteps that triggered the crash since. Atari Inc. did release a Japanese version of the 2600 in Japan called the '''Atari 2800''', but because it came to that region in 1983, it failed to compete with the then-new [[Family Computer|Famicom]]. In 1986, a cost-reduced version unofficially known as '''Atari Jr.''', was released in an attempt to make the Atari 2600 compete with the technologically superior NES alongside the 7800 until its retirement in the early 1990s. 

Despite being a non-Nintendo console, three early games from the ''[[Mario (franchise)|Mario]]'' and ''[https://donkeykong.fandom.com/wiki/Donkey_Kong_(series) Donkey Kong]'' franchises have been released for this system: [[Donkey Kong (video game)|''Donkey Kong'']], [[Donkey Kong Jr. (video game)|''Donkey Kong Jr.'']], and ''[[Mario Bros. (video game)|Mario Bros.]]'', as well as ''[https://popeye.fandom.com/wiki/Popeye_(arcade_game) Popeye]''. The game cartridges for the Atari VCS could also be played on the Atari 5200, ColecoVision, and Intellivision through adapters as well as on the instantly backwards-compatible Atari 7800. 

== Graphics/Hardware ==
[[File:Mario Bros. Atari 2600.png|thumb|294x294px|''[[Mario Bros. (video game)|Mario Bros.]]'' as seen on the Atari 2600]]
Being a home console produced in the late 1970s, the main hardware and graphics of the Atari 2600 are extremely basic in comparison to that of modern consoles and even the Nintendo Entertainment System, but was considered advanced at its time of release, as it is a leap over the Color TV-Game series and especially the unsuccessful Magnavox Odyssey. Its CPU, the MOS Technology 6507, runs at 1.19MHz, and most games' memory size are at only 2 or 4KB but some can reach 8KB (to compare, the NES can hold between 8KB to 1MB). Like the majority of other classic consoles, the Atari VCS was specifically designed to be compatible with the [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathode-ray_tube cathode-ray tube] (CRT) television sets, which the ones produced at that time commonly lack auxiliary video inputs to receive audio and video from another device. Therefore, to connect to a TV, the console generates a radio frequency signal compatible with the regional television standards (NTSC, PAL, or SECAM), using a special switch box to act as the television's antenna.

To make the console handle the analog TV signal, Atari Inc. developed the [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_Interface_Adaptor Television Interface Adaptor] (TIA) chip in its motherboard, a custom IC. It provides a single-color, 20-bit background register that covers the left half of the screen (each bit represents 4 adjacent pixels) and is either repeated or reflected on the right side. There are only five single-color sprites: two 8-pixel wide players; two 1 bit missiles, which share the same colors as the players; and a 1-pixel ball, which shares the background color. The 1-bit sprites all can be controlled to stretch to 1, 2, 4, or 8 pixels. All regional modes of the TIA are 160 pixels wide. NTSC provides 192 visible lines per screen, drawn at 60 Hz, with 16 colors, each at 8 levels of brightness. PAL provides more vertical scanlines, with 228 visible lines per screen, but drawn at a slower 50 Hz and only 13 colors. SECAM, also a 50 Hz format, is limited to just 8 colors, each with only a single brightness level.

The system was designed without a frame buffer to avoid the cost of the associated RAM. Therefore, because of this limitation, the background and sprites apply to a single scan line (the main reason why sprites often flicker in many games), and as the display is output to the television, the program can change colors, sprite positions, and background settings. The careful timing required to sync the code to the screen on the part of the programmer was labeled "racing the beam"; the actual game logic runs when the television beam is outside of the visible area of the screen. Early games for the Atari 2600 used the same visuals for pairs of scan lines, giving a lower vertical resolution, to allow more time for the next row of graphics to be prepared. Later games, such as ''Pitfall!'', change the visuals for each scan line or extend the black areas around the screen to extend the game code's processing time.
[[File:Atari 2600 controller.jpg|left|thumb|161x161px|Atari 2600 CX10 joystick]]
The main controller of the Atari VCS, unlike most later home consoles in general, is that of an [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_CX40_joystick 8-way joystick] with a single button, similarly to arcades. However, as some games such as ''Breakout'' or ''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indy_500_(1977_video_game) Indy 500]'' required "Paddle" or "Driving" controllers, the system has ones specifically designed for them, which appear as a rotating wheel on the top. 

The Atari 2600, in spite of its vintage age, can be played on modern flatscreen HD TVs, but an adapter is required, as this console only supports RF and therefore does not have composite signals. 

==Sales==
In the first year, from 1977 to 1979, Atari 2600 sold just 4 million units. In 1980, sales boosted to 12 million units with the launch of ''[[wikipedia:Space Invaders|Space Invaders]]''. By the time of the video game crash of 1983, Atari 2600 sold 24 million units. It would ultimately sell 30 million as of 2004.

== Trivia ==

* The Atari 2600's library consists of 523 games. It had nine launch titles, with ''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_(video_game) Combat]'' being its pack-in game. Its final officially released games are ''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klax_(video_game) Klax]'' and the Europe-only [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatal_Run ''Fatal Run''], in 1990.
* The Atari VCS is the earliest home console where any ''[[Mario (franchise)|Mario]]'' game or [[Nintendo]] titles in general are released on.
* As the console was originally named Atari Video Computer System or Atari VCS for short, the more well-known "Atari 2600" name was introduced in 1982 by the release of its successor the Atari 5200, as it was rebranded by Atari Inc. to distinguish them.
* While most Atari VCS games are only between 2 to 8KB in memory size (with some known large titles being ''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solaris_(video_game) Solaris]'', ''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_(1981_video_game) Stargate]'', ''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_Lock Radar Lock]'' or ''[https://pacman.fandom.com/wiki/Jr._Pac-Man_(game) Jr. Pac-Man]''), ''Fatal Run'' is the largest game officially made for the console, at 32KB. Despite this, one title exclusively for Brazil, ''Megaboy'', was at 64KB, though it's unconventional and consists of linked games. On the other hand, it is discovered through fan-made homebrews the cartridges' data, when pushed to the very limit, can reach as high as 128KB, as seen ''Casey's Gold'' released in 2024, through 32-banking.
* Unlike most later consoles, the Atari 2600 has many different controllers and models throughout its lifespan.
* One of the most popular features of the Atari 2600, aside from its unique six metal levers meant to power it on, control the games' difficulty, modes, or change the screen from color or black and white, is the front of the console has woodgrain (except for the 2800 and Jr. models), which was a common theme on products in the 1970s.
* ''[[Donkey Kong (video game)|Donkey Kong]]'' is the console's third best-selling game, with over 4.1 million copies sold.
* In terms of dimensions, the Atari VCS is even larger than the [[Nintendo Entertainment System]]. The original "Heavy Sixer" model is also noted by owners as one of the heaviest consoles as well before its multiple later revisions including the "Light Sixer" reduce weight to make it easier to carry. Due to its shape when looked at certain angles, the initial version is seen by some as slightly resembling a car or spaceship of its era.[[File:Atari VCS (Darth Vader model).jpg|thumb|238x238px|The black "Darth Vader" Atari 2600 4-switch model]]
* One of the Atari 2600's console revision models is a unique all-black model released in 1982. It is informally known by fans as the "[[starwars:Anakin_Skywalker|Darth Vader]]", due to its colors closely resembling that of the iconic antagonist from the ''[[starwars:Star_Wars|Star Wars]]'' franchise.
** Although Nintendo had released black-colored versions of a few of their own consoles such as the [[Game Boy]], [[Nintendo GameCube|GameCube]] or [[Wii]], they have not received such a cult-following.
* Contrary to popular belief, the Atari 2600 is '''not''' the first home console to have a microprocessor and use interchangeable game cartridges. The [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairchild_Channel_F Fairchild Channel F] predates it by one year in 1976, though this console did not sold well.
* Like the [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System]], the Atari VCS is well known by fans for having high-quality sound effects, thanks to its custom TIA chip.
* The Atari 2600's original retail price in 1977 was $199. It must be noted, however, although it was indeed successful, when inflated to today's currency, the console itself (not including additional controllers or games) would actually be far more expensive than current consoles, as it would cost a staggering '''$1,057.21''' as of 2026. For comparison, the [[Nintendo Switch 2]]'s base launch price is $449.99. This is because in that era, the home video game industry was at its infancy and therefore considered luxurious among consumers at the time. Despite this, its initial high price did manage to reduce in the early 1980s.
* The [[Atari 7800]], the true successor of the 2600 released in 1986, despite being an update in graphics, shares the exact same sound chip as the original VCS.
* Coincidentally, the Atari 2600's release date, September 11th, is the same as the infamous [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks 9/11 terrorist attack] on the United States in 2001, 24 years prior.
* In June 2025, Citrix engineer Robert Caruso pitted the Atari 2600 against [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ChatGPT ChatGPT] and Microsoft's [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Copilot Copilot] on ''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_Chess Video Chess]'' released for the console in 1979 via an emulator. This was born from a conversation with the chatbot regarding the differences in the open-source chess engine Stockfish and the program AlphaZero. He claimed it took a turn when ChatGPT's chatbot stated it was ''"a strong player in its own right and would easily beat Atari's Video Chess."'' However, and humorously, the classic 8-bit home console's engine in-game, despite being far outdated in Artificial Intelligence, easily won the 90-minute match on its lowest difficulty setting, due to ChatGPT confused the game pieces and lost track of the board state, even with assistance from Caruso correcting board awareness. Tech outlets described the result that large language models are not specialized chess engines, contrasting the Atari 2600 1.19 MHz MOS 6507 CPU's alpha-beta search with ChatGPT or similar AI conversational heuristics.
* Until the release of the 4-switch model and "Jr." where it covers the console's internal area, the Atari VCS initially had one of the smallest motherboards of any home system, as the primary hardware was directly at its cartridge port, as seen in the "Heavy/Light Sixer" version.
* Interestingly, due to the identical shape of its controller ports, the ones used for the MSX, Commodore 64, Amiga, Atari 8-bit computers, and Atari ST are compatible with the Atari VCS. The standard joystick can even work on [[SEGA|Sega]]'s [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_System Master System] and [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega_Genesis Genesis] and vice versa, but in the case of the 2600, functionally is limited as it only contains a single button.
* Ironically, Nintendo's Japan-only Color TV Game series are clones of the 1972 ''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pong Pong]'' arcade game, which itself was produced by Atari Inc, the manufacturer of the Atari 2600.
** Atari released their own home ports of the game starting in 1975.

* In 2023, the [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari,_Inc._(formerly_GT_Interactive) current Atari Inc.] released the [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_2600%2B Atari 2600+], a smaller, modern replica of the original console which can actually play stock 2600 and 7800 games.
