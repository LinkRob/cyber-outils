!Brick Block
----
[[File:NSMBW Brick Block Artwork.png|145x145px]]
|Perhaps one of the most common type of Blocks found throughout the ''Mario'' series is the [[Brick Block]]. First appearing in ''Super Mario Bros.'', Brick Blocks can only be broken by [[Super Mario]] or Super Luigi; they can only achieve this status after they have used a Super Mushroom. Brick Blocks are also known to secretly contain power-ups and 1-Up Mushrooms. Brick Blocks sometimes provide a bridge for the player to walk across abysses. [[Vine]]s also can be found hidden within certain Brick Blocks, allowing the player access to a [[Coin Heaven]]. Certain [[switch]]es can also be unveiled from a Brick Block; this can sometimes be vital to completing a level, depending on whether or not said switch activates an event that helps the player gain further access in the level.

!Coin Block
----
[[File:Rectangleblock.png|145x145px]]
|Debuting in ''Super Mario Bros.'', the [[Coin Block]] has appeared in almost every game in the ''Mario'' series. Coin Blocks are blocks that give the player multiple coins. Most Coin Blocks look identical to Brick or Question Blocks, so the only way to tell them apart is to see how many coins they give. Some Coin Blocks, like those featured in ''Super Mario 3D Land'' can be long and rectangular. In most games, there is a time limit for how long a Coin Block will continue to give the player coins instead of actually having a set number of coins. If the player strikes a coin block fast enough, they can get 10 coins, usually the most coins a player can get from a coin block; however, a few games make it possible to obtain more. In New Super Mario Bros., striking a Coin Block enough times (at least 11) will produce a Super Mushroom. In New Super Mario Bros. Wii if the player hits a Coin Block enough times, five Coins will fly out before the Coin Block turns into a used block.  
|<center>''[[Super Mario Bros.]]''</center>

!Warp Pipe
----
[[File:NSMBDS Warp Pipe Artwork.png|145x145px]]
|Throughout the Mushroom Kingdom, [[Warp Pipe]]s are used as the primary way of transportation. Debuting in ''Mario Bros.'', Warp Pipes would randomly eject various enemies for the player to defeat or avoid. In ''Super Mario Bros.'', Warp Pipes have a much more noticeable role. While they provide access to secret underground areas that often host mass amounts of Coins, they also are commonly clogged with [[Piranha Plant]]s. On occasion, the player would have to use Warp Pipes as platforms to traverse from one area of a level to another. In ''Super Mario Bros. 3'', Warp Pipes serve basically the same purpose. However, the [[Pipe Land|seventh world of the game]] is a massive maze of Warp Pipes that the player has to maneuver through. Certain Warp Pipes would take the player further into the level, while others would send them backwards. In almost all main ''Mario'' games, and in most role-playing games, Warp Pipes serve exactly the same purpose. In ''[[Mario Party 7]]'', there is the [[Warp Pipe Orb]], which serves as a road block; any player that would pass over the space it had been set on would be teleported to a previous location in the board.

!POW Block
----
[[File:NSMBW POW Block Artwork.png|145x145px]]
|Beginning from the ''Mario Bros.'' arcade game, [[POW Block]]s have made great appearances in the ''Mario Series''. One bump under it and every enemy on the ground will be stunned, though for [[Shellcreeper]]s, and later [[Spiny|Spinies]], it's not as easy. [[Sidestepper]]s must be hit twice to be stunned. [[Fighter Fly]]s can only be stunned on the ground, as mentioned before). Usually, a POW Block can be used 3 times, so players must use it wisely. In their debut, they appear in most phases. In ''Super Mario Bros. 2'', they appear again, but are red instead. It can now be picked up, and are only used once. This also defeats any enemy on the screen. In ''Super Mario World 2: Yoshi's Island'', it appears too but enemies on the screen will change into [[Star]]s with feet that can be counted to the counter. It also appears in every ''Paper Mario'' game, doing damage to all enemies on the ground or ceiling. They also appear in ''Mario Kart Wii'' and ''Mario Kart: Arcade GP 2''; in these games, when it is hit every other racer will be spun out.

!Roulette Block
----
[[File:Roulette Block SM3DL.png|145x145px]]
|[[Roulette Block]]s are types of blocks first found in ''[[Super Mario World]]''. They are blocks that contain items that scroll in a roulette from four different items: a Super Mushroom, Fire Flower, [[Cape Feather]] and Star. However, once [[Mario]] or [[Luigi]] releases the item out of the block, it bounces around like a Star, and it still changes from power-up to power-up. Mario or Luigi can grab the power-up at the right time to obtain that power-up's effects. After ''Super Mario World'', can contain a [[Super Leaf]], a [[Blue Shell]], a [[Penguin Suit]] or a [[1-Up Mushroom]]. In ''New Super Mario Bros. 2'', it introduce a yellow numbered Roulette Block. The numbers that appear are 5, 10, 30 and 50, with 50 appearing only once per interval, but a lot often in the coin Roulette Blocks, including the larger one with a maximum number of 100 coins, instead of 50, in the [[Rainbow Course]]s.

!P-Switch
----
[[File:P-Switch NSMB2.png|145x145px]]
|The [[P-Switch]] is an object first appearing in ''Super Mario Bros. 3''. In the game, their most prominent ability allows them to be pressed to turn coins into brick blocks, and vice versa. They can also be used to make hidden coins, question blocks, and doors appear, and also make conveyer belts stop. In ''Super Mario World'', the switches returned. A new grey switch also appeared, which turned enemies into grey coins. Mario can also carry these switches around with him. Later, they appeared in ''New Super Mario Bros.'' and ''New Super Mario Bros. Wii.'' These switches are once again made stationary, and they only change coins into bricks and bricks to coins.
|<center>''[[Super Mario Bros. 3]]''</center>

!Jump Block
----
[[File:Music-block.png|145x145px]]
|[[Jump Block]]s are a special type of block which allow the player to leap onto them and bounce off them. They are also known to contain items, which are revealed when hit. They first appear in ''Super Mario Bros. 3'', where they let the player jump higher if they push the jump button right when they leaped off it. There is also a special type of pink Jump Block which transports the player to [[Coin Heaven]]. They later appear in ''Super Mario World'', where they serve the same purpose. Jump Blocks then appeared in ''[[Super Mario 3D Land]]'', where they  serve the same purpose as the previous installments. However, in this game, they  play a series of musical notes when jumped upon. In ''[[New Super Mario Bros. 2]]'', they appear again the same as in ''[[Super Mario 3D Land]]''.
|<center>''[[Super Mario Bros. 3]]''</center>

!Goal Pole
----
[[File:Flagpole NSMB Wii artwork.png|145x145px]]
|[[Goal Pole]]s (originally known as "Flagpoles") have appeared in seven ''Mario'' platformers so far: ''Super Mario Bros.'', ''Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels'', ''New Super Mario Bros.'', ''New Super Mario Bros. Wii'', ''Super Mario 3D Land'', ''[[New Super Mario Bros. 2]]'' and ''[[New Super Mario Bros. U]]''. Ever since their first appearance in ''Super Mario Bros.'', their purpose is to mark the end of a normal level. In the platformers predating ''New Super Mario Bros.'', it is possible to leap over the flagpole and go beyond the end of the level. Depending on the player's position on the flagpole, they will earn extra points, varying from 100 to 8000, and if they reach the top, they will earn an extra life. In ''New Super Mario Bros.'' and ''New Super Mario Bros. Wii'', there are [[Red Flagpole]]s which mark secret exits. Originally, the flagpole had a skull on the flag, but it was replaced by the Bowser logo. In ''New Super Mario Bros.'', it is possible to smash the flagpole as Mega Mario, filling up one space on the gauge above.

!Vine
----
[[File:Beanstalk.png|145x145px]]
|[[Vine]]s are a type of vine used as ladders in the ''Mario'' series. They originally were just vines, but, as of ''Super Mario Bros.'' 3, the tops of the Vines resemble the head of a Piranha Plant. They are contained within [[Brick Blocks]] (Rotating Blocks in ''Super Mario World''). Vines first appeared in ''Super Mario Bros.'', where, once activated, Mario could climb them and go to secret areas like [[Coin Heaven]]. In ''Super Mario Bros. 2'', the Vines were already there; nothing was needed to activate them. In ''Super Mario Bros. 3'', Vines went back to being in blocks. They were also different colors, based on the world that they were in. They went back to their normal green in ''Super Mario World''. In ''New Super Mario Bros.'' and ''New Super Mario Bros. Wii'', they could grow downward if the player hit a block they spawned from by ground pounding. In ''New Super Mario Bros. Wii'', there is a Vine appearing on the map in World 7 if the player locates the secret exit for the [[fortress]]. This vine would allow the player to access a level on a separate cloud, which, once beaten, allowed the player to access a Vine leading into the castle that led right to the boss door.

!Fire Bar
----
[[File:Firebar SPM.png|145x145px]]
|Debuting in ''Super Mario Bros.'', the [[Fire Bar]] is a common reoccurring obstacle. They often appear in Castle levels. Fire Bars rotate around a block in a clockwise or counter-clockwise direction, damaging the player if they are touched. After appearing in ''Super Mario Bros.'' they would return in other platformers such as ''New Super Mario Bros.'' and ''Super Mario 3D land''. Fire Bars are also featured in a few of the 3D games such as ''Super Mario 64''. They are used on Bowser Jr.'s [[Boomsday Machine]] as part of its weaponry. Fire Bars also appear in the ''[[Paper Mario (series)]]''; here, they are once again  obstacles for the player. However, they can easily be avoided if Mario flips into 3D. Later items like the [[Ball 'n' Chain]] have a similar movement and attack method of the Fire Bar.
