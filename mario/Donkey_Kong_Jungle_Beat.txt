'''''Donkey Kong Jungle Beat''''' is a [[Nintendo GameCube]] 2.5D platform game starring [[Donkey Kong]]. The game is developed by [[Nintendo EAD]], and it was released in 2004 in Japan and in 2005 in other regions. Like the [[Donkey Konga (series)|''Donkey Konga'' series]], ''Donkey Kong Jungle Beat'' utilizes the [[DK Bongos]] as a controller option. In 2009, ''Donkey Kong Jungle Beat'' was ported to the [[Wii]] as a ''[[New Play Control!]]'' title, focusing on the use of motion controls via the [[Wii Remote]].

''Donkey Kong Jungle Beat'' received "generally favorable" reviews across both its releases.<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":1" /> Most reviewers considered it unique and fun to play but rather short given the retail price. It was praised for its wide appeal; possessing both a simplicity targeted at new gamers, and a complex, skill-oriented combo system to attract more hardcore gamers. ''IGN'' praised the GameCube version's graphics, saying that "DK's fur makes ''Star Fox Adventures'' models look primitive".<ref name=":3" /> Criticisms include the game's short length, despite the replay-ability offered by the score system in levels.<ref name=":2" /> There were also complaints of repetitive boss fights, as the same four bosses are used several times throughout the game with limited features to distinguish them.<ref name=":2" /> One of the primary criticisms, however, was the lack of past characters and elements from [[Rare]]'s ''[[Donkey Kong Country (series)|Donkey Kong Country]]'' games. Despite these criticisms it was rated the 95th best game made on a Nintendo System in ''[[Nintendo Power]]'''s Top 200 Games list.<ref>"[https://archive.org/details/nintendo-power-issue-198-december-2005/page/74/mode/2up NP Top 200]". ''Nintendo Power''. Vol. 198. December 2005. p. 74.</ref>

Non video-game publications also praised the GameCube version. ''The New York Times'' gave it a favorable review and called it "wildly entertaining".<ref>Herold, Charles (March 17, 2005). "[https://www.nytimes.com/2005/03/17/technology/circuits/17game.html?ei=5070&en=5b67354ebe161766&ex=1111726800&adxnnl=1&adxnnlx=1111123389-VGlNys9Mb5dhrGJWbjiEIw&_r=0 Realism on the Tracks, Surrealism in the Jungle]". ''The New York Times''. [https://web.archive.org/web/20230212140521/https://www.nytimes.com/2005/03/17/technology/circuits/realism-on-the-track-surrealism-in-the-jungle.html Archived] from the original on February 12, 2023. Retrieved March 30, 2014.</ref> ''The Sydney Morning Herald'' gave it four stars out of five, praising the graphics, environments and gameplay, but complained of the game's short length.<ref name=":5" /> ''Detroit Free Press'' gave it three stars out of four and complimented the use of bongos in the game.<ref name=":4" /> ''Common Sense Media'' gave the Wii version four stars out of five. Although positive to the game, they considered more as rental than purchase due to its short length.<ref>Sapieha, Chad (May 7, 2009). "[http://www.commonsensemedia.org/game-reviews/new-play-control-donkey-kong-jungle-beat ''New Play Control! Donkey Kong Jungle Beat'' Game Review]". ''Common Sense Media''. ==Trivia==
*The game was known for being the first to have an E10+ rating, both for a ''[[Mario (franchise)|Mario]]''-related game and in general.
* This is the first ''Donkey Kong'' game since ''[[Donkey Kong (Game Boy)|Donkey Kong]]'' for the Game Boy to not feature [[Diddy Kong]].
