==''Mario'' game appearances==
===''Mario Kart'' series===
====''Super Mario Kart''====
[[File:SMK Coin Item Box Sprite.png|frame|left]]
In ''[[Super Mario Kart]]'', coins are found on each course. Collecting them makes the [[Kart]]s' top speed faster, though they reach their maximum top speed with ten. When a player jostles another driver, falls off the course, or gets hit by an item (such as a [[Red Shell|Red]] or [[Green Shell]]), the player loses one, two, or four coins (unless they have less than that), respectively. Jostling a driver without having any coins results in sliding. Coins also appear as items which give the player two extra coins.

====''Mario Kart: Super Circuit''====
In ''[[Mario Kart: Super Circuit]]'', coins increase top speed and weight, this time without any cap. There is an option in this game to remove the coins, but it is only applicable to Quick Run and VS mode. There are 50 coins scattered in every track, and up to 5 coins are given to the player at the beginning of the race depending on their starting position, thus the player can have up to 55 coins in a race, since the Coin item is missing. Unlike the first game, an alarm will go off if the player has 1 or 0 coins.

====''Mario Kart DS''====
[[File:MKDS Coin.gif|frame|A Coin seen in ''Mario Kart DS''.]]
In ''[[Mario Kart DS]]'', coins are present only in Mission Mode, where players have missions to get all the coins in a course. They are varied and they can be formed either in a pattern in a battle course or a single line in a race course. The fourth boss fight of that mode, which pits Peach against [[King Boo]], requires her to collect 50 coins while she avoids him. Any hazards can cause the player to drop coins. In these missions, a unique music track exclusive to this mode replaces the usual music played on that course.

====''Mario Kart Arcade GP 2''====
In ''[[Mario Kart Arcade GP 2]]'', coins can be collected in races, similar to ''Super Mario Kart'' and later games. They make the player speed up. Coins can be gained/lost by jostling other racers.

====''Mario Kart Wii''====
''[[Mario Kart Wii]]'' features coins in the [[Coin Runners]] battle game. Players collect them by driving into them, but if they get hit by an item, the players lose coins. The team who has the most coins after the three minutes are up wins.

There were various online tournaments where players had to collect a set number of coins to finish the mission.

====''Mario Kart 7''==== 
In ''[[Mario Kart 7]]'', coins return during races, working in a similar way as they do in ''Super Mario Kart''. Unlike that game, however, coins collected will respawn on the track seconds later, racers can only have up to ten coins per race, and jostling other racers does not affect one's coin count or cause skidding if one does not have any coins. Also, racers will always lose three coins if they are hit by items or fall off the track (excluding being hit by [[fireball]]s, which makes them drop one coin, and if they have two or less). In addition to improving a [[Kart]]'s top speed, collecting a coin also causes a very small speed boost. Starting with this game, every 50 coins still collected at the end of the race will unlock kart parts for the player's vehicle. A glitch causes it to be possible for the player to end the race with more than 10 coins, as long as they don't get hit before finishing, then all coins exceeding the limit will be "destroyed". This exploit allows it to be possible to unlock a new kart part every time a player completes a cup. The Coin Runners Battle Mode also returns, but with a two-minute time limit and it can be played solo; the race courses' max coin limit also applies in this mode.

====''Mario Kart 8'' / ''Mario Kart 8 Deluxe''====
Coins return in ''[[Mario Kart 8]]'' and ''[[Mario Kart 8 Deluxe]]'', serving the same purpose as in ''[[Mario Kart 7]]''. Unlike in that game, players can once again hold more than ten coins, though only in Coin Runners in ''Mario Kart 8 Deluxe''. Vehicle parts are unlocked by collecting coins from races, Time Trials, online races, and battles (the latter only in ''Mario Kart 8 Deluxe''). Coins collected by additional players are counted (only in the original game). Coins will count towards the player's coin total only for completed matches — if the player collects coins then quits during a match, the coins from that race will not be added to their coin total. The Coin item from ''Super Mario Kart'' also returns, working as it did in that game. Due to an update in August 2014, it is now possible to view the exact number of coins collected through matches. 

On [[Hyrule Circuit]], coins are replaced with Rupees, on [[Animal Crossing]], they are replaced with Bells, and on [[Urchin Underpass]], they are replaced with Cash, the currencies of the series of origin (''The Legend of Zelda'', ''Animal Crossing'', and ''Splatoon'', respectively). On [[Mute City]] and [[Big Blue]], coins are not present on the track, but are instead obtained either from driving over recharge strips on the track, knocking them out of rival racers, or from Item Boxes. On [[Yoshi's Island (race course)|Yoshi's Island]], they are replaced by the [[Coin (Yoshi franchise)|Coins]] of the [[Yoshi (franchise)|''Yoshi'' franchise]].

====''Mario Kart Tour''====
Coins appear in ''[[Mario Kart Tour]]'', where they work in a similar manner to previous entries. In this game, the counter displays more than ten coins per race, like in ''Super Mario Kart'' and ''Mario Kart: Super Circuit'', and coins from Item Boxes are automatically used. However, coins do not provide any speed advantages to the player during races. The player is given points for every coin they get in a race. [[Red Coin|Red]] and [[Blue Coin]]s also appear in [[Coin Rush (Mario Kart Tour)|Coin Rush]] and various bonus challenges, and are worth two and five coins, respectively, as in ''[[Super Mario 64]]''. Up to 300 coins can be earned from races daily, or 600 if the user is subscribed to the Gold Pass. The [[Coin Box]] item also releases many coins at once. The Coin Box+ boost also allows Red Coins to be released by the Coin Box. Coins can be used in the shop to buy [[characters|drivers]], [[kart]]s, [[glider]]s, [[item ticket]]s and [[point-boost ticket]]s. Players can send Greeting Coins to their friends once every day, which gives them five coins. If a player does not collect the coins given by their friend, the coins will begin to stack.

During events, coins are usually replaced by [[event token]]s and Team Rally tokens. Some of the coins released from the Coin Box are also turned into event tokens. The Red Coins created by Coin Box+ boost are also replaced by event tokens.<ref>HalfHydra (September 2, 2021). [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OCk5VLpszEg Myth: Are Red Coins Overwritten?] ''YouTube''. Retrieved September 13, 2021.</ref>

[[Glider]]s such as the [[? Block (glider)|? Block]] increase the chance of getting coins from Item Boxes and points from collecting one.

===''Mario vs. Wario''===
In ''[[Mario vs. Wario]]'', 100 coins can be collected for an extra life, like in the ''Super Mario'' series. Each coin is worth only 10 [[point]]s.

===''Super Mario RPG: Legend of the Seven Stars''===
Coins appear in ''[[Super Mario RPG: Legend of the Seven Stars]]'' where they act as currency for buying items, armor, weapons and accessories. There are two sizes of coins: small ones are worth one, and big ones are worth ten. Coins are found from defeating enemies or in chests. The maximum amount of coins that can be held is 999.

There are also a much rarer variant, [[Frog Coin]]s, which serve as their own type of currency.

===''Mario Party'' series===
Coins are needed to win a game in the [[Mario Party (series)|''Mario Party'' series]]. Without coins, the player cannot purchase [[Star (Mario Party series)|Stars]], which are the main items necessary to win. Coins are the second most important item to collect because, in the ending, if at least two player characters finish with the same number of Stars, the winner is determined by who has the most coins.

Coins can be acquired by landing on a [[Blue Space]], certain [[Event Space|Happening Space]]s, and [[Donkey Kong Space]]s. Also, ten coins are acquired every time a player wins a [[minigame]] or when the game begins. Coin minigames exist, which can make the player receive a certain number of coins depending on the coins found during said game. Coins can also be acquired via battle minigames, where a set amount of coins has to be wagered by each character. Once the minigame has ended, the number of wagered is distributed to the players, depending on how well they do in the minigames.

In ''[[Mario Party-e]]'', the minigame [[Waluigi's Reign]] involves [[Mario]] having to collect Coins and [[Red Coin]]s thrown by Waluigi while also avoiding the [[Hammer]]s thrown by him.

In ''[[Mario Party 9]]'', due to the difference in gameplay, coins do not appear and are replaced by [[Mini Star]]s

In ''[[Mario Party: Island Tour]]'', Coins appear in the minigame [[Deck Hunt]].

In ''[[Mario Party 10]]'', Coins serve their original purpose in the [[amiibo]] Party mode, though they are still absent from the game's Mario Party and Bowser Party modes.

===''Paper Mario'' series===
====''Paper Mario''====
[[File:Coins Endless Desert.png|thumb|left|Mario and Sushie get Coins from a defeated [[Bandit]] in ''Paper Mario''.]]
In ''[[Paper Mario]]'', Coins are fairly common, appearing whenever an enemy is defeated. The number of Coins depends on the difficulty of the fight, and the rare [[Amazy Dayzee]]s drop many coins when defeated. Coins can also be found inside [[? Block]]s in ranging quantities, as well as inside bushes, trees, and other hidden areas. To get a single coin from a tree, Mario has to use his [[Hammer]] to make the tree shake so that the single coin falls down. To find the single coin in a bush, Mario simply tries to search a bush when he goes near one. Coins can be used to purchase items and [[badge]]s at the different [[Item Shop]]s. Mario can carry up to 999 Coins.

====''Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door''====
[[File:The Ancient Coin Zone.png|thumb|Mario, [[Yoshi Kid]], and [[Flavio]] get some Coins in the [[Palace of Shadow]] in ''Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door''.]]
In ''[[Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door]]'', Coins are obtained through methods similar to the previous game, including as drops from enemies as well as inside bushes, trees, ? Blocks, and hidden areas. Coins in hidden areas often come in stashes. Amazy Dayzees also return and yield a very high coin reward. In addition to the returning methods, an [[audience]] member may throw a Coin at Mario or his partner during a battle. The game retains a maximum carrying capacity of 999 coins. Fleeing from battles causes Mario to drop Coins, as before.

Mario starts the adventure with 100 coins. On the way to [[Professor Frankly]]'s house, a [[Bandit]] runs by and swipes 50 of them. Mario can chase this Bandit to his house in the back alleys of [[Rogueport]]'s middle sector to retrieve the stolen funds. Initially, [[Gus]] guards access to the east side of Rogueport at first, where the [[Robbo Gang]]'s hideout is located. If Gus is defeated, he flees, nullifying the fee.

Coins are typically used to buy items at Item Shops, as well as during certain missions and sidequests. Using the services of [[Merluvlee]], [[Merlee]], [[Wonky]], [[Grifty]], [[Charlieton]], and [[Chet Rippo]] requires a fee, and cancelling requests at the [[Trouble Center]] costs 30 coins.

Whenever Mario and his partners win a fight in the [[Glitz Pit]], [[Jolene]] awards them with coins. The amount increases as their rank rises; however, if they lose, [[Jolene]] grants a single Coin as a participation award.

====''Super Paper Mario''====
[[File:Mario Coin Card.png|thumb|left|Mario discovering coins while in 3D in ''Super Paper Mario''.]]
In ''[[Super Paper Mario]]'', Coins are found scattered across the terrain, as well as inside ? Blocks and [[Coin Block]]s. There are also secret areas containing Coin stashes. In some cases, [[flip]]ping into 3D reveals additional Coins that are not visible in 2D.

Coins are also obtained by defeating enemies; generally, the stronger the enemy, the more Coins it drops. Amazy Dayzees reappear in this game, retaining their high Coin reward. Unlike previous titles, fleeing from an enemy does not cause the player to lose Coins due to the game's shift toward platformer-style gameplay.

Coins are used to buy items from shops and to purchase [[Tiptron]] during the post-game. They can also be generated by the [[Happy Flower (item)|Happy Flower]] or earned by selling items. The game retains a maximum carrying capacity of 999 Coins, and any Coins collected beyond the limit are lost.

According to the [[Sammer Guy]] warrior [[List of Sammer Guys#Footsteps of Coins|Footsteps of Coins]], he is the source of the Coins found throughout the universe, having dropped them on purpose. However, as this claim is merely part of his opening speech, the statement is dubious. His dialogue makes frequent references to Coins.

====''Paper Mario: Sticker Star''====
[[File:Coin Celebration.png|thumb|Mario and Kersti celebrating at the raining coins in ''Paper Mario: Sticker Star''.]]
In ''[[Paper Mario: Sticker Star]]'', Coins have a closer resemblance to cardboard and paper. They are scattered across the terrain and can be discovered inside ? Blocks, bushes, trees, and secret areas. Mario can also get Coins by defeating enemies in battle, as usual, and achieving a Perfect Bonus by finishing a battle in a single turn without taking damage awards a larger sum determined by the difficulty of the fight. Additional coins can be produced if an enemy is repeatedly struck by a [[Jump]]-based attack, an [[Eekhammer]], or specific [[Thing]] stickers after their HP is reduced to zero, releasing coins with each successive hit.

When Mario collects a [[Comet Piece]], a shower of Coins rains down for him to collect, with the total volume dependent on how many enemies Mario defeated during the level, rewarding a higher defeat count with more currency. In the [[Ice Flow]] area, Coins slide down the frozen waterfalls, and gathering 1000 of them triggers a chime sound that precedes a [[Big 1UP]] sticker sliding down the ice. [[Large Coin]]s also make an appearance and supply Mario with 5 or 10 Coins based on the number depicted on the face.

Coins are used to purchase Items and Stickers at [[Sticker Shop]]s, operate the [[Battle Spinner]], pay the enemies bullying the [[Traveling Toad]] to rescue him, or buy Things from the [[Shady Toad]]. In a departure from previous games in the series, Mario does not lose Coins when fleeing from battle. The inventory limit has been upgraded to 9999 Coins.

====''Paper Mario: Color Splash''====
[[File:Roshambo Temple Coin Win.png|thumb|left|Mario gets Coins from his victory at a Roshambo Temple in ''Paper Mario: Color Splash'']]
In ''[[Paper Mario: Color Splash]]'', coins serve as a common collectible item situated along the terrain and hidden inside bushes, trees, Question Blocks, and secret areas. Large Coins also make a reappearance. The maximum capacity is still 9999 Coins, matching the limit established in ''Paper Mario: Sticker Star''. The main purpose of Coins is to buy [[Battle Card (Paper Mario: Color Splash)|Battle Cards]] or [[Battle Card (Paper Mario: Color Splash)#Things|Things]], or to use the [[Battle Spin]].

Combat presents several methods for accumulating Coins. Enemies drop Coins upon defeat, and earning a Perfect Bonus by finishing a battle in a single turn without taking damage awards extra currency based on the difficulty of the encounter. Additional Coins are produced if an enemy is continuously attacked with Jumps, Eekhammers, or multi-attacks after their HP reaches zero. During battles won within the [[The Emerald Circus]], the audience awards Coins that are subsequently collected by [[Huey]].

Various levels include specific events related to currency. Obtaining a Mini Paint Star provides a Coin reward that increases based on the number of enemies defeated within that level. In the [[Violet Passage]], Mario must utilize [[Cannonball]]s to collect a minimum of 25 Coins suspended above the ocean waters, and he can win Coins as prizes in the [[Roshambo Temple]]s. [[Mondo Woods]] has a [[Giant Coin]] drained of its color, and it cannot be collected even with the [[Paint Hammer]], until the Yellow [[Big Paint Star]] is retrieved from [[Iggy Koopa]] at the [[Golden Colosseum]]. However, when the color is restored to the Giant Coin, it is revealed to be worth only a single Coin, much to Huey's disappointment.

On [[Fortune Island]], Coins function as a weapon used by [[Wendy O. Koopa]]. When Wendy tosses Coins at Mario, he adds them to his inventory but sustains one point of damage from the force of the throw, unlike other games. Near the end of the battle, Wendy utilizes a [[Candy Ring]] to immobilize Mario without any means of defense before raining Coins down on him for a fatal one-shot kill. To survive this technique, Mario must use the Camera to generate a photograph of Wendy that stands in front of him as a shield. The Candy Ring ensnares the photo instead of Mario, allowing the picture to take the attack so Mario can resume the battle.

====''Paper Mario: The Origami King''====
[[File:Coins Treasure Beach.png|thumb|Mario finds Coins by the Beach Caves in ''Paper Mario: The Origami King''.]]
In ''[[Paper Mario: The Origami King]]'', coins can be earned from winning battles or from the overworld through the usual methods. In addition to normal coins, there are 10-Coins, 100-Coins, and 1,000-coin bags. The maximum capacity of Coins is 999,999 coins, much higher than in previous entries.

Coins can also be used to increase the time limit during battles, exchanging 10 coins for one second, and to cheer to help solve a ring puzzle, up to 999 coins for maximum effect.

In [[Shogun Studios]], coins are referred to as "gold bits".

===''Mario Golf'' series===
Coins appear in Coin-collecting challenges on courses in ''[[Mario Golf: Toadstool Tour]]'', ''[[Mario Golf: World Tour]]'', and ''[[Mario Golf: Super Rush]]''. They make up various formations, from basic shapes like circles, squares, and triangles, to unique ones like clouds, fishes, and clovers. In these challenges, when selecting where to aim the shot, the Coins spin faster when the ball's path will go into them, and it attracts the Coins as it flies close to and through them.

====''Mario Golf: Toadstool Tour''====
In ''[[Mario Golf: Toadstool Tour]]'', Coins take their ''Super Mario 64'' appearance, and the challenges are in the form of regular games, where the player must collect as many Coins as possible while finishing on or below par.

====''Mario Golf: World Tour''====
In ''[[Mario Golf: World Tour]]'', Coins return to their regular form, and are awarded to the players for completing challenges, finishing rounds, and as prizes for finishing tournaments. Coins can be used to purchase golf gear in the Castle Club, and are seen in Mario Golf challenges and Character Costume Challenges. These challenges are unique that they do not act like regular rounds, therefore there are no flags and cups on the greens. Instead, without having to worry about sinking the ball, players collect a set amount of Coins by hitting the ball through them in two strokes less than the hole's par. In Quick Round, the Coins may or may not appear on the course when playing on them. But when going on practice rounds through the Castle Club, the Coins are always there. Obtaining Coins in any way adds them to the player's coin total.

====''Mario Golf: Super Rush''====
In ''[[Mario Golf: Super Rush]]'', Coins act as energy instead of items, and they are lost if the player swings too hard after [[Wario]]'s Lightning Blast. In Speed Golf, they are lost from the same condition as regular golf, but they can also be lost if they are hit by a special shot that knocks balls away. They also appear in [[Mario Golf: Super Rush#Battle Golf|Battle Golf]] mode where they are lost if the player makes contact with water, gets crushed by a [[Thwomp]], gets eaten by a [[Chain Chomp]], gets stomped by a [[Whomp]], gets hit by a [[Bob-omb]], slips on a [[Banana]], or makes contact with a special shot that knocks away balls.

===''Mario & Luigi'' series===
====''Mario & Luigi: Superstar Saga'' / ''Mario & Luigi: Superstar Saga + Bowser's Minions''====
[[File:Coin Block Jump.png|thumb|left|Mario jumps to get a Coin from a Block in ''Mario & Luigi: Superstar Saga''.]]
[[File:Coin Block Lagoon.png|thumb|right|Mario and Luigi hit a Block for Coins in ''Mario & Luigi: Superstar Saga + Bowser's Minions''.]]
In ''[[Mario & Luigi: Superstar Saga]]'' and ''[[Mario & Luigi: Superstar Saga + Bowser's Minions]]'', [[Beanbean Coin]]s serve as the primary currency, while standard coins are restricted to the introductory [[Mushroom Kingdom]] section. Players accumulate coins by hitting objects like [[? Block]]s or defeating enemies. The [[Gold Beanie]] notably awards a large prize upon defeat. [[Minigame]]s also provide coins, including those hosted by [[Thwomp (Mario & Luigi: Superstar Saga)|Thwomp]] in the [[Hammerhead Bros.]] cave. A specific exploit allows the brothers to generate currency if [[Luigi]] stacks on top of [[Mini-Mario]] for a [[High Jump]]; when Mario jumps, Luigi bounces and produces a Coin, though this action drains [[HP]]. Coins are required to purchase [[Item]]s, [[Badge]]s, and Gear from shops or to pay for challenges within the [[Winkle Colosseum]]. Using the [[Flee]] command in battle causes Mario and Luigi to drop coins while they escape. The maximum wallet capacity is 9999 Coins in the original game and 9,999,999 Coins in the remake.

Early in the adventure at [[Stardust Fields]], [[Mario]] earns three Coins by stomping on [[Bowser]] to dislodge him from a [[Cannon]], only for [[Tolstar]] to appear and demand 100 Beanbean Coins for the release of Bowser. No matter how many coins Mario and [[Luigi]] have, the exchange rate always translates to 10 Beanbean Coins, and Tolstar orders the Mario Bros. to find 100 Coins around Stardust Fields.

[[Prince Peasley]] later makes a bet of 99,999,999,999,999 Mushroom coins with Mario and Luigi over who would find the pieces of the [[Beanstar]] first. Mario and Luigi win, and [[Prince Peasley]] rewards them with the promised coins. However, since they are Mushroom coins, the exchange rate translates into only 99 [[Beanbean coin]]s.

====''Mario & Luigi: Partners in Time''====
[[File:Coin M&LPIT sprite.png|frame|left]]
[[File:Coin Air Spin.png|thumb|The Baby Bros. spinning for Coins in ''Mario & Luigi: Partners in Time''.]]
Regular coins return in ''[[Mario & Luigi: Partners in Time]]''. They are acquired by hitting [[Question Block]]s and defeating enemies, with the [[Gold Koopeleon]] providing a substantial number of Coins. Players can also win currency in minigames or collect Coins suspended in the air by utilizing the [[Baby Spin]] ability to reach them. As in ''Mario & Luigi: Superstar Saga'', these accumulated Coins are utilized to purchase items, badges, and gear at Item Shop, and they are also required to participate in specific minigames. The Flee command still causes the Mario Bros. to lose Coins as they exit a battle, although equipping the [[Wallet Badge]] significantly reduces the number of coins lost in the process. The Mario Bros. can collect a maximum number of 9,999,999 Coins.

In some places, there are larger yellow coins and blue coins that can only be obtained by spinning [[Baby Mario]] and [[Baby Luigi]] through the air and into the [[tornado]], making them soar farther and descending to collect the coins. [[10-Coin|10 Coin]]s, [[20 Coin]]s and [[100-Coin]]s also appear, and they are worth ten, twenty, and one hundred coins each, respectively.

====''Mario & Luigi: Bowser's Inside Story'' / ''Mario & Luigi: Bowser's Inside Story + Bowser Jr.'s Journey''====
[[File:Coin Trash Pit.png|thumb|right|Mario gets a Coin from the Trash Pit in ''Mario & Luigi: Bowser's Inside Story''.]]
[[File:Coin Pump Works DX.png|thumb|left|Mario and Luigi getting Coins from the Block in ''Mario & Luigi: Bowser's Inside Story + Bowser Jr.'s Journey''.]]
Coins return in ''[[Mario & Luigi: Bowser's Inside Story]]'' and ''[[Mario & Luigi: Bowser's Inside Story + Bowser Jr.'s Journey]]'' as collectible items that are obtainable from ? Blocks as well as through events, minigames, and side-quests. The maximum capacity of coins is still 9,999,999 Coins. Using the Flee command still causes Coins to be dropped in the process, including for [[Bowser]].

Coins can still be defeated by defeating enemies in battle. Both [[Trashure]]s and [[Dark Trashure]]s offer large Coin rewards, and [[Bowser]] can successfully use the [[Vacuum Block|Vacuum]] ability to inhale Coins from either of these enemies while they are open. Furthermore, [[Broque Monsieur]] and [[Broggy]] release Coins when they are struck during their respective battles.

Unique rules regarding currency apply during the encounter with [[Midbus]]. When Bowser executes an excellent hit on Midbus, he earns a Coin reward immediately. If Midbus successfully lands an attack on Bowser, Midbus receives Coins along with [[food]] that heals whomever reaches it first.

Coins are primarily used to purchase items, gear, and badges from the various Item Shops. Coins are also necessary to buy a replacement battery for the [[Monty Bros.]]'s [[Power Drill]].

====''Mario & Luigi: Dream Team''====
[[File:Pillo Lift Coin Event.png|thumb|left|Mario and Luigi collecting Coins on the Elevator in ''Mario & Luigi: Dream Team''.]]
In ''[[Mario & Luigi: Drean Team]]'', Coins can be found all over [[Pi'illo Island]], typically in ? Blocks as well as being acquired by defeating enemies in battle. Gold Beanies still yield a substantial Coin reward upon defeat, being a core characteristic of theirs. Coins are also obtainable through minigames, specific events, and sidequests. Larger Coins, with values up to 100, can be found in hard-to-reach places as rewards. Coins are utilized to purchase items, gear, and badges from Item Shops, and they are required to pay for stays at the [[inn]]s located in [[Pi'illo Castle]] and [[Wakeport]]. Additionally, the sound effect for collecting coins has been altered to a unique chime rather than the classic audio cue that originated in ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]''. The maximum amount of Coins was increased to 99,999,999 Coins.

For the first time, the Mario Bros. do not lose Coins upon [[flee]]ing from battle, a change that was retained in later installments of the {{series|Mario & Luigi}}.

====''Mario & Luigi: Paper Jam''====
[[File:Coin Dash Paper.png|thumb|right|Mario, Luigi, and Paper Mario dashing through the Flowers for some Coins.]]
In ''[[Mario & Luigi: Paper Jam]]'', Coins are items typically collected from ? Blocks or by defeating enemies in battle, especially from the shiny variants introduced in the game. Coins are also obtained as prizes from certain events, minigames, and side-quests. Furthermore, they can also be obtained through specific means that include dashing through [[Spinny Flower]]s using the [[Dash Socks]] or by digging them up through [[Bean Hole]]s. Coins can be used to purchase items, gear, and [[Battle Card (Mario & Luigi: Paper Jam)|Battle Card]] from the different Item Shops. The maximum number of Coins that can be collected is still 99,999,999 Coins.

===''Mario Baseball'' series===
====''Mario Superstar Baseball''====
In ''[[Mario Superstar Baseball]]'', Coins appear in [[Challenge Mode (Mario Superstar Baseball)|Challenge Mode]] and [[Toy Field]].

In Challenge Mode, Coins are required to purchase items at the shop, such as [[Star Skill]]s or single-use power-ups that remain effective for only one game of baseball.

In Toy Field, Coins serve as the primary victory condition, as four characters compete to collect the most Coins. Coins can be obtained by recording an RBI or landing on the Hit spaces within the field. It is also possible for a character to steal Coins from an opponent if they acquire an item by landing the baseball on a ? Space, with the specific amount stolen depending on which item the roulette stops on.

====''Mario Super Sluggers''====
In ''[[Mario Super Sluggers]]'', Coins retain their primary functions in both Challenge Mode and Toy Field, although Challenge Mode now offers a wider variety of purchasing locations, with a specific store situated in each stadium across the overworld. In Toy Field, coins are obtained differently than before, as players can increase their Coin count or steal funds by successfully shooting an item at an opponent.

===''Super Princess Peach''===
[[File:Ladida Plains Coin House.png|thumb|Peach in a house of Coins in ''Super Princess Peach''.]]
Coins return as a frequent collectible item within [[Super Princess Peach]]. They are distributed across the main land and placed inside [[Block]]s, while others remain hidden within specific niches and secret areas. They can also be earned by completing the minigames [[Toad Jump]], [[Toad Tote]], and [[Toad Shot]]. Because the game does not utilize a traditional life system, collecting currency does not grant [[Princess Peach]] extra lives. Instead, Coins are exchanged at the [[Shop (Super Princess Peach)|Shop]] to purchase new moves, power-ups, and bonus content. Standard Coins possess a value of one unit, whereas [[Large Coin]]s are worth five Coins.

The mechanics of combat change if Peach buys the [[Courage Soda]] from the Shop. Under the influence of this item, any enemies she attacks using [[Perry]] release [[Large Coin]]s, and striking multiple enemies conclusively may also generate [[Small Coin]]s. Passing through the [[Goal Gate]] at the conclusion of a level provides an additional opportunity to earn Coins depending on the symbol obtained. Securing a [[Turnip]] grants one Coin, a [[Mushroom]] provides 10 Coins, a [[Smiley Flower]] yields 30 Coins, and a [[Starman]] awards 50 Coins. In the specific instance that Peach lands on the Star symbol on seven levels in a row, the game awards a bonus of 777 Coins.

===''Mario Hoops 3-on-3''===
[[File:MH3o3Coin.png|frame]]
Coins can be obtained by dribbling the ball on the [[? Panel]]s on the ground in ''[[Mario Hoops 3-on-3]]''. The more coins the player obtains, the more points the player can earn if they make a basket. Coins can also be obtained by rubbing the stylus before slam dunking, but it is risky and the player is vulnerable when they do that. Each team can carry up to 100 coins.

===''Mario Sports Mix''===
[[File:MSM 1-1 Hockey.png|thumb|200px|The coin in ''Mario Sports Mix'' as the puck for a hockey game.]]
Coins yet make another appearance in ''[[Mario Sports Mix]]''. Their uses vary between sports and they can be obtained for scoring in the sports [[Basketball]], [[Volleyball]], and [[Hockey]]. Another use for it is to raise the attack power in [[Dodgeball]], and one is used as a puck in Hockey.

===''Mario Tennis Open''===
Coins are awarded for playing Special Games in ''[[Mario Tennis Open]]''. They appear in two of them. One of those is [[Galaxy Rally]], where players earn two Coins when they hit the ball at a [[Star Chip]], and when more are hit consecutively, the number of Coins given increases by two. Hitting the ball at a [[Launch Star]] awards the player with five Coins, and when it is hit more times in a row, the number of Coins it gives increases by five. Coins are also seen in [[Super Mario Tennis]], where hitting the ball at them on the screen on the wall extends the time. Coins are used to buy tennis gear in the Clubhouse.

===''Captain Toad: Treasure Tracker''===
[[File:Coins CTTT.png|thumb|left|200px|Coins in ''Captain Toad: Treasure Tracker'']]
[[File:Gold Coin Treasure Vault.png|thumb|The Captain Toad Treasure Vault.]]
In ''[[Captain Toad: Treasure Tracker]]'', Coins return as a common collectible throughout the levels. Like in most platformers, if [[Captain Toad]] or [[Toadette]] manage to collect 100 Coins, they earn an extra life. Coins can be found all over along the terrain, including secret niches, and can also be earned by defeating enemies. There are also [[Hidden Coin]]s, which can be revealed by walking through them or touching them on the [[Wii U GamePad]]. Coins can also appear in [[Coin Pile|piles]], which provide even more coins when collected.

The game features some unique ways of acquiring Coins. The methods include tapping posters on walls; holding a [[Flaptor]] for a few seconds; using the touchscreen on the GamePad; defeating most enemies (except [[Bullet Bill]]s and [[Missile Bill]]s, which yield only one coin per [[Bill Blaster]]); throwing a [[Turnip]]; or collecting a [[Super Mushroom]] while in Super form, which awards 10 coins.

If Captain Toad or Toadette loses a life, they lose all their coins collected within that level, except in the levels [[Mummy-Me Maze]], [[Mummy-Me Maze Returns]], and [[Mummy-Me Maze Forever]]. The game also features various Bonus Objectives that require the Toads to collect a specified number of coins within a level.

==In other media==
===''Super Mario Bros.'' film===
Although coins are not given an on-screen appearance in the [[Super Mario Bros. (film)|''Super Mario Bros.'' film]], they are alluded to by an elderly woman mugging the Mario Bros., where she refers to them as Koopa Coins.

===''Super Mario'' Chess===
In the board game [[Super Mario Chess|''Super Mario'' Chess]], Coins are used as pawn pieces on the "heroes" side.

===''Monopoly Gamer''===
In the board game ''[[Monopoly Gamer]]'', coins replace the paper money used in standard Monopoly. Coins come in two denominations: "Golden Coins" worth one coin and "Five Coins," which are larger and worth five coins. Coins are made from either cardboard (in the Standard Editions of the game) or plastic (in the "Collector's Edition"). Coins are used to buy Properties on the board or in Auctions and pay Rent & Jail fees. Some portions of the game force players to drop a given number of coins on their current space (usually by a roll of a [[Red Shell]], [[Green Shell]], or [[POW Block]] on the "Power-Up Die"), where the next player who lands on or passes over that space can pick them up. At the end of the game, every five coins a player has (rounded down) earns the player 10 points toward their final score (points are also earned by Properties owned and Bosses defeated in the game). Two-coin sides also appear on the "Power-Up Die". Rolling the coins normally allows the player to collect three coins from the Bank, although certain characters may have a "Power-Up Boost" that enhances or changes this power-up.

===''The Super Mario Bros. Movie''===
In one scene in ''[[The Super Mario Bros. Movie]]'', [[Toad (species)|Toads]] can be seen collecting coins from booths with [[Coin Block]]s. After [[Toad]] bumps through the crowd and knocks a coin out of a Green Toad's hands, [[Mario]] picks it up out of curiosity and then mistakenly hands it to a Red Toad staring at him.
