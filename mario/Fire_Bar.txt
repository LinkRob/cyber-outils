'''Fire Bars''' (also formatted as '''Fire-Bars''',<ref>Hiroo Tochikubo, ''[[How to win at Super Mario Bros.]]'', Tokuma Shoten, 1987, ISBN 4-19-720003-XC. Page 15.</ref><ref>Tatsumi Yamashita, ''The Official Nintendo Player's Guide'', Tokuma Shoten, 1987, ISBN 999832369X. Page 30.</ref><ref>M. Arakawa. ''Super Mario All-Stars'' Player's Guide. Pages 9 and 31.</ref> '''fire bars''', or '''Firebars'''<ref name=Asks>[https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/zelda/1/0/ Iwata Asks - Volume 2 : The History of Handheld The Legend of Zelda Games]</ref> and sometimes known as '''Podoboo Cogs'''<ref>Hodgson, David S. J. ''Mario Kart Wii'' Premiere Edition. Pages 92 and 128.</ref>) are recurring obstacles in the {{franchise|Mario}}. They first appear in ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]''

==Description==
Fire Bars are rotating bars made of several [[fireball]] segments. They are usually attached to a block, which they rotate around, damaging [[Mario]] or [[Luigi]] on contact.

Fire Bars vary in size and can have many fireballs attached to the block, usually ranging from four to six, but in some cases, they can come in higher quantities such as 14. In the 3D games, Fire Bars are commonly seen spinning along the ground and many need to be jumped over. If Mario or another playable character is [[Invincible Mario|invincible]], they do not take damage from a Fire Bar and can safely phase right through them.

Fire Bars also appear in various spinoffs such as the ''[[Mario Kart (series)|Mario Kart]]'' and ''[[Paper Mario (series)|Paper Mario]]'' series. Over the years, similar obstacles have also been introduced such as the [[Roto-Disc]] in ''[[Super Mario Bros. 3]]'' and the [[Ball 'n' Chain]] and ''[[Super Mario World]]'', both functioning almost identically to Fire Bars.

In certain castles of ''[[Super Mario World 2: Yoshi's Island]]'', Fire Bars are depicted as swinging on a three-dimensional plane.

==Creation==
Fire Bars were originally created as obstacles for ''[[w:c:zelda:The Legend of Zelda|The Legend of Zelda]]'', but they ended up being used for the [[Mario (franchise)|''Mario'' franchise]] first.<ref>[https://www.glitterberri.com/a-link-to-the-past/the-men-who-made-zelda/ The Men Who Made Zelda – Staff Interview]. ''GlitterBerri's Game Translations''. May 28, 2011. Retrieved January 10, 2016.</ref><ref name=Iwata>[https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/nsmb/1/3/ Iwata Asks - New Super Mario Bros: Volume 2]</ref><ref name=Asks/><ref name=Prima>''Playing With Super Power: Nintendo Super NES Classics eGuide'', ''The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past'' 16 Bits Tab. "''The rotating Guruguru bars that appear in ''A Link to the Past'' originated in ''Super Mario Bros.'', where they’re called Fire Bars. However, they were originally intended for ''The Legend of Zelda''; the developers felt they looked better in a ''Mario'' game and passed them between titles.''"</ref>

==History==
===''Super Mario Bros.''===
[[File:SMB NES World 5-4 Screenshot.png|thumb|200px|Fire-Bars in [[World 5-4 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 5-4]] in ''Super Mario Bros.'']]
[[File:FireRodSMB.gif|frame|left]]
In ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]'', Fire-Bars are indestructible objects that rotate around a block in a clockwise or counter-clockwise direction. If [[Mario]] or [[Luigi]] touches a Fire-Bar, he takes damage from the fire. Fire-Bars often appear on the ceiling and floors of [[castle]]s, and the first level in which they appear is [[World 1-4 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 1-4]]. Some Fire-Bars are near [[? Block]]s, especially those containing a power-up item, making it harder for Mario or Luigi to hit. The first Fire-Bar in [[World 5-4 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 5-4]] is composed of 12 fireballs, as opposed to the normal six fireball length. In [[World 8-4 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 8-4]], Fire-Bars uniquely appear in an underwater section, and none of them are in the main castle section. In [[Hard Mode]], Fire-Bars appear in all possible areas in castles.
{{br}}

===''Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels''===
[[File:SMBLL World B-2 Screenshot.png|thumb|200px|A long Fire-Bar in [[World B-2]]]]
Fire-Bars also appear in ''[[Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels]]'', working as they do in ''Super Mario Bros.''. Unlike in that game, longer Fire-Bars, composed of 12 fireballs, are more common.
{{br}}
===''Super Mario 64''===
[[File:SM64 FireBar.png|thumb|left|200px|Fire bars in Lethal Lava Land in ''Super Mario 64'']]
[[File:Fire Arm 64.png|thumb|200px|A fire bar in the volcano in ''Super Mario 64'']]
In ''[[Super Mario 64]]'', fire bars<ref>M. Arakawa. ''Super Mario 64'' Player's Guide. Page 67. "''Time your jump from the pole so you don’t get hit by the spinning fire bar.''"</ref> appear for the first time in full 3D. They are designed as short, spinning [[flamethrower]]s, which is somewhat similar to their appearance in ''[[Super Mario World 2: Yoshi's Island]]''. Fire bars appear only in [[Lethal Lava Land]], including its [[Volcano]] sub-area. Unlike ''Super Mario Bros.'', fire bars move horizontally in circles. In some cases, there are two fire bars rotating around a single pivot. Fire bars also visibly burn Mario on contact, causing him to briefly run while losing a few wedges of his health.
{{br}}
===''Super Mario Advance 4: Super Mario Bros. 3''===
[[File:Swinging Bars of Doom.png|thumb|Fire Bars in Swinging Bars of Doom in ''Super Mario Advance 4: Super Mario Bros. 3'']]
While they do not appear in the main game, Fire-Bars do appear in certain [[World-e]] levels in ''[[Super Mario Advance 4: Super Mario Bros. 3]]'', more specifically in [[Swinging Bars of Doom]], [[An Aqueous Adventure]] and [[Classic World 1-4]]. They exist in two variations: either with one Fire-Bar around the axis, or with two Fire-Bars, both variations being of various lengths. In An Aqueous Adventure, where most of them are found underwater, there is even one with two pairs of Fire-Bars rotating in opposite directions.

===''Super Mario 64 DS''===
In ''[[Super Mario 64 DS]]'', Fire Bars retain the same role as in the original ''Super Mario 64''. The Fire Bar in the volcano has been removed. [[Luigi]] and [[Wario]] can become invulnerable to Fire Bars if they are under the effects of the [[Vanish Luigi]] and [[Metal Wario]] forms, respectively.

===''New Super Mario Bros.''===
[[File:NSMB World 8-Tower 2.png|thumb|200px|Fire Bars in ''New Super Mario Bros.'']]
[[File:Podoboo Cog NSMB.png|thumb|left|200px|The sole Fire Bar of Bowser's Castle in ''New Super Mario Bros.'']]
In ''[[New Super Mario Bros.]]'', Fire Bars first appear in [[World 6-4 (New Super Mario Bros.)|World 6-4]]. They also appear in the second tower of World 8, and a lone one appears in Bowser's Castle. Here they act just like they do in ''Super Mario Bros.'', and are once again found more commonly on ceilings and floors.
{{br}}
===''Super Mario Galaxy''===
[[File:SMG Sling Pod Firebar Area.png|thumb|left|200px|Fire Bars in the Sling Pod Galaxy in ''Super Mario Galaxy'']]
[[File:Fire Arms Cosmic.png|thumb|200px|Four Fire Bars traveling around an Empty Block in Bowser's Star Reactor in ''Super Mario Galaxy'']]
In ''[[Super Mario Galaxy]]'', Fire Bars appear as obstacles in a number of different galaxies, including the [[Sling Pod Galaxy]], the [[Toy Time Galaxy]], [[Bowser's Star Reactor]], and [[Bowser's Galaxy Reactor]]. They typically spin horizontally like in ''Super Mario 64'', though a few can be found spinning vertically. In Bowser's Galaxy Reactor, there are four Fire Bars rotating around an [[Empty Block]]. Like in ''Super Mario 64'', touching a Fire Bar burns Mario directly, causing him to run for a few seconds.
{{br}}

===''New Super Mario Bros. Wii''===
[[File:NSMBW World 8-C First Room.png|thumb|200px|A Fire Bar in ''New Super Mario Bros. Wii'']]
In ''New Super Mario Bros. Wii'', Fire Bars only appear in {{world-link|8|tower|World 8-Tower (New Super Mario Bros. Wii)|World 8-Tower}}, {{world-link|8|bowser|World 8-Bowser's Castle (New Super Mario Bros. Wii)|World 8-Castle}} and {{world-link|coin|1|World Coin-1 (New Super Mario Bros. Wii)}}. They are often positioned near [[Star Coin]]s, requiring Mario or another playable character to maneuver around them while attempting to obtain the Star Coin. A double Fire Bar also appears in {{world|8|tower}}. In the castle, they are mainly seen in the first room, and only two are seen in the second room.
{{br}}

===''Super Mario Galaxy 2''===
[[File:BowserGalaxyGenerator-LavaPlanet.png|thumb|left|200px|Fire Bars in Bowser's Galaxy Generator]]
In ''[[Super Mario Galaxy 2]]'', Fire Bars function identically as in ''Super Mario Galaxy''. They mainly appear in [[Bowser Jr.'s Fiery Flotilla]], [[Bowser's Gravity Gauntlet]], the [[Slipsand Galaxy]] (in a secret room), and [[Bowser's Galaxy Generator]]. Fire Bars are used during the boss battle with [[Bowser Jr.'s Boomsday Machine]] in [[Bowser Jr.'s Boom Bunker|his Boom Bunker]], where they are integrated directly into the Boomsday Machine.
{{br}}

===''Super Mario 3D Land''===
[[File:Firebars SM3DL screenshot.png|thumb|200px|Some Fire Bars in {{world-link|S8|crown|Special 8-Crown}}]]
Fire Bars reappear in ''[[Super Mario 3D Land]]''. Here, they appear in most castles and [[World 8-6 (Super Mario 3D Land)|World 8-6]]. As with all 3D installments of the ''Super Mario'' series, Fire Bars spin horizontally. The game introduces [[Spike Bar]]s, similar obstacles which have spiked balls in place of fireballs.

===''New Super Mario Bros. 2''===
[[File:NSMB2-WorldStar-7.png|thumb|left|Fire Bars in {{world-link|star|7|World Star-7 (New Super Mario Bros. 2)}}]]
Fire Bars appear again in ''[[New Super Mario Bros. 2]]''. They have the same pattern from both predecessors and may also sometimes hide Star Coins. Fire Bars only seem to be one fireball longer than previous games. They mostly appear in castle levels and some [[World 6 (New Super Mario Bros. 2)|World 6]] levels.
{{br}}

===''New Super Mario Bros. U''===
[[File:NSMBU Fire Bar Cliffs Screenshot.jpg|thumb|200px|Huge Fire Bars in Fire Bar Cliffs in ''New Super Mario Bros. U'']]
Fire Bars reappear in ''[[New Super Mario Bros. U]]'', where they appear in [[Screwtop Tower]] and [[Slide Lift Tower]]. They are based on their appearance in ''New Super Mario Bros. Wii'', although some of the Fire Bars spin a lot faster.

[[Huge Fire Bar]]s also make their first appearance. They are first encountered in Slide Lift Tower. Huge Fire Bars have an entire level based around them in [[Superstar Road]] called [[Fire Bar Cliffs]]. Despite the level title, no standard Fire Bars appear in Fire Bar Cliffs.
{{br}}

===''Super Mario 3D World'' / ''Super Mario 3D World + Bowser's Fury''===
[[File:Honeycomb Skyway.jpg|thumb|left|Fire Bars in ''Super Mario 3D World'']]
[[File:Small Fire Bars.png|thumb|200px|Fire Bars attached to ? Blocks in ''Super Mario 3D World'']]
Fire Bars appear in ''[[Super Mario 3D World]]'' and its [[Nintendo Switch]] port ''[[Super Mario 3D World + Bowser's Fury]]''. This time, some of them rotate around [[Brick Block]]s and [[? Block]]s. Fire Bars retain their usual indestructible properties. [[Spiked Roller]]s also appear with a similar function to the Spike Bars.
{{br}}

Fire Bars reappear as a course element in ''[[Super Mario Maker]]'', ''[[Super Mario Maker for Nintendo 3DS]]'', and ''[[Super Mario Maker 2]]''. Their length and direction can be adjusted. The center block has a new sprite in the ''Super Mario Bros.'' theme, and new sprites were made for the ''[[Super Mario Bros. 3]]'' and ''[[Super Mario World]]'' styles, as Fire Bars do not appear in either source game.

In ''Super Mario Maker 2'', they are absent from the ''[[Super Mario 3D World]]'' style, despite appearing in the source game.
{{br}}

===''Super Mario Run''===
Fire Bars reappear in ''[[Super Mario Run]]'', as obstacles in the levels [[Fire Bar Castle! Youch!]] and [[Bowser's Bob-ombing Run]]. In Fire Bar Castle! Youch!, a Fire Bar appears during the battle with the [[fake Bowser]].

===''Super Mario Bros. Wonder''===
Fire Bars reappear in ''[[Super Mario Bros. Wonder]]'', first appearing in [[Shining Falls Special Triple Threat Deluge]], and appearing in various levels afterwards, acting the same as in previous appearances.
