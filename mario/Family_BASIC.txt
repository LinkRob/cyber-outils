'''Family BASIC''' is an accessory for the [[Family Computer]] that allows users to create programs in {{w|BASIC}} programming language. It was created as a joint effort between [[Nintendo]], [[Sharp Corporation]], and [[Hudson Soft]]. Family BASIC more specifically utilizes its own variation of BASIC, NS-HuBASIC, with "NS-Hu" being a prefix abbreviation of the three respective companies' names. Family BASIC is a [[reissue]] of Playbox BASIC for the [[Sharp Nintendo Television]]. 

The first edition of the Family BASIC application cartridge is bundled with a computer style keyboard and instructional textbook. Family BASIC is comparable to the Commodore line of computers. User-generated BASIC programs could be saved on any cassette recorder, the standard one being the [[Famicom Data Recorder]].

The Family BASIC keyboard plugs into an expansion port on the front of the Famicom, and the Data Recorder can be plugged into the back of the keyboard labeled "SAVE" and "LOAD." A more recent method is using a computer or smart device's microphone and headphone jacks to record the analog signal from the keyboard and then play it back when the game is requesting data to be loaded.

On February 21, 1985, an updated version of the application cartridge, titled '''Family BASIC V3''', was released.

==Relevance to ''Mario''==
===Sprites===
There are 16 animated sprites built-in, many of which are sprites and tilesets reused from a few ''[[Mario (franchise)|Mario]]''-related [[Nintendo Entertainment System]] games, including ''[[Donkey Kong (video game)|Donkey Kong]]'', ''[[Donkey Kong Jr. (video game)|Donkey Kong Jr.]]'', ''[[Mario Bros. (video game)|Mario Bros.]]'', and ''[[Pinball]]''. It is possible to change the color of the sprites, which is shown in the manual, along with a tutorial on creating a [[Luigi]] sprite.

===Family BASIC V3 minigames===
[[File:FamilyBASICV3 Mario World.png|thumb|200px|Gameplay of Mario World]]
Four mini-games were included on Family BASIC V3. All of them utilize the ''Mario'' programming sprites, to some extent:

*Game 0 "Heart": There is an outline of a heart, and the player can fill it by either speaking or breathing into the second controller's microphone. When done, Mario and Lady will come together, in a recreation of a cutscene of Nintendo's 1979 arcade game, ''[[w:c:nintendo:Sheriff|Sheriff]]''.
*Game 1 "Penpen Maze": The object is for Penpen, who is the player's character, to collect numbers on a grid sequentially while avoiding two Sidesteppers. Penpen starts off with three traps, which can temporarily stun the Sidesteppers.
*Game 2 "Mario World": Mario has to collect [[apple]]s and numbers sequentially while avoiding two Nitanitas. The minigame takes place on a single-screen area with platforms, ladders, and springs. There is a [[time limit]], and if it runs out, it is a [[Game Over]].
*Game 3 "Star Killer": An endless space shooter on a wraparound screen. It supports either one or two players, who control Star Killer and Starship respectively. Some of the enemies encountered include Spinners, Fighterflies, Fireballs, and Achilleses.
