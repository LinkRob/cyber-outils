'''1-Up Mushrooms''' (alternatively '''1 up Mushrooms''',<ref>''Super Mario Bros.'' English instruction booklet, page 7.</ref> '''1UP Mushrooms''',<ref>''Super Mario 64'' English instruction booklet, page 18.</ref> or '''1-UP Mushrooms'''<ref>''Super Mario 64 DS'' English instruction booklet, page 29.</ref>), also called '''Extra Life Mushrooms''',<ref>[[Nintendo PlayChoice-10]] ''Super Mario Bros.'' instructions, page 2.</ref> are recurring items in the {{franchise|Mario}}. They first appear in ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]''

==Description==
1-Up Mushrooms have a green cap with white spots (originally orange caps with green spots). They are usually hidden in invisible [[Brick Block]]s, [[Question Block]]s or hard-to-reach areas. They grant whoever eats one an [[1-Up|extra life]] upon being collected, in some games, 1-Up Mushrooms are also used as a means of reviving those that are dead or have been KO'd. 1-Up Mushrooms can also be earned by winning them from minigames and other events. Originally, 1-Up Mushrooms were by far one of the rarest items and were critical to succeeding; however, more recent games have made 1-Up Mushrooms more common, making typical adventuring easier. 

Since their debut, there have been various types of [[Mushroom]]s with similar effects to the 1-Up Mushroom. These include the [[Life Shroom]] from the {{series|Paper Mario}} (which revives a fallen ally with some [[HP]]), the [[Life Mushroom]] (as well as the [[Life-Up Heart]]) which increases the [[Health Meter]]'s maximum health amount. The [[1-Up Super]] from the {{series|Mario & Luigi}} is a much stronger variety of the 1-Up Mushroom.

==Main appearances==
===''Super Mario Bros.''===
[[File:1-Up Mushroom SMB sprite.png|frame|left]]
[[File:1-Up Mushroom Classic.png|thumb|Mario finds a 1-Up Mushroom.]]
The 1-Up Mushrooms first appear in ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]'' In this game, they have green spots on a yellowish-orange mushroom cap. (In underground levels they have teal spots on a brown cap, as they share the same color palette as the green enemies like the Koopa Troopas.) They are usually found in [[Hidden Block]]s (with the first one being found in [[World 1-1 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 1-1]] before the first [[pit]]) or in areas where [[Mario]] must risk his life to acquire one. They are rather uncommon in this game; the ones in the worlds' first levels can only be earned by grabbing all the coins of the previous world's third course. (This rule does not apply in ''[[Super Mario Bros. Deluxe]]''.) In [[World 1 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 1]] and [[World 8 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 8]], however, there are two hidden 1-Up Mushrooms, more than Mario can find than any other world in the game.

1-Up Mushrooms are commonly found in the first level of each world and only one is found per level, they can be found in World 1-1, [[World 1-2 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 1-2]], [[World 2-1 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 2-1]], [[World 3-1 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 3-1]], [[World 4-1 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 4-1]], [[World 5-1 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 5-1]], [[World 6-1 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 6-1]], [[World 7-1 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 7-1]], [[World 8-1 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 8-1]], and [[World 8-2 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 8-2]].

If the player acquires 10 or more lives, the screen displays a crown and a symbol instead of the number.

===''Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels''===
[[File:1-Up Mushroom NES.png|thumb|200px|A 1-Up Mushroom found in ''Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels'']]
[[File:SMBTLL 1-Up Mushroom Sprite.png|frame|left]]
[[File:SMBLL 1-Up Mushroom Sprite 2.png|frame|left]]
The 1-Up Mushroom returns in ''[[Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels]]'', functioning the same as before. Like the Super Mushroom and the debuting [[Poison Mushroom]], the 1-Up Mushroom was updated to have eyes, which continue to be present in most installments afterwards. Due to the higher difficulty level, 1-Up Mushrooms are slightly less common than before.

===''Super Mario Bros. 2''===
{{multiframe|align=left|[[File:1-Up Mushroom SMB2 sprite.png]] [[File:1-Up Mushroom SMAS SMB2 sprite.png]] [[File:SMA 1-Up sprite.png]]}}
[[File:1-Up Mushroom Pluck.png|thumb|[[Princess Peach|Princess Toadstool]] finds a 1-Up Mushroom below the ground and grabs it.]]
While 1-Up Mushrooms are present in ''[[Super Mario Bros. 2]]'', these versions are also simply known as '''1 UPs'''<ref>''Super Mario Bros. 2'' NES instruction booklet, page 19.</ref> and look very different from the usual green-capped mushrooms. They have a solid red cap with "1 UP" written on their side, rather than having eyes. 1 UPs are found by pulling up a [[grass]], the game's equivalent to hitting a [[? Block]]. Though the method of acquiring a 1 UP is different, pulling up a [[grass]] with one below (the equivalent of hitting a [[? Block]] in the game), 1 UPs retain their exact function of granting an extra life when collected, but as with [[Mushroom]]s, they must be picked up first.

In ''[[Super Mario Advance]]'' only, a 1 UP can also be acquired by throwing three items at a [[bubble]] containing one.
{{br}}
===''Super Mario Bros. 3''===
[[File:1-Up Mushroom SMB3 sprite.png|frame|left]]
[[File:1-Up Mushroom Plains.png|thumb|A 1-Up Mushroom being discovered in ''Super Mario Advance 4: Super Mario Bros. 3''.]]
1-Up Mushrooms reappear in ''[[Super Mario Bros. 3]]'', once again as rare mushrooms in certain [[block]]s or in risky areas. Their behavior has not changed much, but they now have green-spotted white caps (light blue in artillery levels). Extra lives are also regularly obtained via the [[card]] system, as well as the [[Spade Panel]]s. [[Mega ? Block]]s in bonus rooms can also give three 1-Up Mushrooms.

In the [[Nintendo Entertainment System]] version only, a pure white 1-Up Mushroom appears in [[World 6-3 (Super Mario Bros. 3)|World 6-3]] due to sharing its palette with a [[vine]].

In the ''[[Super Mario All-Stars]]'' and ''[[Super Mario Advance 4: Super Mario Bros. 3]]'', 1-Up Mushrooms had their design updated to the standardized white-spotted green caps.

''Super Mario Advance 4: Super Mario Bros. 3'' allows the player to scan 1-Up Mushroom cards to gain extra lives as well, ranging from one to 100 extra lives. An unreleased switch card could also make every block with 1-Up Mushrooms give three of them instead (with the Mega ? Blocks giving nine of them), and the [[1UP Kinoko ga 3UP Moon ni kawaru!|blue switch card]] turns all 1-Up Mushrooms in normal-sized blocks into [[3-Up Moon]]s when activated.

===''Super Mario World''===
[[Image:1-Up Mushroom Flipper.png|thumb|A 1-Up Mushroom revealed in ''Super Mario World''.]]
[[File:1-Up Mushroom SMW sprite.png|frame|left]]
In ''[[Super Mario World]]'', 1-Up Mushrooms (also called '''Extra 1-Up Mushrooms'''<ref name=World>''Super Mario World'' English instruction booklet, page 8.</ref> or '''Extra Mario Mushrooms'''<ref name=World/>), return, functioning as they do in ''Super Mario Bros. 3'', though the game introduces their now-standard white-spotted green cap design.

1-Up Mushrooms can found in a more varied range of areas. Aside from ? Blocks and Hidden Blocks, 1-Up Mushrooms also appear in two other types of blocks, [[Flip Block]]s and [[Roulette Block]]s.

[[Fishin' Lakitu]]s also carry a 1-Up Mushroom as lure, and if Mario or Luigi acquires it, the Fishin' Lakitu starts behaving like a regular Lakitu, moving after Mario or Luigi while throwing [[Spiny|Spinies]] at him. If Mario of Luigi is riding [[Yoshi (character)|Yoshi]] and hits a ? Block containing an [[Yoshi Egg|egg]], a 1-Up Mushroom hatches from the egg instead of Yoshi. As with other power-up items, Yoshi can also collect a 1-Up Mushroom with his tongue.

1-Up Mushrooms are the main feature of [[1-Up Chamber]]s. In these areas, there are five horizontal floorboards, each with three ? Blocks within them. For each row, if Mario and Luigi jump at the ? Blocks in a certain order, which is always randomized, they earn a 1-Up Mushroom. If the order is incorrect, all the ? Blocks turn brown, and Mario or Luigi do not earn a 1-Up Mushroom for that row.

Getting 100 [[Goal Star]]s from [[Giant Gate]]s will send Mario to the [[Bonus game (Super Mario World)|bonus game]], where he must hit eight moving and scrolling blocks to match [[Super Mushroom]], [[Fire Flower]], or [[Star|Starman]] icons with the stationary one in the center. Every three-of-a-kind match will give him a 1-Up Mushroom, and he can win up to eight of them if all the icons match.

There is another item in ''Super Mario World'' that can grant Mario lives &mdash; the [[3-Up Moon]], which gives Mario three extra lives instead of one, but is even rarer than the 1-Up Mushroom and is only found in hidden areas, such as behind the Giant Gate in some levels.

[[File:SMWbetamushroom.gif|frame|right]]
A flying winged golden 1-Up Mushroom exists [[Super Mario World/Beta elements|unused]] in this game.

===''Super Mario 64''===
[[File:SM64 1-up.png|frame|left]]
[[File:1-Up Mushroom Secret.png|thumb|200px|A secret 1-Up Mushroom found in the wall of the fortress in [[Whomp's Fortress]] in ''Super Mario 64''.]]
1UP Mushrooms return in ''[[Super Mario 64]]'' as the only obtainable mushrooms. 1UP Mushrooms have a slightly different design, appearing green with yellow spots, and are presented as 2D graphics. 1UP Mushrooms appear in a number of areas, such as after going through or collecting a ring of coins or by [[climb]]ing to the top of certain [[pole]]s and [[tree]]s. Most 1-Up Mushrooms slide away from Mario, whilst others follow him until he collects it. Other 1UP Mushrooms are idle, and Mario can simply touch one to collect it. There is also a 1UP Mushroom in the room of the [[Mushroom Castle (Super Mario 64)|Mushroom Castle]] with the [[Jolly Roger Bay]] [[painting]], in the opening within the wall on the left. There are also three 1UP Mushrooms at the top of the Mushroom Castle that Mario can get by using the [[cannon]] to go there. In some courses, some 1UP Mushrooms may appear in a small field of [[Flower (Super Mario series)|flowers]]. Touching or punching certain [[Butterfly|butterflies]] may also spawn 1UP Mushrooms, although some spawn homing bombs instead, similar to [[Big Steely|Big Steelies]]. 1UP Mushrooms appear inside certain [[! Block|yellow block]]s during some [[mission]]s.

===''Super Mario Sunshine''===
[[File:1-Up Mushroom Sunshine.png|thumb|left|200px|Artwork of a 1-Up Mushroom for ''Super Mario Sunshine'']]
[[File:1-Up Mushroom River.png|thumb|[[Mario]] near a 1-Up Mushroom above the [[Poisoned Water|Poison River]] in ''Super Mario Sunshine''.]]
1-Up Mushrooms are seen again in ''[[Super Mario Sunshine]]'' as the only obtainable type of mushroom once more. As usual, [[Mario]] can gain an extra life by collecting one, but the 1-Up Mushroom now also refills [[F.L.U.D.D.|FLUDD]]'s [[water]] supply as well as Mario's [[Health Meter]]. 1-Up Mushrooms are uncommon, appearing mainly in bonus courses or in paths accessed through [[manhole]]s. Certain [[crate]]s also contain a 1-Up Mushroom, which Mario can obtain by destroying it. Furthermore, 1-Up Mushrooms can appear during the final battle by defeating a [[Gold Bullet Bill]].

Similar to ''Super Mario 64'', some 1-Up Mushrooms move away from [[Mario]] upon being discovered whilst others do not move. Unlike before, most 1-Up Mushrooms are seen hovering idly in the air.

===''Super Mario 64 DS''===
[[File:1-Up Mushroom Roof Party.png|thumb|[[Mario]] near one of the 1-Up Mushrooms on the roof of [[Princess Peach's Castle|Mushroom Castle]] in ''Super Mario 64 DS'']]
[[File:SM64DS 1-Up Mushroom.png|frame|left]]
In ''[[Super Mario 64 DS]]'', the 1-Up Mushrooms function mostly the same as in the original ''Super Mario 64'', but their appearance was updated to reflect their standard appearance in recent releases of the time, such as ''Super Mario Sunshine'' and ''[[Mario Kart: Double Dash!!]]''. 1-Up Mushrooms were given some similarities to their appearances in 2D games of the ''Super Mario'' series, namely how "1-Up" displays briefly onscreen whenever a 1-Up Mushroom is collected, but [[Yoshi (character)|Yoshi]] can now also acquire 1-Up Mushrooms using his tongue, as in ''Super Mario World''.

The game adds some 1-Up Mushrooms not found in the original version, while others have been relocated. The [[Super Mushroom]] was also added as a power-up item, meaning that 1-Up Mushrooms are no longer the sole type, and a 1-Up Mushroom appears once eight objects have been destroyed in the temporary [[Mega Mario|Mega]] form resulting from the Super Mushroom. From there, the player's character gets a 1-Up for every subsequent object they destroy.

===''New Super Mario Bros.''===
[[File:NSMB 1-up.png|frame|left]]
1-Up Mushrooms are seen again in ''[[New Super Mario Bros.]]''. They retain most of their role from earlier 2D entries of the ''Super Mario'' series, granting an extra life if collected by Mario and Luigi. 1-Up Mushrooms are relatively common, unlike before.

As usual, the standard way of obtaining 1-Up Mushrooms is primarily from in [[Hidden Block]]s and other risky areas, they can also be found in regular [[? Block]]s, especially those supposedly out of place. 1-Up Mushrooms also have a chance of spawning from a [[Red Flying ? Block]], but only if Mario and Luigi have fewer than 99 lives. A 1-Up Mushroom can also be obtained by grabbing eight [[Red Coin]]s from a [[Red Ring]] while under the effects of a [[Fire Flower]], [[Blue Shell]], or a [[Mega Mushroom]].

One notable method for acquiring 1-Up Mushrooms occurs after the effects of the Mega Mushroom wear off. Up to five 1-Up Mushrooms can spawn from the top of the screen, depending on how much of the gauge—which is divided into five segments and displayed on the top screen while the Mega Mushroom is active—has been filled with light green as Mega Mario or Mega Luigi destroy objects.

Another notable method is that several 1-Up Mushrooms can be obtained in the [[Green Toad House]]s. These 1-Up Mushrooms appear from cards which can include the following: a 1-Up Mushroom Card, three 1-Up cards, one card with three 1-Ups; one card doubling the amount of 1-Ups already collected; and a card with [[Bowser]]'s face on it. The game ends once a card with Bowser's face is hit or if it has not been collected but the other five have. The 1-Up blocks already bashed grant extra lives.

===''Super Mario Galaxy''===
[[File:SMG 1-Up Mushroom.png|thumb|130px|left|1-Up Mushroom from ''Super Mario Galaxy''.]]
[[File:1-Up Mushroom Surf.png|thumb|A 1-Up Mushroom on the surfing track of Loopdeeloop Galaxy in ''Super Mario Galaxy''.]]
1-Up Mushrooms return in ''[[Super Mario Galaxy]]'', once again granting Mario or Luigi an extra life upon being obtained. 1-Up Mushrooms are rather common, often appearing in niches or in risky locations, like near a [[black hole]]. 1-Up Mushrooms can also be found close to [[Life Mushroom]]s (which increase the plumbers' maximum health) and at the bottom of certain planets. A 1-Up Mushroom can also appear by collecting all the [[Music Note]]s spawned by a [[? Coin]]. Large enemies such as [[Mega Gringill]]s also leave behind a 1-Up Mushroom upon defeat. In various [[galaxy|galaxies]], [[Lumalee]] also sells 1-Up Mushrooms (alongside Life Mushrooms) for 30 [[Star Bit]]s. [[Mailtoad]] also delivers Mario [[letter]]s from [[Princess Peach]] that contains five 1-Up Mushrooms (20 1-Up Mushrooms when playing as Luigi, although the letter is still addressed to Mario).

The [[Comet Observatory]] has five 1-Up Mushrooms:

*Inside a crate by the [[Starshroom]] at the Garage.
*Below the Library along the moving platforms circling the [[Beacon]].
*Underneath the stairs leading to the Kitchen.
*Underneath the island where the Gate is located (can only be reached with [[Flying Mario]]).
*At the very top of the Comet Observatory on the pointy tip (can only be reached with [[Flying Mario]]).

===''New Super Mario Bros. Wii''===
[[File:NSMBW 1-Up Mushroom Icon.png|frame|left|]]
[[File:1-Up Mushroom Room.png|thumb|right|Mario and his friends discover a 1-Up Mushroom in ''New Super Mario Bros. Wii''.]]
1-Up Mushrooms return in ''[[New Super Mario Bros. Wii]]'', working identically as in ''New Super Mario Bros.''

1-Up Mushrooms are pretty rare in the levels and are usually well hidden in Hidden Blocks or specific ? Blocks. They can also be obtained by getting all eight [[Red Coin]]s from [[Red Ring]]s when in [[Fire Mario|Fire]], [[Ice Mario|Ice]], [[Propeller Mario|Propeller]], or [[Penguin Mario|Penguin]] form. If a [[Yoshi (species)|Yoshi]] eats five [[Berry|Berries]] while the playable characters are under the effects of a Propeller Mushroom or [[Penguin Suit]], a 1-Up Mushroom appears for them to collect. 1-Up Mushrooms can also spawn from [[Roulette Block]]s. There is an esoteric method of acquiring 1-Up Mushrooms by having Mario or another playable character land on eight [[Para-Beetle]]s or [[Heavy Para-Beetle]]s in a row without touching the ground, which causes a 1-Up Mushroom to drop from the sky.

The [[Toad House|Green Toad House]]s return with a new minigame, [[1-up Blast]], in which the playable characters must hop into cannons and shoot at balloons with numbers ranging from one to four, each giving the same number of lives when popped.

===''Super Mario Galaxy 2''===
[[File:1-Up Mushroom Trio Secrets.png|thumb|left|''Super Mario Galaxy 2'' screenshot of secret 1-Up Mushrooms at the end of the first planet in the [[Slipsand Galaxy]] during the second mission.]]
1-Up Mushrooms return in ''[[Super Mario Galaxy 2]]'', where they are slightly more common than in ''Super Mario Galaxy'' and retain their usual role of granting an extra life upon being collected. Just as before, 1-Up Mushrooms can be found in various locations, ranging from open fields to secret and risky areas. They can also be made to appear by collecting all the [[Music Note]]s spawned from a [[? Coin]], usually three of them. They can also be obtained by defeating larger enemies such as [[Mega Gringill]]s. [[Mailtoad]] once again gives Mario or Luigi letters from [[Princess Peach]] containing five 1-Up Mushrooms. A [[Bee]] from the [[Honeybloom Galaxy]] also gives them a 1-Up Mushroom in a letter (delivered by Mailtoad) after they obtain the "Bumble Beginnings" [[Power Star]]. [[Lumalee]] once again sells 1-Up Mushrooms (alongside [[Life Mushroom]]s) for 30 [[Star Bit]]s, this time on [[Starship Mario]]. Furthermore, if Mario or Luigi already has six wedges of [[Health Meter|health]] from a [[Life Mushroom]], collecting another Life Mushroom grants a 1-Up instead. [[Teleporter (Super Mario Galaxy 2)|Teleporters]] can also send Mario or Luigi to distant planets where they must eliminate all the enemies present within a time limit. If all enemies are defeated, three 1-Up Mushrooms will appear for the plumbers to collect. Additionally, [[Chance Cube]]s feature a 1-Up Mushroom icon on two sides; rolling this icon will cause a 1-Up Mushroom to spawn. [[Lucky Cube]]s also have single 1-Up Mushroom icons, in addition to triple and quintuple 1-Up Mushroom icons. Rolling these will spawn three and five 1-Up Mushrooms, respectively.

===''Super Mario 3D Land''===
[[File:1-Up Mushroom Real VS Fake.png|thumb|Two 1-Up Mushroom cardboard cutouts and a real one in ''Super Mario 3D Land''.]]
1-Up Mushrooms return in ''[[Super Mario 3D Land]]'' with the same function as before. 1-Up Mushrooms can also be seen among [[wooden cutout]]s of 1-Up Mushrooms. 1-Up Mushrooms are also much more common, being found in [[? Block]]s, small niches, higher areas, and risky areas. Getting five [[Red Coin]]s from a [[Red Ring]] can also reward Mario or Luigi with a 1-Up Mushroom. 1-Up Mushrooms can also be found within the [[Mystery Box]]es.

A different version of the 1-Up sound plays if the player has 999 or 1109 lives while collecting a 1-Up Mushroom.
{{br}}
===''New Super Mario Bros. 2''===
[[File:1-UpMushroomNSMB2.png|thumb|left]]
[[File:1-Up Mushroom Skies.png|thumb|A 1-Up Mushroom from a block in ''New Super Mario Bros. 2'']]
1-Up Mushrooms return in ''[[New Super Mario Bros. 2]]''. They are once again just as common as they before and fulfill their usual purpose of granting Mario or Luigi an extra life when collected. 1-Up Mushrooms can be found in [[? Block]]s and [[Hidden Block]]s. Other ways to earn 1-Up Mushrooms include getting eight [[Red Coin]]s via a [[Red Ring]] while under the effects of a [[Fire Flower]], [[Gold Flower]], [[Mini Mushroom]], [[Super Leaf]], or [[Invincibility Leaf]]. Also, destroying the [[Goal Pole]] while under the effects of a [[Mega Mushroom]] rewards three 1-Up Mushrooms to Mario or Luigi. The Green Toad Houses contain a minigame that involves using a punching glove to knock 1-Up Mushrooms fro the upper platform to collect them afterward.

In [[Coin Rush]], all 1-Up Mushrooms are replaced with [[Gold Mushroom]]s, which provide 50 bonus [[Coin]]s.
{{br}}
===''New Super Mario Bros. U'' / ''New Super Luigi U'' / ''New Super Mario Bros. U Deluxe''===
[[File:1-Up Mushroom Beginnings.png|thumb|Mario and his friends discover some 1-Up Mushrooms at the start in ''New Super Mario Bros. U''.]]
1-Up Mushrooms are seen yet again in ''[[New Super Mario Bros. U]]'' as well as ''[[New Super Luigi U]]'' and ''[[New Super Mario Bros. U Deluxe]]''. In these games, they function identically to earlier entries of the ''New Super Mario Bros.'' sub-series. Just as before, they can be found in [[? Block]]s and [[Hidden Block]]s. 1-Up Mushrooms can also be obtained by getting all eight [[Red Coin]]s from a [[Red Ring]] while under the effects of a [[Fire Flower]], [[Ice Flower]], [[Penguin Suit]], [[Propeller Mushroom]], [[Mini Mushroom]], [[Super Acorn]], or a [[P-Acorn]]. 1-Up Mushrooms can also be obtained by eating 5 [[Berry|Berries]] as [[Yoshi (character)|Yoshi]] while under the effects of a Penguin Suit, Propeller Mushroom, Super Acorn, or a P-Acorn.

Following the lead of the three previous ''New Super Mario Bros.'' titles, 1-Up Mushrooms are also included as part of a unique minigame in Green Toad Houses. Here, they are featured as 1-Up Mushroom Balloons, similar to ''New Super Mario Bros. Wii'', and each one collected rewards one extra life.

In ''New Super Mario Bros. U Deluxe'' only, all 1-Up Mushrooms within blocks are replaced by [[3-Up Moon]]s if the player is playing as [[Toadette]].

===''Super Mario 3D World'' / ''Super Mario 3D World + Bowser's Fury''===
[[Image:1-Up Mushroom High Up.png|thumb|left|Some 1-Up Mushrooms atop the building in ''Super Mario 3D World''.]]
1-Ups Mushrooms return in ''[[Super Mario 3D World]]'' and its enhanced port ''[[Super Mario 3D World + Bowser's Fury]]'' as rather common items. They have the same function as previous games and are based specifically upon their appearance in ''Super Mario 3D Land''. 1-Up Mushrooms are commonly found in secret areas or other risky locations. They can also be obtained by grabbing eight Red Coins from a Red Ring. Certain characters also give 1-Up Mushrooms upon being interacted with.

1-Up Mushrooms do not appear in the ''[[Bowser's Fury]]'' mode of ''Super Mario 3D World + Bowser's Fury'' since it does not feature a lives system.

=== ''Super Mario Maker'' / ''Super Mario Maker for Nintendo 3DS''===
[[Image:1-Up Mushroom Maker.png|thumb|A 1-Up Mushroom in a closed off alcove in ''Super Mario Maker''.]]
1-Up Mushrooms appear in ''[[Super Mario Maker]]'' and ''[[Super Mario Maker for Nintendo 3DS]]'' as a placeable course element in any style. They give the player an extra life only if they are playing a world in [[Coursebot]], the 10 Mario Challenge, and the 100 Mario Challenge. In the 10 Mario Challenge or 100 Mario Challenge, up to three 1-Up Mushrooms can be obtained and added to a counter in the top left where [[Mario]] receives the mushrooms he collected upon clearing the course.

=== ''Super Mario Maker 2'' ===
1-Up Mushrooms reappear in ''[[Super Mario Maker 2]]'' as a placeable course element. It acts the exact same as in the previous game, but its behavior in Story Mode and [[Super Mario Maker 2#World Maker|Super Worlds]] is the same as it was in [[Coursebot]], and its behavior in [[Endless Challenge]] is the same as it was in the 10 Mario Challenge and 100 Mario Challenge. If the night effect is applied and the ground theme is selected, they transform into [[Rotten Mushroom]]s.

=== ''Super Mario Bros. 35'' ===
1-Up Mushrooms appeared in ''[[Super Mario Bros. 35]]'' in the same locations as in ''Super Mario Bros.''. Since there were no lives in this game, the mushrooms gave 20 coins instead, the price to use the Item Roulette once. The Hidden Blocks containing 1-Up Mushrooms were always present, without having to meet any unlocking conditions, and would reappear if the same course was played more than once in the same match.

=== ''Super Mario Bros. Wonder'' ===
1-Up Mushrooms reappear in ''[[Super Mario Bros. Wonder]]'', functioning the same as they do in past ''Super Mario'' games.

[[Prince Florian]] gives the player five 1-Up Mushrooms after receiving a [[Game Over]]. [[Flower coin]]s can be used to buy 1-Up Mushrooms in [[Poplin Shop]]s.
