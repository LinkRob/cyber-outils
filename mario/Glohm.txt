'''Glohm''' is a toxic energy or substance from ''[[Mario & Luigi: Brothership]]''. It is one of the main weapons of the [[Zokket Army]], and is first mentioned in a cutscene where [[Zokket]], during his first visit to [[Shipshape Island]], mentions its name and describes its role in his plan to destroy [[Concordia]]. The [[Zokket Army]] weaponises this dangerous substance for just that.

== Description ==

=== As a Substance ===
Glohm is a red, volatile vapour that has the appearance of fire. Anyone infected by Glohm will be tinted red, and will become seek isolation; at high-enough dosages, the victim becomes more violent, as [[Bowser]] exemplifies. The disease can be cured by feeding the victim with an Ampberry.

=== As a Status Effect ===
Attacks by Glohm-infected monsters can cause the [[Mario (character)|Mario]] [[Luigi|Bros.]] to get infected by Glohm. Should Glohm infect a Bro., combo moves, [[Special Attack|Special Attacks]], and Luigi Logic [[Prohibited Command|cannot be performed as though the other brother were dead]]. Curing Glohm with a [[Refreshing Herb]] may fail half the time, but an [[Ampberry]] always cures it. [[Zokket]], the [[Soli-Tree Flower Monster|flower monsters]] in the [[Soli-Tree]] created when [[Seedlusa]]s bury their bodies into the floor, and [[Reclusa]] can also give the status effect with their attacks.

== Known Victims ==
* [[Mario (character)|Mario]] and [[Luigi]] -- May be infected if hit by certain attacks from enemies infected with the substance. [[Zokket]] can also cause this during the second half of his fight and turn the infected Bro. onto the one who is not infected.
* Several Citizens
* Bulbfish Island Guard -- Infected by a [[Mettozokettohei|Būmeranzokettohei]].
* [[Prince Chilliam]] -- Infected when the [[Extension Corps]] sneaks aboard [[Shipshape Island]]. His lovebird, [[Princess Burnadette]], is horrified by the transformation and requests that Mario and Luigi take away the infection. He is later cured via [[Ampberry]] thanks to another Concordian named [[Doctor Vulko|Vulko]].
* [[Slippenglide King]] -- Seen infected, later cured by when Doctor Vulko used his [[Ampberry|Ampberries]].
* Countless [[Slippenglide Island|Slippengliders]] -- Infected with one exception who is worried that the same fate would happen to him later.
* [[Furen]] -- Furen gets infected when the [[Extension Corps]] kidnaps [[Bowser Jr.]]; cured via Ampberry a few minutes later.
* [[Bowser]] -- Bowser gets zapped by [[Zokket]]'s Glohmatic Quad-Ray during the first raid on [[Fortress Zokket]] in an attempt to rescue his son from the latter. The resultant monstrosity is called "[[Glohm Bowser]]" during combat. He is cured after Glohm Bowser's destruction.
* [[Gorumbla]] -- Gorumbla gets infected by the virus during the endgame, causing much damage much to the wrath of [[Chief Willma]].

=== Glohm Enemies ===
Sometimes, enemies can be corrupted by this toxic substance. Enemies that get infected with Glohm have darker red tints and glowing eyes and stronger stats than their regular forms, and can gain unnatural abilities, such as forming a shield around their bodies which can be jumped on but breaks after one or two attacks (examples: [[Soreboar]], [[Snaptor]]), creating copies of themselves and shuffling around (not unlike [[Cackletta]] or [[Beta Kretin]]; examples include [[Magmad]]s and a spiked version of the [[Icead]] introduced in the game), launching a counterattack upon getting struck (examples: [[Bumbleshoot]], Parashoot, [[Guardarm]]), launching a fiery ring attack akin to the [[Ring Burner]], [[Ring Beamer]], or [[Pulse Beam]] when slain (examples: [[Seedle]], [[Boo]]), and/or getting a temporary stat boost (especially to [[Power|Attack Power]]; examples include [[Magikoopa]]s). Some Glohm-infused enemies may also lose their turn, telegraphed by a speech bubble over their head with an ellipsis (with the [[Curlup]] being an example). If the player defeats at least 150 total Glohm-infected enemies, the Glohm Buster challenge is fulfilled.

Defeating an enemy infected with Glohm may reveal Glohm Crystals, Glohm Fragments, and/or Glohm Dust along with regular loot drops.

== Behind the Scenes ==

=== Etymology ===
Glohm's name could come from the word "gloom" or "gloomy." It's also derived from Ohm, which is the unit of Electrical Resistance.

== Nomenclature ==
In Japanese, Glohm is called ''Zetsuentai'' ("insulator"; written ぜつえんたい in Hiragana, ゼツエンタイ in Katakana, and 絶縁体 in Kanji; influenced by ''zetsuen'' ("to break up with"; written 絶縁 in Kanji, ぜつえん in Hiragana, and ゼツエン in Katakana)). In Chinese, it's called ''Juéyuántǐ'' ("insulator"; written 絕緣體, influenced by ''juéyuán'' ("to break up with"; written 絕緣)). In Spanish, the substance itself is called ''Aislanéctar'', from ''aislar'' ("to insulate" or "to isolate") and ''néctar'' ("nectar"), with its victims having the suffix ''inconexo'' (or ''inconexa'' for victims referred to with feminine names, such as the [[Seedle]] (Milla); from ''conexo'' ("connected") with the ''in-'' prefix added as a negation), which is also used as the name of the eponymous status effect gained from it. Portuguese refers to Glohm as ''Sohmbra'', from ''sombra'' ("shadow," "shade") and the Ohm, a unit measuring resistance in a circuit. In Italian, Glohm, is called ''Isolàntium'', from ''isolante'' (present participle of ''isolare'' ("to insulate," "to isolate")) and the Latin suffix ''-ium''. French refers to it as ''Fléohm'', from ''fléau'' ("calamity," "disaster") and the Ohm, the unit of resistance in a circuit. Its German name, ''Ohmen'', comes from ''Omen'' ("omen") and the Ohm, the unit of resistance in a circuit, with the substance's victims gaining the prefix ''Ohminös'' (pun on ''ominös'' ("ominous") and, again, the Ohm). In Dutch, it is called ''Doemstroom'' (literally "doom current") with enemies it infects having the word ''Doem'' as a suffix (Such enemies thus have their names literally translate as "Doom X"; for example, a [[Seedle#Variants|Glohm-infected Seedle]]'s Dutch name, Doëm-Seedle, literally means "Doom Seedle.") In Korean, the substance itself is called ''Jeoryeonche'' ("insulator"; written 절연체), influenced by ''Jeoryeon'' ("to break up with"; written 절연; also used as a prefix for enemies suffering its effects).
