The '''Bumbleshoot''' is an enemy from ''[[Mario & Luigi: Brothership]]''. They are encountered on [[Twistee Island]].

== Description ==
Bumbleshoots have eight blue and cyan leaves comprising their body with the front leaf bearing a set of bulbous eyes, all eight of their leaves attached to a central core which has a green propeller made of four leaves that keeps them aloft.

== Behaviour ==
Undisturbed, Bumbleshoots will fly aimlessly, but they will swoop down to charge at anyone who comes too close to them.

=== Combat ===
Bumbleshoots [[Flying (Enemy type)|cannot be hit by ground-based weaponry due to their ability to fly]]. They can attack in the following manner:

* Bumbleshoots can zip around their target before charging from the side. If their final position is right in front of their target, the creature flies into him. This must be avoided by jumping. Jumping just as the enemy begins to charge results in the Bumbleshoot getting stomped. If their final position is in the back, the enemy flies overhead, and the target must stay on the ground.
* In their second attack, Bumbleshoots can swoop down at a Bro. from above, descending like a leaf. This attack can be countered by jumping with proper timing to land on them. However, jumping too early or too late will result in impact and damage incurred onto their target. They can also be dodged more safely by not jumping at all, although this means that Bumbleshoots will also be safe from damage.
Bumbleshoots can be encountered either with more of their own kind or with the [[Soreboar]] enemy.

Bumbleshoots are of Level 4, and have a [[Heart Point|health]] maximum of 32, an [[Power|attack power]] of 17, a [[defense]] of 7, and a [[Speed (Initiative)|speed]] of 17. Their deaths net six [[Experience Point|experience]], five [[Coin|coins]], and potentially a [[Refreshing Herb]] (1 in 25 chance), regular [[Nut]] (1 in 100 chance), or a Twistee Leaf (quarter chance) apiece.

== Variants ==

=== Parashoot ===
{{Infobox Enemy|name=Parashoot|first=''[[Mario & Luigi: Brothership]]'' (Nintendo Switch; November 7, 2024)|games=''[[Mario & Luigi: Brothership]]''|homeland=[[Concordia]]|located=[[Raynforst Island]]|species=Floating Plant Monster|related=Bumbleshoot|attacks=Swooping<br>Ramming|defeated=Jumping}}
A stronger version of the Bumbleshoot called the Parashoot can be found in Raynforst Island. They look the same, but with red and orange leaves instead of blue and cyan leaves. They also act the same in the overworld. In combat, Parashoots have the same zip-around-the-target attack performed by their Twistee Island counterparts, but their leaf glide attack must be countered by jumping on them rather than idling. Additionally, Parashoots can be accompanied by [[Invizilizard|Invizilizards]] instead of Soreboars.

The Parashoot is an enemy of Level 5, with a health maximum of 46, an attack power of 19, a defense of 8, and a speed of 17. When slain, it gives seven experience and seven coins, with the same non-coin rewards as the Bumbleshoot, except with the Twistee Leaf being replaced by a Raynforst Vine with a 20% drop rate.

=== Glohm Bumbleshoot ===
Later in the game (more specifically when the gang enters the [[Gulchrock Sea]]), Twistee Island gets forcibly torn away from [[Shipshape Island]] when [[Bowser]] attacks it during his battle against the [[Zokket Army]]. As a result, certain Bumbleshoots contract the Bond-destroying [[Glohm]] virus. These contaminated Bumbleshoots are stronger, and their attacks are faster and can spread the infection to the target. If it survives damage after getting hit by an attack, the Glohm-infused Bumbleshoot will immediately launch an attack on its attacker. When it [[Reclusa World|conquers Concordia]], [[Reclusa]] infects the Bumbleshoots with stronger Glohm dosages during its rampage, making the Glohm Bumbleshoot even stronger.

A Glohm Bumbleshoot is of Level 30 with 360 hit points, an attack power of 140, a defense of 75, and a speed of 131. When destroyed, it gives 386 experience. Its loot drop now has 52 coins, along with some Glohm Dust (25%), a Glohm Fragment (11%), or a Glohm Crystal (6%), along with the same non-coin loot drops as the regular Bumbleshoot. During the [[Reclusa World]] chapter of the game, the hit point value is increased to 623, and the rewards are increased to 991 experience and 150 coins.

=== Glohm Parashoot ===
After [[Reclusa]] awakens, it spreads Glohm all throughout Concordia, causing regular enemies to contract the virus. Parashoots are no exception to this rule, and Glohm Parashoots are the result, having the same counterattack gimmick that their Twistee Island counterparts use.

A Glohm Parashoot is of Level 39 with 900 hit points. When destroyed, it gives 953 experience and 144 coins.

== Behind the Scenes ==

=== Etymology ===
The Bumbleshoot was named after "bumbershoot," which is an older term for an umbrella, which it resembles. The Parashoot is named after a "parachute," referencing how it and the Bumbleshoot can act like one.

== Nomenclature ==

=== Bumbleshoot ===
In Japanese, Bumbleshoots are called ''Hoppu Sō'' (written ホップソウ), with ''Sō'' (written 草 in Kanji and そう in Hiragana) meaning "grass" and ''Hoppu'' being a transliteration of "hop." In Chinese, they are called "Nimble Grass," or ''Qīngkuài Cǎo'' (written 輕快草). Their Spanish name, ''Desesparabrote'', comes from ''desesperado'' ("desperate"), ''paracaidas'' ("parachute") or ''parasol'' ("parasol"), and ''brote'' ("shoot" or "sprout"). Portuguese calls them ''Guarda-broto'', or "Shoot guarder." In Italian, their name is ''Pestalante'', from ''peste'' ("pest"), ''petalo'' ("petal"), and either ''volante'' (present participle of ''volare'' ("to fly")) or ''petulante'' (present participle of ''petulare'' ("to annoy")). French translates their name as ''Volilas'', from ''vol'' ("flight") or ''volar'' ("to fly") and ''lilas'' ("lilac"). In German, they are called ''Powerflor'' ("Power Flower"). Korean translates their name as ''Poljjakpul'' ("hop grass"; written 폴짝풀).

=== Parashoot ===
In Japanese, Chinese, and Korean, Parashoots are called "Falling Grass" (Japanese ''Rakka Sō'' (written ラッカソウ), Chinese ''Luòxià Cǎo'' (written 落下草), Korean ''Nakhapul'' (written 낙하풀). In Spanish, they are called ''Parabrote'', which is formed from ''paracaidas'' ("parachute") or ''parasol'' ("parasol") and ''brote'' ("shoot"/"sprout"). The creatures have their named translated to ''Brotoquedas'' (portmanteau of ''broto'' ("shoot") and ''paraquedas'' ("parachute")) in Portuguese, and Italian calls them ''Petalante'' (portmanteau of ''petalo'' ("petal") and either ''volante'' or ''petulante'' (present participles of ''volare'' ("to fly") and ''petulare'' ("to annoy")). French calls them ''Volotus'' (from ''vol'' ("flight") and "lotus"). In German, their name is ''Sauerflor'', or "Mad Flower." Many of the names they got for the localisations are similar in formation to the Bumbleshoot, with the exception of the Portuguese name, which is the only one that is significantly different.
