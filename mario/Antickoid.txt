The '''Antickoid''' is an enemy from [[Donkey Kong Bananza|''Donkey Kong Bananza'']]. They are the first enemy type to be encountered by [[Donkey Kong (character)|Donkey Kong]].

== Description ==
Antickoids are purple four-legged ant-like monsters with golden antennae. They have a large crystal-like abdomen that can contain a variety of materials.

== Behaviour ==
Antickoids attack by chasing [[Donkey Kong (character)|Donkey Kong]] and trying to crush him with their abdomen. Donkey Kong can kill one with his punch, causing them to drop whatever material was in the abdomen.

== In ''Donkey Kong Bananza'' ==
Donkey Kong has his first encounter with Antickoids after his arrival on [[Ingot Isle]] in [[Donkey Kong Bananza|''Donkey Kong Bananza'']], where a group of them attacks some [[Prospector Monkey|Prospector Monkeys]].

=== Battle Challenges ===
The Lagoon Layer's second Battle Challenge features six Antickoids. In the Freezer Layer, five Antickoids are featured in the third Battle Challenge and six are featured in the fifth one. The Forest Layer's third Battle Challenge has five Antickoids. Eight are featured in the Tempest Layer's third battle challenge. The Racing Layer's second Battle Challenge has 15 Antickoids. The Radiance Layer's first Battle Challenge has six Antickoids. The Feast Layer's first Battle Challenge has five Antickoids.

== Behind the Scenes ==
Antickoids are based on ants, and their name comes from "antic" (a clownish act that draws attention, or something grotesque and bizarre), "ant," and "tick." The suffix -oid in their name can be found in the names of other enemies in the Underground World, and could also reference the [[VoidCo|Void Company]].

== Nomenclature ==
In Japanese, Antickoids are called ''Anchiko'', from "antic" and presumably the suffix "-oid." Their Spanish name, ''Hormitóide'', is a merger of ''ermitaño'' ("hermit crab"), ''formiga'' ("ant"), and the suffix -''óide'' ("akin to"). Portuguese refers to them as ''Carrapatóide'', from ''carrapato'' ("tick") and the suffix -''óide'' (which denotes likeness to something). In Italian, the enemy is called ''Formicóide'', from ''fórmica'' ("ant") and the suffix -''óide'' ("like..."). Their French name, ''Fourmoïde'', comes from ''fourmie'' ("ant") and the suffix -''oïde'' ("akin to...," "like...," "-oid"). In German, they are called ''Amzekoide'', from ''Ameise'' ("ant"), ''Zecke'' ("tick"), and the suffix "-''oid''" (which denotes likeness to something).
