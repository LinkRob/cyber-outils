"'''No Sleep till Brooklyn'''", also referred to as "'''No Sleep 'til Brooklyn'''",<ref name="musicnotes">"[https://www.musicnotes.com/sheetmusic/mtd.asp?ppn=MN0064089 Beastie Boys "No Sleep 'Til Brooklyn" Sheet Music in Eb Major]". Universal Music Publishing Group. Retrieved September 2, 2025.</ref> is a song by American {{wp|rap rock}} group [[Beastie Boys]], released on March 1, 1987 as the sixth single from their debut studio album, ''{{wp|Licensed to Ill}}''. 50 seconds of the song are used in ''[[The Super Mario Bros. Movie]]'', when the Mario brothers run through Brooklyn to fix a leaking sink as their first plumbing job. As with the other licensed songs in the movie, "No Sleep till Brooklyn" is not included on the [[The Super Mario Bros. Movie (Original Motion Picture Soundtrack)|Original Motion Picture Soundtrack]].

==Composition==
"No Sleep till Brooklyn" is a moderate rap rock,<ref>Grierson, Tim. "[https://rock.about.com/b/2012/05/04/beastie-boys-adam-yauch-has-died.htm Beastie Boys' Adam Yauch Has Died]". About.com. "As a member of Beastie Boys, Yauch (who recorded under the name MCA) helped pioneer rap-rock with (...) classic tracks like "(You Gotta) Fight for Your Right (To Party)" and "No Sleep Till Brooklyn,""</ref> {{wp|hard rock}}<ref name="philly">Tucker, Ken (February 1, 1987). "[https://web.archive.org/web/20140606220617/http://articles.philly.com/1987-02-01/entertainment/26180188_1_king-ad-rock-adam-yauch-beasties Rough-rapping Beastie Boys Cut A Path From Cult To Chart]". Philly.com. "The Beastie Boys also pepper their music with bits of heavy metal and hard rock - the squalling guitar solo on "No Sleep Till Brooklyn," for example, is provided by Kerry King"</ref> and {{wp|Heavy metal music|heavy metal}} song.<ref name="philly"/> By extension, it is one of the first {{wp|rap metal}} songs ever produced but may not have had the proper label because rap rock was new at the time "No Sleep till Brooklyn" was composed. Written in the key of E♭ major, the song is set in time signature of common time with a tempo of 96 beats per minute.<ref name="musicnotes"/> The main guitar riff uses a repeating E-G-A-B-G power chord progression, as the song alternates from rap verses to vocal hooks

The song was shortened from five minutes and nine seconds, to 50 seconds when included in ''The Super Mario Bros. Movie''. The sample used is a mash-up of the opening riff and end of the song, with the third chorus, outro and emphasis on the guitar solo, played by {{wp|Slayer}} co-lead guitarist {{wp|Kerry King}}. {{wp|Michael Diamond}}, {{wp|Adam Yauch}} and {{wp|Adam Horovitz}}, the three members of Beastie Boys, provide all vocals and share songwriting credit with {{wp|Rick Rubin}}, who produced the song, and played its guitar riff.

==''The Super Mario Bros. Movie''==
[[File:The Super Mario Bros. - No Sleep Til Brooklyn.jpg|thumb|200px|Mario and Luigi in the [[Super Mario Bros. Plumbing]] van, before "No Sleep till Brooklyn" plays]]
In the ''The Super Mario Bros. Movie'', the song plays when the [[Super Mario Bros. Plumbing]] van breaks down on the way to fix a leaking sink, the brothers first plumbing job. Luigi says "Oh no! We're gonna be late!" Mario, then packs his bag and pulls Luigi out of the van to run instead. The third chorus of the song plays, while the brothers run through Brooklyn, as trash cans, cats, people, car doors and a road closure slow their progress. 

The guitar solo plays in the construction site, as many references to Nintendo games are present, such as [[Mr. Game & Watch]] with a shovel on a yellow sign, a [[Girder|red girder]] from ''[[Donkey Kong (game)|Donkey Kong]]'', and a restaurant, [[Castle Burger]], resembling the fortresses that appear at the ends of many levels in ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]'', which Mario slides down the signpost of, as if it were a [[Goal Pole]]. The outro plays as Mario jumps from the signpost, and opens the gate for Luigi, with the song concluding out front of the Brooklyn couple's house.

==Critical reception==
While considered one of the Beastie Boys' {{wp|signature song}}s,<ref>[https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-lists/readers-poll-the-best-beastie-boys-songs-of-all-time-19727/ Readers' Poll: The Best Beastie Boys Songs of All Time]". Rolling Stone. Retrieved September 2, 2025.</ref> "No Sleep till Brooklyn" was poorly received in the ''The Super Mario Bros. Movie'' for being included over an original or ''Mario'' song. Gabriela Perez Orta of ''{{wp|Daily Titan}}'' thought the song was "completely out of place as Mario and Luigi parkour through the streets of Brooklyn,"<ref>Gabriela Perez Orta (April 12, 2023). "[https://dailytitan.com/lifestyle/review-the-super-mario-bros-movie-rides-high-in-nostalgia/article_c0f6c4fa-d4fb-11ed-927f-d70f191dd7db.html Review: "The Super Mario Bros. Movie" rides high in nostalgia]". Daily Titan. Retrieved September 2, 2025.</ref> while John Serba of ''{{wp|New York Post#Decider|Decider}}'' used the guitar solo as proof of something that was "edgy and cool several decades ago" now being a "deathless nostalgia borg."<ref>Serba, John (December 5, 2023). "[https://decider.com/2023/12/05/super-mario-bros-movie-streaming-netflix-review/#:~:text=Our%20Call%3A%20STREAM%20IT.,is%20entirely%20acceptable%20family%20entertainment. Thompson of ''{{wp|Yahoo|Yahoo! Entertainment}}'' was also unimpressed, and poked fun at the song as coming from "Hollywood's Obvious Choices 101 folder."<ref>Luke Y. Thompson (April 4, 2023).
