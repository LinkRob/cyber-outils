'''''The Legend of Zelda''''', often referred to as '''''Zelda''''' in shorthand, is a fantasy-themed {{wp|media franchise}} primary consisting of video games produced mainly by [[Nintendo]]. It began with the release of the [[Family Computer]] game of the [[w:c:zelda:The Legend of Zelda|same title]] in Japan in 1986, later released for the [[Nintendo Entertainment System]] in North America, Europe, Australia, and various other countries during the late 1980s. ''The Legend of Zelda'' franchise usually follows the adventures of [[Link]], a courageous hero predestined to battle the forces of evil and protect the magical kingdom of [[Hyrule]], and [[Princess Zelda]] is usually the deuteragonist and [[Ganon]] the primary antagonist.

The mainline entries of ''The Legend of Zelda'' franchise are dynamic, action-adventure games with puzzle-solving elements. However, they are occasionally recognized as being role-playing games, leading others to consider it a contemporary of similar works like ''[[Dragon Quest]]'' and ''[[Final Fantasy]]'', atop the fantasy setting. Unlike its contemporaries, ''The Legend of Zelda'' makes minimal use of the user-interface, difficulty selection, or menu navigation, an intentional choice derived from [[Shigeru Miyamoto]]'s personal distaste for such elements. However, there is a ''Final Fantasy'' spinoff titled ''[[w:c:mana:Mana (series)|Mana]]'', a series of action role-playing games that is a middle ground between the action-adventure style of ''The Legend of Zelda'' and the role-playing style of both ''Dragon Quest'' and ''Final Fantasy''.

Despite its genre, ''The Legend of Zelda'' has a lot more direct relation to the [[Mario (franchise)|''Mario'' franchise]] than to its contemporaries, especially the [[w:c:zelda:The Legend of Zelda (series)|mainline series]] to the [[Super Mario (series)|''Super Mario'' series]] of platforming games. The connections between the two series have existed from the start, and around that time, the ''Super Mario'' series would replace the [[Mario Bros. (series)|''Mario Bros.'' series]] as the mainline ''Mario'' titles. The ''Super Mario'' and ''The Legend of Zelda'' series have often had a game developed concurrently by the same team over at [[Nintendo EAD]], and one of the closest connections between the two series is that some enemies and obstacles from the ''Super Mario'' series are included in a ''The Legend of Zelda'' game, as well as varieties of enemies from the ''Super Mario'' series exclusive to ''The Legend of Zelda'' franchise in general and vice versa.

The similarities between the mainline ''The Legend of Zelda'' and ''Mario'' titles have extended over into their franchises at large, with one notable example being that [[The Legend of Zelda (television series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' television series]] was produced as part of ''[[The Super Mario Bros. Super Show!]]'' syndication package. Link has made several appearances in the ''Mario'' franchise, including as a playable character in ''[[Mario Kart 8]]''. Overall, ''The Legend of Zelda'' and ''Mario'' have shared enough synergy to the extent that ''The Legend of Zelda'' can be considered a partner of the ''Mario'' franchise, among the ranks of ''[[Donkey Kong (franchise)|Donkey Kong]]'', ''[[Wario (franchise)|Wario]]'', and ''[[Yoshi (franchise)|Yoshi]]''.

==Connections to the ''Mario'' franchise==
===''[[w:c:zelda:The Legend of Zelda|The Legend of Zelda]]'' ([[Nintendo Entertainment System|NES]])===
[[File:Zelda1-Manhandla.png|frame]]
*According to the Japanese manual for the game, the boss [[Manhandla]] is actually a massive [[Piranha Plant]].<ref>方向に手を持つ大型のパックンフラワー。手が減るごとに速い動きになる。ややつよい攻撃力を持つ。 (Translation: ''A four-limbed, jumbo-sized Piranha Plant. Speeds up with each limb lost. Possesses somewhat powerful offensive strength.'') - ''The Legend of Zelda'' Japanese manual, page 36.</ref>
*In the instruction manual, sample file names are "Nintendo," "Mario," and "Luigi."

===''[[w:c:zelda:The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past|The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past]]'' ([[Super Nintendo Entertainment System|SNES]])===
[[File:ss lozalttf.png|thumb]]
*Some of the houses in the [[w:c:zelda:Light World|Light World]] village have paintings of [[Mario]] in them. If [[Link]] pulls on the painting, [[Rupee]]s will come out.
*[[Chain Chomp]]s appear as enemies in the [[w:c:zelda:Turtle Rock (A Link to the Past)|Turtle Rock]] dungeon. In the [[Game Boy Advance|GBA]] version, they also appear in [[w:c:zelda:Palace of the Four Sword|Palace of the Four Sword]].
*The File Select music and the theme for the [[Fairy Fountain (theme)|Fairy Fountain]]/[[w:c:zelda:Great Fairy Fountain|Great Fairy Fountain]]s is a rendition of [[Water Land]]'s music from ''[[Super Mario Bros. 3]]'', which would be used in many subsequent ''The Legend of Zelda'' games in the same situations.

===''[[w:c:zelda:The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening|The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening]]'' ([[Game Boy|GB]]) / ''[[w:c:zelda:The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening DX|Link's Awakening DX]]'' ([[Game Boy Color|GBC]]) / [[w:c:zelda:The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening (Nintendo Switch)|''Link's Awakening'' (2019 remake)]] ([[Nintendo Switch|Switch]])===
[[File:LA Wart.gif|frame|right|Wart]]
*[[w:c:zelda:Tarin|Tarin]] resembles [[Mario]]. He even transforms into a raccoon by eating a mushroom, as Mario transforms into [[Raccoon Mario]] by using a [[Super Leaf]]. Likewise, the [[w:c:zelda:Cucco Keeper|Cucco Keeper]] resembles [[Luigi]].
*There are many enemies from the ''Super Mario'' franchise, though some of them were named differently in English guides. There are [[Goomba]]s, [[Piranha Plant]]s, [[Podoboo]]s, [[Spiny|Spinies]] (called Spiked Beetles), [[Cheep Cheep]]s (called Cheep-Sheep), [[Blooper|Bloober]]s, [[Thwomp]]s (called Spiked Thwomps), [[Thwimp]]s (called Thwomps), [[Pokey]]s, [[Boo]]s, and [[Shy Guy]]s (called Mask-Mimics). Also, the [[Bombite]] enemy bears a striking resemblance to [[Bob-omb]].
**In the Nintendo Switch [[w:c:zelda:The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening (Nintendo Switch)|remake]], figures of BowWow, CiaoCiao, a Goomba, Spiny, Boo, Cheep Cheep, Blooper, Shy Guy, Piranha Plant, and Pokey can be acquired in the Trendy Game. With the exception of the Chain Chomp characters, all of the aforementioned enemies are referred to by their ''Super Mario'' franchise names.
**Also in the remake, Cheep Cheeps and Bloopers can be caught in the [[w:c:zelda:Fishing Hole|Fishing Hole]].
*A [[Yoshi doll]] is found at the [[w:c:zelda:Trendy Game|Trendy Game]] shop in [[w:c:zelda:Mabe Village|Mabe Village]]. Link can win the Yoshi doll by controlling the crane and hovering over it. When the doll is received, the text box states that it has appeared in many games recently, another reference to the ''Super Mario'' games. In the Nintendo Switch remake of the game, the text is changed to state that the player cannot count how many games it has appeared in now.
*[[BowWow]], known in the ''Super Mario'' franchise as a Chain Chomp, is featured in the game and "befriends" Link as it actually helps him access the second dungeon. Another, smaller Chain Chomp named [[CiaoCiao]] also appears.
*The character [[Wart]] reappears in this game under his original Japanese name Mamu and acts as an ally to Link by teaching him the Frog's Song of Soul.
*When [[w:c:zelda:Mr. Write|Mr. Write]] shows the player the picture sent to him by [[w:c:zelda:Christine|Christine]], a goat living in [[w:c:zelda:Animal Village|Animal Village]], the picture of "Christine" is actually one of Princess Peach. In the Nintendo Switch version, promotional artwork of Princess Peach with her parasol is shown.
