==''{{wp|2010 Asian Games}}''==
During the fourth quarter of the match between South Korea and China on November 25, 2010, a Level Clear sound from ''Super Mario Bros.'' was heard.

==''{{wp|30 Rock}}''==
In the episode "{{wp|Mamma Mia (30 Rock)|Mamma Mia}}", television writer {{wp|Liz Lemon}} ({{wp|Tina Fey}}) makes an allusion to the arcade game ''[[Donkey Kong (game)|Donkey Kong]]'' and [[Mario|Mario's]] Italian heritage when talking to her boss {{wp|Jack Donaghy}} ({{wp|Alec Baldwin}}), who is of Irish descent. She says to him, "Really? You're going to do the repressed Irish thing? Be Italian for like one second." She then, in a faux Italian accent, begins to mock Jack: "I'm-a Jack. I don't know who my father is. I'm-a so emotional. I'm gonna [[Hammer|smash]] these [[barrel]]s!"

In another episode, "Brooklyn Without Limits", Liz walks up to Jack wearing blue and white striped "shorteralls", which Jack responds to by making a remark about the Mario Brothers.

==''Nintendomanía'' / ''Power Up! Gamers''==
''{{wp|Nintendomanía}}'' was a Mexican program from March 1995 to July 2000 broadcast by {{wp|TV Azteca}} in sponsorship with Nintendo showing the latest news, updates, tricks, moves, secrets, and other content about ''Super Mario'' and other Nintendo games. Gustavo "Gus" Rodríguez, one of the show's hosts, was also co-founder and one of the first editors of [[Club Nintendo (Mexican magazine)|''Club Nintendo'']], [[Nintendo]]'s official magazine for Latin America from 1991 to 2014 (Mexico) and 2019 (rest of Latin America).

This program was revived at the end of February 2014 under the name ''Power Up! Gamers'', this time broadcast by {{wp|Televisa}} ({{wp|N+ Foro|FOROtv}}'s channel) and transformed into a program of multi-platform video games for the eighth-generation consoles as well as the older-generation {{wp|PlayStation 3}} and {{wp|Xbox 360}}. Also, the program integrated the original cast of ''Nintendomanía''.

When entering the game, the characters morph to game-appropriate "Mustachio Brother" forms which grant them [[Mario Cap]]-esque hats and Mario-esque mustaches. Abby and A.B.C-More run and jump through ''Super Mario''-esque environments, singing a song that resembles the "Ground BGM" from ''Super Mario Bros.'' Visual references to [[Hard Block]]s, [[Trampoline]]s, [[Girder]]s, [[Semisolid Platform]]s, [[swinging vine]]s, and [[Pipe]]s appear, with pipes being replaced by screws whose heads resemble the lip of the pipe. Art director Michael Lapinski confirmed on his personal blog in a "making-of" post that "Super Mustachio Bros." is intended to evoke ''Super Mario''. Later on in the movie, there is a parody of racing games such as ''Mario Kart''.

==''{{wp|You're Skitting Me}}''==
[[File:TV Ref-Talk With Agent.jpg|thumb|Agent Tracy Murray (played by Cosmo Renfree) tells [[Mario]] (Hayden McKertish) and [[Luigi]] (Rowan Hills) that they need to get a new look.]]
[[File:TV Ref-Mario Shave Mustache.jpg|thumb|[[Luigi]] holding a razor blade and telling [[Mario]] to shave off his mustache.]]
Throughout many episodes of the Australian sketch comedy ''{{wp|You're Skitting Me}}'', [[Mario]] and [[Luigi]] can be seen having many comical disputes over various situations. The Bros. (Hayden McKertish as Mario and Rowan Hills as Luigi) own a plumbing company. During parts of the skit, 8-bit music similar to "[[Ground BGM (Super Mario Bros. 3)|Ground BGM]]" from ''[[Super Mario Bros. 3]]'' can be heard in the background.

In one episode, Mario and Luigi's agent Tracy Murray (played by Cosmo Renfree) says that after thirty years, the Mario Bros. are losing their touch. The "phones aren't ringing anymore" in the plumbing company, and Tracey says that the whole plumbing thing in a complete waste of their talent. He suggests that they need a new look in order to "get with the times" and to "get back into the game". Luigi is in favor of the idea, saying that it could be their big chance, but Mario is unsure, as he thinks their plumbing career is going well enough. Luigi suddenly gets mad at Mario, saying that he "wants to smash his head on to a [[Brick Block|Brick]]", and he is "not even looking for a [[List of Power-Ups|Power-Up]]". Mario finally gives in, and says that it is worth giving a go. Agent Tracey then says that he will get some fashion consultants in and bring in some stylists to do something with their hair, but first and foremost, he wants to get rid of their "ridiculous mustaches". He brings out some razor blades and places them on the desk. Mario, crossing his arms and shaking his head, is strongly against the idea, but Luigi says that they have to if they want to get with the times. Mario still refuses, leading Luigi to pick up the razor blade off the desk and point it towards Mario's face. He says softly but firmly, "You shave your mustache". Mario then replies with "I no shave my mustache!". The dispute between them goes on for another few minutes, and agent Tracy tells them to stop arguing. 

The next scene of the episode interviews the actors of the show and asks for their opinions of the last skit. They discuss whether Mario should shave his mustache or not. Some of the opinions given by the actors are "it's the only thing keeping him going" (Molly Daniels), "what is Mario without the mustache?" (Cosmo Renfree), "it's the reason I like him" (Jake Fehily), and "I think the mustache should shave off Mario" (Cosmo Renfree).
