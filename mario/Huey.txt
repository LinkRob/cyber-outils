'''Huey''' is a character in ''[[Paper Mario: Color Splash]]'', and is [[Mario]]'s partner and ally throughout the game.

== Profile ==
=== Physical appearance===
Huey is a silver floating paint can with eyes and a brown handle that acts as arms. He appears in both a 3D form and a 2D form, the latter assumed upon getting squeezed by [[Mario (character)|Mario]]. His eye color changes when his mood changes, for example, yellow means happy and red means angry. In the game itself, Huey's lid functions as a mouth, but his ''[[Super Mario-kun]]'' design gives him a more human-like mouth beneath his eyes.

=== Personality traits ===
Huey is very friendly and cheerful, though he becomes angry precisely. He has a large connection to paint, and is angered to see it get misused or wasted. He is also slightly greedy, which is shown when he sees the Giant Coin in [[Mondo Woods]], though he values friendship more than money. Huey is also shy in front of crowds, which is shown in [[The Emerald Circus]].

Additionally, Huey hates lying, though he will lie if he needs to.

=== Powers and Abilities ===
Besides flight, Huey can smell Paint Stars from long distances. He can also absorbe large amounts of [[Black Paint|black paint]], as seen in Sunglow Ridge and the battle with the corrupted [[Bowser]]. He is also able to hear the Big Paint Stars' voices, an ability that he shares with [[Mario (character)|Mario]]. Huey is also fairly strong, able to hold Mario with just his handle and wring out Bowser's entire castle (albeit with the aid of the Big Paint Stars).

== Biography ==
[[Mario]] and [[Princess Peach]] first find Huey in 3D form as a seemingly inanimate object in the inactive [[Prisma Fountain]] at the town square of [[Port Prisma]]. After Mario whacks him with his hammer, however, he wakes up and gets angry at him for hitting him while he was sleeping, but calms down shortly afterwards. Peach introduces herself and Mario to Huey, but soon after they hear [[Toad]] scream and run to go help him. When they find him, his color is being drained by a [[Slurp Guy]]. Huey teaches Mario how to battle with [[Battle Cards]] and Mario squeezes Huey into paper form so that he can gain paint. After the battle, Huey tells Mario that he now has the ability to use the [[Paint Hammer]]. 

Later, in [[Cherry Lake]], Huey introduces the Cutout technique so that they can get through the gate at the beginning of the level. The technique allows Mario to use Thing cards to solve puzzles, to cut out clear lines of the background in order to traverse areas if he isn't able to continue, and to find hidden Battle Cards. 

In [[Daffodil Peak]], while Mario and Huey are helping the park ranger repaint his cabin, Huey teaches Mario how to use the [[Unfurl Block]]. They use the Unfurl Block to unfurl the ranger's favorite chair.

Huey teaches Mario how to use the [[Battle Spin]] after a Slurp Guy attacks them right after the [[Card Connoisseur Toad]] borrows all of Mario's Battle Cards. The Battle Spin allows Mario to get extra cards for ten coins when he runs out. Huey gives Mario enough coins to use the Battle Spin until he defeats the Slurp Guy.

At [[Mustard Café]], Mario briefly loses Huey due to wind blowing both of them into the giant hole that the purple Big Paint Star repainted. Mario must save Huey from a Green [[Pokey]] to get him back. While the Pokey has Huey, Mario cannot use his [[Paint Hammer]] or the Cutout technique.

During the battle with [[Bowser]], Huey turns back into his 3D form so that he can absorb the black paint that Bowser uses to attack Mario with. If Mario takes too long to defeat Bowser, Huey will be knocked out, giving Mario an instant [[Game Over]]. When Bowser is at his weakest, but if the player has exhausted all of their offense cards, Huey will conjure a Hammer card. After rescuing Princess Peach, Huey tells Mario, Peach, and [[Luigi]] to go back to Port Prisma without him, so that he can contain the eliminated black paint and release it where it can do no more harm. Drawing upon the power of the Paint Stars, Huey wrings out the castle, turning the castle into a card in the process (which falls into Port Prisma). Huey was proud that he stopped the paint from harming anyone, and takes it away into the sky with him. While Mario and Peach watch the festival, Peach looked at Mario, looking at the starry night sky understanding he was sad missing Huey and told him that she can feel that Huey is happy.

When Mario achieves all of the Super Flags in Port Prisma, Huey can be seen falling back into Prisma Fountain at the end of the staff credits, apparently having survived his departure into space.

==In ''Paper Mario: Color Splash''==
Huey acts as Mario's companion throughout the adventure, with his role being similar to [[Kersti]] in ''[[Paper Mario: Sticker Star]]''. He was a secondary hero by coloring the silhouette of Peach; he even lied to Mario, Luigi and Peach although he hates to lie, and he allowed the eliminated coat of paint of Bowser's Castle to get in him, which he left the black and white picture of Bowser's Castle landing on a ground. Then Huey happily left to the sky so no paint will harm anyone in the world. During Battles he was used by Mario to block the dark paint from Bowser. When doing [[Paper Luigi]] Cutout he says that he was doing a "Green Mauro Cosplay".

== Other Appearances ==
Huey appears as a spirit in ''Super Smash Bros. Ultimate'', depicted in his flat form from when his body is squeezed by Mario. Any fighter who equips him takes reduced damage from water and ice-based attacks. Players can purchase Huey at a shop run by Timmy & Tommy Nook for 500 Spirit Points or battle him in the Spirit Board. In the Spirit Board, the Huey Spirit Battle involves Inklings who prefer to use Special Moves.

== Behind the Scenes ==
Huey derives his name from a diminutive of "Hubert" and the word "hue."

== Nomenclature ==
In Japanese, Huey is called ''Penkī'' (written ペンキー), from ''penki'' ("paint"; written ペンキ), which was originally derived from Dutch ''pek'' ("pitch"). His Spanish name, ''Baldo'', is a common male surname, also influenced by ''balde'' ("bucket"). Portuguese gives him the name ''Pintas'', which means "you (informal singular) paint." In Italian, his name is ''Tinto'', meaning "dyed." French translates his name as ''Peinturion'', from ''peinture'' ("paint") and "centurion." In German, he goes by Farbian, from the given name "Fabian" and ''Farbe'' ("colour"), and in Dutch, he is called "Toon," from the Dutch word for "tone."
