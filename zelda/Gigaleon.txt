{{Term/Store|Gigaleon||Series, TFH}} is the {{Term|TFH|Boss}} of {{Term|TFH|Deception Castle|link}}, which serves as the {{Term|TFH|Sub-Boss}} of {{Term|TFH|Sky Realm|link}}, in {{TFH}}.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= E |publisher= |page= 181}}</ref>

==Characteristics==
{{Term|TFH|Gigaleon}} is a floating, saucer-shaped creature with a large, tentacle-covered hold on its top side. On its underside is a single, exposed eye that acts as its weak point.

The {{Plural|TFH|Links|link}} fight {{Term|TFH|Gigaleon}} upon a floating platform in the sky. {{Term|TFH|Gigaleon}} will drop dark balls of fire onto the platform while flying through the air, which will burn the {{Plural|TFH|Links}} on contact and remain on the ground for several seconds. At each corner of the platform is a wind turbine that can be activated by using the {{Term|TFH|Gust Jar|link}} on them, which will create a large twister from their fans. To attack {{Term|TFH|Gigaleon}}, the {{Plural|TFH|Links}} must activate the wind turbines when it flies over them. This will stun it, allowing either of the other {{Plural|TFH|Links}} to use the {{Term|TFH|Gripshot|link}} to pick up a {{Term|TFH|Bomb Flower|link}} from one of the floating ledges on either side of the arena, and throw it into the fan, causing it to explode once it hits {{Term|TFH|Gigaleon}}'s eye, damaging it.

Once damaged enough, {{Term|TFH|Gigaleon}} will throw flames at the {{Term|TFH|Bomb Flower}} ledges, causing the {{Plural|TFH|Bomb}} to explode and the ledges to fall. It will also flip itself over, so that its vulnerable eye faces upwards. {{Term|TFH|Gigaleon}} now moves faster and floats above the wind turbines for less time. It continue its attack by dropping flames more aggressively, occasionally chasing a single {{Term|TFH|Links}} while doing so. The {{Plural|TFH|Links}} must again stun {{Term|TFH|Gigaleon}} by using the {{Term|TFH|Gust Jar}} on the wind turbines, however, since a {{Term|TFH|Bomb Flower}} can no longer be thrown at it, one {{Term|TFH|Links}} must be thrown into the turbine's gust instead. This will propel him onto {{Term|TFH|Gigaleon}}'s saucer body, allowing him to strike its eye with his {{Term|TFH|Sword|link}}. After enough {{Term|TFH|Sword}} strikes, {{Term|TFH|Gigaleon}} will fall onto the platform, allowing all three {{Plural|TFH|Links}} to attack it until it is defeated.

==Nomenclature==
{{Term|TFH|Gigaleon}}'s name comes from {{Wp|Giga-|giga}}, meaning giant, and "leon" from {{Term|TFH|Keeleon|link}}. This suggests that it is actually a giant {{Term|TFH|Keeleon}}. Both monsters also have similar tentacles that they use to throw explosives. This is also reflected in its Japanese name, as both creatures have similar names.

{{Term|TFH|Gigaleon}}'s original Japanese name was {{Romanize|ダムダム|Dum Dum}}, but it was changed as that name fitted {{Term|TFH|Prismantus|link}} better.<ref>{{Cite Person|quote= To tell you the truth, the original "Dum-Dum" was actually this. It's the mid-boss of the Sky Realm, which is called "Gigaleon" in the retail version. We originally called it "Dum-Dum" because of the sounds it makes when it shoots projectiles, but then we realised that the "Cyclops" also makes a booming noise when it moves, and we thought that the name "Dum-Dum" would probably fit it better. So in the end "Dum-Dum" became "Gigaleon", and "Cyclops" became "Dum-Dum".|name= Mikiharu Oiwa|url= https://web.archive.org/web/20171104184631/https://miiverse.nintendo.net/replies/AYMHAAACAAADVHkosKaUUA|title= Tri Force Heroes Miiting part 2}}</ref>
