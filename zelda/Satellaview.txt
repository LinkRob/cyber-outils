The '''[[wikipedia:Satellaview|Satellaview]]''' was a satellite modem peripheral for the Super Famicom, the Japanese version of the [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System]], released in April 23, 1995. It is attached to the bottom of the console, like other [[Nintendo]] add-ons like the [[Family Computer Disk System|Famicom Disk System]], [[Nintendo 64DD]] or Game Boy Player, for the [[Nintendo Entertainment System|Famicom]], [[Nintendo 64]] and [[Nintendo GameCube]], respectively.

==Features==
The Satellaview service could be accessed via the [[St. GIGA]] satellite station between 4:00 and 7:00 PM. The user was required to have a subscription to the St. GIGA station, as well as a BS tuner in order to operate the Satellaview. During this time, users could download and play games. The BS system broadcasted for five years, up until June, 2000. A large catalog of games previously released in cartridge format, as well as a few new games, were made available for download through the service. A deluxe package came with a rewritable 8-megabit memory card that plugged into a slot on the top of the boot cartridge. Without this cartridge, the ''BS-X'' cartridge saved downloaded content to its 512 KB of built-in memory. Two exclusive [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' series]] titles, ''[[BS The Legend of Zelda|BS Legend of Zelda]]'' and ''[[BS The Legend of Zelda: Ancient Stone Tablets|BS Ancient Stone Tablets]]'', were released for the peripheral in 1995 and 1997, respectively, where its [[Player|mascot characters]] used to interact with the service's hub world (itself resembling the overworld of the then-new ''Earthbound'') are featured as the protagonists rather than [[Link]].

A feature called SoundLink allowed the use of streaming music or voice clips, most famously used in ''Ancient Stone Tablets'', while playing a game. However, such games can only be played in one sitting, as they have a live schedule.

== Trivia ==
[[File:LanmolaAST.png|thumb|233x233px|Gameplay screenshot of ''BS Ancient Stone Tablets'', one of the two ''Zelda'' games specifically for the platform]]
* As the Satellaview was broadcast only in Japan for a limited time and its exclusive games are no longer commercially available as the official service has long since discontinued, it is considered [[wikipedia:Lost_media|lost media]] to the public. It has, however gained a cult-following since the late 2000s where video game enthusiasts overseas aim to preserve its content as much as possible and host games and other fan services online. Some games originally released on the Satellaview are mostly restored by dumping memory packs to be played on emulators.
* There are [[wikipedia:List_of_Satellaview_broadcasts#Broadcast_game_list|114 games]] in total released for the Satellaview service. [[Nintendo]] often held tournaments for certain titles that allowed players to compete for prizes.
* At the time of release, the Satellaview can only be purchased by mail delivery and specific electronic stores.
* The reason why the Satellaview was never released outside of Japan was due to expensive costs of digital satellite broadcasting and a supposed lack of appeal to North American consumers.
* Alongside ''[[The Legend of Zelda (series)|The Legend of Zelda]]'', several other Nintendo franchises were featured in the Satellaview are ''[[Mario (franchise)|Super Mario]]'', ''F-Zero'', ''Kirby'', and ''Fire Emblem'', with their games being remakes or updates of previously released titles on the [[Nintendo Entertainment System|Famicom]] or [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System|Super Famicom]].
* Due to the lack of the expansion port on the bottom of the console, it is impossible to play the Satellaview on the Super Famicom Jr. model.
** Although the American Super NES has a port that technically attaches to the Satellaview, due to the shape of the device specifically for the Super Famicom and PAL SNES, this cannot be done.
* Although "BS-X" technically stands for "Broadcast Satellite X", the lettering has become a rather comical meme among some fans, as it strongly resembles the taboo slang "BS", which represents "bulls**t".
* By 1998, Nintendo's relationship with St.GIGA was beginning to collapse due to the latter's refusal of a debt-management plan and failure to secure a government broadcasting license. Nintendo withdrew support for Satellaview in March 1999, with St.GIGA continuing to supply content until June 30, 2000, when it was fully discontinued due to lack of sponsors, as they had no sponsors since April of that year.
* Despite being successful for St.GIGA and amassed a wide playerbase at the time, Nintendo viewed Satellaview as a commercial failure.
