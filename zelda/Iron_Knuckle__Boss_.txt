{{Term/Store|Iron Knuckle|Iron Knuckles|Series, OoT, OoT3D, MM, MM3D|plural}} are recurring {{Plural|Series|Sub-Boss|link}} in {{TLoZ|Series}}.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= E |publisher= |page= 186 {{Exp Game|OoT, MM}}}}</ref>

==Characteristics==
[[File:Beneath the Graveyard6.png|left|thumb|229x229px|An Iron Knuckle inside the second tomb of the [[Ikana Graveyard]] from {{MM|-}}]]
Iron Knuckles are large, heavily armored [[Gerudo]] warriors, armed with powerful, huge axes capable of inflicting four [[Heart Container|Hearts]] of damage to [[Link]], making them among the most dangerous [[Enemy|enemies]] in [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|{{TLoZ|-}} series]]. Appearing exclusively in the [[Nintendo 64]] titles {{OoT}} and {{MM}} and are different from the [[Iron Knuckle|enemies of the same name]] seen in {{TAoL}}, Iron Knuckles wear a cloth tunic bearing Gerudo symbols. Though quite similar to [[Darknut]]s, Iron Knuckles usually play the role of stoic guardians as opposed to the more aggressive behavior of a Darknut (though they themselves do become aggressive when low on health). The main difference, however, is that they do not share the Darknut's traditional weakness to attacks from behind. Like [[Armos]], they first appear as harmless statues, but come to life upon being struck. These rare foes are dueling elites, incorporating some of the same swordplay techniques that Link himself does during battle to great effect, including the [[Spin Attack]] (which they can perform suddenly). The deadly knights are also known for their unmatched defensive capabilities and long vitality, having nearly the same amount of health as [[Shadow Temple]]'s boss, [[Bongo Bongo]], which can prove troublesome for Link if he fights them with the [[Kokiri Sword]]. Their armor, expectedly made of iron, allows them to take many hits, but their slow movement due to the their heavy weight leave them wide open to retaliation.<ref>{{Cite|You know about the Iron Knuckle, right? Stay away from its ax attack, then look for a chance to retaliate.|Tatl|MM}}</ref> The armor of Iron Knuckles can be damaged and removed with [[Sword]] blows, [[Bomb]]s, [[Bombchu]]s, or the [[Megaton Hammer]]. In {{MM|-}}, [[Goron Link]]'s punches are also effective. Once part of their armor is removed, they become enraged and start to run, but are then weak to [[Arrow]]s, opening up the opportunity for Link of long-range attacks.

Iron Knuckles appear in the [[Spirit Temple]] and [[Ganon's Castle]] in {{OoT}}, with five in total. They are tall [[Gerudo]] warriors covered in solid armor.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= E |publisher= |page= 214}}</ref> The first one that [[Link]] encounters is found at the end of the Spirit Temple when Link is a child. After defeating this Iron Knuckle, the young hero can proceed outside to receive the [[Silver Gauntlets]] from a [[Treasure Chest]]. Once Link returns to the Spirit Temple as an adult, he will again encounter another Iron Knuckle in a mirrored room on the opposite side of the Spirit Temple. Once defeated, Link can proceed outside to receive the [[Mirror Shield]] from a Treasure Chest. [[File:Nabooru Iron Knuckle.webp|thumb|268x268px|The unique Iron Knuckle in the Spirit Temple, which is the brainwashed Nabooru, from {{OoT|-}}]]A special Iron Knuckle also appears directly before the fight against the dungeon's [[Boss]], [[Twinrova]]. It is distinguished for having a larger helmet and unique red color on the armor's sides. However, this particular enemy is actually [[Nabooru]], the second-in-command of the [[Gerudo]] and [[Sage|Sage of Spirit]], brainwashed by Kotake and Koume to fight the [[Hero of Time]] in Iron Knuckle armor.<ref>{{Cite|By the way... I really messed up... I was brainwashed by those old witches and used by Ganondorf to do his evil will...|Nabooru|OoT}}</ref> When it begins its attack, the Mini-Boss can be seen reacting that it was not holding its axe before making the weapon appears on its hand by snapping its fingers and then gives a battle cry. Unlike its regular counterparts, although the Nabooru Iron Knuckle's health, damage output and general behavior is the same, it has the ability to instantly counterattack in the event Link hits the foe from behind with his sword. Therefore, the use of [[Nayru's Love]] is recommended. After defeating Nabooru as an Iron Knuckle, she becomes free but quickly gets recaptured by the witches before Link can walk directly into the boss room.

Link also encounters a pair of Iron Knuckles while ascending the tower of Ganon's Castle, specifically the third room, in which he must exercise caution to not accidentally spring both into action at once (though expert players tend to enjoy this type of challenge).

Two extra Iron Knuckles also appear in the [[Fire Temple]], the [[Gerudo's Training Ground]], and Ganon's Castle in {{MQ}}.

[[File:MM3D Link fighting Iron Knuckle.jpg|left|thumb|307x307px|The First Day Iron Knuckle of the Ikana Graveyard fighting Link, from ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask 3D|Majora's Mask 3D]]'']]
Three Iron Knuckles appear in {{MM}}. Two of them are found within the tombs of the [[Ikana Graveyard]], with one on the [[First Day]] and one on the [[Second Day]]. One is guarding the resting place of [[Flat]] and the other is guarding a [[Piece of Heart]]. The third Iron Knuckle can be found in the [[Link Dungeon]] inside the [[Moon]] during the hide-and-seek game played by the [[Moon Child]] while Link is in his normal form.

They are surprisingly more agile and aggressive than in {{OoT|-}}, delivering faster ax swings and immediately counterattack whenever Link is behind the enemy, especially when their armor is removed, making them even more dangerous than before and giving the player little time to dodge their deadly attacks (using the [[Bunny Hood]], ducking and/or attacking from afar without [[Targeting|Z-Targeting]] is an advantage). Unlike in the game's prequel, [[Light Arrow|Light Arrows]] are able to damage Iron Knuckles in full armor (though [[Fire Arrow|Fire]] and [[Ice Arrow|Ice Arrows]] remain ineffective).

In addition, a golden suit of Iron Knuckle armor can be seen on display in the [[Curiosity Shop]]. It is used as a peep hole from the back room where [[Kafei]] spies on the Curiosity Shop's dealings. This empty Iron Knuckle suit uniquely wields a sword instead of an ax.

An Iron Knuckle is the second boss in {{TWoG}}. He is located in [[Tykogi Tower]], where he had imprisoned the residents of [[Sakado]], including [[Mayor Cravendish]] and [[Harbanno]] the baker.<ref>{{Cite|Oh dear! == Trivia ==
* In the Japanese releases, Iron Knuckles are known as '''Iron Nakku'''.
* The Iron Knuckle is widely known among [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|{{TLoZ|-}} series]]' fanbase as one of the most infamous non-[[Boss]] enemies in the franchise, due to their intimidating appearance and high level of challenge explicitly from inflicting heavy damage and having strong defensive capabilities, especially among less experienced players. [[File:Iron Knuckle Gerudo Face.webp|thumb|277x277px|The hidden Gerudo head inside an Iron Knuckle's helmet]]
* Interestingly, if one clips the camera into any of the standard Iron Knuckles models' helmets in both {{OoT|-}} and {{MM|-}}, the head of a seemingly shocked [[Gerudo]] can be seen. This is actually a leftover of what was the head of [[Nabooru]] until it was changed later in development. This head was removed in the [[Nintendo 3DS]] remakes in-game, but still exists in their model data files. Normally, with the exception of Nabooru as such in {{OoT|-}}, the Gerudo head is never shown, as Iron Knuckles vanish upon being defeated. It is possible that Iron Knuckles were originally intended to be [[Gerudo Thief|Gerudo Thieves]] brainwashed by [[Ganon]] or [[Twinrova]] against their will, similar to Nabooru herself.
** Although impossible under normal gameplay, if Nabooru's unique Iron Knuckle model were to have its helmet being clipped into by the camera via debugging, her actual head will indeed be seen.
*A design from beta builds of {{OoT|-}} suggests Iron Knuckles were originally going to be as [[Iron Knuckle|how they first appeared]] in {{TAoL}}.
*Strangely, despite Iron Knuckles are supposedly female Gerudos under the armor, they share the [https://noproblo.dayjo.org/zeldasounds/OOT/OOT_IronKnuckle_Attack.wav same deep masculine voice] as [[Ganondorf]] (who himself ironically is such race). This also applies to Nabooru as such. The voice is however likely an intentional choice by the developers to make the [[Sub-Boss|Mini-Bosses]] appear more threatening.
**This could possibly be a reference to [[metroidwiki:Samus_Aran|Samus Aran]], the main protagonist of the ''[[metroidwiki:Metroid_(series)|Metroid]]'' franchise, as although canonically female, the bounty hunter's physical appearance under her [[metroidwiki:Power_Suit|Power Suit]] (specifically in its [[metroidwiki:Varia_Suit|Varia]]/[[metroidwiki:Gravity_Suit|Gravity]] upgrades) and rather tall height of 6'3" gives a somewhat masculine impression and is consequently often mistaken as male by fans. Even the [[metroidwiki:Metroid_(game)|original game]]'s instruction manual claims Samus as such gender until the player learns the reality when completing early installments quickly and/or accomplishing 100% completion where she reveals her true identity as a woman.
*Iron Knuckles normally come in three colors: steel, black, and white. Nabooru's in {{OoT|-}} is in a red and steel color, while the empty suit in the Curiosity Shop in {{MM|-}} is gold.[[File:OoS Onox Artwork.png|thumb|235x235px|General Onox from ''Oracle of Seasons'' is similar to an Iron Knuckle in appearance]]
*[[Onox]], the main antagonist of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Oracle of Seasons|Oracle of Seasons]]'', in his humanoid form, has his armor strongly resembling that of an Iron Knuckle's, though in the final battle he initially fights with a chained spike ball rather than an ax, like a [[Ball and Chain Soldier]]. However, concept art exists of himself wielding a two-handed battle-axe with a flail attached.
*Although easily missed by some players, when Iron Knuckles take damage in full armor, despite continuing to move seemingly unaffected by the impact, the powerful foes can be heard [https://noproblo.dayjo.org/zeldasounds/OOT/OOT_IronKnuckle_Hurt.wav grunting in pain].
*Being an elite unit among the Gerudo, Iron Knuckles are among the highest ranking minions of Ganon.
*For unknown reasons, during the battle against the Nabooru Iron Knuckle, the standard [[enemy]] battle theme begins to play when it first takes damage by [[Link]].
**In {{MM|-}}, the regular enemy theme is also heard when Iron Knuckles are in their passive statue-like state.
*[[wikipedia:Cast_iron|Cast iron]], the type of metal which is used for the deadly enemies' thick armor, is known to be hard but extremely brittle. This explains why the torso area of the Iron Knuckles' armor eventually breaks apart upon taking enough damage.
**Although likely an exaggeration in-game, the mini-bosses taking damage by Link's [[Sword]] attacks is to an extent accurate, as in historical [[wikipedia:Middle_Ages|Middle Age]] cases, pure or low-carbon iron is soft and will bend or blunt easily when struck by a proper steel weapon such as war hammers, flails or morning stars (making the use of the [[Megaton Hammer]] a realistic choice). While a sword generally cannot cleanly slice through thick, hardened iron plates, as they were designed to deflect such weapons, powerful direct blows can dent, scratch, and break through it over time, as well as passing blunt force trauma to the wearer. In addition, specialized thrusts can penetrate weak spots in the armor, such as armpits or joints.
*Unlike regular Iron Knuckles, when the Nabooru version is defeated, rather than vanishing, its entire armor splits from head to bottom and crumbles to the ground, revealing the character inside.
*A known strategy to defeat Iron Knuckles quickly, due to their normally slow movement, is to [[Jump Slash|Jump Attack]] and then immediately backflip as the enemy swings its axe down to the ground or in the case of the pair in [[Ganon's Castle]], repeatedly [[Spin Attack]] with them close together, especially with stronger [[Sword|Swords]] such as the [[Biggoron's Sword]] or [[Great Fairy's Sword]]. Some fans, however, have discouraged these tactics and view them as a cheap move, as it often takes away the challenge these rare foes provide.
**Some players believe the increased difficulty of the Iron Knuckles in {{MM|-}} is [[Nintendo]]'s answer to this and similar strategies.
*The symbol seen on the Iron Knuckles' chest in {{OoT|-}}, in which appears to be a cross or a plus sign, similar to the [[Crest of the Gerudo]], has been removed in later versions of the game as well as {{MM|-}}. The reason for this change is unknown.
*Although Iron Knuckles share the same damaging effect onto Link as Ganon in {{OoT|-}}, who is the game's [[Final Boss]] and main antagonist, inflicting a hefty four Heart Containers and send him flying upon impact, surprisingly, they do '''not''' have the highest strength among all enemies; Ganondorf's special charging attack during the [[Dead Man's Volley]] duel where he fires multiple very powerful magical beams toward the [[Hero of Time]] at once, when hit, costs ''five'' Hearts. However, unlike the aforementioned two's attacks, this highly dangerous move isn't executed quickly and can be easily dodged or stopped via a [[Light Arrow]]. With this in mind, the player must be especially careful when facing each foe in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time 3D|Ocarina of Time 3D]]''<nowiki/>'s ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Master Quest|Master Quest]]'', where damage taken is doubled alongside mirrored gameplay.
*Though Gerudo are normally average in size like Adult Link, their Iron Knuckle counterparts are notably taller, standing a head higher than the hero.
**With this in mind, it is unknown how Nabooru gained height as such an enemy before returning to normal when defeated.
*Perhaps ironically, the word "iron knuckle" is sometimes used for the [[wikipedia:Brass_knuckles|brass knuckles]] weapon designed for hand-to-hand combat, as some can be made in iron rather than brass.
*The French localization names the Iron Knuckle enemy '''Meat Grinder''', which is rather odd, as they themselves do not grind meat at all. However, this may be in reference to axes being commonly used for slaughtering animals as well as [[wikipedia:Capital_punishment|public executions]] in the medieval era.
