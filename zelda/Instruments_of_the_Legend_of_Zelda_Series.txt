Throughout {{TLoZ|Series}}, there are numerous '''instruments''' used to make [[music]]. Some of these serve important purposes, while others are simply for entertainment.

==Types of Instrument==
===Woodwind Instruments===
[[File:Link Ocarina 2.png|right|100px|Adult Link playing the eponymous Ocarina of Time]]
The most frequently occurring and most notable woodwind instrument in the Zelda series is the [[Ocarina]]. This includes the [[Bone Ocarina]], the [[Fairy Ocarina]], the [[Ocarina of Wind]] and most importantly, the [[Ocarina of Time (Item)|Ocarina of Time]]. Of these, only the Fairy Ocarina and the Ocarina of Time are actually played note by note under [[Link]]'s control, using the four C-Buttons and the A Button.

Other woodwind instruments include various [[Flute]]s, such as the [[Recorder]], the [[Strange Flute]] and the [[Spirit Flute]] (also known as the ''Spirit Pipes''). Once again, only the Spirit Flute can be played note by note, using the touch screen and microphone of the [[Nintendo DS]]. [[Carben]] and [[Rael]] also play a flute and an oboe respectively. 

The [[Horse Call]] and the [[Grass Whistles]] from {{TP}} maybe also be considered woodwind instruments, as well as the [[Howling Stone]]s and [[Air Stone]]s, though it is [[Wolf Link]]'s vocals that are played in the case of the former.

===Brass Instruments===
The [[Deku Pipes]] from {{MM}} is a multi-belled horn. It is a transformation of the Ocarina of Time, activated when Link dons the [[Deku Mask]]. In {{TP}}, the [[Skull Kid]] uses a strange four-bell horn to summon his [[Puppet]]s. Skull Kid's horn is very similar to the Deku Pipes, both instruments being multi-belled horns, but the Deku Pipes has one more bell than Skull Kid's horn.

===String Instruments===
[[File:SS Zelda Artwork.png|left|110px|Zelda holding the Goddess's Harp in artwork of Skyward Sword]]
There are many string instruments which feature in the Zelda series, many of which play a major role in the quest.

The first to appear was [[Sheik]]'s [[Goddess's Harp|Harp]], which she used to teach Link warping songs.

The [[Zora Guitar]] is also a string instrument, but again, it is simply a transformation of the Ocarina of Time, activated when Link dons the [[Zora Mask]]. [[Japas]] is also the bass player of [[The Indigo-Gos]].

The [[Harp of Ages]] was next to come, which was the first string instrument to be played by Link. There are three possible songs to be played on it, all of which allow Link to travel through time in a similar way to the Ocarina of Time. The [[Sea Ukulele]], another string instrument in {{OoA|-}}, is one of the final objects of the {{Term|OoA|Trading Quest|link}}. It is never seen in use, however.

Later, during the events of {{TWW}}, two string instruments were wielded by the [[Sage]]s of Earth and Wind. The first was [[Medli's Harp]] which resembles a golden {{Wp|lyre}}, while the second was [[Makar's Violin]], which resembles a {{Wp|cello}} crafted from a large leaf, due to [[Makar]]'s size.

In {{ST|-}}, [[Gage]] plays a cello, and [[Steem]] plays a biwa.

Most recently was the appearance of the Goddess's Harp in {{SS}}, which was first owned by [[Princess Zelda|Zelda]] as a descendant of the Goddess, [[Hylia]]. However, Link did eventually claim it and used it to play various songs which would open the gates to the [[Silent Realm]]s. It is played by swinging the Wii Remote in time with a tempo or beat, using the [[Wii|Wii Motion Plus]]'s added precision motion-sensing controls. This harp is supposedly the same harp that Sheik used later on during {{OoT}}.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= The harp in {{SS|-}} looks a lot like Sheik's harp in {{OoT|-}}. Could it be the same one...?|book=HH|page= 11}}</ref>

===Percussion Instruments===
[[File:WW Link.png|right|180px|Artwork of Link conducting with the Wind Waker]]
There are only two percussion instruments which are playable in the Zelda series; the [[Bell]], which Link uses to transport himself around Hyrule and [[Lorule]] in {{ALBW}}, and the [[Goron Drums]] (though these are a transformation of the Ocarina of Time activated when Link dons the [[Goron Mask]]). However, various {{Wp|gong}}s are seen throughout the series, including within the [[Swordsman's School]]. Link can strike these with his sword and they will sound one note.

Likewise, [[Tijo]] and [[Evan]] from The Indigo-Go's play a drumkit and piano, respectively, and the [[Happy Mask Salesman]] teaches Link the [[Song of Healing]] on a piano. In {{ST|-}}, [[Embrose]] plays a timpani.

===Other Instruments===
The [[Instrument of the Sirens|Instruments of the Sirens]] are musical instruments obtained from each dungeon in {{LA}} and used to awaken the [[Wind Fish]]. They span multiple music families and include a cello, a bell, and a marimba, among others.

Ganondorf was shown to be skilled at playing the pipe organ in {{OoT}}, most notably playing it in order to lure Link up to the top of his castle to retrieve his and Zelda's respective triforce pieces. The Happy Mask Salesman later summoned a similar pipe organ in {{MM}} shortly after Link retrieved his Ocarina of Time to teach the latter how to play the [[Song of Healing]] to restore him to his original state (as Link was cursed to become a Deku Sprout by Skull Kid earlier).

The [[Wind Waker (Item)|Wind Waker]], although not exactly an instrument, is treated as one in [[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker|the game of the same name]]; when used, musical notes play according to the direction the Wind Waker is pointed at. It can control the [[Wind's Requiem|direction of the wind]], as well as the [[Song of Passing|passage of day and night]] and can even [[Ballad of Gales|summon cyclones]] to carry Link around the [[Great Sea]].

In {{MM|-}}, there is the [[Music Box House]], a building which contains a giant version of a normal music box's mechanism, which is powered by a waterwheel. 

Finally, vocals play a part in a few games, most prominently [[Wolf Link's Howl]] in {{TP|-}}, which can mimic the sounds of the Grass Whistles and Howling Stones. [[Fi]] also appears to sing along to the notes of the Goddess Harp each time Link plays a song.
