{{Term/Store|Wind|Winds|Series, TWW, TMC}} is a recurring {{Term|Series|Object|link}} in {{TLoZ|Series}}.<ref>{{Cite Guide|quote= |game= TWW|publisher= Prima|page= 12|edition= |year= 2003}}</ref><ref>{{Cite Guide|game=TMC|publisher=Nintendo|page=34}}</ref> Over the course of the years, it has played different roles storyline-wise and gameplay-wise. These roles include being a helpful element, being an obstacle, and having a symbolic value.

==Role in the Series==
===Wind as a Symbolic Element===
The game where wind takes the biggest role is {{TWW|-}}. It is given sacred value by two Wind Gods: [[Zephos]] and [[Cyclos]], both lending Link their respective wind-related powers. There is also a temple dedicated to this element, the [[Wind Temple]] (whose puzzles and obstacles make use of it), as well as a melody, the [[Wind God's Aria]]. Also, when Link retrieves the [[Triforce of Courage]], he is chosen by the gods as the ''Hero of Winds''.

In the backstory of {{TWW|-}}, [[Daphnes Nohansen Hyrule]] used the [[Wind Waker (Item)|Wind Waker]] to conduct the sages as they prayed to, among other things, keep the [[Power to Repel Evil]] within the [[Master Sword]].<ref>{{Cite|That Wind Waker you hold was used long ago to conduct us sages when we played our song to call upon the gods. In those days, it was always the king who conducted for us...|Fado (The Wind Waker)|TWW}}</ref> A common quote among the sages back in the day was to wish the "wind of fortune" to blow with one.<ref>{{Cite|May the wind of fortune blow with you|Laruto|TWW}}</ref>

[[Ganondorf]], before battling Link and [[Princess Zelda]], tells the former that, during the time of {{OoT}}, he used to covet the lively wind blowing on [[Hyrule]], rather than the harsh weather conditions under which he and [[Gerudo|his people]] lived.<ref>{{Cite|My country lay within a vast desert. When the sun rose into the sky, a burning wind punished my lands, searing the world. And when the moon climbed into the dark of night, a frigid gale pierced our homes. No matter when it came, the wind carried the same thing... Death.|Ganondorf|TWW}}</ref><ref>{{Cite|But the winds that blew across the green fields of Hyrule brought something other than suffering and ruin. I coveted that wind, I suppose. It can only be called fate.|Ganondorf|TWW}}</ref>

Besides {{TWW|-}}, a few ''The Legend of Zelda'' games make use of wind as a major theme. The most significant is {{TMC}} featuring a two-dungeon search for the [[Wind Element]] via interactions with the [[Wind Tribe]], the use of the [[Ocarina of Wind]] to teleport between [[Wind Crests]] and the [[Gust Jar]]. The villain, [[Vaati]] is also a wind mage. Another one of these games is {{TP}}, where Link finds the [[Gale Boomerang]], which houses the power of the [[Fairy of Winds]]. In {{PH}}, the [[Temple of Wind]] not only features wind-related puzzles as well, but is also located on an island filled with windy geysers and gusts blowing from varying direction.

===Wind as a Gameplay Element===
[[File:Wind Change.jpg|thumb|right|150px|The [[Wind Waker (Item)|Wind Waker]] allows Link to change the direction of the wind.]]
Gameplay-wise, wind plays two roles: As an obstacle, and as an ally. In several ''The Legend of Zelda'' games, fans often blow wind intensely to make Link's exploration more difficult, or even impossible if he doesn't have an item to counter the eolic force (such as [[Iron Boots]]). Wind doesn't always have to come in the opposite direction to Link's to be constituted as an obstacle (in {{TP|-}}, a gust of wind may drag Link to a side-placed bottomless pit in the [[City in the Sky]]; in {{TWW|-}} the small cyclones on the sea abruptly change the position of the [[King of Red Lions]] as Link tries to sail towards an island). As an ally, wind can favor Link's movement, either to reach more quickly to a place or even to an otherwise inaccessible spot; another benefit is that, when expelled from {{Plural|Series|Updraft|link}}, wind can help Link glide as [[Deku Link]] in {{MM}}, or with the [[Deku Leaf]] in {{TWW|-}}, [[Ezlo]] in {{TMC}}, the [[Sailcloth]] in {{SS}}, or the [[Paraglider]] in {{BotW}} and {{TotK}}.

During the events of {{TWW|-}}, [[Link]] receives the titular baton from the [[King of Red Lions]], and shortly afterwards learns the [[Wind's Requiem]] from [[Zephos]]. This melody allows the young hero to manipulate the direction of the wind's flow, which significantly favors his sea travel, as well as his flight with the [[Deku Leaf]], and reveals chests pinpointed by wind insignias on the ground. A second song, the [[Ballad of Gales]], allows Link to warp from one island to another by creating a cyclone surrounding the King of Red Lions, and is taught by [[Cyclos]].

Several other items in the series have exhibited eolic properties as well. The [[Deku Leaf]], in addition to helping Link fly, can also create gusts of wind able to activate fan-shaped switches, move wooden elevators and obstruct enemies; the [[Boomerang|Gale Boomerang]] serves a similar purpose in {{TP|-}}, thanks to the cyclone surrounding it, as does the {{Term|ST|Whirlwind|link}} in {{ST}}. The [[Swift Sail]], introduced in {{TWWHD}}, manipulates the wind to automatically blow in the direction Link is sailing. The [[Korok Leaf]]s in {{BotW|-}} are similar to the Deku Leaf, being able to create gusts that can blow away certain light items and enemies as well as propelling rafts. In {{TotK|-}}, items such as the [[Korok Frond]] (in conjunction with the [[Fuse]] ability) and the [[Fan]], a [[Zonai Devices|Zonai Device]], can also be used to produce wind.

===Relationship with Forests===
[[File:TWW-WindSageSuccession.jpg|thumb|right|200px|At least two of the known Wind Sages originated from the forests, further evidencing the relationship between forest and wind.]]
Over the course of the series, there has been an overlap between the elements of forest and wind. Although [[Farore (Goddess)|Farore]] is commonly associated with forests, her signature spell is [[Farore's Wind]]; in addition, the Forest route of [[Ganon's Tower]] is actually wind-themed. In {{TWW|-}}, the Wind Temple does make an appearance but, conversely, it has a few elements brought from the [[Forest Haven]], namely the grass on the ground, the usage of the [[Deku Leaf]], and some of the enemies being originally from [[Forest Haven]]; its sage was [[Fado (The Wind Waker)|Fado]], a [[Kokiri]], before being succeeded by [[Makar]], a [[Korok]] from the Forest Haven. The {{Term|TPHD|Forest Temple|link}} of {{TP|-}} and the {{Term|ST|Forest Temple|link}} in {{ST|-}} both house a wind-related item (the {{Term|TPHD|Gale Boomerang}} and the {{Term|ST|Whirlwind}}, respectively).

===Wind and [[Music]]===
The majority of [[:Category:Instruments|musical instruments]] Link has played (or at least had) through the games are {{Wp| Wind instrument|wind instruments}}. The list includes the [[Fairy Ocarina]], the {{Term|OoT3D|Ocarina of Time (Item)|link}}, the [[Ocarina of Wind]], the [[Flute]], the [[Recorder]], the [[Horse Call]], the [[Horse Grass]], the [[Hawk Grass]], the [[Strange Flute]], and the [[Spirit Flute]].
