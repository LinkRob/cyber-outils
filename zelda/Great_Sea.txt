The {{Term/Store|Great Sea||Series, TWW, TWWHD}} is the main setting of the events of {{TWW}}<ref>{{Cite|When the Hero of Time was called to embark on another journey and left the land of Hyrule, he was separated from the elements that made him a hero. It is said that at that time, the Triforce of Courage was split into eight shards and hidden throughout the land. Even I do not know where they rest, but this much I do know: they lie hidden somewhere in this Great Sea.|King of Red Lions|TWW}}</ref> and {{PH}}. For the latter game, it only makes a brief appearance during the introduction and ending sequences. The ocean as a whole, as revealed by the [[Great Flood]] event and when [[Link]] enters the [[Hyrule Castle]] for the first time in the former installment, is actually a tremendously flooded world by the [[Golden Goddess|Golden Goddesses]], with its many [[Locations in The Wind Waker|islands]] being the highest mountaintops of [[Hyrule]] far below its depths.

In-game, the Great Sea is restricted to an area of 7x7 1000-unit squares, each containing an island of its own.

==History and Mythology==
{{Main|Great Flood}}
[[File:TWWHD Great Sea Artwork.png|left|199x199px]]
Centuries before the events of {{TWW|-}}, the land of Hyrule was flooded by the [[Deity|Gods]] as a last resort to stop [[Ganon]]'s forces when the [[Hero of Time]] (the [[Link]] from the [[Nintendo 64]] games) did not return to destroy them. Its surviving people fled to the mountaintops and tried to restore their peaceful lives. These summits became the many islands of the Great Sea. Many years passed, and the people forgot about the history of "Old" Hyrule, except as a bedtime story told to children, and in a coming-of-age ceremony for young boys. But the legendary Hero's legacy managed to survive the winds breath.

The Great Sea is "illusory", and its waters from the heavens are different from Hyrule's native waters. Because of this, only monsters and [[Fishman|Fishmen]] are able to live in the Great Sea.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote=The Great Sea in {{TWW|-}} is an illusory ocean created by a torrential downpour from the heavens. Its ethereal "water" is unlike the water natural to Hyrule, and so only monsters and Fishmen are able to live there.|book=E|page=65}}</ref><ref>{{Cite|Oceans... Oceans... Oceans... Oceans as far as the eye can see. They are vast seas... None can swim across them... They yield no fish to catch...|Ganondorf|TWW}}</ref> It is because of these ethereal waters that the [[Zora]] race had become the [[Rito]].<ref>{{Cite Book|quote=The Zora, unable to live in the Great Sea's ethereal waters, evolved into the Rito, who are able to fly in the skies.|book=E|page=69}}</ref> Meanwhile, the [[Kokiri]] also become the [[Korok]].

==Governance==
The majority of the islands in the Great Sea are actually uninhabited, meaning that no one governs them. Neither [[Outset Island]] nor [[Windfall Island]] are governed officially, despite their dense population. Only [[Dragon Roost Island]] (with a [[Rito Chieftain]] and the Sky Spirit [[Valoo]]), [[Forest Haven]] (with the [[Great Deku Tree]] as the Earth Spirit), and [[Greatfish Isle]] (with [[Jabun]] as the Sea Spirit) are known to have guardian deities. In addition, certain islands are owned by individual characters for private purposes.

==Economy==
Like in most lands in [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|{{TLoZ|-}}]] universe, and because of the fact that the Great Sea is born from Hyrule, [[Rupee]]s are the means of currency, which is accepted in every inhabited island in the game. However, the economy is a bit more diverse than in the former land of Hyrule, given the different businesses run; for example, [[Goron#The Wind Waker|Traveling Merchants]] fuel trading sequences by selling their goods to travelers and also sending them to major shops and retails to give townspeople access to them. Auctions are run in Windfall Island every night, and [[Beedle's Shop Ship]] can be seen sailing around numerous islands as well.

==Technology in the Great Sea==
{{Main|Technology in The Legend of Zelda Universe#The Wind Waker}}
The weaponry in the Great Sea is more advanced than it was in Hyrule before its flood. Navigating across the waters are [[Warship]]s capable of shooting at enemies from long distances. It's unknown whether these boats are piloted manually or work automatically (in the latter case, it would indeed prove how much advancement is shown in regards to technology in the Great Sea). Mounted in coral reefs and high platforms are cannons that can shoot bombs to distant enemies, and operate on their own.

Advanced tools and devices can be found as well, such as the [[Tingle Tuner]] (which allows communication between the user and [[Tingle|an assistant]] for various purposes), color-recording [[Picto Box#Deluxe Picto Box|Picto Box]]es (used to take pictographs that become important for [[Nintendo Gallery|figurine making]]), and pieces of ancient technology (as seen in the [[Tower of the Gods]] and the [[Wind Temple]]). Aside from this, it is unknown how much Hyrule had progressed in this aspect before being flooded, thus it is unknown how much was lost before the aforementioned divine intervention.

==Geography==
{{Main|Great Sea Islands}}
[[File:Zeldawindwaker01.jpg|thumb|Link navigating the Great Sea|261x261px]]
The Great Sea serves as the [[overworld]] of {{TWW}}. Most of the islands in the Great Sea are quite small, with little intelligent life inhabiting them. The chief island is Windfall, which houses various shops and attractions, and has the only major human village, except for a small settlement on Outset Island, another important location. Aside from these islands, some of the more prominent ones in the Great Sea include:

*[[Dragon Roost Island]], home of the [[Rito]] tribe, where the Great Sea's main messenger company is located.
*[[Forest Haven]], home of the [[Korok]] tribe, whose inhabitants are dedicated to traveling across the Great Sea to cultivate trees in order to create new forests.
*[[Forsaken Fortress]], former home of a clan of pirates, and source of the evil present throughout the events of {{TWW|-}}, as it is [[Ganondorf]]'s stronghold.
*[[Greatfish Isle]], formerly the home of numerous people and the Water Spirit [[Jabun]], it is destroyed by Ganondorf at a point in the game.
*[[Tower of the Gods]], hidden fortress said to wait for a [[Hero of Winds|Hero]] to raise it from the Sea's depths.
*[[Headstone Island]], which houses the [[Earth Temple (The Wind Waker)|Earth Temple]] where a [[Sage]] prays to ensure the power of the [[Master Sword]] to remain. It is located directly east of Outset.
*[[Gale Isle]], which houses the [[Wind Temple]] where a Sage prays for the power of the Master Sword to remain like its Earth counterpart. It is located directly north of Windfall.

Among the remaining islands, abandoned [[Triangle Islands|triangle-shaped isles]], sacred [[Fairy Islands|seashell-shaped islands]], giant reefs, and [[Star Isles|archipelagos]] adorn the vast territory of the Great Sea. Additionally, it is told by [[Fishman|Fishmen]] that other islands guard charts leading to the location of [[Triforce Shard|sacred golden fragments]] referred to as ''Triumph Forks'', as well as other legendary treasures (notably in [[Fire Mountain]], [[Ice Ring Isle]], and [[Mother & Child Isles]]).

==Population==
There are many people in the Great Sea, but the main group is [[Hylian]]. They live on [[Windfall Island]] and [[Outset Island]], but some are also scattered around the Sea. The [[Rito]] live on [[Dragon Roost Island]] with the Sky Spirit [[Valoo]], and the [[Korok]]s reside on [[Forest Haven]] with the [[Great Deku Tree]]. [[Goron]]s in this game are in the form of [[Traveling Merchant]]s. No signs of the [[Kokiri]] or the [[Zora]]s remain, as they gradually evolved into the aforementioned Korok and Rito, respectively. Also, a fish creature called [[Jabun]], the aforementioned Water Spirit, is in this game, resembling [[Lord Jabu-Jabu|Jabu-Jabu]] and the [[Fishman|Fishmen]] (the latter inhabiting the waters of the Sea). Finally, there is at least one group of pirates, [[Tetra's Pirate Crew]], along with other seafarers, such as the [[Salvage Corp]]. [[Link]], the main protagonist, lives on Outset Island, where his adventure would begin.

==Trivia==
*The Great Sea being a majorly flooded environment, with earth ([[Hyrule]]) far below the ocean waters due to the [[Golden Goddess|Golden Goddesses]]' [[Great Flood]], and sailing as the only means of travel is a reference to the [[wikipedia:Genesis_flood_narrative|Great Flood of Noah]] from the Book of Genesis in the [[wikipedia:Bible|Holy Bible]].
*If [[Link]] decides to leave the Great Sea, the [[King of Red Lions]] prevents it by telling him that it is too dangerous and then immediately turning around.<ref>{{Cite|It is too dangerous to go any further in this direction! We must turn back!|King of Red Lions|TWW}}</ref> It is possible, especially with cheats, to continue to swim in this direction, but this will eventually lead to going out of bounds.
*In the original [[Nintendo GameCube]] version of {{TWW}}, because the Great Sea itself is a rather large [[overworld]], due to limited processing power, the console can only properly load one grid of the map - specifically Link's current location, requiring the slower sailing speed to mask loading times. In its [[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker HD|HD remaster]], the [[Wii U]], being more technologically advanced, is capable of fully loading the entire Great Sea at once and allow the [[Sail|Swift Sail]]'s increased speed, though this does, however, result in occasional framerate issues, especially during rainfall.
*The Great Sea has two different themes. One is the normal overworld theme, which is similar to the franchise's [[The Legend of Zelda (Theme)|main theme]], and another darker version which occurs during [[Ganondorf]]'s endless night rainstorm curse when Link must search for [[Jabun]] and the [[Nayru's Pearl]].
**If one uses a cheating device to obtain the Nayru' Pearl prematurely and trigger the [[Tower of the Gods]] during the curse, due to a [[Glitches in The Wind Waker|glitch]], it is possible to continue sailing the Great Sea with the dark theme still playing for the rest of game, even during daytime.
*The sailing gameplay of the Great Sea is considered a major departure from the traditional adventuring of most games of [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|{{TLoZ|-}} series]] and is met with [[Controversy in The Legend of Zelda series|controversy]].[[File:WW Treasure02.png|thumb|269x269px|Link obtaining a fragment of the Triforce of Courage from beneath the Great Sea]]
*[[Triforce Shard|Eight shards]] of the [[Triforce of Courage]] are hidden deep within the Great Sea. This is because during the ending of {{OoT}}, when [[Princess Zelda]] sends the [[Hero of Time]] to the past permanently, his piece of the [[Triforce]] ultimately shatters and is scattered throughout the land, as Link no longer exists in the [[The Legend of Zelda timeline|Adult Timeline]] (despite the King of Red Lions claiming he went to another journey and left Hyrule, referencing {{MM}} in the Child Timeline). It is unknown who stored them inside [[Treasure Chest|Treasure Chests]] for the Hero of Winds to eventually restore the sacred triangle like the [[Master Sword]] in order to defeat Ganondorf.
*During the final battle in {{TWW|-}}, [[Daphnes Nohansen Hyrule|King Daphnes Hyrule]] makes a wish with the completed Triforce to wash away the Kingdom of Hyrule via the Great Sea pouring down onto the entire land through its sealed barrier.
