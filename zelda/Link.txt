'''Link''' (リンク ''Rinku'') is the main protagonist of [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' series]]. One of [[Nintendo]]'s flagship characters, he is characterized as a youthful yet exceptionally capable and intrepid swordsman, typically attired in green clothing, whose overarching mission is the defense of the [[Universe|world]] against pervasive malevolent forces, most prominently the [[Hyrule|Kingdom of Hyrule]]. His narrative consistently emphasizes acts of extraordinary courage, strategic acumen, and resilience in overcoming formidable adversities and intricate challenges.

Link’s debut occurred in the eponymous 1986 [[wikipedia:Action-adventure_game|action-adventure]] title ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]'', released for the [[Nintendo Entertainment System]] (originally in Japan on the [[Family Computer Disk System]]). Since that inaugural appearance, he has been a constant presence in virtually all mainline entries in the franchise, with the sole exceptions being the Japan-exclusive [[Satellaview]] titles, which are regarded as non-canonical.

Within the narrative canon, Link occupies a central triadic relationship alongside [[Princess Zelda]] and [[Ganon]]. Zelda frequently functions as both his strategic ally and, in certain iterations, a romantic interest, whereas Ganon embodies his persistent and defining arch-nemesis. Across the franchise, Link’s incarnations exhibit recurring motifs of heroism, moral fortitude, and mastery of both martial and mystical skills, thereby establishing him as an enduring emblem of Nintendo’s legacy in interactive entertainment.

Link is most often the bearer of the [[Triforce of Courage]], a powerful sign of himself being chosen by the [[Golden Goddess]]es as well as his exemplary courage. Aside from being a skilled warrior, he is famous for his [[wikipedia:Elf|elf]]-like appearance and usually being armed with the legendary [[Master Sword]] and [[Hylian Shield]], his signature weapons since ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]''. In addition, as explicitly defined by being the wielder of the Master Sword or other sacred weapons such as the [[Light Arrow]]s, [[Four Sword]], or [[Magical Sword]], as well as restoring peace with the completed [[Triforce]] as seen in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past|A Link to the Past]]'' or ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link Between Worlds|A Link Between Worlds]]'', Link also has a pure heart, similar to Princess Zelda.

Unlike most other central Nintendo protagonists, Link himself does not exist as a single individual within the franchise, but has appeared as many different iterations across the [[The Legend of Zelda timeline|history of Hyrule]], which, depending on the game, depicted as an ordinary boy or young man, much like the eponymous Princess Zelda. Specific incarnations of Link may be distinguished by titles such as the famed [[Hero of Time]] in ''Ocarina of Time'' or the Hero of Winds in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker|The Wind Waker]].'' Regardless of each version of the character, however, Link possess the spirit of the [[Hylia's Chosen Hero|Legendary Hero]] once chosen by the [[The Goddess Hylia|Goddess Hylia]] and is thereby always destined to fight evil, specifically his arch-nemesis Ganon, usually under Princess Zelda's orders (several games since ''The Wind Waker'' have the princess herself assist him in defeating the main villain, though she attempted to do so in ''Ocarina of Time'').

Despite this integral role, some mainline games have Link faced different lead antagonists such as [[Demise]] or [[Majora's Mask (Item)|Majora's Mask]], and few such as ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening|Link's Awakening]],'' ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask|Majora's Mask]]'' or [[The Legend of Zelda: Tri Force Heroes|''Tri Force Heroes'']] did not even have the hero encountering Ganon or Zelda themselves in their plot, adding a feel of mystery in those specific quests. Link also has an [[Dark Link|evil reflection of himself]] which is rarely seen and fought in the series, most prominently in ''[[Zelda II: The Adventure of Link|The Adventure of Link]]'' and ''Ocarina of Time''.

Link himself is not only the series' main character, but also its mascot, and because of its long-running multi-billiondollar success as one of Nintendo's flagship franchises (alongside ''[[mariowiki:Mario_(franchise)|Mario]]'' and ''[[pokemon:Pokémon_(franchise)|Pokémon]]''), he is widely known as one of the most recognized and iconic video game characters of all-time.

== Characteristics ==

=== Physical appearance ===
[[File:TLoZ Link Holding Sword and Shield Artwork.png|thumb|227x227px|Link's debut appearance, as seen in ''The Legend of Zelda'']]
Link's appearance, like Princess Zelda and Ganon, has varied often throughout the long-running series. In general, he is a young, handsome [[Hylian]] with blond hair, long pointy ears and vivid blue eyes who wears a [[Green Clothes|green tunic with a floppy hat]], alongside brown boots and [[gauntlets]] resembling that of an elf's or [[Kokiri]] (though he does not always wear handgear). Though his traditional outfit is normally green, it can be in different colors such as blue, red, violet, or even [[Dark Link|black]], usually through [[Defense|defensive upgrades]], [[Links|splitting himself into four]], or in the case of spin-off appearances, as an alternate palette swap. Due to the dangerous nature of his many [[wikipedia:Middle_Ages|medieval]]-like adventures and is destined to protect [[Hyrule]] from evil, Link's most famous physical feature, similar to certain other Nintendo protagonists such as [[metroidwiki:Samus_Aran|Samus Aran]] or [https://kidicarus.fandom.com/wiki/Pit Pit] (from ''[[metroidwiki:Metroid_(series)|Metroid]]'' and ''[[w:c:kidicarus:Kid Icarus (franchise)|Kid Icarus]]'', respectively) and being a swordsman, is he is armed with weapons, specifically a [[Sword]] and [[Shield]], to fight or defend himself in combat, and is rarely seen unequipped (though he usually starts without them in the main games). In addition, most incarnations wear a strap over their shoulder to carry his [[scabbard]] and shield (although this is inaccurate to how real-world scabbards are used, as they're worn on the hip). Link is normally a child but some games have him as a teenager or young adult. Although not very muscular in frame, Link is powerfully athletic due to his constant duty as the primary hero of Hyrule, as for example, in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess|Twilight Princess]]'', he can physically overpower [[Goron|Gorons]] - a hulking race known for super strength - in a [[Sumo Wrestlin'|sumo wrestling match]] in spite of using the [[Iron Boots]]. In rare cases, he can also carry cumbersome weapons ([[Megaton Hammer]], [[Biggoron's Sword]], etc.) with only one hand.

In the earliest games until ''Ocarina of Time'', Link was originally depicted with brownish hair with dark sleeves on his clothes and sometimes even simple black dot-like eyes in the very first installment's official artwork despite his in-game sprites have shown him with different shades of color or appearances due to hardware limitations of the Nintendo Entertainment System, [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System]], and [[Game Boy]]. The [[Nintendo 64]] titles had since given the main protagonist his now-standardized look, but some later 3D games such as ''Twilight Princess'', ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Skyward Sword|Skyward Sword]]'', and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild|Breath of the Wild]]'' depicted Link with more realistic details (''Ocarina of Time'', however, had given him unique blue earrings as an adult). ''Tri Force Heroes'', ''Breath of the Wild'' and its sequel ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom|Tears of the Kingdom]]'', in particular has allowed Link to wear many different outfits, although his standard clothing for the latter is of a [[Champion's Tunic|cyan shirt]] without a hat similar to Princess Zelda's in-game, unlike seen in prior installments. For the former, these unique clothing, known as [[Outfit|Outfits]], are often based on characters from earlier games and even other Nintendo franchises including ''[[Mario (franchise)|Mario]]''.
[[File:BotW Link Shooting Artwork 2.png|thumb|Link's modern appearance, as seen in ''Breath of the Wild''|282x282px]]
Other games such as ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker|The Wind Waker]]'' or ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Four Swords Adventures|Four Swords Adventures]]'', which use cel-shaded graphics (which was initially met with [[Controversy in The Legend of Zelda series|controversy]]), had depicted Link with a much more cartoonish appearance. Here, his eyes are usually black rather than blue, and, uniquely compared to his "realistic" appearance is shown to be significantly expressive.

In the games where Link is explicitly shown as the bearer of the [[Triforce of Courage]] such as ''Ocarina of Time'' or ''The Wind Waker'', unlike Princess Zelda and Ganon (as [[Ganondorf]]) where their respective [[Triforce of Wisdom|Wisdom]] and [[Triforce of Power|Power]] pieces are [[Mark of the Triforce|marked]] on their right hands, Link has his own on his left hand instead.

=== Personality ===
[[File:Linkface.png|thumb|Link, while pure of heart, often displays a fearless or stoic behavior even when not in combat, as seen with the ''Twilight Princess'' incarnation, for example|205x205px|left]]
Compared to the vast majority of other characters in the series, Link is popularly known for being an individual who has a very strong sense of justice but prefers not to directly speak in the mainline games and most other media - even in the latest installments, and therefore generally only communicates through grunts. But perhaps his most iconic personality feature is during combat, Link often performs sharp yells such as "''Hyaaa!!''" when he attacks [[Enemy|enemies]] using advanced sword techniques (except for the earliest games). Outside of fighting, the "realistic" incarnations such as the Hero of Time or the Links in ''Twilight Princess'' and ''Breath of the Wild'' are shown to be rather stern or "quiet" who smiles infrequently compared to others such as the Hero of Winds where they have the polar opposite range of thoughts and emotions, and at times can even be seen laughing. Despite this, as he possesses the spirit of the Hero and follows the moral code of the warrior, Link as a whole is humble, smart, extremely brave, honest, caring towards others, and he is generally fearless when battling against the forces of evil including his archenemy Ganon. Because of this, he maintains friendships with various characters such as [[Saria]], [[Medli]], [[Impa]], [[Link (Goron)|Link the Goron]] (in ''Ocarina of Time''), [[Tetra]], [[Mipha]], [[Romani]], [[Midna]], [[Skull Kid]], and more. In the case of ''The Wind Waker'', he also is very close to his younger sister [[Aryll]]. Although not shown explicitly, Link has feelings for Princess Zelda and vice versa in most 3D games. On a similar note, Link himself is also friends with [[Mario]], [[Nintendo]]'s mascot and the main protagonist of the [[mariowiki:Mario_(franchise)|''Mario'' series]], as seen in some other media related to both iconic franchises by the gaming giant.

Although technically a child character, some games since ''The Adventure of Link'' and especially ''Ocarina of Time'' featured the hero older than usual and even occasionally reaching adult age, making the latter case a popular image for Link among the fanbase and other media. === Powers and Abilities ===
[[File:ALttP Link Fighting Stalfos Knights Artwork.png|thumb|318x318px|Link fighting two [[Stalfos Knight|Stalfos Knights]] in an artwork of ''A Link to the Past'']]
Being the main character of [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' franchise]] and the incarnation of the Legendary Hero (usually but not always) chosen by [[The Goddess Hylia|Hylia]] and the [[Golden Goddess|Golden Goddesses]] destined to destroy the great evil which constantly threaten the Kingdom of Hyrule with the power of [[light]] and justice, as well as carrying the bloodline of the [[Knight of Hyrule|Knights of Hyrule]] and is the possessor of the [[Triforce of Courage]] - giving him the essence the Goddess [[Farore (Goddess)|Farore]] which drastically augments his raw strength (it must be noted that not all core installments featured him harnessing or even obtaining the divine piece), Link's best-known ability, in spite of his young age, is not only having unmatched courage but also his exceptional natural talent in the martial arts of [[wikipedia:Swordsmanship|swordsmanship]] at a superhuman level - able to perform incredibly fast sword swings or reflexes with his shield no matter the technique, surpassing other proficient sword wielders such as the [[Gerudo]] and possesses speed and agility rivaling that of the [[Sheikah]], proving himself a fierce fighter and easily being worthy of the [[Royal Family of Hyrule|Royal Family]]'s attention, especially Princess Zelda's. The hero is formidable enough to hold his ground against his invaluable ally Zelda - herself the bearer of the [[Triforce of Wisdom]], and fight and defeat many [[Final Boss|ferocious foes]] that cause great terror across various lands at the games' climax, such as [[Vaati]], [[Onox]], [[Veran]], [[Majora's Mask (Item)|Majora]], his own wicked shadow known as [[Dark Link]] (arguably his most dangerous enemy), and even [[Demon King|Demon Kings]] such as [[Demise]], [[Malladus]], and of course [[Ganon]] himself (often as [[Ganondorf]]), effectively making Link one of, if not the most powerful warriors in all of Hyrule and possibly the ''Zelda'' universe. However, in some occasions he does require assistance from Zelda or other allies such as [[Sage|Sages]], [[Navi]], the [[Champions]], and so on to help him complete [[Dungeon|Dungeons]] or take down such antagonists, especially his archenemy who is the possessor of the [[Triforce of Power]]. In other media such as the [[Super Smash Bros. (series)|''Super Smash Bros.'' series]] and ''[[SoulCalibur II]]'', in spite of being completely non-canon to ''The Legend of Zelda'' series itself, Link is also capable of combating and even defeating other Nintendo heroes and villains such as [[mariowiki:Mario|Mario]], [[mariowiki:Donkey_Kong|Donkey Kong]], [[pokemon:Pikachu|Pikachu]], [https://kirby.fandom.com/wiki/Kirby Kirby] (an [[Kirby|enemy]] directly based on this character is fought in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening|Link's Awakening]]''), [[mariowiki:Bowser|Bowser]], etc., as well as Namco's [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_series ''Soul'' series] characters like [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Raphael Raphael Sorel], [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Nightmare Nightmare], or [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Inferno Inferno].

[[File:Hyrule Warriors Spin Attack Video.gif|thumb|279x279px|Link performing the [[Spin Attack]], one of his signature special moves, on a group of Ganon's minions, as seen in ''[[Hyrule Warriors]]''|left]]
Not much is known about how Link became such a highly skilled swordsman that only few can match such as Ganondorf or [[Gramps]], as the earliest installments had himself without a teacher (implying certain incarnations to be self-taught), but some later games have the protagonist being taught by an individual who knows the art of the sword (such as [[Orca]] or, ironically, [[Hero's Spirit|himself as a restless spirit]]), and Link himself being a descendant of the Hylian Knights (or, in the case of ''Breath of the Wild'', actually such) is a major contributing factor. In combat, despite the original ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]'' had Link only execute a basic forward thrust with his Sword, every subsequent game of the longtime series since ''[[Zelda II: The Adventure of Link|The Adventure of Link]]'' has the hero able to perform many complex techniques that not only help him defeat powerful enemies but also solve certain [[Puzzle|puzzles]]. These signature moves include the [[Spin Attack]], [[Jump Slash|Jump Attack]], [[Jump Thrust]], [[Down Thrust]], [[Dash Attack]], [[Parry Attack]], [[Ending Blow]], [[Flurry Rush]], and so forth. He also begins to manually defend with his Shield starting in ''Link's Awakening''. In rare cases and other media, Link can perform punches and kicks as well, proving himself able to battle enemies unarmed. The hero is even capable of [[Dashing|running at high speeds]] (when equipped with the [[Pegasus Boots]]). In ''Super Smash Bros.'', Link is able to skillfully fight with two swords in hand, as seen when using the [[smashwiki:Beam_Sword|Beam Sword]] item (itself a striking resemblance to the popular [https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Lightsaber Lightsaber] weapon from the ''[https://starwars.fandom.com/wiki/Star_Wars Star Wars]'' franchise). Although appearing to be the same in general gameplay across all media, it must be noted that like Princess Zelda, not all incarnations of Link are equal at skill level story-wise, as some are more trained and/or proficient than others and have different abilities. Nevertheless, as seen in all games, every single version of the character is strong enough to kill a [[demon]] or similar monsters alone, which is very evident during [[boss]] battles. Similarly to Ganon, Link also has various different forms which he can use to his benefit, such as a [[Deku Link|Deku Scrub]], [[Goron Link|Goron]], [[Zora Link|Zora]], [[Wolf Link|Wolf]], [[Bunny Link|Rabbit]] (though this renders him defenseless), and even a [[Fierce Deity Link|Deity]].

[[File:BoTW Link stealthily using Bow.png|left|thumb|219x219px|Link in ''Breath of the Wild'' using stealth while armed with his Bow, one of his signature items]]
Outside of his swordfighting abilities, Link is also well-known for carrying a massive arsenal of [[Item|items]] to invaluably aid him on his adventures and solve most puzzles, no matter how big or small. Famously and perhaps comically, whenever Link obtains a new item, including Swords or Shields and even the [[Triforce]] (usually [[Triforce Shard|shards]]), often from a [[Treasure Chest]], Link turns to the screen and holds it up in the air for the player to see with a distinctive upbeat jingle (few other characters perform this act as well). It is unknown how he actually stores his [[inventory]] in the core series, as the gameplay mechanic simply allows the player to manually choose them, though [[The Legend of Zelda (television series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' TV series]] showed Link's items inside a magic pouch being able to magically shrink into a tiny scale and then grow back to normal when in use. Although these greatly varied from game-to-game like his sword and shield, some common ones that accompanied the hero in the vast majority of installments and other media include the [[Bow]], [[Bomb]], and [[Boomerang]], making these three heavily associated with the character. Most other games also have unique items which serve as important key factors of Link's success to stop threats aside from his trademark [[Master Sword]], such as the [[Silver Arrow|Silver Arrows]], [[Light Arrow|Light Arrows]], [[Mirror Shield]], [[Ocarina of Time (Item)|Ocarina of Time]], [[Wind Waker (Item)|Wind Waker]], [[Four Sword]], [[transformation]] [[Mask|Masks]], [[Power Bracelet|Power Bracelets]] and many more. Link is also an expert [[wikipedia:Archery|archer]], [[horse]] rider, musician, driver, and is skilled at stealth.
[[File:Farore's Wind 1.png|thumb|271x271px|Link using [[Farore's Wind]], as seen in ''Ocarina of Time'']]
Even though not a master of this technique on the same level as Princess Zelda or Ganon due to his contrarily focus on sword fighting, Link is an advanced user of [[magic]]. In games where the [[Magic Meter]] is used, he can channel energy onto his sword to perform a powered-up version of his Spin Attack move (such as the [[Great Spin|Great]]/[[Great Spin Attack|Hurricane Spin]]), fire elemental [[Arrow|Arrows]] such as the [[Fire Arrow|Fire]] and [[Ice Arrow|Ice Arrows]], use magic spells such as the [[Din's Fire]] or [[Ice Rod]], or unleash [[Sword Beam|Sword Beams]] and similar attacks like the [[Quake Medallion]] onto his enemies.  Other media such as the ''Super Smash Bros.'' series had shown Link being able to use the Triforce of Courage to trap opponents into a void strongly resembling the full Triforce before performing rapid sword slashes and then launching them with a powerful blow which shatters it, known as the [[Triforce Slash]] (which itself is his [[Final Smash]] since ''[[Super Smash Bros. Brawl|Brawl]]'').

[[File:Ganon Punch2.jpg|thumb|277x277px|Link getting beaten up by Ganondorf, as seen in ''The Wind Waker'', one of the few cases of the hero nearly being defeated by his arch-nemesis]]
With all the aforementioned feats in mind, Link himself is one of the strongest playable protagonists in Nintendo's history, among the likes of Mario or Kirby. However, despite his incredible skills as a combatant and in terms of gameplay he can defeat Ganon or other lead antagonists quickly via an expert player, Link is '''not''' in any way unbeatable, story-wise; various cases across his many adventures had himself sometimes being caught with his guard dropped or even almost defeated, such as Ganondorf easily knocking the Hero of Winds down twice in ''The Wind Waker''. In addition, official source materials such as ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Hyrule Historia|Hyrule Historia]],'' the [[The Legend of Zelda: Encyclopedia|''Zelda Encyclopedia'']], as well as other media within the franchise (such as the [[comics]], [[Manga|manga adaptations]], etc.) have depicted Link's final battles against Ganon regardless of the archvillain's beast or human forms as fierce in nature, and therefore not an easy outcome for either character (similar to Mario with Bowser in the ''Mario'' series). In fact, [[Hero of Time|one certain incarnation]], as explained in the [[The Legend of Zelda timeline|Downfall Timeline]], has a [[Game Over|possible canonical defeat]] by the series' main antagonist.

Other central heroes controllable by the player such as the [[Player|BS-X avatar characters]] in the Japan-exclusive [[Satellaview]] titles ''[[BS The Legend of Zelda]]''/''[[BS The Legend of Zelda: Ancient Stone Tablets|Ancient Stone Tablets]]'', the Warriors in the ''[[Hyrule Warriors]]'' games, or in the case of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Echoes of Wisdom|Echoes of Wisdom]]'', Princess Zelda herself, appear to have similar skills as Link and usually share the same role.

==Mainline appearances==
==={{TLoZ|-}}===
[[File:TLoZ Link Kneeling Artwork.png|left|frameless|161x161px]]
[[File:TLoZ Link Sprite 6.png|right|frameless]]
In the original ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]'', Hyrule is engulfed in chaos after an army of monsters led by Ganon invaded it and stole the [[Triforce of Power]]. In an attempt to prevent Ganon from acquiring the [[Triforce of Wisdom]], Princess Zelda splits it and hides the [[Triforce Shard|eight fragments]] in secret dungeons throughout the land. Before the princess is kidnapped by Ganon, she commands her nursemaid, [[Impa]], to find someone courageous enough to save the kingdom. While wandering the land, the old woman is surrounded by Ganon’s henchmen, though a young swordsman named Link appears and rescues her. After hearing Impa’s plea, he resolves to save Zelda himself and sets out to reassemble the scattered fragments of the Triforce of Wisdom, to become powerful enough to defeat Ganon. Link located the [[Dungeons in The Legend of Zelda|eight underground labyrinths]], defeats several guardian monsters, and retrieves the Triforce fragments. With the restored Triforce of Wisdom, Link is able to infiltrate Ganon’s [[Level 9|hideout]] and ultimately destroys him with the [[Silver Arrow]]. Link picks up the Triforce of Power from Ganon’s ashes for himself and returns both pieces of the Triforce to Princess Zelda, restoring peace to Hyrule. 

==={{TAoL|-}}===
[[File:Determined Link.png|right|135px]]
[[File:TAoL Link Sprite.png|left|frameless]]
In ''[[Zelda II: The Adventure of Link|The Adventure of Link]]'', set six years after {{TLoZ|-}}, the now 16 year old Link notices a strange [[Mark of the Triforce|mark]] on the back of his left hand, resembling the [[Royal Crest|crest of Hyrule]].<ref name="HH 108">{{Cite Book|quote= |book= HH |publisher= |page= 108}}</ref> He seeks out Impa, who responds by taking Link to the [[North Castle]], where a door has been magically sealed for generations. Impa places the back of Link's left hand on the door, and it opens, revealing a sleeping maiden inside. Impa tells Link that the maiden is the original Princess Zelda of Hyrule from long ago (therefore a different incarnation from the previous game), and the origin of the "Legend of Zelda". Long ago, Zelda's brother, the [[Prince of Hyrule]], had tried to force her into telling their recently deceased father's secrets concerning the last of the three golden treasures of his kingdom, the Triforce of Courage. Princess Zelda refused to reveal its location, and the [[Magician]], who had accompanied the prince, tried to strike her down with a spell in anger. Zelda fell under a powerful sleeping spell, but the wizard was unable to control the wildly arcing magic and was killed by it. The prince, filled with remorse and unable to reverse the spell, had his sister placed in the castle tower, in the hope that she would one day be awakened. He decreed that princesses born to the Royal Family from that point on would be named Zelda, in remembrance of this tragedy. Impa says that the mark on Link's hand means that he is the hero chosen to awaken Princess Zelda. She gives Link a chest containing six [[Crystal|crystals]] and ancient writings that only a great future king of Hyrule can read. Link finds that he can read the document, even though he has never seen the language before; it indicates that the crystals must be set into statues within [[Dungeons in The Adventure of Link|six palaces]] scattered all over Hyrule. This will open the way to the [[Great Palace]], which contains the Triforce of Courage. Only the power of the combined Triforces can awaken the enchanted Zelda. Taking the crystals, Link sets out to restore them to their palaces. Meanwhile, although Link had defeated Ganon, the remnants of his army remain scattered across Hyrule. They plan to revive Ganon by killing Link and spilling his blood onto the Demon King's ashes (as shown in the [[Game Over]] screen).

Ultimately, Link restores the crystals to the six palaces, defeating a [[Bosses in The Adventure of Link|strong guardian]] within each one to do so, and enters the Great Palace. After venturing deep inside, Link battles a deadly flying creature known as the [[Thunderbird]], followed by his own shadow, infamously known as [[Dark Link]], guarding the third piece of Triforce. After finally defeating them both, Link at last claims the Triforce of Courage and returns to Princess Zelda. The three triangles unite into the collective Triforce, and Link's wish awakens Zelda, who acknowledges him as the hero of Hyrule (with the ending cutscene just before the credits showing them apparently giving affection as the curtain closes).
==={{ALttP|-}}===

In [[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past|''A Link to the Past'']], Link lives with his [[Link's Uncle|uncle]] in a house near [[Hyrule Castle]]. During this time, the [[Light World|land of Hyrule]] was plagued by a sudden disaster, until the wizard [[Agahnim]] appeared at the court of the [[King of Hyrule (A Link to the Past)|King of Hyrule]] and quelled the upheaval. Named chief adviser to the throne, he soon seized power from the king and kidnapped the seven [[Maiden]]s, descendants of the Seven Wise Men ([[Sage|Sages]]) of long ago. The Maidens were taken to the castle tower and never seen again. One stormy night, Link is awakened by a telepathic message from Princess Zelda, who says that she is locked in the castle dungeon. As the message closes, Link finds his uncle ready for battle, telling Link to remain in bed. After his uncle leaves, however, Link ignores his uncle's command and follows him to the dungeons under the castle. When he arrives, he finds his uncle mortally wounded. Link's uncle tells Link to rescue Princess Zelda from her prison, giving him his sword and shield. Link navigates the castle and rescues Zelda from her cell, and the two escape into a secret passage through the sewers that leads to the [[Sanctuary (A Link to the Past)|Sanctuary]], where they meet the [[Loyal Sage]]. Link is informed by them that Agahnim intends to open the seal to the [[Dark World]], a realm once sealed off long ago by the seven Sages when Ganon and his army of evil were banished into the [[Sacred Realm]], which became the corrupted Dark World. The kidnapped Maidens were sent into the Dark World to open this seal, and Agahnim intended to do the same to Zelda, who is the final one. Link is told by both the Sage and Zelda that the only thing that can defeat Agahnim is the legendary [[Master Sword]], and the former tells him to seek the elder [[Sahasrahla]] to learn more.

Link finds the elder near the [[Eastern Palace]]. To prove that he is worthy to wield the Master Sword, Sahasrahla sends Link to find the three [[Pendants of Virtue]], hidden in dungeons across Hyrule and guarded by mythical defenders. Sahasrahla becomes Link's mentor, offering him hints and advice at key stages of his journey. After retrieving the Pendants, Link takes them to the resting place of the Master Sword deep in the [[Lost Woods]]. As Link draws the sword from its pedestal, Princess Zelda telepathically calls him to the Sanctuary, informing him that [[Soldier (Enemy)|soldier]]s of Hyrule Castle have arrived. Link arrives at the Sanctuary moments after the soldiers have vacated, where he learns from the dying Sage that Zelda has been taken back to Hyrule Castle. Link goes to rescue her but arrives too late; Agahnim sends Zelda to the Dark World which opens the seal. Link then faces Agahnim in battle and defeats him, but fails to kill him as Agahnim teleports Link to the Dark World as well.

To save Hyrule, Link is required to rescue the seven captive Maidens from dungeons scattered across the Dark World. Once the seven Maidens are freed, including Princess Zelda, they use their power to break the barrier around [[Ganon's Tower (A Link to the Past)|Ganon's Tower]], where Link faces Agahnim again. Link defeats him a second time, killing him, and in a shocking turn of events, Ganon himself rises from Agahnim's dead body and flies away. Link chases after the pig-like demon, finally confronting him inside the [[Pyramid of Power]] at the center of the Dark World. After an intense battle resulting in Ganon's demise, Link finally touches the Triforce and with a wish, ultimately restores both the Dark World and Hyrule to their former states before Ganon intervened. This incarnation of Link has the title of '''Hero of Legend'''.

In ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]'', Link has been raised as one of the [[Kokiri]], the children of [[Kokiri Forest]]. Unlike the other Kokiri, including his friend [[Saria]], Link does not have a [[Fairy]] [[Link's Partners|companion]] and is thus shunned by their self-proclaimed leader, [[Mido]]. Link has a nightmare involving a young princess fleeing from a mysterious wicked man who then attacks him. The boy's life changes one day when the [[Great Deku Tree]], the forest's guardian, sends [[Navi]] the Fairy to Link with instructions to bring him immediately. A curse has been cast on the Great Deku Tree, and he asks Link to break it. While Link is successful in defeating the monsters inside the Tree, the Great Deku Tree was doomed to die before Link started.<ref>{{Cite|Though your valiant efforts to break the curse were successful, I was doomed before you started... He warns Link that this evil man seeks the [[Triforce]], and fears a great calamity will befall Hyrule should he seize it. Before passing away, the Great Deku Tree directs Link to [[Hyrule Castle]] where he is to find [[Princess Zelda]], who is none other than the princess from his nightmare. 

At the [[Hyrule Castle]] garden, Link meets Princess Zelda. She believes that Ganondorf is seeking the Triforce. Zelda's description of Ganondorf matches that of the man who killed the Great Deku Tree, as well as the dark figure from Link's nightmare. Zelda asks Link to obtain the three [[Spiritual Stones]] so that he can enter the [[Sacred Realm]] and claim the Triforce himself before Ganondorf reaches it, and ultimately save Hyrule. Link collects the other two stones: the first from [[Darunia]], the leader of the [[Goron]]s (declaring himself his Sworn Brother), and the second from [[Princess Ruto|Ruto]], the princess of the [[Zora]]s (who claims him her future husband when rescued). Link returns to Hyrule Castle, where Ganondorf is pursuing Zelda and her caretaker Impa on horseback, as in his earlier nightmare. Link attempts to stop Ganondorf, who warns him not to interfere with his plans. After Ganondorf rides off, Link retrieves the [[Ocarina of Time (Item)|Ocarina of Time]] that Princess Zelda had left behind for him. Inside the [[Temple of Time]], Link uses the Ocarina of Time and the Spiritual Stones to open the [[Door of Time]] to the Sacred Realm. Through the door, Link finds the [[Master Sword]], a legendary sword forged to destroy evil. As he pulls the Master Sword from its pedestal, Link is suddenly sealed away, and then Ganondorf appears and claims the Triforce for himself. Therefore, Link and Zelda's plan had failed.
[[File:Linkposeoot.png|thumb|273x273px|Link as an adult in ''Ocarina of Time'']]

After the five Sages awaken, Sheik reveals himself to be Princess Zelda in disguise, and the seventh and final Sage, as their leader. She tells the Hero of Time that Ganondorf's heart was unbalanced, causing the Triforce to split into three pieces. With the princess completely exposed, Ganondorf then kidnaps Zelda in a [[crystal]] and imprisons her in his [[Ganon's Castle|castle]] known as Ganon's Castle where Hyrule Castle once stood. The other six Sages help Link enter the castle, where he fights to the Evil King's sanctum. There, Link frees Zelda and seemingly defeats Ganondorf, but the wounded villain destroys the castle in an attempt to kill his sworn foes. The duo safely escapes the collapsing castle, not long before Ganondorf suddenly emerges from the rubble, trapping Princess Zelda and engages at Link once more. Although Link himself makes a bold move by angrily drawing the Master Sword to dare the Gerudo warlock to challenge the hero again, using the Triforce of Power at its maximum power, he furiously transforms from his human form into a large, nearly invulnerable boar-like [[demon]] named [[Ganon]] - his true self, and immediately knocks the Master Sword from Link's hand. The fierce battle now over, Princess Zelda, as a Sage, uses the Ocarina of Time and possibly Triforce of Wisdom to permanently send Link back to his original time to live out his lost childhood (unknowingly creating [[The Legend of Zelda timeline|new history]]), at which point Navi departs. In ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess|Twilight Princess]]'', Link is a teenage youth at 17 who has been raised as a wrangler outside of [[Hyrule]] territory in [[Ordon Village]]. He is fascinated by swordsmanship and horse riding.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= E |publisher= |page= 19}}</ref> His mentor (and adoptive older brother), [[Rusl]], requests that Link go in his place to deliver a sword he has made for the [[Royal Family of Hyrule]]. Before Link can go on this journey, his village is attacked by [[King Bulblin]] and his band of [[Bulblin]]s, who carry off the village children. Pursuing them, Link encounters a wall of [[Twilight]] blocking the way. A [[Shadow Beast]] pulls him beyond the wall (which scares him) into the Twilight, where he is transformed into a [[Wolf Link|wolf]] and imprisoned in Hyrule Castle's dungeon. Link is soon freed by an imp-like [[Twili]] being named [[Midna]]. She agrees to help him if he obeys her unconditionally. She guides him to Princess Zelda in the castle tower. Zelda explains that [[Zant]], the King of Twilight, has stolen the light from three of the four [[Spirits of Light]] and conquered Hyrule. In order to save the kingdom, Link must first restore the Light Spirits by entering the Twilight-covered areas and, as a wolf, recover the Spirits' lost light. Accompanied by Midna, Link restores the light to the Light Spirit [[Faron (Spirit)|Faron]] and is returned to his Hylian form. During this time, Link also helps Midna locate the fragments of the [[Fused Shadow]], relics containing powerful dark magic. In return, she helps Link find Ordon Village's kidnapped children. Once Link has restored the Light Spirits and found all of the Fused Shadow Fragments, they are suddenly ambushed by Zant. After he relieves Midna of the Fused Shadow fragments, she ridicules him for abusing his tribe's magic, but Zant responds that his power comes from another source as he uses it to turn Link back into a wolf. He then leaves Midna to die from the world's light forced upon her, prompting Link to bring her to Zelda to help her. Link learns that he needs the Master Sword to return to human form. Princess Zelda sacrifices herself to heal Midna with her power before vanishing mysteriously. Moved by her sacrifice, Midna agrees to help Link find the Master Sword. After finding the legendary blade, Link is cleansed of the magic that kept him in wolf form, transforming it into the [[Shadow Crystal]]. Using this crystal, Link is able to switch between both forms at will.

Midna leads Link to the [[Mirror of Twilight]] located deep within the [[Gerudo Desert]], the only known gateway between the [[Twilight Realm]] and Hyrule. However, they discover that the mirror is shattered. The ghostly Sages there explain that Zant tried to destroy it, but he was only able to shatter it into fragments; only the true ruler of the Twili can completely destroy the Mirror of Twilight. They also reveal that they used it a century ago to banish Ganondorf, the Gerudo leader who attempted to steal the Triforce, to the Twilight Realm when executing him failed. Assisted by an underground group called [[The Resistance]], they meet in Castle Town, Link and Midna set out to retrieve the missing shards of the mirror, defeating those they infected. Once the portal is restored, Midna is revealed to be the true ruler of the Twilight Realm, usurped by Zant when he cursed her into her current form. Confronting Zant, Link and Midna learn that Zant's coup was made possible when he forged a pact with Ganondorf, who asked for Zant's assistance in conquering Hyrule. After Link defeats Zant, Midna recovers the Fused Shadows, but destroys Zant after learning that only Ganondorf's death can release her from her curse. Returning to Hyrule, Link and Midna find Ganondorf in Hyrule Castle, with a lifeless Princess Zelda suspended above his head. Ganondorf fights Link by [[Possessed Zelda|possessing Zelda's body]] and eventually by transforming into a [[Dark Beast Ganon|boar-like beast]], but Link defeats him and Midna is able to resurrect Zelda. Ganondorf then revives, and Midna teleports Link and Princess Zelda outside the castle so she can hold him off with the Fused Shadow. However, as Hyrule Castle collapses, it is revealed that Ganondorf was victorious as he crushes Midna's helmet. Ganondorf engages Link on horseback, and, assisted by Zelda and the Light Spirits, Link eventually knocks Ganondorf off his horse and they duel on foot before Link strikes down the King of Evil and ultimately [[Ending Blow|plunges the Master Sword into his chest]], a move he learned from the [[Hero's Spirit]] (who is actually the restless soul of his ancestor, Link the legendary Hero of Time from ''Ocarina of Time''). With Ganondorf dead, the Light Spirits not only bring Midna back to life, but restore her to her true form. After bidding farewell to Link and Princess Zelda, Midna returns home before destroying the Mirror of Twilight with a tear to maintain balance between Hyrule and the Twilight Realm. Near the end, as Hyrule Castle is rebuilt, Link temporarily leaves Ordon Village heading to parts unknown.

This incarnation of Link is given the title of '''Hero of Twilight'''.<ref name="Hero of Twilight" />

In ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Spirit Tracks|Spirit Tracks]]'', taking place 100 years after the Hero of Winds found the [[Hyrule|new Hyrule]], Link is a child who lives with [[Niko]] in [[Aboda Village]]. He is an apprentice of a master engineer, named [[Alfonzo]], who has helped him in order to become a [[Royal Engineer]]. When Princess Zelda presents Link with a [[Engineer Certificate|certificate]] for this achievement,<ref>{{Cite|I hereby recognize our newest royal engineer... Link! Work hard, for we all rely on you, Link.|Princess Zelda|ST}}</ref> she also gives him a letter, telling him to meet with her, to discuss her suspicions over the mysterious [[Chancellor Cole]] as well as the disappearing {{Term|ST|Spirit Tracks (Object)|link}}. When Link sneaks Zelda out of the [[Hyrule Castle|Castle]], they meet up with Alfonzo, who escorts them to the [[Tower of Spirits]] by train. However, as the Spirit Tracks begin to fade, they crash, and are then ambushed by the chancellor, who attacks the trio. Link and his master are knocked unconscious while Zelda's spirit is separated from her body, which is seized by Cole's apprentice, [[Byrne]]. Link awakens back in [[Hyrule Castle]] to discover that Zelda is now a ghost and that Link is the only person who can see her. After visiting [[Anjean]] in the Tower of Spirits, he agrees to help her in defeating Chancellor Cole and recover her body in the process.

After Link and Zelda travel up to the Tower of Spirits to recover a [[Rail Map]], a Phantom Knight attacks him, but Princess Zelda herself charges at the Phantom just in time to take control of its body. After this, Link and Zelda begin their journey to restore the Tower of Spirits and the Spirit Tracks, traveling across the overworld using the [[Spirit Train]] that is granted to Link by [[Anjean]]. Whenever Link returns to the Spirit Tower to obtain another Rail Map, Princess Zelda possesses a Phantom's armor in order to aid the young hero. As the pair continue to travel, Link and Zelda establish a close friendship as the two get to know each other better.

Once Princess Zelda recovers her body from Malladus with the aid of Link, the two together face up against the Demon King, who has now taken over Chancellor Cole's body. Together, Link and Zelda deal the final blow on Malladus using the [[Lokomo Sword]], and shortly after, Anjean and the rest of the Lokomos depart towards the heavens. As they leave, Anjean entrusts Princess Zelda to watch over the land, asking Link to help her as well.<ref>{{Cite|Please watch over this land, my dear. And, Link... You must help her.|Anjean|ST}}</ref> Link and Zelda are then seen holding hands as they witness the departure of the Lokomos. Unlike other instances where Link departs from the land after having saved it, the young hero stays in Hyrule to fulfill Anjean's request, where he continues to be either an engineer or a warrior, depending on which Link chose. This is witnessed when Princess Zelda gets up from her seat in her office and waves out the window after having heard either a train's whistle or the clanking of swords.

==={{ALBW|-}}===
[[File:ALBW Link Drawing.png|left|203x203px]]
In ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link Between Worlds|A Link Between Worlds]]'', Link dreams that he is in an unknown realm with a [[Ganon|familiar character]]. He is about to engage in battle, but [[Gulley]] comes into Link's room and wakes him up from his sleep. In the [[Blacksmith's Forge]], the [[Captain]] of the Guard comes to pick up the new sword that the [[Blacksmith (A Link Between Worlds)|Blacksmith]] forged for him. But he forgets it as he leaves, and Link is sent to return it to him. Shortly afterward, he encounters [[Yuga]], who has transformed the Captain into a [[Painting]] on the [[Sanctuary (A Link Between Worlds)|Sanctuary]]'s wall after he tried to defend [[Seres]], the [[Priest]]'s daughter. He then traps Seres herself in a Painting. Using the [[Forgotten Sword]], Link attacks Yuga, but Yuga evades the attack by [[Wall Merge|Wall Merging]]. Link crashes into the wall and is knocked unconscious. After this, Yuga leaves with Seres' Painting in hand. Link eventually wakes up in his house and meets a mysterious person named [[Ravio]]. After telling Ravio about Yuga, Link is instructed to go see [[Princess Zelda]]. Asking if he can stay in Link's house, Ravio gives him a strange bracelet in exchange. When Link meets Zelda, she gives him the Pendant of Courage and tells him to go meet [[Sahasrahla]]. Once Link does this, he is then told to find [[Osfala]], who is in the Eastern Palace, and is a descendant of the [[Sage|Seven Sages]]. Sahasrahla worries that Yuga may go after him next. Link meets Osfala at the entrance to the Eastern Palace, but Osfala simply laughs off Link's warning about Yuga, telling the young hero that he is just as powerful as the Seven Sages were. He then leaves to confront Yuga. However, after traversing the Eastern Palace, Link runs into Yuga yet again, who turns Osfala into a Painting, just as he did Seres. Link battles Yuga, managing to best him with the help of the [[Bow]] he rented from Ravio. In a fit of rage, Yuga traps Link as a Painting and leaves. However, Link escapes with the help of [[Ravio's Bracelet]] and discovers he could now turn himself into a Painting and back at will with the bracelet. 

Meanwhile, having collected the Seven Sages, Yuga quickly takes over [[Hyrule Castle]]. Link meets Sahasrahla again, who instructs Link to obtain the Pendants of Courage, Wisdom, and Power. Only then will he be able to wield the [[Master Sword]] in order to access the castle. After completing this task, Link obtains the Master Sword and delves into the castle, where he finds Yuga with a Painting of Zelda. Yuga then escapes through a mysterious crack in the wall via his ability to Wall Merge. Link pursues Yuga to the other side, where Yuga has begun the process of reviving the evil Ganon, using the power of the Sages to do so. Once Ganon is resurrected, Yuga merges with the Demon King, becoming even more powerful, and is now [[Yuga Ganon]]. Link is saved by [[Princess Hilda]] and is told he is in the kingdom of [[Lorule]] - a counterpart of his own Hyrule. Link is then given a new task - to rescue and awaken the Seven Sages. Upon awakening all of the Sages, Link is granted the power of the [[Triforce|Triforce of Courage]]. After Hilda's failed attempt to steal the Triforce from him to save her kingdom, Link faces off against Yuga Ganon and defeats him. Afterwards, Link and Zelda are taken to the Lorulean version of the [[Sacred Realm]], through which they return home to their own Sacred Realm. There, they find Hyrule's Triforce and wish for the Lorulean Triforce to be restored. After the credits, Link returns the Master Sword to its pedestal like in some earlier games.

=== {{EoW|-}} ===
[[File:EOW Link.png|left|223x223px]]
In ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Echoes of Wisdom|Echoes of Wisdom]]'', Link is a young swordsman from [[Suthorn Village]]. Some time before the beginning of the game, he and several other children were "stolen away" by a Rift. Amazingly, all of them returned, the first, and then only, time this had occurred. Though they all lost something as a result, including the ability to speak, only Link remembered being "stolen away" in the first place, and even to his own surprise, Link gained the ability to sense when Rifts formed, and so dedicated himself to fighting off the Dark Monsters that emerged from the Rifts, to prevent anyone else from being "stolen away" like he was.

One day, Link saved an old man named Lueburry from assault by a monster in the forest. The next day, Lueburry visited Link, learned the boy's story, revealed his own research into Might Crystals, and gifted Link the [[Sword of Might]], which was more effective against the Dark Monsters. Over time, Lueburry would also give Link the Bow of Might and the Bombs of Might.

Eventually, Link would learn of [[Princess Zelda]]'s abduction by [[Ganon#Echoes of Wisdom|a certain blue porcine monster]], and would rush off to save her. While she returned safely, Link did not, having been pulled into yet another Rift (although not before launching an arrow at Zelda's crystal prison to ensure she was able to escape). His equipment would be taken by "[[Echo Link|echoes]]" of himself, though Princess Zelda would defeat them and take his de-powered equipment for herself.

During Princess Zelda's own journey, Link traveled the [[Still World]], though his goal, be it to escape or somehow resolve the crisis from inside the Still World, and objectives for accomplishing it were unknown. Eventually, Link arrived at Stilled Hyrule Castle just after Zelda did, eventually appearing to save her when [[Null|a strange dark mass]] attacked her after she defeated a [[Echo Ganon|seemingly-revived Ganon]]. This would lead to Link being captured in Zelda's place, and brought to the Stilled Ancient Ruins.

Some time later, [[Triforce of Courage|a fragment]] of the [[Triforce|Prime Energy]] would make its way to Link, with Princess Zelda and an Echo of herself arriving not long after, the former in pursuit of the latter, and both possessing the remaining two fragments. After the two battled, Echo Zelda would flee into [[Null's Body]] with [[Triforce of Power|its fragment of the Prime Energy]] while Zelda freed Link. Returning his weapons to him, Zelda would rely solely on the Echoes she'd recorded in the Tri Rod as the two fought their way through [[Null]]'s Body and against Null itself. They split up during the final fight, with Zelda pursuing Null underwater and Link fending off several Null Hands. After both of them scored victories, they reunited to deal with Null once and for all. Reuniting the Prime Energy, they used its complete power to ultimately destroy Null.

After [[Tri]] bids them farewell and returns with their friends to the [[Golden Goddess|goddesses]] to slumber until needed again, Link and Princess Zelda would be returned to Castle Town, where it is soon discovered that Link has regained his ability to speak, to everyone's shock.

Unlike all other games, Link, although featured, is '''not''' the main protagonist of ''Echoes of Wisdom'', but rather the tritagonist, as Princess Zelda takes his traditional role for the player, a first for the franchise's core series.

Link asks Princess Zelda for a kiss for luck before leaving, but is rejected. The young hero leaves with Gwonam to Koridai on his magic carpet. Once there, Link ventures on his quest to defeat the masters of the "Faces of Evil"; mountain peaks that bare resemblance to Ganon's minions who now control certain areas of Koridai. During his quest, Link and Gwonam learn that Zelda was kidnapped by Ganon and put under a sleeping spell during Link's absence. At the end of his quest, Link fulfills his destiny by defeating Ganon at his [[Ganon's Lair|lair]] and trapping him in the [[Book of Koridai]], an item Link found during his quest and the only thing that could defeat Ganon. Link wakes Princess Zelda from her spell by ringing a gong, and exclaims that he just saved her, though she teasingly denies this. They are taken back to Hyrule by Gwonam, who proclaims Link as the Hero of Koridai. Link once again asks Zelda for a kiss, but is again rejected.

==={{HW|-}}===
[[File:HW Link Render.png|252x252px|right]]
Prior to the events of {{HW}}, a hero in green had appeared and sealed away an evil entity in order to restore the Triforce's balance, which had been disrupted by the evil. The hero split the evil soul into four parts and banished each one to a different area.

During the events of {{HW|-}}, Link is a young, but powerful, knight in training. Due to his previous incarnation's close connections with the princesses of Hyrule, [[Cia]] falls under the influence of the sealed evil and becomes corrupt out of her envy. After Cia's forces attack Hyrule Castle, Link sets out on a journey to save Hyrule and its princess from the forces of darkness. The Link in this game appears to be based on the Hero of the Sky incarnation from ''Skyward Sword''.

Link has the most styles out of any character with five. His default is [[Hylian Sword]] which has him equipped with a sword and shield. The [[Magic Rod#Hyrule Warriors|Magic Rod]] has longer range and great crowd control, but trades it in for lower defensive capabilities. His third weapon, the [[Gauntlets#Hyrule Warriors|Gauntlets]], grants him super strength in order to wield huge, heavy objects such as the [[Ball and Chain]] or even the giant pillars from {{OoT|-}}. They also allow him to dig into the earth and fire electrical bursts along the ground. His fourth and final weapon is the [[Great Fairy]], where is not Link who fights, but instead the Great Fairy who carries the young hero around in a [[Bottle]]. It is a very powerful style, having huge area of effect attacks and easy to exploit. The [[Master Sword#Hyrule Warriors|Master Sword]] is considered its own style and has a powered up version of the Spin Slash. The Master Quest DLC pack also gives Link a new type of weapon, his [[Horse (Item)|horse]] [[Epona]].

Once he gets the [[Green Clothes#Hero's Clothes|Hero's Tunic]], he also gets pauldrons, a chain mail under the tunic, and a blue scarf with the [[Royal Crest]]. His {{TP}}, {{SS}}, and {{OoT}} incarnations come with the Courage Set. A [[Dark Link]] costume also becomes available by purchasing the Hero of Hyrule Pack. By purchasing the Master Quest Pack, Link's purple tunic becomes available as one of the unlockable costumes in the Master Quest Adventure Mode Map.

The Hero of Time incarnation based on ''Majora's Mask'' is also available as a separate downloadable character, by purchasing the Majora's Mask pack. He attacks using a larger version of the [[Kokiri Sword]], and can wear the mighty [[Fierce Deity's Mask]] to perform certain powerful attacks.

==Other Heroes of Hyrule==
Besides the iterations of Link that appear in the games, the backstory to some of the games feature additional unnamed heroes of Hyrule who have a similar appearance to Link.

===The Hero of Men, from the {{Term|TMC|Minish Legend|link}}===
[[File:TMC Stained Glass Sprite 2.png|right|160x160px]]
{{Main|Hero of Men}}
The back story of {{TMC|-}}, tells of the {{Term|TMC|Minish Legend|link}}. In the {{Term|TMC|Minish Legend}}, the [[Hero of Men]], a hero clad in green clothes but lacking Link's traditional green hat, arose to fight the demons attacking the world. This {{Term|TMC|Minish Legend}} is depicted in a series of stained glass windows beneath the [[Elemental Sanctuary]]. One of the stained glass windows depicts the Hero of Men receiving the [[Picori Blade]] and the [[Light Force]] from the [[Minish]], while in the other, it shows the hero confronting the monsters that were attacking Hyrule.

===The Hero of the Four Sword===
The back story of {{FS|-}} describes how prior to the events of the game, Vaati attacked Hyrule kidnapping young girls. The knights of Hyrule fought bravely against Vaati, but could not defeat him. A lone traveling boy appeared wielding the [[Four Sword]]. The boy's sword split his body into four and they defeated Vaati and sealed him away into the Four Sword.  After defeating Vaati, the boy vanished into the forest, leaving behind his sword. The people of Hyrule built the [[Four Sword Sanctuary]] protect the Four Sword and its seal on Vaati.

According to {{HH}}, this hero is known as the Hero of the Four Sword and is '''not''' the same hero who appears in {{TMC|-}} or {{FS|-}}, but is another hero who appears in between the two games. {{HH|-}} describes him as someone about whom little is known, but he is possibly a descendant of the hero who previously defeated Vaati in {{TMC|-}}.<ref name="HH 82" />

===The Hero from 10,000 Years Before {{BotW}}===
[[File:BotW Wii U channel.jpg|300px|right]]

10,000 years before the events of {{BotW}}, an incarnation of the Hero branding the Master Sword, united with the Princess of that era, the four [[Divine Beast]]s and numerous [[Guardian (Breath of the Wild)|Guardian]]s to seal [[Calamity Ganon]] away beneath Hyrule Castle. This legend is told in the prologue and retold to Link separately by [[Impa]] and [[Kass]].<ref>{{Cite|And when the hero wielding the sword that seals the darkness delivered his final blow... The princess used her sacred power to seal away Calamity Ganon.|Impa|BotW}}</ref><ref>{{Cite|The hero with the sealing sword struck the final blow,<br/>And the holy power of the princess sealed Ganon so.<br/><br/>And that is the story of the brazen attack,<br/>On Calamity Ganon 10,000 years back.|Kass|BotW}}</ref> This version of the Hero is never seen directly, but appears in artistic form on an ancient tapestry design. The artwork depicts him with green eyes, fiery red hair and wearing a light blue [[Champion's Tunic]] like the one his successor wears in the distant future. In [[The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom|Tears of the Kingdom]], this incarnation is given the title of '''Ancient Hero''', whose armor can be worn as a single piece known as the Ancient Hero's Aspect. When Link talks to Impa while wearing the Ancient Hero's Aspect, she will notice the striking resemblance between Link and the Ancient Hero's depiction on the [[Calamity Ganon Tapestry|tapestry]], suggesting that the Ancient Hero was likely one of the [[Zonai]].<ref>{{Cite|Ah. I see that you are wearing the attire of the ancient hero. How amusing. You almost look like that figure depicted on the screen...|Impa|TotK}}</ref>

===The Hero of Light===
[[File:Hero of light.gif|right|130px]]
In ''[[BS The Legend of Zelda]]'' and {{AST}}, a game for the [[Satellaview]], the [[BS-X Avatar|avatar characters]] of the BS-X replace Link as the central heroes (though they still resemble him), who could be male or female depending on the player's settings, and become known as the [[Hero of Light]] (sharing the same title as the ''Four Swords Adventures'' incarnation of Link). For the latter game, six years after the events of {{ALttP|-}} and during Link's absence, Hyrule's long peace comes to a sudden end as Princess Zelda suffers from reoccurring dreams of evil to come. She and [[Aginah]] then witness a flash of light in the forest and upon arriving to investigate, find an unconscious youth and bring them to rest at Sahasrahla's old hideaway. Zelda senses that the youth may be endowed with the power of courage, and believes that they may be the Hero of Light. 

The young hero was then given the task to locate several stone tablets and eventually learns that though Link had defeated Ganon previously in the last game, his spirit had lived on through his malice. Ganon had learned of and coveted the power of the Hero of Light, and had subsequently managed to summon the hero into Hyrule, hoping to obtain the hero's power to be fully reborn. Once Ganon was defeated, the Hero of Light returned the Master Sword they had used to its pedestal and returned to where they had come from in a flash of light.

==Trivia==
[[File:Link 30th anniversary.jpg|thumb|325x325px|The many incarnations of Link in the series' 30th anniversary (2016) artwork]]
*Being the mascot of the highly successful [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' series]] and one of the first protagonists in video game history, Link has been widely described by critics and fans alike as one of the greatest or most influential video game characters of all time in a similar level to [[Mario]] from the [[Mario (franchise)|''Mario'' franchise]]. He is even considered the most critically acclaimed playable video game character and most ubiquitous [[wikipedia:Action-adventure_game|action-adventure]] character according to ''Guinness World Records''. Although never outright stated by [[Nintendo]], some sources have also claimed Link as its secondary overall mascot behind Mario himself.
*As Link made his first appearance in 1986, he is one of Nintendo's oldest known characters not from ''Mario''.
*[[Shigeru Miyamoto]] has stated that Link's [[Green Clothes|green tunic and hat]] (and likely overall appearance) were inspired by [https://disney.fandom.com/wiki/Peter_Pan_(character) Disney's version of Peter Pan], as he himself is a fan of the [https://disney.fandom.com/wiki/Peter_Pan_(film) 1953 animated film].<ref>{{Cite Person|quote= {{Exp|A l'époque, qui disait personnage avec des oreilles pointues disait aussi Peter Pan, et comme j’aime beaucoup Disney, nous avons commencé à nous en inspirer.|At the time, who said a character with pointy ears said Peter Pan, and as I love Disney, we began to  get inspired.}}|name= Shigeru Miyamoto|url= https://www.gamekult.com/actualite/miyamoto-la-wii-u-et-le-secret-de-la-triforce-105550.html|title= MIYAMOTO, LA WII U ET LE SECRET DE LA TRIFORCE}}</ref> Himself having a [[Fairy]] companion in some games such as [[Navi]] is also a reference to the character [https://disney.fandom.com/wiki/Tinker_Bell Tinker Bell]. To a lesser extent, his being raised by the [[Kokiri]] and [[The Great Deku Tree|Great Deku Tree]] in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]'' also references the Lost Boys. This also applies to [[Dark Link]] being based on Peter Pan's shadow. Peter is also talented at playing a flute, which reflects toward Link playing woodwind instruments such as the [[Flute|Recorder]], [[Ocarina of Time (Item)|Ocarina of Time]], or the [[Spirit Flute]].
*According to [[Yoshiaki Koizumi]], during the development of the character, there were initially plans to give Link a button nose similar to that of Mario, but his wife rejected it, citing that Nintendo already gave too many funny noses to their characters and suggested a more handsome nose for a change, ultimately giving him a pointed one instead.[[File:ALTTP Link swinging Master Sword with right hand.png|thumb|An example of Link seemingly using his right hand to swing his sword - the Master Sword, while he is facing east in the early games, as seen in ''A Link to the Past'']]
* In the original ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]'', ''[[Zelda II: The Adventure of Link|The Adventure of Link]]'', ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past|A Link to the Past]]'', ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening|Link's Awakening]]'', or any other 2D-based game of the series, whenever Link faces east, he swings his {{Term|Series|Sword|link}} as if he was right-handed and holding his [[Shield]] on his left. This is due to Link's sprite being flipped horizontally; this also happens when Link performs a {{Term|Series|Spin Attack|link}} while facing east, spinning the opposite direction to what he normally would (with the exception of ''A Link to the Past'').
* Despite most {{TLoZ|-}} games featuring the older Link (and Zelda) depicts him supposedly as an adult at around 17 years of age, he is not technically such by real-life standards but rather just short, as most countries' [[wikipedia:Age_of_majority|legal age of adulthood]] - including Japan, is 18, making him still an adolescent. The only games so far where he is explicitly such are ''Ocarina of Time'' and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom|Tears of the Kingdom]]''.
* Although technically created by Shigeru Miyamoto (who also created Mario, Donkey Kong, etc.), Link's original sprite in ''The Legend of Zelda'' was designed by fellow longtime employee and close friend [[Takashi Tezuka]].
* Although Link himself is the most well-known character by Nintendo to be a powerful swordsman, various others exist such as [[fireemblem:Marth|Marth]], [[fireemblem:Roy|Roy]] or [[fireemblem:Ike|Ike]] from the ''[[fireemblem:Fire_Emblem_(series)|Fire Emblem]]'' series, as well as [[Richard (Link's Awakening)|Prince Richard]] seen in ''Link's Awakening''. The first aforementioned three are featured in the ''Super Smash Bros.'' series. Compared to most such characters, Link does not wear a cape and they lack a shield.
* In {{ALttP|-}}, for unknown reasons, Link's hair appears as pink; in official art for the game, it is a light brown. It is possibly due to the graphical limitations of the [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System|Super NES]].
** On a similar note, ''Link's Awakening'', {{OoS}}, and {{OoA}} depicted Link with dark hair during standard gameplay, and with blond hair during cutscenes such as the opening and endings of the game (and in the case of {{LA|DX}}, the photoshoot events), due to a limited color palette of the [[Game Boy]] and [[Game Boy Color]].
** In ''The Legend of Zelda'', the hardware limitations of the [[Nintendo Entertainment System|NES]] resulted in Link's eyes being the same palette as his tunic (alongside certain characters, items, and even [[Pols Voice|enemies]]). Therefore, if equipped with the [[Blue Ring|Blue]] or [[Red Ring]], they will also appear a whitish blue or red, respectively.
** A similar case in ''Ocarina of Time'', due to the graphical or storage limitations of the [[Nintendo 64]], although seen in official art, the upper belt Link usually wears to carry his [[Scabbard]] and [[Shield]] is missing. This is however fixed in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask|Majora's Mask]]'' via the memory increase by the Expansion Pak. The games' [[Nintendo 3DS]] remakes have Link wear this belt.
** Interestingly, in his ''SoulCalibur II'' appearance on the [[Nintendo GameCube]], whenever Link chooses weapons other than the [[Master Sword]] and [[Hylian Shield]], his scabbard seemingly vanishes.
[[File:BotW Link Riding Master Cycle Zero Artwork.png|thumb|316x316px|''Breath of the Wild'' artwork of Link riding the Master Cycle Zero, which fans have mixed reactions of seeing the hero being able to drive]]
*Despite ''The Legend of'' ''Zelda'' series' setting being in a [[Medieval elements in The Legend of Zelda series|medieval fantasy]], Link is capable of driving high-tech vehicles. As seen in ''Breath of the Wild'' as well as ''[[mariowiki:Mario_Kart_8|Mario Kart 8]]''/''[[mariowiki:Mario_Kart_8_Deluxe|Deluxe]]'' he can ride the [[Master Cycle Zero]] and [[mariowiki:Master_Cycle|Master Cycle]], respectively, which are based on a [[wikipedia:Motorcycle|motorcycle]], and in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Spirit Tracks|Spirit Tracks]]'' he can operate the [[Spirit Train]], which is a [[wikipedia:Locomotive|locomotive]]. In the aforementioned [[mariowiki:Mario_Kart_(series)|''Mario Kart'' series]] game, Link can also ride other vehicles based on a [[wikipedia:Go-kart|go-kart]], [[wikipedia:All-terrain_vehicle|ATV]], or [[wikipedia:Car|automobile]]. This, however, sparked [[Controversy in The Legend of Zelda Series|controversy]].
*Although Link's exact height is unknown and somewhat inconsistent in some media, according to units used by developers to make ''Ocarina of Time'', the Hero of Time as an adult (with his bones straightened, his feet adjusted to be flatter to the ground, and the scene projected orthographically) stands 160.56cm, or 5'3.21". In the same conditions, Child Link stands 111.46cm, or 3'7.88". Based on resolution output, the meter stick in {{Term|OoT3D|Lakeside Laboratory|link}} usually puts Adult Link between 160.5cm and 160.7cm. Despite this, Link based on either age appear to be around the average size in most 3D games.
**Although technically a transformation, [[Fierce Deity Link]] in ''Majora's Mask'' and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask 3D|Majora's Mask 3D]]'' stands at a very similar height as [[Ganondorf]] (who is measured 230cm or 7'6¾" in ''Ocarina of Time'' according to ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Hyrule Historia|Hyrule Historia]]''), making himself one of the tallest humanoids of the series in this state.
** To date, despite claims of him being rather 5'7" by revised graphics of the lab, no one has accurately measured Link in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time 3D|Ocarina of Time 3D]]'', with current measurements relying on perspective projection and inaccurate understandings of how the meter stick texture repeats.

*Due to Link's handsome appearance and sometimes his heroic actions, some games and other media within the franchise have at least one female character having a love interest in him, usually [[Princess Zelda]]. In ''Ocarina of Time'', however, the Hero of Time has a notable number - six (Zelda, [[Princess Ruto]], [[Saria]], [[Malon]], and possibly [[Nabooru]] and [[Navi]]). This has led many fans to strongly believe that Link as a whole, especially the aforementioned incarnation, is popular with the ladies.
**The rather unusual case of ''Ocarina of Time'' was actually the result of the development team originally planning to have elements of a [[wikipedia:Dating_sim|dating simulation game]] (which are surprisingly popular in Japan). Although ultimately scrapped, remnants of the idea clearly remained in the final release.
**Although very uncommon in the franchise (or Nintendo games in general), a few games, specifically ''The Adventure of Link'', ''Majora's Mask'', the [[Linked Game|linked ''Oracle'' game]], and ''Spirit Tracks'' depicted Link taking direct affection. All of those cases were from Zelda, except for ''Majora's Mask'', which was given by [[Cremia]].
**In spite of this, in the core series, although Link is well-known to have a pure heart, his seemingly romantic relationships with the female characters often appears to be unintentional, as Link rarely displays such an interest in return even with Princess Zelda, a stark contrast of Mario towards [[mariowiki:Princess_Peach|Princess Peach]] in the ''Super Mario'' series.
**In non-canonical media such as the comics or manga series, Link's relationships are more explicit. For example, in the ending of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess (Himekawa)|Twilight Princess]]'' [[The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess (Himekawa)|(Himekawa)]], [[Midna|Minda]] as her true form gives Link a direct kiss before saying goodbye.

*Link's use of his scabbard to carry the various Swords on his back with his Shields in the 3D games and other appearances, including his signature Master Sword, is rather unrealistic, as they are in fact designed to be worn on a user's hip. This was, however, an intentional choice by Nintendo to make Link be instantly recognized.[[File:SCII Link fighting Astaroth.webp|thumb|254x254px|Link's rare appearance in ''SoulCalibur II'', a non-''Zelda'' (or Nintendo in general) title which featured the character in a fitting arcade-style fighting game]]
*Link's crossover appearance in the 2002 [[wikipedia:Namco|Namco]] game ''[https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soulcalibur_II SoulCalibur II]'' exclusively for the [[Nintendo GameCube]] release (and features the Hero of Time from ''Ocarina of Time'') marks the first, and so far only, time the character appears in a true [[wikipedia:Fighting_game|fighting game]], unlike the [[smashwiki:Super_Smash_Bros._(series)|''Super Smash Bros.'' series]] as that franchise involves characters battling on platforms with a completely different ruleset and therefore considered distinct from conventional fighting games. It is also the only game where Link is featured in a non-Nintendo title (something extremely rarely seen in any case). This was met with critical acclaim by ''Zelda'' and ''[https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_series Soul]'' series fans alike, as they often find Link's inclusion as a playable guest being rather a perfect choice for the game's setting, which is similar to that of his home franchise, due to the other ports on [[wikipedia:PlayStation_2|PlayStation 2]] and [[wikipedia:Xbox_(console)|Xbox]] had [https://tekken.fandom.com/wiki/Heihachi_Mishima Heihachi Mishima] (from ''[https://tekken.fandom.com/wiki/Tekken_(series) Tekken]'') and [https://spawn.fandom.com/wiki/Spawn Spawn] as their respective guests instead (plus Link himself is a very popular character). Because of these factors, this version of ''SoulCalibur II'' is unsurprisingly the best-selling among the three home releases of the arcade title. The specific port also makes its first re-release on the [[wikipedia:Nintendo_Switch_2|Nintendo Switch 2]]'s Nintendo Classics service.
**This can be seen as a parallel to how Mario, [[Luigi]], and Princess Peach were also featured as playable guests in two sports games by [[wikipedia:Electronic_Arts|Electronic Arts]] released in 2005 exclusively for the GameCube port - ''[[wikipedia:SSX_on_Tour|SSX on Tour]]'' and ''[[wikipedia:NBA_Street_V3|NBA Street V3]]''.
**Following the above note, Link, alongside Samus from ''Metroid'', were once planned to be unlockable characters in the [[Wii|Nintendo Wii]] version of ''[https://mua.fandom.com/wiki/Marvel:_Ultimate_Alliance Marvel: Ultimate Alliance]'', but when the developers presented it to Nintendo, it was from the PS2 version - a console by Sony, resulting in the company angrily demanding their removal.
*Although Link normally has distinctively blue eyes like Princess Zelda, his cel-shaded appearance in games such as ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker|The Wind Waker]]'' depict him with black eyes. In rare cases, however, the design does depict blue eyes, as seen with his sprites in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Four Swords|Four Swords]]'' and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Minish Cap|The Minish Cap]]''.[[File:TWW Final Blow gif.gif|thumb|273x273px|Arguably Link's most popular feats in the franchise are his climactic ending blows seen at the climax of some 3D installments, such as ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker HD|The Wind Waker HD]]'', for example]]
*One of the major contributing factors of Link's (and to an extent the ''Zelda'' franchise itself) phenomenal popularity are his finishing blow victories onto the lead antagonists seen in certain games' [[Final Boss]] battles; ''Ocarina of Time'', ''The Wind Waker'', ''Twilight Princess'', ''Skyward Sword'', and ''Tears of the Kingdom'' are widely seen as among the greatest in not only the series itself, but video game history.
**''Ocarina of Time''<nowiki/>'s, however, had received controversy for being violent by the franchise's standards, as the original N64 release had Link shoving the Master Sword directly into Ganon's mouth with red blood spewing upon every hit (v1.0/v1.1 NTSC only), which is forced to be toned down in subsequent ports and especially ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time 3D|Ocarina of Time 3D]]''. Although ''The Legend of'' ''Zelda'' is no stranger to having rather shocking or exciting moments compared to most other Nintendo franchises such as Link genuinely stabbing an antagonist or even death due to its medieval-like setting, the company still focuses on keeping the flagship series' content family-friendly (often resulting in cartoonish events unlike most other games based on the Middle Ages).
*In spite of Link usually being the bearer of the [[Triforce of Courage]] in some games and other media, he is the first and so far only character in the entire series to obtain every piece of the [[Triforce]] by individual terms; In the original ''The Legend of Zelda'', he had restored the shattered [[Triforce of Wisdom]], which normally belongs to Princess Zelda, before eliminating [[Ganon]] with a [[Silver Arrow]] and absorbing the [[Triforce of Power]] the villain had stolen (the only case in the franchise this had ever yet happened). This continues in ''The Adventure of Link'', where the hero eventually defeats his shadow counterpart, Dark Link, to gain the Triforce of Courage by a [[Sage]], completing the sacred golden relic altogether.[[File:ALBW Ravio Unmasked.png|thumb|163x163px|Ravio, Link's Lorulean counterpart]]
*Similar to Princess Zelda having few counterparts that are of separate entities such as [[Princess Hilda]], Link himself has one - [[Ravio]]. Both Hilda and Ravio respectively are [[Lorule]] versions of the main characters from ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link Between Worlds|A Link Between Worlds]]''. Ironically, unlike Link who is known to be quite courageous, Ravio is fearful. Although [[Kafei]] from ''Majora's Mask'' shares an identical personality and animations as the young hero (and even be controlled by the player in a [[Sakon's Hideout|certain sidequest]]), contrary to popular belief, this resident from [[Termina]] is '''not''' a direct counterpart of the main protagonist, as he isn't physically identical, making his own similarity a mere coincidence. What Ravio and Kafei themselves have in common, however, is they are associated with violet rather than green.

* Although Link himself does not technically have a recurring theme song in the franchise unlike Princess Zelda and Ganon, certain games and other media heavily suggest it to be the [[The Legend of Zelda (Theme)|series' main theme]], as he is its main character and mascot.[[File:OoT Link Artwork.png|thumb|273x273px|Although not the first Link in the series release-wise or chronologically, the Hero of Time is seen as a trademark image of the character to many due to this iconic incarnation setting the standard for most later Heroes]]
* Among the many iterations of Link throughout ''The Legend of Zelda'' series, the [[Hero of Time]] is by far the most popular among the franchise's community, due to this particular hero's legendary status by not only the revolutionary success of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]'' and its [[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask|sequel]] plus having a [[Voice acting|distinctive voice unique from other Links]] by [[List of Voice Actors|Fujiko Takimoto/Nobuyuki Hiyama]]'s performances, but also his major importance in the [[The Legend of Zelda timeline/Fallen Hero Timeline|Zelda Timelines]] and [[Mythology in The Legend of Zelda Series|mythology]] in comparison to all others according to ''Hyrule Historia'', despite having one of, if not the most sorrowful stories. He is even mentioned in ''The Wind Waker'' and is seen in ''Twilight Princess'' in the form of a tall skeletal specter and golden [[wolf]] known as the [[Hero's Spirit]] (though this was met with controversy). In addition, in either his [[Young Link|Young]] or Adult form, he makes an appearance in any video game media more than every other incarnation of Link yet. Because of his influence to the designs of later incarnations of the series, most notably the Hero of Twilight and Hero of the Wild from ''Twilight Princess'' and ''Breath of the Wild'', respectively, most fans of the franchise have considered the Hero of Time to be the one true incarnation of the main protagonist in spite of not being the first in chronological order unlike the Hero of the Sky from ''Skyward Sword''.
** Princess Zelda and Ganon's iterations in the former N64 game also received major attention in the fanbase for similar reasons.
** The Hero of Time, however, has often overshadowed the original game-release incarnation from ''The Legend of Zelda'' into some believing he is the first adolescent or adult Link. This actually debuted in its direct sequel ''The Adventure of Link'', meaning the famous Hero from the [[Nintendo 64]] games was technically influenced by the one from the [[Nintendo Entertainment System]] titles.
* The Hero of Winds, the Link from ''The Wind Waker'' and ''Phantom Hourglass'', as seen in the former game, is the first incarnation to not wear the traditional [[Green Clothes|green clothes]], as he at first wears the [[Hero's New Clothes]], which are a blue crawfish T-shirt and pajamas with no hat (he keeps it on his whole adventure in the [[Second Quest]]). This would not repeat until ''Breath of the Wind'', where Link once more by default wears blue clothing called the [[Champion's Tunic]] alongside having many different optional outfits, some from previous games.
* As Link is specifically designed to be a "[[wikipedia:Silent_protagonist|silent protagonist]]", similar to Mario from the ''Mario'' franchise and few other Nintendo protagonists such as [[metroidwiki:Samus_Aran|Samus Aran]] or [[pokemon:Red|Red]] (from [[metroidwiki:Metroid_(series)|''Metroid'']] and ''[[pokemon:Pokémon_(franchise)|Pokémon]]'', respectively), aside from saying "Yes" or "No", he only displays distinctive grunt and yell vocalizations and therefore seemingly doesn't talk. However, like the aforementioned characters, he is not necessarily mute, as in the rare exception seen in ''The Wind Waker'', he says "[http://noproblo.dayjo.org/ZeldaSounds/WW_New/WW_Link_Call.wav ''Come on!'']" to [[Medli]], [[Makar]], and the statues in the [[Tower of the Gods]] when they must follow him. In addition, in the 3D games, when speaking to certain characters, the optional texts the player can choose implies Link does actually talk if required. The Hero's Spirit, confirmed to be the Link from ''Ocarina of Time''/''Majora's Mask'', does fully speak. According to Nintendo, Link does not directly speak in the games so the player is able to have their own thoughts and feelings as how would the hero answer other characters instead of having scripted responses.
** In ''The Adventure of Link'', Link appears to speak when he finds the {{Term|TAoL|Mirror|link}} in {{Term|TAoL|Saria Town|link}}, saying "''I found a mirror under the table.''" Link also appears to speak while inspecting the fireplace in the town of {{Term|TAoL|Kasuto|link}}, saying "''Looks like I can get in the fireplace.''" In ''Link's Awakening'', a similar scene occurs when discovering [[Koholint Island]] is only an illusion.
** In ''A Link to the Past'', when Link picks up a [[Fake Master Sword]] while attempting to find the [[Master Sword|actual weapon]] in the [[Lost Woods]], he appears to be speaking, saying "''This is it! The Master Sword! ... ... ... No, this can't be it... Too bad.''".
** In ''Ocarina of Time'', when he takes fall damage, as an adult, Link can be humorously heard [https://noproblo.dayjo.org/ZeldaSounds/OOT/OOT_AdultLink_Landing.wav angrily stammering in pain] (which some fans jokingly believe he uttered profane language).
** In ''Breath of the Wild'', he speaks when examining the cooked fish around the {{Term|BotW|Campfire|link}} in {{Term|BotW|Lurelin Village|link}}, commenting, "''These grilled fish look really good. I wonder if they were caught locally...''". This also occurs when he looks at [[Paya]]'s diary, saying, "''Must be [[Paya's Diary|Paya's diary.]] Must not look... Must not look... Must not look...''". Furthermore, in the Japanese version of ''Breath of the Wild'', the {{Term|BotW|Adventure Log}} is written entirely from Link's perspective. In the ending of ''Echoes of Wisdom'', he appears to regain his ability to speak.
** In other media of the franchise, such as the manga adaptations, cartoons, etc., Link speaks in full sentences like other characters.
** Although possibly not intended by Nintendo, when Link is defeated as adult and consequently receives a [[Game Over]] in ''Ocarina of Time'', one of his dying grunts closely resembles himself saying "''[https://noproblo.dayjo.org/zeldasounds/OOT/OOT_AdultLink_Choke2.wav Yuck!]''" as he collapses.

**Some fans speculated that Matsumoto provided the voices of the [[Gerudo]] in ''Majora's Mask'', as they sound remarkably similar to the Hero of Winds, but this is not confirmed by Nintendo.
*According the official Zelda Timeline, despite ''Ocarina of Time'''s actual ending of himself being triumphant over the archvillain, if the Hero of Time were to be ultimately defeated by Ganon during the [[Final Boss]] battle regardless of his human or beast forms and the player consequently receives a Game Over, the "[[The Legend of Zelda timeline|Downfall Timeline]]" branch would occur story-wise. This would make this incarnation of Link to be the only one to have canonically lost.[[File:Link and Mario in Nintendo Power.webp|thumb|294x294px|Link and Mario together, as seen in an early [[Nintendo Power]] issue]]
*Despite their completely different settings, due to ''The Legend''<nowiki/> ''of'' ''Zelda'' and ''Mario'' franchises have many parallels between each other (as they were originally created by the same development team), Link can be considered a mirror to Mario in that both main protagonists have the role in the majority of their core games of protecting a kingdom ([[Hyrule]]/[[mariowiki:Mushroom_Kingdom|Mushroom Kingdom]], respectively) from a demonic recurring antagonist whose longtime primary goal is to take over that kingdom (Ganon/[[Bowser]], respectively) and rescue a princess normally as the damsel-in-distress (Zelda/Peach, respectively). Some ''Zelda'' titles, most prominently ''Link's Awakening'' and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tri Force Heroes|Tri Force Heroes]]'', even have elements from the latter flagship Nintendo series being encountered by Link, such as various ''Super'' ''Mario'' enemies and similar items like [[Goomba|Goombas]], [[Chain Chomp|Chain Chomps]], [[Fire Bar|Fire Bars]], the [[Hammerwear]], [[Fire Gloves]], and so forth. Excluding merchandise, Link and Mario have met each other since the 1989 release of ''[https://nintendo.fandom.com/wiki/Tetris_(NES) Tetris]'' on the NES as a [[Cameos of The Legend of Zelda in other media|cameo]], but the first prominent case of themselves seen together occurred in ''Super Smash Bros.'' on the Nintendo 64. They even teamed up in ''[[Super Smash Bros. Brawl|Brawl]]'' alongside several other fellow Nintendo heroes such as Kirby to stop Ganondorf and Bowser and rescue their princess friends once more.
*Although most of Ganon's defeats by Link have himself being wo<nowiki/>unded followed by sealed away with the Triforce, Sages or similar instances, some installments of the series have the hero outright destroy his archnemesis like other lead antagonists. The mainline games so far that have this occurred are ''The Legend of Zelda'', ''A Link to the Past'', the linked ''Oracle'' game (though he was not fully revived), ''The Wind Waker'', ''Twilight Princess'', and ''Tears of the Kingdom''. Most of those cases, however, have the villain as Ganondorf, and only the early 2D games are where he was killed by the hero in his actual monster form. The weapons Link used for the final blows are the [[Silver Arrow|Silver Arrows]] and Master Sword.[[File:ALBW Octoball Derby.png|thumb|264x264px|Link playing the Octoball Derby in ''A Link Between Worlds'', which is similar to the real-world baseball sport]]
*Aside from the [[Dead Man's Volley]] cases in many games which its<nowiki/>elf resemble a game of [[wikipedia:Tennis|tennis]], Link is capable of playing [[wikipedia:Baseball|baseball]]. The [[Octoball Derby]] mini-game in ''A Link Between Worlds'' shows the hero serving 30 balls pitched to him using a stick rather than a bat to knock them onto [[Jar|pots]] while clearly standing in a field strongly resembling the baseball diamond.
**Both of those cases can be seen as a reference to the ''Mario'' fr<nowiki/>anchise, as that series indeed had spin-off games based on [[mariowiki:Mario_Tennis_(series)|either]] [[mariowiki:Mario_Baseball_(series)|sport]]. However, the first ''[[mariowiki:Mario_Tennis_(Nintendo_64)|Mario Tennis]]'' was released in 2000 on the N64, nine years after ''A Link to the Past'' on the SNES, where the Dead Man's Volley debuted with [[Agahnim]].

* ''Hyrule Historia'' suggests the possibility that each "Link" may have all had different names, a reference to how Link can be named anything in the games by the player.<ref name="HH 68">{{Cite Book|quote= |book= HH |publisher= |page= 68}}</ref>
** The only exception to this is ''Breath of the'' ''Wild'''s Link, who is explicitly named as such and whose name cannot be customized by the player.
[[File:TLoZ Female Link Artwork 4.png|thumb|210x210px|The female Link artwork depiction by ''Shonen Captain'']]
*Although Link is canonically male, early press releases for {{TLoZ}}, in particular the 1986 ''Shonen Captain'' Magazine, depicted the character as a female, causing some fans to mistaken Link as such gender to this day.<ref>[https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2022/01/random-artwork-in-retro-japanese-magazine-depicts-link-as-a-female Random: Artwork In Retro Japanese Magazine Depicts Link As A Female | Nintendo Life]</ref>
**Intriguingly, exactly three decades later in 2016, a female doppelganger of Link in ''[[Hyrule Warriors Legends]]'' named [[Linkle]] exists, whose playable Warrior was originally planned to be the hero's younger sister, but was rejected by [[Eiji Aonuma]], as he does not want her to conflict with [[Aryll]], the sibling of the Hero of Winds incarnation from ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker|The Wind Waker]]''/''[[The Legend of Zelda: Phantom Hourglass|Phantom Hourglass]]''. Unlike Link, however, her main weaponry are two [[Crossbow|Crossbows]] rather than a sword and shield, and she does not wear a green hat (but still has similar clothing).
[[File:LinkPraying.png|thumb|214x214px|The ''A Link to the Past'' artwork of Link praying to [[wikipedia:Jesus_in_Christianity|Jesus]] on a cross seen in its Japanese strategy guide book]]

* Link himself could possibly be a Christian. Alongside wielding Shields engraved with the [[wikipedia:Christian_cross|Christian Cross]] or the [[Book of Magic]] being originally the [[wikipedia:Bible|Bible]] in the early ''Zelda'' media, a rare artwork of ''A Link to the Past'' exists showing the hero privately praying to a [[wikipedia:Crucifix|crucifix]] inside the [[Sanctuary]] (which is referred to as a church in the Japanese version). However, this is likely no longer canon, as since ''Ocarina of Time'', all real-world religious material was virtually omitted by Nintendo for the series to have its own fictional culture and avoid controversy.
* Contrary to popular belief, ''The Wind Waker'' is not the first game in the series to depict Link in his cartoon appearance, as ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Four Swords|Four Swords]]'' in the [[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past & Four Swords|Game Boy Advance remake of ''A Link to the Past'']] had already showed him as such through official artwork and his in-game sprite. However, the GameCube title is its first occurrence to a wide audience.
* British teen actor [[wikipedia:Benjamin_Evan_Ainsworth|Benjamin Evan Ainsworth]] will play as Link in the upcoming [[The Legend of Zelda (film)|''The Legend of Zelda'' film]].

==Nomenclature==
Despite being famously associated with [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' series]]' main character, in the real-world, although not unheard of as it is found in American and British records, the word "link" - which technically means a connection or bond - is very rarely used as a given masculine name (primarily due to the way it sounded with certain last names). However, it's sometimes referred to as a shortened version of 'Lincoln' or simply mean "ring". As a surname in German, Dutch, and Jewish, which is more common, it can mean "left-handed" or "linker/links", which is a reference to Link himself being usually left-handed.

According to the series' creator [[Shigeru Miyamoto]], Link's name comes from the fact that, during the development of the original ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]'', as the [[Triforce]]'s [[Triforce Shard|fragments]] were once planned to be electronic chips, he was the link between the past and the future, which was the original setting for the game (''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]'' would later reuse this concept to an extent).<ref>{{Cite Person|quote= {{Exp|C'est une anecdote qui n’est pas très connue, mais à l'époque, quand on a commencé la conception de The Legend of Zelda, on s'imaginait que les fragments de la Triforce seraient en fait des puces électroniques ! Il devait s'agir d'un jeu vidéo qui se passerait à la fois dans le passé et le futur. Comme le héros faisait le lien entre l'un et l'autre, on l'a appelé Link, le lien en anglais.|It is a story that is not well known, but at the time, when we started the design of The Legend of Zelda, we imagined that the fragments of the Triforce would actually be microchips! The video game would happen in both the past and the future. As the hero was a link between one and the other, it was called Link.}}|name= Shigeru Miyamoto|url= https://www.gamekult.com/actualite/miyamoto-la-wii-u-et-le-secret-de-la-triforce-105550.html|title= MIYAMOTO, LA WII U ET LE SECRET DE LA TRIFORCE}}</ref> Miyamoto has also stated that Link is named after the fact that he connects people together and he was supposed to spread the scattered energy of the world through the ages.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= HH |publisher= |page= 2}}</ref> Although Link doesn't have a last name, he once joked that the hero's full name is "Link Link", a reference to [[Mario]] as "Mario Mario".

According to the series' current director [[Eiji Aonuma]], Link is named after the fact that he links the player to the games, as suggested by the character's name can be chosen by the player to match their own in most installments via the save file.<ref>{{Cite Person|quote= When a player is playing a Zelda game, my desire is for the player to truly become Link -- that's why we named him Link, so the player is linked to the game and to the experience. Of course, the player can always change Link's name to their own name to further that notion should they want.|name= Eiji Aonuma|url= https://web.archive.org/web/20120324104055/http://www.1up.com/do/feature?pager.offset=1&cId=3163610|title= Linked-In: Meet the Man Behind Zelda}}</ref> This strongly implies that Link himself was designed to represent as the player's avatar, similar to few other [[Nintendo]] franchises such as ''[[pokemon:Pokémon_(franchise)|Pokémon]]'' or ''[[animalcrossing:Animal_Crossing_(series)|Animal Crossing]]''.

Coincidentally, Link's name is also a reference to the character being a link to himself, due to every incarnation throughout the ''Zelda'' series possessing the spirit of his original being, [[Hylia's Chosen Hero]] (Hero of the Sky), from ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Skyward Sword|Skyward Sword]]''.
