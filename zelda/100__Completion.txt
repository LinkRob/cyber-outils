{{Name|Fan|100% Completion}} refers to having mastered all aspects of a game within [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|{{TLoZ|-}} series]], completing tasks that are addressed permanently to the quest's status. Except in {{LA}}, where completing this installment without achieving a [[Game Over]] at all unlocks a bonus ending, actions like not dying or always having a maximum amount of items, such as {{Plural|Series|Rupee|link}}, {{Plural|Series|Bomb|link}}, or {{Plural|Series|Arrow|link}}, do not count toward completion due to their permanent subject to change status, unlike some other video games in different media. On the other hand, {{MM}} is widely known to encourage players to fully complete the game in order to experience the full content and [[credits]]. In general, 100% completion of the main installments of the franchise are not necessarily required to beat the storyline.

Like the [[Three Heart Challenge]] and [[Speedrun]]s, on social media such as YouTube or Twitch, this type of optional challenge is often posted (and also the most common), usually for bragging rights among players to earn a high amount of views, though some intended to give others the directions where to find rare items such as [[Piece of Heart|Pieces of Hearts]] they may have possibly missed, especially ones that can be [[Permanently Missable Items|permanently lost]] (which is the primary purpose of the ''Zelda'' series according to [[Nintendo]], as it's created to encourage communication among others to solve tasks).

In order to have a 100% completed file, it must contain ''all'' {{Plural|Series|Heart Container|link}}, [[Upgrading|upgrades]], [[Item|items]], and [[collectibles]]. Unlike other Nintendo gaming series such as ''[[Mario (franchise)|Super Mario]], [[metroidwiki:Metroid_(series)|Metroid]]'' or ''[[starfox:Star_Fox_(series)|Star Fox]],'' ''The Legend of'' ''Zelda'' franchise generally does not acknowledge 100% completion with in-game rewards such as alternate endings, although some of the games have given rewards for various subtypes of completion.

==General==
The following tasks apply to every installment in the series. For a more detailed description, refer to the respective game section.

*Complete all {{Plural|Series|Map|link}}.
*Collect all {{Term|Series|Inventory|link}} {{Plural|Series|Item|link}}, upgrades, and collectibles.
*Collect the {{Term|Series|Dungeon Map|link}}, {{Term|Series|Compass|link}}, {{Term|Series|Big Key|link}}, and all {{Plural|Series|Small Key|link}} in every {{Term|Series|Dungeon|link}} where available.
*Open every {{Term|Series|Treasure Chest|link}} that remains opened.
*Open every locked door.
*View every cutscene that only plays once.
*View all one-time dialogues from other characters.
*Clear all structures or objects (such as {{Plural|Series|Rock|link}}, {{Plural|Series|Cracked Wall|link}}, barricades, etc.) that remain cleared.
*Collect all {{Plural|Series|Rupee|link}} that do not reappear.
*Defeat all {{Plural|Series|Enemy|link}} that do not respawn.
*Beat the game.

*Complete every {{Term|HWAoC|Challenge|link}} at least once.
*Complete every {{Term|HWAoC|Quest|link}}.
*Unlock all {{Plural|HWAoC|Warrior}}.
*Discover every location of a {{Term|HWAoC|Korok Seed|link}}.
*Unlock every Tutorial.

*Reach level 100 with every {{Term|HWAoC|Warrior}}.
*Complete every {{Term|HWAoC|Scenario|link}} on every {{Term|HWAoC|Difficulty}}.
*Complete every {{Term|HWAoC|Challenge|link}} on every {{Term|HWAoC|Difficulty}}.
*Discover every location of a {{Term|HWAoC|Chest}}.
