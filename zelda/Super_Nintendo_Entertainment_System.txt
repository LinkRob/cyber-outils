The '''[[wikipedia:Super_Nintendo_Entertainment_System|Super Nintendo Entertainment System]]''' (スーパーファミコン, ''Sūpā Famikon'', Super Famicom), often abbreviated as the '''SNES''', '''Super NES''', or informally 'Super Nintendo', was a 16-bit [[wikipedia:ROM_cartridge|cartridge]]-based console and is [[Nintendo]]'s second home system released internationally, in August 1991 for North America and 1992 for Europe, and originally released as the '''Super Famicom''' (SFC) in November 1990 for Japan. Like the [[Nintendo Entertainment System|NES]] before it, this console is known for having different model designs between regions in spite of having identical hardware; The American model has a boxy shape, while other regions use the more rounded Super Famicom design. The Super NES is iconic among classic gamers and critics alike for its high-level technology and large variety of equally great games.

A powerful and revolutionary console for its time, and part of the [[wikipedia:Fourth_generation_of_video_game_consoles|16-bit era]] alongside the slightly older [[Game Boy]], true to its name, the Super NES was essentially a significantly more advanced release of its already successful predecessor and its library consists of improving the groundbreaking franchises seen before such as ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_World|Super Mario World]]'', ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past]]'', ''[https://finalfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Final_Fantasy_VI Final Fantasy VI]'', ''[[metroidwiki:Super_Metroid|Super Metroid]]'', ''[https://megaman.fandom.com/wiki/Mega_Man_X_(series) Mega Man X]'', ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_All-Stars|Super Mario All-Stars]]'', ''[https://streetfighter.fandom.com/wiki/Street_Fighter_II:_The_World_Warrior Street Fighter II]'', and so on, and has its own set of critically acclaimed introductions such as ''[[wikipedia:F-Zero_(video_game)|F-Zero]]'', [[mariowiki:Super_Mario_Kart|''Super'' ''Mario Kart'']] (latter two used the [[wikipedia:Mode_7|Mode 7]] graphics), ''[https://donkeykong.fandom.com/wiki/Donkey_Kong_Country Donkey Kong Country]'', ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_World_2:_Yoshi's_Island|Yoshi's Island]]'', ''[[starfox:Star_Fox_(game)|Star Fox]]'' (latter two used the [[wikipedia:Super_FX|Super FX]] chip), ''[https://chrono.fandom.com/wiki/Chrono_Trigger Chrono Trigger]'' and many more, giving the SNES its legendary status and is widely considered as Nintendo's best, if not ''the'' best system ever created, to this day. But this console's huge accomplishments did not come without a fight, however, for during this era, Nintendo notoriously had a fierce rivalry with [[wikipedia:Sega|Sega]], especially in North America, with the release of the [[wikipedia:Sega_Genesis|Sega Genesis/Mega Drive]] in 1988 and introducing the [https://sonic.fandom.com/wiki/Sonic_the_Hedgehog_series ''Sonic the Hedgehog'' series] in 1991 to directly compete with ''[[mariowiki:Mario_(franchise)|Super Mario]]'' throughout the decade, infamously known as the "[[wikipedia:Console_war|Console Wars]]". Ultimately, the Super NES was the best-selling console of its generation (though it sold less than the NES due to intense competition), barely surpassing its main rival's sales. It also competed with the much less successful [[wikipedia:TurboGrafx-16|TurboGrafx-16]] (PC Engine), [[Philips CD-i|Phillips-CD-i]], and the arcade-based [[wikipedia:Neo_Geo|Neo Geo]].

The Super NES sold 49.1 million units worldwide and was discontinued in 1999, though Japan continued its support until 2003.

Like the NES before it, the Super NES were to have a compact, cost-reduced version late in its lifespan in 1997 called the '''New-Style Super NES''' or SNS-101 (called "SNES Jr." by fans). The Japanese version is called '''Super Famicom Jr.'''. This redesign was never released in Europe, however.

The Super NES' predecessor is the [[Nintendo Entertainment System]] and was proceeded by the [[Nintendo 64]].

Like other classic Nintendo consoles, several games of the SNES have been re-released on official emulation by Nintendo such as the Virtual Console and [[Nintendo Switch Online]].

== Super NES Classic Edition ==
[[File:Super NES Classic Edition Box.png|thumb|271x271px]]
Like the [[NES Classic Edition]] before it, the [[Super NES Classic Edition|SNES Classic Edition]] is a smaller, limited-edition emulated-based version of the Super Nintendo Entertainment System released in 2017 that does not accept cartridges. Much like its predecessor, it quickly sold out upon release due to popular demand and illegal scalpers.

It instead comes with 21 pre-installed games, including the unreleased ''[[starfox:Star_Fox_2|Star Fox 2]] -'' a cancelled sequel of ''Star Fox'' that was replaced by ''Star Fox 64'' for the Nintendo 64 during its supposed release in 1995 or 1996. ''A Link to the Past'' is among them.

== Trivia ==

* The slogan of the Super NES is "Now you're playing with power; SUPER POWER!", a modification of the NES'.
* The SNES' original retail price in 1991 was $199.99, which is the equivalent of $476.92 as of 2025.
* The original launch titles of the Super NES as the Super Famicom are ''Super Mario World'' and ''F-Zero''.
* This console's [[wikipedia:List_of_Super_Nintendo_Entertainment_System_games|library]] consists of 1,749 games. Only 717 of such were released in North America.
* The SNES is one of the first home consoles to have standard stereo audio.[[File:Genesis vs Super NES.webp|thumb|280x280px|The Super NES with the Sega Genesis (left), which both are highly known in the United States for having arguably the most intense gaming competition in history]]
* The console's main rival, the [https://sonic.fandom.com/wiki/Sega_Mega_Drive Sega Genesis] (or Mega Drive in other regions), itself released two years prior and among the most critically acclaimed, was aggressively advertised with "Blast Processing" in North America due to the fast-paced ''Sonic the Hedgehog'' series and having a 7,67MHz CPU. The Super NES, despite having only 3.58MHz processing speed, does have several games with very fast gameplay such as ''F-Zero'' and ''[[wikipedia:Top_Gear_3000|Top Gear 3000]]''. However, it should be noted that Sega usually uses it for ''platform'' games, while the aforementioned titles of Nintendo's system are racing games.
** Although the SNES is more technologically advanced in general, fans of both Nintendo and Sega at the time (and sometimes today) frequently compare the two 16-bit consoles in capabilities of certain games aside from ''[[Mario (franchise)|Mario]]'' and ''Sonic'' such as ''[https://streetfighter.fandom.com/wiki/Street_Fighter_II:_The_World_Warrior Street Fighter II]'' and more. Even their own official advertisements and games had their respective companies boasting about which is better (though Nintendo did not do it as often). It wasn't until the release of the [[Nintendo GameCube]] or [[Wii]] when the business rivalry had completely settled.
* ''A Link to the Past'' is the 7th best-selling game on the Super NES, with 4.6 million sold. For comparison, its highest seller, ''Super Mario World'', sold 20.6 million (though it is a pack-in title in all regions).
* The [[wikipedia:Super_Scope|Super Scope]], a bazooka-like light gun accessory for the SNES for a few certain games such as ''[[mariowiki:Yoshi's_Safari|Yoshi's Safari]]'' and the successor of the NES Zapper, is famously featured as a weapon item in the [[Super Smash Bros. (series)|''Super Smash Bros.'' series]].
* Despite the Super NES' well-built hardware, there is a rarely known design flaw of its video output, where, most often seen with its earlier revisions, have a faint vertical line on the center of the TV screen as well as being slightly blurred. Though often missed through its intended use with CRT televisions, it is more noticeable in modern HD screens. The later revision in its lifespan, known as the "1Chip", due to the motherboard combining its PPU and CPU together to become a more reliable [[wikipedia:System_on_a_chip|system-on-chip]], has improved contrast and mostly eliminating the vertical line (at the cost of less saturated colors and minor glitches), especially when using RGB signals. This had led this particular revision of the console to be highly sought after by collectors today, including the much rarer compact SNS-101 model, although that version of the SNES requires a modification to make the S-Video and RGB signals function (as they were internally disabled by Nintendo to reduce cost).
* Coincidentally, ''A Link to the Past'' and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]''<nowiki/>'s Japanese release date, November 21st, is the same as the Super Famicom's, one and eight years later, respectively.
** Another major Nintendo franchise, ''[[pokemon:Pokémon_(franchise)|Pokémon]]'', has a celebration date of one of its most iconic Pokémon, [[pokemon:Eevee|Eevee]], known as Eevee Day, that takes place on the exact same date since 2018.
* With an add-on cartridge called the [[wikipedia:Super_Game_Boy|Super Game Boy]], the Super NES is capable of playing every Game Boy title in full color, similar to that of its successors such as the [[Game Boy Color]].
* The SNES' controller is arguably the most influential ever designed, as many later consoles, including Nintendo's own since the [[Nintendo DS]], were to reuse its button layout.[[File:Yellowed SNES.webp|thumb|236x236px|An example of a yellowed Super NES unit]]
* Many, though not all SNES/SFC consoles, and even its game cartridges and controllers, are infamously known to turn into a dirty yellow color over age and become relatively brittle. This is because of factory casing issues of the gray ABS plastic during its mass production causing it to oxidize with longtime exposure to UV light or heat (especially common with neglect owners). Some units can have a "two-tone" effect by only having one half of the system being yellowed (usually the bottom). This also applies to the later SNS-101/Super Famicom Jr. models, albeit rarely.
** It is widely believed by older fans that a yellowed Super NES means the console is damaged or unusable. On the contrary, however, the distorted coloration of the shell, does '''not''' harm the internal hardware, as the system itself in fact runs without issue. It is possible to somewhat restore the ABS plastic through a complex method called "[[wikipedia:Retrobright|Retrobrighting]]", though the effects are only temporary and can actually damage the original casing due to bleaching.
** The [[Nintendo Entertainment System|NES]], [[Game Boy]], [[Nintendo 64|N64]], and even the GameCube can also suffer from yellowing as well, due to them sharing the same manufacturing plastic on their own casings.
[[File:SNES S-SMP chip.jpg|thumb|The advanced sound effects of the SNES is actually the result from a Sony chip. Note the processor says a copyright year of 1989.|213x213px]]
* The iconic high-quality audio of the Super NES comes from the S-SMP processor, which, interestingly, is the only part of the console's motherboard not made by Nintendo, but by [[wikipedia:Sony|Sony]] (which was well-known to have created hi-tech hardware in the past) and designed by none other than [[wikipedia:Ken_Kutaragi|Ken Kutaragi]]. The company itself would later create the very successful [[wikipedia:PlayStation|PlayStation line of consoles]] starting in 1994 with the [[wikipedia:PlayStation_(console)|PlayStation]].
** Kutaragi's development of the SPC700 for Nintendo was a risky move, as it almost got him fired. At that particular time prior to the release of the PS1, Sony's executives had no interest in video games, but when they found out about the secret collaboration with a rival company, they were furious. Only with then-CEO Norio Ohga's approval was Kutaragi able to complete the sound chip and keep his job.
** Ironically, Nintendo once planned to have a [[wikipedia:Super_NES_CD-ROM|CD-based add-on for the SNES]] called "PlayStation", but a conflict between the companies resulted in it being cancelled. Only one known functional prototype exists.

* The [[Wii U]] and [[Nintendo Switch 2]] are a repeat of the Super NES in which these Nintendo home consoles are more advanced releases of their predecessor (Wii and [[Nintendo Switch|Switch]]), but those respective systems have mixed results. 
** The SNES itself at one point in development was originally going to be called "Nintendo Entertainment System 2" or "NES 2".
