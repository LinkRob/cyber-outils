A '''[[wikipedia:Speedrunning|speedrun]]''' is a playthrough of a video game with the intention of rapidly completing it in the shortest amount of time possible, often by extremely skilled players. Unlike the [[Three Heart Challenge]], optional [[Upgrading|upgrades]] are acceptable but are generally ignored if the amount of time they save is less than the time it takes to obtain them. Most speedrunners attempt to break personal or record times by using a variety of [[glitches]] to save time drastically, which often leads to bizarre effects during gameplay not intended by the developers (such as reaching the end of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]'' as [[Young Link|Child Link]] or bypassing an entire map in a 2D game). However, cheating devices, mod hacks, and custom game controllers are generally not allowed. A video of the gameplay is usually required in order to verify that a run was performed without cheating. Speedruns are often performed live with the player being fully visible via streaming services, most commonly on [[wikipedia:Twitch_(service)|Twitch]] or [[wikipedia:YouTube|YouTube]]. Although emulators are discouraged, as the community in general prefers original console hardware for optimum performance (especially true for older games in the era where [[wikipedia:Cathode_ray_tube|CRT televisions]] are required, specifically [[Nintendo GameCube]] or earlier), they are sometimes used, which is only allowed per community discretion. For most video games in general, however, speedruns are entirely optional. 

Although professional competitions exist and some intend to find new ways how a game functions, more often than not, players do speedruns to simply show off for views, bragging rights, or even creating internet memes (where one fails an otherwise good run) in such online streaming or regular videos due to the utmost level of knowledge of the game needed to perform this kind of playthrough. Like the [[100% Completion|100% completion]], this type of challenge is posted on social media frequently.

==Community==
{{Lacking Sources}}
[[File:OOT Young Link speedrun.png|thumb|317x317px|An example of a speedrun on ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]'', the most frequently used ''Zelda'' game by the community due to its popularity]]
The speedrun challenge has given rise to its own community consisting of casual and world-class speedrunners and their spectators. Much of the community is hosted on the website {{wp|Speed Demos Archive}} (SDA) as well as the live-streaming site {{wp|Twitch (website)|Twitch}}. The SDA forums are often used to discuss route planning and new techniques in order to improve run times. Many speedrunners – especially world-class ones – will stream their runs live to spectators; most do so on Twitch using timing software. Through Twitch, spectators can support runners monetarily by donating or paying for a subscription to the runner's channel. Donors can add comments to their donations which runners will usually read aloud during lulls in the run, or after it is completed. Many speedrunners allow their donors to choose a file name for each run. Twice a year, SDA hosts charity marathons called "Awesome Games Done Quick" (peak winter event) and "Summer Games Done Quick" (off-peak summer event), where world-class speedrunners congregate and live-stream speedruns to elicit donations from viewers. 

''[[The Legend of Zelda (series)|The Legend of Zelda]]'' games, not unlike ''[[Mario (franchise)|Mario]]'' or some other [[Nintendo]] franchises, are highly contested and constitute some of the most popular games for speedrunning. ''Zelda'' speedrunning has its own expansive sub-community. [[Community:ZeldaSpeedRuns|ZeldaSpeedRuns]] compiles information on speedruns and formerly hosted the series' definitive speedrun leaderboards, which are now found at Speedrun.com.

===Terminology===
{{Main|Speedrun/Glossary}}
Speedrunners evolve unique terminology and abbreviations for each game. It usually refers to important techniques or strategies ("strats"), most of which are exploitative glitches. Abbreviations can also refer to things such as items, locations, or events.

==Glitches==
Because professional-level knowledge of the games are required, most speedruns rely on exploitative [[glitches]] and sequence breaks in order to finish the game more quickly. Glitches are a rallying point for the speedrunning community. The discovery of new glitches, usually a result of extensive research by members of the community, can lead to breakthroughs in a [[#Categories|run category]]. This can reignite interest in a "dead" category, resulting in further competition and run improvement. A category "dies" when a strong, decisive record is made that cannot likely be beat. Competition in the category thus wanes until new strategies or glitches are discovered.

Some ''The Legend of'' ''Zelda'' games are more "broken" than others, which is more often seen in older installments. In {{OoT}} and {{LA}}, massive sequence-breaking glitches exist that skip most of the game's campaign. These games can be completed in only a matter of minutes – {{LA|-}} can be completed in under 3 minutes; {{OoT|-}} in under 4. Games such as {{TP}}, {{TPHD}}, and {{SSHD}} feature less drastic skips and still require many hours to complete.

==Game Versions==
Due to slight design changes between game versions, a specific version of a game may be more desirable than others for speedrunning. Many speedrunners prefer to play with the NTSC version of the game, because a PAL (European) version of the same game is almost never optimized for the difference in frequencies (~60Hz for NTSC versus 50Hz for PAL) and thus would run more slowly. Despite the language barrier for most players, Japanese versions are frequently used for ''The Legend of Zelda'' games, not only because Japan uses NTSC like North America, but also because there is less text to wait through (The Japanese writing system utilizes a combination of Chinese logograms and its own syllabaries. These amount to a smaller volume of text than an equivalent translation written with the Latin alphabet).

The Chinese version of {{OoT|-}} for the [[iQue Player]] was at one point ideal for [[#any%|Any%]] speedruns. This version has the fastest text and the least lag.<ref>{{Cite Person|quote= This run was done on the official Chinese release of the game. I chose this version because of faster text and less lag. |name= {{wp|Narcissa Wright}}, world record holder for {{OoT|-}} any% as of August 1, 2014|url= http://youtu.be/aq6pGJbd6Iw?t=3m42s|title= Zelda: Ocarina of Time Speedrun in 18:10 by Cosmo }}</ref> It is less suitable for other {{OoT|-}} categories because of the iQue's slow start screen. The Japanese version is used for some other {{OoT|-}} categories.

==Categories==
Multiple speedrunning categories exist for each game. Each category sets unique conditions under which the run must be completed. 

'''{{Section|Any%}}''' is the standard speedrunning category. The goal is simply to complete the game as quickly as possible, without any conditions.

'''{{Section|All Dungeons}}''' requires the completion of every dungeon before finishing the run, even if some dungeons are skippable using exploits.

'''{{Section|100%}}''' runs aim to complete the game after obtaining every item, including all [[Heart Container]]s, [[quest item]]s, and [[inventory]] items, for example. What counts toward 100% in speedruns are determined by the game's speedrunning community, and might not necessarily include all [[100% Completion]] criteria.

'''{{Section|Glitchless}}''' runs must be completed without the use of [[glitches]]. What qualifies as a glitch is determined by the game's speedrunning community.

Various categories exist that are unique to each game. For example, {{MM}} features an "All Masks" category, and {{OoT|-}} features several unique categories, including "Medallions, Stones, and Trials" (MST), "Defeat [[Ganon]]", and "No [[Wrong Warp]]".

== Trivia ==

* Although speedrunning have technically existed since the earliest days of video gaming, its broad interest began its roots in the early 1990s by the release of early [[wikipedia:First-person_shooter|first-person shooters]] such as ''[https://doom.fandom.com/wiki/Doom Doom]'' or ''[https://quake.fandom.com/wiki/Quake Quake]'', and video-streaming become popular since the mid-2000s at the dawn of the widespread era of the internet.
* According to Guinness World Records in March 2020, ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_64|Super Mario 64]]'' from the [[mariowiki:Mario_(franchise)|''Mario'' series]] is the most frequently used video game for speedrun competitions, with as many as 14,009 players logged into Speedrun.com.
* Extreme or rather absurd cases of speedruns exist, such as players attempting to beat a video game this way while blindfolded or the game itself being sped up.
* A different type of speedrun, known as a [[wikipedia:Tool-assisted_speedrun|tool-assisted speedrun]] or TAS, is where a player creates a "perfect" playthrough using precise inputs recorded with tools, often by emulators, to counter existing world records. They are, however, not officially recorded for competitions.
* The most commonly used [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' series]] games by the speedrun community outside of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]'' include ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past|A Link to the Past]]'', ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild|Breath of the Wild]]'', and ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]''.
* Speedrunning has been met with [[Controversy in The Legend of Zelda series|controversy]]. Due to the high stakes and top times often gain fame, there have been cases of players cheating, unclear verification, exploiting unfair advantages, or even manipulating the footage to create fake records, making the game appear "inhumanely fast", which often leads to a ban or their records disqualified. For the ''Zelda'' series, others have argued that since this kind of playthrough is commonly posted on social media or experienced players sometimes use similar tactics in a non-speedrun (eg. defeating [[Boss|bosses]] as fast as possible), it has led some to believe that certain installments are too easy.
