'''Ganon''' (ガノン ''Ganon''),{{Exp Game|TLoZ, ALttP, OoT, OoS, OoA, TWW, FSA, TP, ALBW, TCT, BotW, EoW}}<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= E |publisher= |page= 179 {{Exp Game|TLoZ, ALttP, OoT, OoS, OoA, FSA, TP}} & 300 {{Exp Game|ALBW}}}}</ref><ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= TCT|publisher= |page= 1}}</ref> also known as the '''Prince of Darkness''', '''[[Demon King]]''', '''Great King of Evil''', '''Dark Beast''', or by his human form '''[[Ganondorf]]''',{{Exp Game|OoT, TWW, FSA, TP, TotK}}<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= '''Ganondorf'''|book= E |page= 179 {{Exp Game|OoT, TWW, TP}}}}</ref><ref name="E 10" /> is the main antagonist of [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|{{TLoZ|-}} series]]. A large, ruthless and powerful [[demon]], as well as a master sorcerer of [[darkness]], he is the leader of the vast army of evil monsters that constantly attack the [[Hyrule|Kingdom of Hyrule]] in most games of the franchise. He made his debut appearance in {{TLoZ}}, and has since been present or referred to in most subsequent titles. Ganon the Demon King is the archenemy of [[Link]] and [[Princess Zelda]] as well as the bearer of the [[Triforce of Power]], which he usually obtains by stealing it. His longtime primary goal is to take over Hyrule and rule the [[Universe|world]] under his evil reign as well as to defeat his mortal enemy Link and steal the complete [[Triforce]] in order to become unstoppable. Ganon is also well-known for his [[wikipedia:Suidae|suidae]]-like appearance resembling that of a [[Moblin]]'s especially in early installments, but few games since {{OoT}} (itself the physical debut of Ganondorf) depicted him in a more fearsome manner. As his alter-ego Ganondorf, the archvillain is a [[Gerudo]] rather than a beast. Because of this, 'Ganon' is also a nickname for Ganondorf and the name given to him when he transforms into his true form.<ref name="name">{{Cite Book|quote=Though "Ganon" is also a nickname for Ganondorf, here it refers to the beastlike form adopted by the thief following his transformation. The Demon King resembles a giant boar.|book=HH|page=90}}</ref>

Usually, but not always, Ganon himself is the [[Final Boss]] in the core series, but is playable in spin-off appearances, often as his human form Ganondorf.

In stark contrast to Link and Zelda, in spite of his various different forms in some games, Ganon does not have many incarnations throughout [[The Legend of Zelda timeline|Hyrule's history]]. Being a demon, the Dark Beast is in fact an endless reincarnation of another antagonist, [[Demise]], from {{SS}}. 

Being one of [[Nintendo]]'s flagship villains and a central character in one of the company's most successful franchises, Ganon is easily recognized and often considered to be one of the most iconic video game primary antagonists of all-time. 

==Biography==
[[File:SS Demise Render.png|thumb|267x267px|Demise, the original incarnation of Ganon from {{SS|-}}]]
Introduced in {{TLoZ}} as the monstrous Prince of Darkness and central antagonist of the franchise, Ganon has seen much development throughout the long-running series, from being a simple monster to a powerful warlock with deeper motives to his actions.<ref name=covet>{{Cite|My country lay within a vast desert. When the sun rose into the sky, a burning wind punished my lands, searing the world. And when the moon climbed into the dark of night, a frigid gale pierced our homes. No matter when it came, the wind carried the same thing... Death. But the winds that blew across the green fields of Hyrule brought something other than suffering and ruin. I coveted that wind, I suppose.|Ganon|TWW}}</ref> He is one of the three most important characters of the series, alongside [[Link]] and [[Princess Zelda]]. With the infamous monster's origins revealed in {{SS}}, as the incarnation of the Demon King [[Demise]]'s hatred for those with the blood of the [[Hylia|Goddess]] and the spirit of the [[Hylia's Chosen Hero|hero]] (or, according to the {{HH}}, the reincarnation of Demise himself),<ref>{{Cite Person|quote=This game talks about the birth of the Master Sword, and it touches on why Ganondorf showed up. If you play it, I think you'll get some understanding on that. It connects to Ocarina, so if you play Ocarina of Time 3D and move on to this game, I think you'll catch on to a lot of things.|name=Eiji Aonuma|url=http://www.1up.com/news/eiji-aonuma-wraps-zelda-skyward|title=Eiji Aonuma interview}}</ref> Ganon is destined to eternally reincarnate to pursue world domination, meaning he is immortal, unlike Link and Zelda.<ref name="Demise's final words">{{Cite|Extraordinary. You stand as a paragon of your kind, human. You fight like no human or demon I have ever known. Though this is not the end. My hate... never perishes. It is born anew in a cycle with no end! I will rise again! Those like you... Those who share the blood of the goddess and the spirit of the hero... They are eternally bound to this curse. An incarnation of my hatred shall ever follow your kind, dooming them to wander a blood-soaked sea of darkness for all time!|Demise|SS}}</ref><ref>{{Cite Book|quote=A cyclical tale arose from an ancient battle...|book=HH|page=91}}</ref><ref>{{Cite Book|quote=However, the destruction of Demise was not the end of the battle. It was the beginning of a curse: a never ending cycle of the reincarnation of the Demon King, whose hatred for those with the blood of the Goddess and the Spirit of the Hero is everlasting|book=HH|page=76}}</ref> In most cases of the series, though, he makes a return after being resurrected or breaking a seal. {{FSA}} is a notable exception, featuring a reincarnated Ganondorf.<ref name=FSA-reincarnation>{{Cite Book|quote=Four Swords Adventures: The Reincarnation of Ganondorf |book=HH|page= 69}}</ref> The [[Ganondorf|original Ganondorf]] from {{OoT}} appears in most of the later games since, making him stand out from other antagonists as the same character from game-to-game.

With his sheer lust for ruling the world with an iron fist, Ganon is almost always the possessor of the [[Triforce of Power]], imbued with the essence of the Goddess [[Din (Goddess)|Din]]. This divine relic makes Ganon unimaginably strong and grants him boundless mystical power, making him a grave threat to the land of [[Hyrule]] and the world. Additionally, Ganon is the source of darkness.<ref>{{Cite Web|quote=Ganon: The source of darkness, awaiting his return to power. |retrieved=November 16, 2015 |url=http://zelda.com/link-between-worlds/history/ |title=Official Site - The Legend of Zelda: A Link Between Worlds for the Nintendo 3DS and Nintendo 2DS family of systems. |site=Zelda.com}}</ref> As stated in prophecy, the only one capable of defeating Ganon is the [[Link|Hero]] who is chosen by the [[Golden Goddesses|Goddesses]], Link, the possessor of the [[Triforce of Courage]], though occasionally, as seen in some games, he does need the assistance of Zelda, the princess of Hyrule and the possessor of the [[Triforce of Wisdom]] (and rarely, others), due to Ganon's great strength and durability.
[[File:OoT Ganondorf Artwork.png|left|thumb|361x361px|Ganondorf, Ganon's human form and King of the Gerudo, as seen from {{OoT|-}}]]
Ganon is first given a backstory in {{aLttP}}. It was revealed that he was not always the massive boar-like demon introduced in {{TLoZ|-}}. As explained by some among the seven [[Maiden|Maidens]], Ganon was shown to actually once have been a man by the name of '''Ganondorf''' '''Dragmire''' and leader of a group of thieves. With the help of his followers, he became the first to enter the [[Sacred Realm]] in ages. He claimed the [[Triforce]], turning the Sacred Realm into the [[Dark World]] and himself into a vastly powerful being.<ref name="ALttP Wish">{{Cite|Ganon's wish was to conquer the world. That wish changed the Golden Land to the Dark World.|Essence of the Triforce|ALttP}}</ref> However, he was often trapped within the Sacred Realm by the combined efforts of the [[Knights of Hyrule]] and the [[Sage|Seven Wise Men]] (Sages).<ref>{{Cite Book|quote=The Seven Sages of that era sealed the realm in a ferocious battle known as the Imprisoning War. The clan of knights tasked with guarding the sages was nearly wiped out in the process.|book=E |publisher=[[Dark Horse Books]] |page=24 }}</ref>

In {{OoT}}, it is stated that the first incarnation of Ganondorf was born a powerful [[Gerudo]] sorcerer.<ref name="ZeldaEncyclopedia">{{Cite Book|quote= Chronologically, Ocarina of Time is when Ganondorf first appears, as he was not yet born during the events of Four Swords, The Minish Cap, and Skyward Sword. |book=[[The Legend of Zelda: Encyclopedia]] |publisher=Dark Horse Books |date=June 2018 |page= 20}}</ref> The Gerudo twin witches [[Koume and Kotake]] were his surrogate mothers.<ref name="Koume">{{Cite|Twinrova Koume: Ganondorf's surrogate mother. Sorceress of Flame. She seems vulnerable to low temperature.|Navi|OoT}}</ref><ref name="Kotake">{{Cite|Twinrova Kotake: Ganondorf's surrogate mother. Sorceress of Ice. She seems vulnerable to high temperature.|Navi|OoT}}</ref> The Gerudo are a race of all-female warriors and thieves, to whom only one male is born every century. Ganondorf is that male. Because of this, the male is by birthright the King of the Gerudo. [[Nabooru]], the second-in-command, mentions to the young [[Hero of Time]] in the [[Spirit Temple]] that she is never following Ganondorf because he stole from women and children and even murdered people.<ref>{{Cite|With his followers, he stole from women and children, and he even killed people! A kid like you may not know this, but the Gerudo race consists only of women. Only one man is born every hundred years... Even though our laws say that lone male Gerudo must become King of the Gerudo, I'll never bow to such an evil man!|Nabooru|OoT}}</ref> Ganondorf uses his political position to betray the [[King of Hyrule]], as well as his cunning, manipulative skills, and mystical powers to gain entrance to the Sacred Realm, the abode of the Triforce. However, due to his unbalanced heart, he does not obtain the full Triforce; he is left with only the Triforce of Power, which he uses to great effect in his conquest of Hyrule. Nevertheless, the Evil King stops at nothing to retrieve the other pieces hidden within two certain individuals chosen by destiny to truly become invincible, who happen to be Princess Zelda and Link, the bearers of the Triforce of Wisdom and Triforce of Courage, respectively. 

In {{TWW}}, Ganondorf's character is given greater depth and a clearer motive for his villainy. During the final battle, he states that his desire to conquer Hyrule stems from the arduous life he and his people were subjected to living in the harsh [[Gerudo Desert]], a wasteland plagued by sandstorms and an unstable climate, which he compares unfavorably to the verdant landscape of [[Hyrule Field]] and the amenable life lived by the [[Hylian]]s.<ref name=covet/>

Ganon is reborn in {{FSA}}, where, although he is still a Gerudo, he is not the king. He begins his transformation into the King of Darkness by violating the Gerudo taboo on the [[Pyramid]] and seeking out the [[Trident]].<ref name=Pyramid>{{Cite|The pyramid is sacred to our people. To have a criminal such as him enter... His presence stains its holy ground.|Gerudo|FSA}}</ref><ref name=Elder>{{Cite|Once every 100 years, a special child is born unto my people. In {{SS}}, Ganon is ultimately revealed to be the incarnation of the everlasting hatred of the Demon King [[Demise]] for those with the blood of the [[Hylia|Goddess]] and the spirit of the [[Hylia's Chosen Hero|Legendary Hero]], by a [[Curse of Demise|deadly curse]] placed upon Link after ultimately defeating him in-game. Thus, he is destined to eternally reincarnate, becoming the source of various conflicts in the history of Hyrule, which involve many various incarnations of Princess Zelda and Link.<ref name="Demise's final words"/>

==Characteristics==

===Physical appearance===
[[File:TLoZ Ganon Million Publishing Artwork.png|thumb|221x221px|Ganon's debut appearance, as seen in {{TLoZ|-}}|left]]
In contrast to [[Link]] and [[Princess Zelda]] who are [[Hylian|Hylians]], Ganon is a porcine [[Demon]]. The Prince of Darkness' appearance is even more inconsistent throughout the long-running series than the aforementioned two. In general, he is a large and burly being appearing as an anthropomorphic boar, though some games such as {{TP}} depicted him on fours like a real-life suid and at a gigantic size. In the early 2D games as well as ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Four Swords Adventures|Four Swords Adventures]]'', {{aLBW}}, and {{EoW}}, Ganon was originally depicted with having bluish skin, a face resembling a [[Moblin]]'s, black spiky bracelets on his arms similarly to [[Bowser]]'s from the ''[[Mario (franchise)|Mario]]'' series, a large red cape on his back, spiked armor on his shoulders and head and some clothing with a skull necklace on his neck and wrist. Although the aforementioned look does exist in concept art from the original {{TLoZ}}, another artwork and the in-game sprite of Ganon shows the monster with nothing more than a red shirt with a skull in the center and red boots, giving him a rather basic appearance by antagonistic standards. In some cases, he is armed with a massive [[Trident]] which serves as his main weapon to combat his enemies such as Link.
[[File:OoT Ganon Artwork.png|thumb|326x326px|Ganon as he appears in {{OoT|-}}, depicting a more vicious look than his early design]]
In {{OoT}}, {{TP|-}} and ''[[Hyrule Warriors]]'', Ganon was given a significantly more ferocious appearance, having large ram-like horns on his head, glowing eyes which lack pupils, a tail, thick red hair similar to his human form and in the case of the former, reptilian skin and some bits of Gerudo clothing. However, he lacks his royal red cape, as this was now normally given to Ganondorf instead ({{OoT|-}} however still has a ripped portion on his shoulders). Much like Bowser, despite his huge bulk and sometimes appears to be slow, although Ganon usually resorts to teleportation, the Demon King is capable of running and jumping, his size allows him to easily pound the ground as he moves, and can be surprisingly agile. Notably, his iconic {{OoT|-}} incarnation depicted Ganon in a much thinner body frame compared to his original appearance. {{BotW}} features Ganon as an angry rampaging spirit known as [[Calamity Ganon]], made entirely out of [[malice]]. There, he later gains two incomplete forms during the final battle, with the first having a particularly grotesque shape while the other, [[Dark Beast Ganon]], resembles how he appeared in {{TP|-}}. In {{aLBW|-}}, when he becomes [[Yuga Ganon]] upon merging with [[Yuga]], the lead antagonist of this game, that villain gains Ganon's body, giving him purple skin and his own clothing, and adding his hair onto the notorious beast.

[[File:OoT3D Ganondorf Artwork.png|left|thumb|290x290px|Artwork of Ganondorf from ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time 3D|Ocarina of Time 3D]]'']]
As his alter-ego [[Ganondorf]], Ganon becomes a very tall and muscular [[Gerudo]] warlock (and the only male of such). Like his monster form, Ganondorf's appearance indeed varied as well in the series, though not to the same degree. In {{OoT|-}} where this version of the main antagonist debuted, although drastically different from his true form, Ganon as a man retains his red hair and wicked eyes, his royal cape in some games, and in the case of {{OoT|-}}, even wears the same general clothes. Ganondorf wears a large gem on his forehead and has some bit of armory on his body. Although normally average in weight for his size, his appearance in {{TWW}} gave the King of Evil a thicker build. Ganondorf appears relatively young in {{OoT|-}}, though later games such as {{TWW|-}}, {{TP|-}} and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom|Tears of the Kingdom]]'' depicted him at an older age. Ganondorf can shapeshift himself back into Ganon or vice versa at will using the [[Triforce of Power]] as a [[transformation]], though usually but not always, Ganon in his actual form is the result of using the sacred triangle at full power, as he appears to revert down to his Gerudo form when defeated or extracted completely, seen from a few games, and in {{aLttP|-}}, he was said by one of the [[Maiden|Maidens]] to initally be in this shape before obtaining the completed Triforce to transform the [[Sacred Realm|Golden Land]] into the [[Dark World]] (something which repeats in {{OoT|-}}) and become the more dangerous pig-like demon, implying that him as Ganondorf was his original form rather than merely a "weakened" state, story-wise. In ''Tears of the Kingdom'', he has another form upon using the [[Draconification|draconifcation]] technique to become a giant wingless snake-like [[dragon]] known as the [[Demon Dragon]], which itself is similar to [[Volvagia]] from {{OoT|-}}. Ganondorf is by far Ganon's most popular alternate identity in the franchise and other Nintendo media. Coincidentally, because the Demon King [[Demise]] was also humanoid, Ganondorf is a much closer resemblance to the archvillian's original incarnation than as Ganon.

[[File:ALttP&FS Agahnim Artwork.png|thumb|Artwork of Ganon's first alter ego, Agahnim, from ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past & Four Swords|A Link to the Past & Four Swords]]''|242x242px]]
The evil wizard [[Agahnim]] in {{aLttP|-}}, despite his role being the game's secondary antagonist and Ganon's soul appearing out of his body upon his ultimate defeat (suggesting him as a separate entity), is actually another alter ego of that villain prior to Ganondorf's first appearance. Although appearing and acting similar to a [[Wizzrobe]], some features of Ganon are evident on Agahnim, such as his light blue skin with black clawed fingernails and association with the color red (though his robe is green in-game).

===Personality===
[[File:HWL Ganondorf Thief's Trident Artwork.png|left|thumb|264x264px|Ganon, or Ganondorf, has a reputation of being a notorious warlord who eliminates anyone who opposes him without hesitation, including his own minions]]
Being the main villain of the franchise as well as Demise's reincarnation, Ganon is a ruthless and very destructive being. Conquest and destruction seem to be one and the same to him,<ref>{{Cite|Regardless... I will reshape this world as it was meant to be. I will crush any opposition. I will rule. That is what a king must do.|Ganondorf|ToTK}}</ref> with examples of his rule have shown him willingly turning his dominion into a wicked place of terror and chaos and harming others with little care. Alongside being known to murder people in {{aLttP|-}} and {{OoT|-}}, his {{TotK|-}} incarnation even went as far as to unleash his new demon hordes against his own followers. Some games such as {{OoT|-}} and {{TWW|-}} have shown Ganon to be surprisingly cunning, even sometimes one step ahead of Link and Princess Zelda. However, his arrogance does get the best of him. On a similar note, Ganon is also known to have somewhat of a relationship with [[Bowser]], who himself is the main antagonist of the [[Mario (franchise)|''Mario'' series]] and archenemy of Nintendo's mascot, [[Mario]]. The two even teamed up in the [[Super Smash Bros. (series)|''Super Smash Bros.'' series]], specifically ''[[Super Smash Bros. Brawl|Brawl]]''.

He resents defiance from anybody, but as seen from a few games, he seems to grudgingly respect the Hero of Time's willingness to stand up to him in {{OoT|-}},<ref>{{Cite|So, you think you can protect them from me... You've got guts, kid.|Ganondorf|OoT}}</ref> as well as the Hero of Winds' tenacity to brave the [[Forsaken Fortress]] and reach him in {{TWW|-}}, due to Link being a fellow brave swordsman and a worthy opponent to Ganon.<ref>{{Cite|It's been a while, boy. You have done well to sneak into my fortress and wriggle your way all the way up here. I suppose the least I can do is commend you for your reckless courage.|Ganondorf|TWW}}</ref>

As seen in many games of his appearances, Ganon, because he is the autocrat of darkness, usually resorts to let his army of minions do his dirty work, but the King of Evil himself will fight ones whom he deems worthy enough to challenge him, such as Link and, rarely, Princess Zelda.

Ganondorf was also implied to hold mental instability in two instances. The first was shortly after King [[Daphnes Nohansen Hyrule]] wished for Hyrule and Ganon to be flooded completely with the completed Triforce where he proceeded to engage in hysterical laughter as the land was being flooded permanently during the [[The Legend of Zelda timeline|Adult Timeline]] in {{TWW|-}}, and the other time was when he proceeded to engage in a similar hysterical laugh when witnessing Zelda try to scramble to aid [[Queen Sonia]] shortly after he mortally wounded the latter just before becoming the Demon King in ''Tears of the Kingdom''.

Ganon was shown in {{ALttP}}, {{FSA}} and to a lesser extent {{TLoZ}} to have intellect in his demon form, having plotted the events of both games, as well as being shown to talk in the former two instances. Later games starting with {{OoT}} tended to downplay this aspect to his demon form largely due to focusing on his human form Ganondorf, though it was brought back to an extent with [[Echo Ganon|his depiction]] in {{EoW}}.

===Powers and Abilities===
[[File:HWDE Liberation of the Triforce.png|left|thumb|345x345px|As he is the central villain of {{TLoZ|-}} series and due to the Triforce of Power in his possession, Ganon is widely known as one of the most dangerous beings in the franchise, regardless of his beast or human forms, and only few such as [[Link]] can fight or even defeat the Demon King]]
As expected for his longtime role as the main antagonist of ''[[The Legend of Zelda (series)|The Legend of Zelda]]'' and himself recurringly serving as the [[Final Boss]] in most main installments, unlike the vast majority of other villains who are eventually slain by Link's hand in one game's story (such as [[Ghirahim]], [[Onox]], [[Malladus]], [[Majora's Mask (Item)|Majora]], [[Yuga]], [[Veran]], etc.), Ganon, as he is in fact the hero's archnemesis, is universally feared all across Hyrule, including the [[Royal Family of Hyrule|Royal Family]] and his own minions, and is well-known to be more than just a mere battle against a monster or warlock for Link based on either of his traditional forms. The Prince of Darkness himself is the most notoriously difficult being for the hero to outright kill or at least defeat even with the help of Princess Zelda or other allies thanks to his immortality, near-constant control of the [[Triforce of Power]], and ability to resurrect from death or break a magical seal caused by [[Sage|Sages]] or the [[Master Sword]], primarily due to [[Demise]]'s [[Curse of Demise|endless curse]] of being destined to destroy the special individuals having the [[Princess Zelda|blood of the Goddess Hylia]] and [[Link|spirit of the Legendary Hero]] as a reincarnation of his hatred, who are none other than Zelda and Link, respectively, proving himself a deadly foe virtually impossible to permanently wipe out and arguably the strongest monster in the franchise. In short, Ganon simply ''refuses'' to die and never gives up on his everlasting goal of ruling the Kingdom of Hyrule under his evil reign and stealing the [[Triforce]] no matter how many times Link successfully stops the Dark Beast, similar to certain other central Nintendo antagonists, specifically Bowser of the ''Mario'' franchise.

Even before gaining the Triforce of Power, as his human form Ganondorf, Ganon was already arguably one of the most powerful men in the entire land of Hyrule, largely due to his political position as the King of the [[Gerudo]]. In addition, as seen in a few games and other media, Ganon is capable of powerful dark magic whether he has the Triforce or not.[[File:OoT Link Fighting Ganondorf Artwork.jpg|thumb|Official artwork of Link fighting against Ganon's human form, Ganondorf, in [[Ganon's Castle]] from {{OoT|-}}|332x332px]]His human form, like some other lead antagonists in the series, was also widely known as an immensely powerful sorcerer with vast mystical resources. Additionally, Ganondorf has proven himself to be a skilled swordsman like Link, as seen in the final battles between him and the young hero in various games such as {{TWW|-}}, {{TP|-}}, and ''Tears of the Kingdom'', making the archvillain one of the few to directly combat the main protagonist in his own style, alongside [[Dark Link]], [[Shadow Link]] and [[Gramps]], although unlike the aforementioned characters, Ganondorf does not possess a shield and rather uses his swords to block attacks. He is also very physically strong enough to wield a two-handed axe sword with one grip or the massive [[Trident]] himself as Ganon used in some games such as {{aLttP|-}} or ''[[Hyrule Warriors]]'', explained by his extremely tall height and muscular body. In addition, similar to Link owning and riding a [[horse]] named [[Epona]], Ganondorf has a [[Ganondorf's Steed|dark steed]] of his own, which this particular horse is quite large as seen in {{TP|-}} and apparently has armor around its head and body.
Regardless of his humanoid pig monster or Gerudo forms, once Ganon gained the Triforce of Power, both his physical and mystical abilities were augmented by the essence of the Goddess [[Din (Goddess)|Din]] to god-like proportions - his power unrivaled by any mortal, for he himself ceased to be mortal upon obtaining the mystical relic. Becoming a monstrously powerful demon king, Ganon became utterly unstoppable, conquering Hyrule with ease. He also strongly implies that the Triforce of Power was sufficient enough to allow him to weaken any seals placed on him over time.<ref name=":0">{{Cite|YOU... CURSE YOU...ZELDA! CURSE YOU...SAGES!! CURSE YOU...LINK! Someday... When this seal is broken.... That is when I will exterminate your descendants!! As long as the Triforce of Power is in my hand....|Ganondorf|OoT}}</ref>
[[File:OOT3D Link defeating Ganon gif.gif|left|thumb|244x244px|The Master Sword is the standard weapon in the franchise used by Link to defeat Ganon, as seen in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time 3D|Ocarina of Time 3D]]'', though it does not always eliminate him in spite of its holy power]]
[[File:TLoZ Ganon defeated.gif|thumb|229x229px|Ganon utterly vanquished after being hit by a Silver Arrow in {{TLoZ|-}}, even dropping the Triforce of Power afterwards]]
As seen in the events of {{OoT}} and {{TP}}, his signature Triforce piece also grants Ganon near-invulnerability. Using its power, he is capable of enduring mortal wounds (such as having his chest pierced by a [[Sword of the Six Sages|holy sword]] during his [[Failed Execution of Ganondorf|attempted execution]] as Ganondorf), and only the likes of the most powerful sacred weapons such as the [[Master Sword]] or [[Silver Arrow|Silver Arrows]] appear to be able to kill him. Needless to say, due to the essential rule of Link obtaining this specific weapon in order to defeat his archenemy in virtually all core games he himself appears in with the power of [[light]], the Master Sword itself is a nuisance for the main antagonist. Because it possesses the [[Power to Repel Evil|power to repel evil]], deters his minions and is indeed effective on himself, Ganon undoubtedly fears the legendary blade to the point that in {{OoT|-}} he immediately knocks it off of Link's hands at the final battle, tells the Hero of Winds its important role in {{TWW|-}}, and in ''Tears of the Kingdom'', even expresses genuine disbelief when his deadly [[Gloom]] [[Decayed Master Sword|breaks the sword apart]]. However, the Blade of Evil's Bane does not guarantee the boar-like demon's demise even if it appears fatal otherwise as seen in certain games, specifically {{aLttP}} and {{OoT|-}}; In the former, the beast was merely injured and stunned by the Master Sword including with its [[Master Sword Lv2|upgraded]] [[Master Sword Lv3|states]] and the traditional version of the sacred blade requires a [[Spin Attack]] to even harm him, a clear testament to his durability in-game. In the latter, the [[Hero of Time]] aggressively defeated Ganon via slashing his face three times and even impaling him, but the villain still gets back up afterwards and attempts to continue fighting Link in spite of being greatly wounded (despite few other cases he did perish by it as Ganondorf). On the other hand, unlike [[Light Arrow|Light Arrows]] which can only stun him temporarily, the Silver Arrows are able to actually disintegrate the Great King of Evil entirely into ashes as well as ultimately losing the Triforce of Power for Link to obtain for himself, as seen in {{aLttP|-}} and especially the original {{TLoZ}}, making these very [[Arrow|Arrows]] technically the most dangerous threat among all possible [[Item|items]] in the entire series. The Triforce itself, in its complete state, can also seal or even destroy Ganon when used against him, as seen in {{BotW|-}} and ''Hyrule Warriors''.

Likewise, Ganon was also capable of superhuman strength after gaining the Triforce of Power that only few can match no matter what shape he is in, with Ganondorf's blows being capable of demolishing portions of the floor that weren't directly connected to the floor below as seen in {{OoT|-}}, shaking the ground when he stomps as Ganon in {{aLttP|-}} and the [[Linked Game|linked ''Oracle'']] battle, as well as breaking chains restraining him with minimum effort in {{TP|-}}. Ganon can also easily smash down the remains of [[Ganon's Castle|his castle]] with one swipe of his arms in {{OoT|-}}. As Ganondorf, he was also capable of destroying even disembodied spirits with his bare hands, such as his murder of the Sage of Water after his failed execution. Even without access to his full capabilities in {{TWW|-}}, Ganondorf was able to cast a curse to keep the [[Great Sea]] in an endless stormy night. As seen in {{TP|-}}, the Triforce of Power grants him many more powers such as size manipulation and terrain alteration: while Ganondorf is not personally shown using many of these powers, he temporarily gifts the Usurper King [[Zant]] with some of them.<ref>{{Cite|I shall house my power in you... If there is anything you desire, then I shall desire it, too.|Ganondorf|TP}}</ref> Like his surrogate mothers Koume and Kotake, also known as [[Twinrova]], Ganondorf also has an unnaturally long lifespan due to his black magic, having lived through ages of Hyrulean chronology. In ''Hyrule Warriors'', Ganon often uses his giant fists or claws to pound his enemies into oblivion, can jump great distances, and throw his Trident with pinpoint accuracy. Coincidentally to Bowser, he also has the ability to breathe fire. Although both of Ganon's forms are clearly fearsome in strength, it's strongly implied in most games and other media within the franchise that the Dark Beast is greater than Ganondorf in terms of raw power due to his sheer hatred and massive size as well as a higher degree of influence by the Triforce of Power, and therefore even more dangerous. This is supported by the heavy damage inflicted on Link had he fought the demon without any [[defense]] upgrades in certain installments.
[[File:OoT manga Link vs. Ganon.png|thumb|256x256px|Non-game media such as the [[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (Himekawa)|{{OoT|-}} (Himekawa)]] manga have shown Ganon being a difficult opponent for Link in a one-on-one fight|left]]
In the main series, despite in terms of gameplay Ganon (or other lead antagonists) can be quickly defeated by Link via an experienced player, official source materials such as ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Hyrule Historia|Hyrule Historia]]'' and the ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Encyclopedia|Zelda Encyclopedia]]'', as well as other media within the franchise ([[comics]], [[Manga|manga adaptations]], cartoons, etc.) have depicted the final battles between himself and the hero as fierce in nature, and therefore not an easy outcome for either character, story-wise. In fact, [[Hero of Time|one legendary incarnation]], as explained in the [[The Legend of Zelda timeline|Downfall Timeline]], has a [[Game Over|possible canonical defeat]] by Ganon, confirming the Great King of Evil with no regard of his forms is indeed potent enough to destroy Link - the mightiest warrior in all of [[Hyrule]].

Ganon's dark magic, likely due to his position as the Demon King, grants him the power to create his vast armies of [[Enemy|monsters]] and [[Boss|powerful beasts]]. In some games, he even creates a [[Phantom Ganon|phantom version of his human form]].

His power, with or without the Triforce, appears to grow over time. The sages in [[Arbiter's Grounds]], when discussing his evil power, mention it being pure malice and hatred.<ref>{{Cite|His abiding hatred and lust for power turned to purest malice...|Sage|TP}}</ref> As the incomplete Calamity Ganon, his very essence is known as {{Term|BotW|Malice|link}}, possibly meaning that his demonic magic and power is fueled as much by his own hatred as the Triforce, and his capacity for hatred is the only true limit on how powerful he can grow.<ref>{{Cite|The demon king was born into this kingdom, but his transformation into Malice created the horror you see now.|King Rhoam|BotW}}</ref>

The reincarnated Ganon, during the events of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Four Swords Adventures|Four Swords Adventures]]'', was largely similar to the original, although it is implied that this incarnation was notably weaker, to the extent that he lacked the ability to cover Hyrule in darkness by the time he had been defeated by the four [[Links]] and sealed in the [[Four Sword]], a sacred blade with powers similar to that of the Master Sword.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote=[Ganon] was heard to lament, "The power I have attained from the people of Hyrule is not yet enough." From Ganon's words, we can surmise that he did not have the power he needed to cover Hyrule in darkness.|book=HH|page=121}}</ref>
[[File:BotW Calamity Ganon face.jpg|thumb|214x214px|The face of Calamity Ganon in {{BotW|-}}, giving the fiend his most demonic look in the series to date]]
In {{BotW}}, Ganon is portrayed in a particularly dark way, even by his usual standards, and at first did not have a body, as he appears in the form of an evil spirit known as the [[malice]]. The death and utter destruction he brought to Hyrule long before the events of the game were such that for the first time in the ''The Legend of'' ''Zelda'' series, he was known as the Calamity. His especially dark essence notably took over the very [[Guardian|Guardians]] of the kingdom, and turned them against their creators, bringing forth the fall of Hyrule and the near death of Link himself. His wicked spirit being imprisoned by Princess Zelda within [[Hyrule Castle]], Ganon patiently tried to regenerate in a corporeal form that was supposed to be shaped after Ganondorf's physical appearance. However, because of Zelda's seal upon him, he was forced to pick up pieces and weapons of the Guardians here and there that he tried to amalgamate with his malice, becoming [[Calamity Ganon]]. As a result, though not highly powerful, Ganon in this unusual form is arguably the most terrifying monster ever seen in the series yet in appearance; Looking like a giant scorpion with spider legs and having a disproportionate skull-like head while retaining his reddish hair in large amounts, his combat abilities are somehow hampered by his grotesque and incomplete body, built in spite of common sense. Completely devoid of any magical powers, this artificial creation had no choice but to rely on what the Guardians have to offer. Later on at the final battle, he becomes a [[Dark Beast Ganon|gigantic boar]] but is still incomplete. In ''[[Hyrule Warriors: Age of Calamity]]'', Calamity Ganon was depicted with a more properly bipedal body but retains his twisted appearance and once again not resembling a boar in any way.
[[File:TotK Hyrule Compendium Demon King Ganondorf (2nd Form).png|thumb|219x219px|Ganondorf in ''Tears of the Kingdom'' as the Demon King, giving Ganon's human form a more inherently evil look than his standard shape compared to pre-{{TP|-}} games and resembling Demise|left]]
In {{TotK}}, Ganondorf is depicted at his most dangerous so far, even compared to his previous appearances in [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The Legend of'' ''Zelda'' series]], specifically {{OoT|-}} and {{TP|-}}. Similar to the game's prequel, long before the events of the game, his power has immensely increased beyond limits after he gained one of the [[Secret Stone|Secret Stones]], as demonstrated when he summoned his hordes of [[Demon|demons]] to take over [[Hyrule]]. With his minions, Ganondorf successfully submitted the free peoples one after the other, until [[King Rauru]] and all the [[Sage|Sages]] finally faced him beneath [[Hyrule Castle]]. Even so, their combined strength was no match for the Demon King, and only Rauru's sacrifice ultimately saved his peers and Hyrule from complete destruction. 

When Ganondorf finally broke free from the seal 10,000 years later, even as a seemingly powered-down mummified shape, his strength was so great that he effortlessly [[Decayed Master Sword|shattered the Master Sword]] and nearly killed Link himself by severely damaging his right arm. Although still weakened by his millennial imprisonment, the Demon King proved to be a nuisance for Hyrule as he triggered several cataclysms that affected every major region of the kingdom. He was also omniscient, as demonstrated when he summoned a [[Phantom Ganon|puppet]] with the appearance of Princess Zelda to trick Link and the inhabitants of the kingdom. This nasty surprise also indirectly caused the death of [[Queen Sonia]], and proved decisive in allowing Ganondorf to gain a virtually unlimited power.

Last but not least, it is no secret that Ganon's original human form is an accomplished [[Gloom Sword|swordsman]] as seen in past games, but, in ''Tears of the Kingdom'', he proves to be even more redoubtable when using a [[Gloom Spear|spear]], a [[Gloom Club|club]] and even a [[Demon King's Bow|bow]]. Summoning his weapons from the [[Gloom]] and constantly switching from one to another, his surprisingly swift attacks deal massive damage if struck, with gloom-based projectiles able to take away Link's maximum [[Heart Container|Hearts]] permanently from of the sheer evil fueling the villain's weapons. Ganondorf in this encounter also has many attacks the hero himself can perform, such as a [[Perfect Dodging|Perfect Dodge]] or [[Flurry Rush]], making himself nearly a mirror to [[Dark Link]], and is even capable of blocking [[Arrow]] shots (by swiping his arm on impact if the arrows are fired towards his head twice in a row, when Link isn't airborne) - these feats unseen prior in the entire franchise. In the same game, alongside having a particularly devilish form where he gains a reddish mane and fiery body which bares some resemblance to [[Demise]], he also has a new transformation called [[Demon Dragon]], although this state costs him his personality and mind other than total destruction.

With all of the aforementioned feats, Ganon is effectively one of the most powerful major antagonists in Nintendo's history, among the likes of [[mariowiki:Bowser|Bowser]], [[metroidwiki:Ridley|Ridley]], [https://earthbound.fandom.com/wiki/Giygas Giygas], [https://kidicarus.fandom.com/wiki/Hades Hades], or [[pokemon:Mewtwo|Mewtwo]]. Normally, alongside using holy weaponry, it is necessary for [[Princess Zelda]] and [[Link]] to have their own [[Triforce]] pieces, the [[Triforce of Wisdom]] and [[Triforce of Courage]], respectively, with their strength and abilities greatly enhanced by the blessings of [[Din (Goddess)|Din]]'s sisters [[Nayru (Goddess)|Nayru]] and [[Farore (Goddess)|Farore]], in order to be powerful enough to challenge and defeat him.

==Names==
[[File:TLoZ Introduction.png|thumb|Ganon was initially named "Gannon", as seen in the poorly translated introduction of {{TLoZ|-}} |254x254px]]
Since the release of the [[The Legend of Zelda|original game]] for the [[Family Computer Disk System|Famicom Disk System]]/[[Nintendo Entertainment System|NES]] in 1986, Ganon has had several different names. During the development of {{TLoZ|-}}, he was referred to as {{Romanize|牛魔王 八戒|Gyūmaō Hakkai|Bull Demon King Hakkai}}, which is said to be derived from the ''{{Wp|Journey to the West}}'' characters {{Wp|Zhu Bajie|Chohakkai}} and {{Wp|Bull Demon King|Gyūmaō}}.<ref>{{Cite Web|retrieved=December 27, 2015 |url=http://www.nintendo.co.jp/ds/interview/bkij/vol2/index5.html |title=社長が訊く『ゼルダの伝説 大地の汽笛』 番外篇1　昭和６０年に書かれた文書 |site=Nintendo.co.jp}}</ref><ref>{{Cite Web|retrieved=December 27, 2015 |url=https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/zelda/1/4 |title=Iwata Asks: The Legend of Zelda: Spirit Tracks: Bonus Stage 1: Ancient Documents from 1985 |site=Nintendo.com}}</ref> Before the game's release, the name was changed to {{Romanize|ガノン|Ganon}}, an accurate katakana representation of different roman spellings. Both the Japanese and English language versions of {{TLoZ|-}} spell the character's name as "'''Gannon'''".<ref>{{Cite|MANY YEARS AGO PRINCE DARKNESS {{Color|TLoZ Red|"GANNON"}} STOLE ONE OF THE {{Color|TLoZ Blue|TRIFORCE}} WITH POWER. PRINCESS {{Color|TLoZ Red|ZELDA}} HAD ONE OF THE {{Color|TLoZ Blue|TRIFORCE}} WITH WISDOM. SHE DIVIDED IT INTO "{{Color|TLoZ Blue|8}}" UNITS TO HIDE IT FROM {{Color|TLoZ Red|"GANNON"}} BEFORE SHE WAS CAPTURED. {{Color|TLoZ Red|GO FIND THE "8" UNITS}} {{Color|TLoZ Green|"LINK"}} {{Color|TLoZ Red|TO SAVE HER.}}|N/A|TLoZ}}</ref> The game's English instruction manual instead opted for the spelling "Ganon".<ref>{{Cite Manual|quote=This army was led by Ganon, the powerful Prince of Darkness who sought to plunge the World into fear and darkness under his rule.|game=TLoZ|page=3}}</ref> This disparity gave rise to the notion that "Gannon" was a misspelling introduced during the translation of the first installment's in-game text, inspiring the site and phrase "Gannon-Banned". The misspelling theory is contradicted by the Japanese versions of {{TAoL}} and {{ALttP}}, whose in-game texts kept the spelling of "Gannon".<ref>{{tcrf|Zelda II: The Adventure of Link/Regional Differences}}</ref><ref>{{tcrf|The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past#Regional Differences}}</ref> Ever since the English release of {{TAoL|-}} in 1988, official [[Nintendo]] translations have consistently used the spelling "Ganon". However, it was not until 1992 that the translators completely settled on the "Ganon" spelling: while {{ALttP|-}} was in development, the English draft script still referred to the character as "Gannondorf" and "Gannon", implying that the localization team comprised of Daniel Owsen and Hiroyuki Yamada deliberated a long time about which spelling to use.<ref>{{tcrf|Development:The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past/Script Differences}}</ref> The first instance of the "Ganon" spelling also being used by the Japanese developers is in the source code of {{OoT}}.<ref>{{tcrf|Proto:The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Master Quest/Console Output Strings}}</ref> With the Japanese release of ''[[Super Smash Bros. Melee]]'' in 2001, the games finally adopted the official English spelling for the Japanese audience. Although the etymology of [[Link]] and [[Princess Zelda]]'s names are commonly known, the original inspiration behind the name "Ganon" or "Ganondorf" has yet to be revealed by the Nintendo developers since the franchise's debut.

When {{ALttP|-}} was released, Ganon's human form, Ganondorf, was introduced, although only in name, as {{OoT|-}} fully reveals him afterwards.<ref>{{Cite|The person who rediscovered the Golden Land was Ganondorf the evil thief.|Maiden|ALttP}}</ref><ref>{{Cite|You are the only one who can destroy Ganondorf, the thief-no, Ganon, the evil King Of Darkness!|Maiden|ALttP}}</ref> In the Western manual of {{ALttP|-}} for the [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System|Super NES]], Ganondorf was referred to by the full name "Ganondorf Dragmire".<ref name="ALTTPM">{{Cite Manual|quote=The name of this king of thieves is Ganondorf Dragmire, but he is known by his alias, Mandrag Ganon, which means Ganon of the Enchanted Thieves.|game= ALttP|page=5-6}}</ref> Similarly, Ganon was also referred to in the manual by an alias, "Mandrag Ganon", which means "Ganon of the Enchanted Thieves".<ref name="ALTTPM"/> These names do not appear in {{ALttP|-}} itself, in any of its re-releases/remakes, or in later games, although the name "Ganondorf Dragmire" is used in Ganondorf's character profile on the [[Zelda.com|official ''Zelda'' website]] in 2017.<ref>{{Cite Web|quote=Once known as the King of Thieves, Ganondorf Dragmire used the power of the Triforce to become the beast, Ganon.|author=Nintendo|published=2017|retrieved=April 21, 2017|url=http://zelda.com/online-guide/|title=Online Guide - The Legend of Zelda: The official home of the Nintendo game series|site=The Legend of Zelda series for Nintendo systems}}</ref> According to the {{HH}}, the name "Ganondorf" refers to his human form, while the name "Ganon" is both a nickname and the name given to him when he transforms into the mighty pig-like beast, his actual form.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote=Though "Ganon" is also a nickname for Ganondorf, here it refers to the beastlike form adopted by the thief following his transformation. ==Mainline appearances==
==={{TLoZ|-}}===
[[File:TLoZ Link Using Triforce Artwork.png|right|thumb|300px|Link exposing Ganon with the Triforce of Wisdom in {{TLoZ|-}}]]
[[File:TLoZ Ganon Sprite.png|left|frameless]]
In the original {{TLoZ}}, Ganon leads an invasion in the kingdom of [[Hyrule]] and manages to steal the [[Triforce#Triforce of Power|Triforce of Power]]. Before he can do the same with the [[Triforce#Triforce of Wisdom|Triforce of Wisdom]] to gain complete power, [[Princess Zelda]] is able to break it into [[Triforce Shard|eight pieces]] and scatters it throughout Hyrule, then sends her attendant [[Impa]] away to search for help. Ganon captures Zelda and holds her hostage in his lair, deep in [[Level-9]] ([[Death Mountain]]) under [[Spectacle Rock]], and sends his minions after Impa. [[Link]], having outwitted his underlings and learned of the perils that the land was under, takes up a [[Wooden Sword|sword]] and goes alone to assemble the pieces of the Triforce of Wisdom. Using it, he enters Level-9 and shines it upon the King of Evil's hiding place, causing the monster to cover his face from the [[Triforce]]'s light. Ganon fights ruthlessly with his seemingly invincible Technique of Darkness, but Link is able to vanquish him with his blade and by using the powerful [[Silver Arrow|Silver Arrows]] to deliver the final blow, ultimately reducing Ganon to dust and revealing the Triforce of Power. Following the archvillain's death, Link takes the Triforce of Power for himself to help Zelda, with the restored Triforce of Wisdom to bring peace to Hyrule.

Much like Princess Zelda, Ganon in this early [[Nintendo Entertainment System|NES]] title, unlike most subsequent appearances, is only seen in person at the very end - during the final battle within the final [[Dungeons in The Legend of Zelda|dungeon]].

==={{TAoL|-}}===
[[File:TAoL Game Over.png|left|frameless|206x206px]]
Many years prior to both {{TLoZ|-}} and {{TAoL|-}}, an offshoot/follower of Ganon only known as the "[[Magician]]" attempted to gain the location of the [[Triforce of Courage]] by manipulating the [[Prince of Hyrule]], the Magician dies casting a spell on Princess Zelda to make her fall asleep for eternity in revenge for her not revealing the Triforce of Courage's location.

Years later, in the events of {{TAoL}}, although Ganon himself was already killed in {{TLoZ|-}}, the evil power left behind by his vile heart was causing chaos and ruin in Hyrule. The remnants of Ganon's army make an attempt to revive their master by sprinkling Link's blood onto Ganon's ashes, but this method ultimately fails. In this game, because he is absent, Ganon is not the Final Boss (the first for the series), as Link must battle his evil shadow - [[Dark Link]], instead, and upon his doppelganger's defeat allows the hero to wake up Zelda from her deep slumber with the united Triforce. 

In the ending scenario where [[Game Over|Link is killed during his journey]], Ganon's minions are able to collect his blood and sprinkle it onto Ganon's ashes, reviving him. The [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System|Super NES]] game is also the first where the Prince of Darkness has a puppet doing his dirty work before ultimately revealing himself. ==={{OoT|-}}===
[[File:The Triforce of Power.png|thumb|247x247px|Ganondorf, Ganon's human alter ego, makes his first appearance in this title]]
{{OoT}} marks the debut of Ganondorf, Ganon's human form, in {{TLoZ|-}} series, in which he appears as such throughout the vast majority of the game's story; Ganondorf manages to enter the [[Sacred Realm]] with the unwitting assistance of Link and Princess Zelda during their childhood, touched the Triforce (where it split and gains only the [[Triforce of Power]] while Zelda and Link respectively acquired the [[Triforce of Wisdom|Wisdom]] and [[Triforce of Courage|Courage]] pieces), transforming it into the [[Dark World|Evil Realm]] and he reigned over Hyrule as its new king for 7 years, meaning the archvillain was almost completely successful at conquering the entire world. 

Ganon's first 3D appearance in the [[Nintendo 64]] title compared to his original depiction from the earlier 2D games, while still massive, has a considerably skinnier body shape with minor features of a [[Dynalfos]] such as dark green skin and a tail, without a cape. However, he is more demonic with large goat-like horns on his head, glowing eyes, and has red hair and some bits of [[Gerudo]] clothing like that of his human counterpart. 
[[File:Ganon in OoT.png|left|259x259px|thumb|Ganon as seen in-game]]
The monster himself is fought at the very end of the game during the second phase of the Final Boss battle that began as Ganondorf, in which Link, the [[Hero of Time]], nearly kills him in a one-on-one [[Dead Man's Volley]] duel. Following this, [[Ganon's Castle]] collapses after the Evil King attempts to make his foes die under its rubble, which Link and Princess Zelda manage to escape safely. Soon after, Ganondorf furiously rises from the ruins of his castle. Determined to destroy Link and likely Princess Zelda once and for all, showing his [[mark of the Triforce]], he activates the Triforce of Power to its maximum ability and becomes Ganon - his actual form, who is armed with two large and powerful golden swords rather than his usual Trident seen in previous games. Immediately after the transformation, Ganon knocks away Link's [[Master Sword]], which is sent flying out of the fire barrier and the blade landing squarely on the ground near Princess Zelda (who is also trapped behind) in which the climactic battle takes place. Consequently, Link must use the [[Megaton Hammer]] (or [[Giant's Knife]]/[[Biggoron's Sword]] if obtained, though the former tends to break) to gain a fighting chance against his archenemy. Although Ganon's body is nearly invulnerable due to the Triforce of Power, [[Light Arrow|Light Arrows]] can temporarily stun him, and his tail is the only unprotected area. If Link is struck by Ganon's swords, he takes four [[Hearts]] of damage and knocked back some distance (especially if wearing the [[Hover Boots]]), much like that of [[Iron Knuckle (Boss)|Iron Knuckles]], while Princess Zelda [https://noproblo.dayjo.org/zeldasounds/OOT/OOT_AdultZelda_Scream1.wav screams in horror].

With the power of the Triforce of Courage, after several strikes, Ganon collapses about partway through the battle, and the ring of fire disappears momentarily, allowing Link to regain the Master Sword before the fight resumes, in which the deadly beast will become more aggressive and using the sacred sword is required. After taking yet more hits on his tail, Ganon collapses again, and is immobilized from Zelda using her powers and telling the hero to finish him off, but she quickly runs out of energy. From here, Link must ultimately land the final blow to Ganon's head and mouth (forehead in the [[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time 3D|3DS remake]]) using the Master Sword (attempting to use other weapons including the Biggoron's Sword will simply miss), defeating him entirely. Although the brutal finishing attack on the Demon King by the Hero of Time appears lethal and Princess Zelda claims the Master Sword itself could destroy him, Ganon still survives (likely from the Triforce of Power preventing death), and then gets up [https://noproblo.dayjo.org/zeldasounds/OOT/OOT_Ganon_Die.wav roaring in agony] as he flails about in an attempt to attack Link again in a raging retaliation, barely missing him (''Ocarina of Time 3D'' however has a different animation where the demon swings less wildly). Following his ultimate defeat and reverted to his human form again, Ganondorf is sealed away to the Evil Realm by the Seven Sages, before angrily declaring he will bring revenge to Link, Zelda, and the Sages' descendants in the distant future (foreshadowing {{TWW}}) as long he still holds the Triforce of Power.<ref name=":0" />

At the final battle, after besting him in a sword-to-sword struggle, Link ultimately plunges the [[Master Sword]] directly into Ganondorf's open wound by an [[Ending Blow]] attack taught by the Hero's Spirit, which proved lethal. He does not die immediately, but only when the Triforce of Power fades completely from his hand, he finally succumbs. Ganondorf sees a vision of Zant breaking his own neck, then his eyes gloss over and he ultimately dies while still standing. Immediately before his death, however, Ganondorf, implies that future battles between his reincarnation and his foes' descendants will occur and warns Link and Princess Zelda that the history of light and shadow will be "written in blood".<ref>{{Cite|Do not think this ends here... The Demon King now stood proudly in front of Link. Dressed in a superb kimono embellished with Gerudo patterns, a katana on his left hand, he was less of the king he pretended to be and more of a warrior priest. The two enemies then began a duel of fates, and Ganondorf displayed his mastery of the sword, fighting with swift and surgical precision. It should be noted that his sword charges are reminiscent of the final fight in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess|Twilight Princess]].'' Ganondorf proves to be a versatile opponent, as he occasionally summons a giant mace, known as the [[Gloom Club]], to try and crush Link, in a style strangely reminiscent of [[Ghirahim]]'s final fight in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Skyward Sword|Skyward Sword]],'' as well as a bow, demonstrating adeptness with various weapon types. He sucked the very essence of his four Phantoms, gathered their dark power within himself and brutally unleashed a powerful shockwave that knocked out of commission the companions of Link. He then summoned his katana and gratified Link with a satisfied smile, daring him to attack (beckoning gesture). His attacks in this phase are as fast and unpredictable as ever (even going as far as dodging Link's Flurry Rush, requiring it to be performed twice, and that shooting an arrow to his head no longer stuns him), as demonstrated by his mastery of the bow.

Furious that a mere mortal like Link would beat him, he declared defiantly that his rule has yet to come and Link won't be able to stand in his way. Resorting to drastic measures to defeat Link and subject Hyrule to an Endless Night, he removed Sonia's Secret Stone from his forehead and committed the forbidden act of draconification, fully acknowledging this will mean sacrificing everything, including his mind and body. He then laughed as a stream of Malice engulfed the whole area. Unleashing his full potential, Ganondorf grabbed Link in his maw before leaving the pit and ascending to the surface. As the evil spirit finally emerged from beneath [[Hyrule Castle]] and started swarming around it, the [[Malice]] progressively materialized into a full body, revealing a gigantic black dragon. In {{EoW}}, Ganon, once again in his classic appearance from the early 2D-based games and {{aLBW|-}}, managed to somehow abduct Princess Zelda, trap her in a [[crystal]], and then place her in his lair. He was confronted by Link, with their entering a duel and even having Ganon use [[Dead Man's Volley]] against Link, similar to their battle as Ganondorf in {{OoT|-}}. He was later revealed to be capturing the [[Tris]], spirits who were tasked with preventing rifts to the [[Still World]] from forming within Hyrule.

Later, Princes Zelda and [[Tri]] confront Ganon when he seemingly reappeared. However, it would eventually transpire that "Ganon" was actually a [[Dark Echo|dark echo]] created by [[Null]], a primordial evil whose existence acted as the reason why the [[Golden Goddess|Golden Goddesses]] created the world of Hyrule in the first place in order to contain it. They soon defeated [[Echo Ganon]], causing it to completely disappear. A shadowy version of his echo would later appear during the final battle against Null itself as part of its attacks, using its trident.

Based on the aforementioned plot, Ganon, although featured, is '''not''' the Final Boss of {{EoW|-}} but rather a regular boss, as Null takes his traditional role as this title's main antagonist, instead.

Comments made by the various characters indicated that enough time had passed by the time of Ganon's "return" that the Hyrule populace had largely forgotten his existence, with only a scant reference to his being an ancient demon king being found after extensive research by Minister Lefte.

==Other appearances==

Ganon is also featured in other media, including comics based on the games, animated shows, and the [[smashwiki:Super_Smash_Bros._(series)|''Super Smash Bros.'' series]] (as Ganondorf's [[Final Smash]]). Although his appearance varies from the main series, Ganon is still the wielder of the Triforce of Power who seeks to rule Hyrule by using the power of the sacred relic, fighting against Princess Zelda and Link to reach his goal. Ganon also appeared in ''[[animalcrossing:Animal_Crossing:_New_Leaf|Animal Crossing: New Leaf]]'' as a [[Cameos of The Legend of Zelda in other media|cameo]]. He can be summoned through Wisp by using the Ganondorf, [[Bokoblin]], or 8-Bit Link [[amiibo]].

==Ganon's swords==
:''See also: [[Sword of the Six Sages]]''
[[File:Ganon's Sword TWW.png|left|35px]]
Although they go unnamed throughout {{TLoZ|Series}}, the main antagonist of the franchise has several bladed weapons that he often uses for combat in addition to the '''[[Trident|Trident of Ganon]]''' seen in early games, {{EoW}} as well as ''[[Hyrule Warriors]],'' meant for countering Link's swordfighting abilities. They are inscribed with various pictures or writings depending on the game. The '''Sword of Ganon''' was featured for the first time in {{OoT}}. In the final battle of the [[Nintendo 64|N64]] game, Ganon wields two large golden blades called the '''Gladius Swords of Ganon'''. Ganondorf is seen wielding a pair of different blades again in {{TWW}} during the final battle, called the '''Dual Swords of Ganon''', which are razor sharp with guillotine-like tips and he uses them as his offensive strategy. They are engraved with the names "Kotake" and "Koume.", his surrogate mothers from {{OoT|-}}.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book=E |publisher= |page=21 }}</ref> Later on, in {{TP}}, Ganondorf wields the [[Sword of the Six Sages]], a weapon that the [[Sages]] tried to use against him in an [[Failed Execution of Ganondorf|attempt to kill him]]. The [[Sword of the Six Sages]] serves as Ganondorf's weapon in the final battle against Link. As Calamity Ganon in {{BotW}}, he wields a number of energy blades similar to Guardian Scouts and his elemental avatars. In ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom|Tears of the Kingdom]],'' Ganondorf wields an ornate katana with a dark blade, called the [[Gloom Sword]], a weapon he uses in his final battle against Link like in {{TWW|-}}.
[[File:SSBMGanon.png|right|202x202px|Ganondorf with the Sword-Axe of Ganon as seen in ''Super Smash Bros. Melee'']]
Non-canon appearances of a sword-wielding Ganon include one of his human form's victory poses and Smash Attacks in the [[Super Smash Bros. (series)|''Super Smash Bros.'' series]] starting in ''[[Super Smash Bros. Melee|Melee]]'' and the [[SpaceWorld 2000 GameCube Tech Demo|SpaceWorld 2000 demo]], which included a brief sword duel between Link and Ganondorf. This new '''Sword-Axe of Ganon''' was shown to be much bulkier than the swords he uses in the main series, and had a rounded tip. Ganondorf also uses a Sword in his battle with the [[Hero of Time]] in the [[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (Himekawa)|{{OoT|-}} manga]]. This sword is capable of having Ganondorf's evil magic channeled through it. He also wields two [[Great Swords]] in {{HW|-}}, the Swords of Despair. They can be upgraded to the Swords of Darkness and Swords of Demise.

==Trivia==
[[File:HW Ganon Overshadow.png|thumb|263x263px|Ganondorf with the shadow of his beast counterpart behind him in ''Hyrule Warriors'', showing both of the archvillain's forms simultaneously]]
*Being the main villain of the highly successful [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|{{TLoZ|-}} series]] and one of the first antagonists in video game history, Ganon is widely considered by critics and fans alike as one of the greatest or most influential villains of all-time in a similar level to [[mariowiki:Bowser|Bowser]] from the ''[[mariowiki:Mario_(franchise)|Mario]]'' series.
*[[Topaz]] is Ganon's favorite gemstone. In {{OoT}}, he decorates his armor and weapons with them.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= HH |publisher= |page= 148}}</ref><ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= E |publisher= |page= 21}}</ref>
*In {{TotK}}, the traditional gemstone adorning Ganondorf's forehead is replaced by a [[Secret Stone]], which became the source of his unlimited power.
*After {{TP}}, released in 2006, Ganondorf would not appear again in a ''Zelda'' mainline game until 17 years later, with {{TotK|-}}, in 2023.
*Much like [[Link]] ([[Hero of Time]]) and [[Princess Zelda]] in-game, the incarnation of Ganon in {{OoT|-}} is the most popular among the franchise's community for his role that would set the standard for later games of the series as well as debuting his human alter-ego Ganondorf.
*In the international [[Nintendo Entertainment System|NES]] version of {{TAoL}}, Ganon could be heard laughing in the [[Game Over]] screen. The laugh sound effect itself was reused from the characters Bald Bull, Soda Popinski, and Mr. Sandman from ''[https://punchout.fandom.com/wiki/Punch-Out!!_(NES) Mike Tyson's Punch-Out!!]''. In the Japanese FDS version, however, a roar was heard in a black screen instead.
*Due to a [[Glitches in A Link to the Past|glitch]], it is possible to defeat Ganon in {{aLttP}} with the [[Master Sword]] instead of [[Silver Arrow|Silver Arrows]]. During the final phase, when Ganon is about to teleport, Link must time his sword attack so that Ganon could be hit when he does teleport. Using a [[Spin Attack]] increases the chance of the attack connecting. Even with the [[Master Sword Lv3|Golden Sword]] it will take several successful attempts, but it can be done.
*Like Princess Zelda, Ganon is well-known to have a recurring [[Recurring Music in The Legend of Zelda Series|theme song]] when he appears in the main games since {{aLttP|-}}.
*Although the English version of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Hyrule Historia|Hyrule Historia]]'' clearly states that Ganon is [[Demise]]'s reincarnation, the Japanese version only suggests a connection between the two while leaving it ambiguous as to their precise ties to each other.
*In {{TWW}}, according to the original Japanese text, the Master Sword was only sealing Ganondorf's minions and not actually his magic, as can be observed in-game.<ref name="Magic Seal" /><ref>{{Cite|{{Romanize|その剣は、魔を撃退する{{Color|TWW Vermilion|退魔の剣}} であると同時に、 我が魔族を封じていた、いまいましい{{Color|TWW Vermilion|封印}} そのものなのだよ！|Sono Ken wa, Ma o Gekitai suru Taima no Kendearu to Dōjini, Waga Mazoku o Fūjite ita, imaimashī Fūin sonomonona noda yo!|That sword is the Anti-Demon Sword that repels demons. However, it is also the annoying seal that was sealing my demon tribe.|3}}|Ganondorf|TWW|Japanese version}} <small>(Translation by [http://forums.legendsalliance.com/topic/14202-translation-of-japanese-game-texts/page-27#entry404295 jacensolo06])</small></ref>
*The shape of Ganondorf's ears have varied throughout the series. His initial appearance in ''Ocarina of Time's'' child timeline depicts him with rounded ears. After the time gap in which he was able to obtain the Triforce of Power, his ears become more pointed, similar to those of [[Hylian|Hylians]] (this detail was removed in the [[Nintendo 3DS|3DS]] [[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time 3D|remake]]). Ganondorf retains his pointed ears in ''The'' ''Wind Waker'' and {{TP|-}}.[[File:TLoZ Link Shooting a Silver Arrow at Ganon Artwork.png|thumb|266x266px|Link preparing to shoot a Silver Arrow at Ganon in a [[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening — Nintendo Player's Guide|{{LA|-}} Nintendo Player's Guide]] artwork referring to {{TLoZ|-}}, which is capable of eliminating the villain in an instant, unlike the Master Sword or Light Arrows in almost all later games]]
*Contrary to popular belief among the fanbase of {{TLoZ|-}} series and despite their essential role in most games and other media, the Master Sword and [[Light Arrow|Light Arrows]] are '''not''' the most effective weapons to defeat Ganon or his human form, as these, although commonly used by Link and Princess Zelda respectively to bring the archvillain or his incarnations such as Demise down, they simply stun or wound him. Silver Arrows, on the other hand, are the '''only''' weapon in the franchise designed to completely destroy Ganon, as seen in {{TLoZ}} and {{aLttP|-}}. In the former, Ganon was killed instantly by a single Silver Arrow for the final blow.
*The laugh of the [[Poe|Poes]]/[[Poe Sisters]] seen in {{OoT|-}} and {{MM|-}}, as well as the [[Garo Master]] for the latter, are a [https://noproblo.dayjo.org/zeldasounds/OOT/OOT_Poe_Laugh.wav sped-up version] of [https://noproblo.dayjo.org/zeldasounds/OOT/OOT_Ganondorf_Laugh.wav Ganondorf's laugh].
**This can be seen as a parallel to how [[mariowiki:Boo|Boos]] in ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_64|Super Mario 64]]'' and a few later games from the ''Mario'' franchise have their own laugh being a [https://soundeffects.fandom.com/wiki/Sound_Ideas,_HUMAN,_LAUGH_-_COMICAL_LAUGH:_MALE sped-up version of Bowser's].
*In {{OoT|-}}, Ganondorf plays his own theme on a pipe organ inside [[Ganon's Castle|his castle]]. In {{TP|-}}, a similar effect occurs when Link progresses through [[Hyrule Castle]], as the background music shifts from the Hyrule Castle theme to Ganon's theme. This makes Ganondorf one of a handful of characters in the series to generate their own theme music; the others being Princess Zelda, [[Midna]], [[Ghirahim]] and [[Yuga]], all of whom hum their themes. The first occurrence of this aside from Zelda, however, was with [[Agahnim]] in {{aLttP|-}}, which plays Ganon's theme.
*Although in most cases within the franchise Ganon only communicates by roaring, a few games have the Demon King laughing or even speaking much like as in his human form, as seen {{TAoL|-}} (international version only), {{aLttP|-}}, ''[[BS The Legend of Zelda]]'', the linked ''Oracle'' game, ''Four Swords Adventures'', and ''[[Hyrule Warriors]]''.
**In other media such as [[The Legend of Zelda (television series)|{{TLoZ|-}} TV series]], [[comics]] and [[Manga|mangas]], Ganon is also shown talking in full sentences.
*Early artwork for {{OoS|-}} and {{OoA|-}} shows [[:File:OoS Ganondorf Concept Artwork.png|a design for Ganondorf]], implying he was originally planned to appear in the games. According to the notes for the artwork, the image was meant to depict [[Phantom Ganon|Ganondorf's phantom]].[[File:ALttP ganon with other nintendo characters.jpg|thumb|284x284px|{{aLttP|-}} concept artwork of Ganon's size in comparison to some ''The Legend of'' ''Zelda'' and ''Super Mario'' series characters]]

**[[Fierce Deity Link]] in {{MM}} appears to be about the same height as Ganondorf.
**His beast form unsurprisingly stands even taller. Concept art of {{aLttP|-}}, shows Ganon in scale with several mainline characters from both {{TLoZ|-}} and ''Mario'' franchises, including Agahnim. His overall size appears to be similar to that of Bowser, the main antagonist of the latter [[Nintendo]] series, due to his very large stature. This can easily be observed in {{OoT|-}}. However, some games such as {{TP|-}} or ''Hyrule Warriors'' depict Ganon at a rather monstrous size.
**His original incarnation [[Demise]] is on-par with Ganondorf in size but has a considerably more muscular physique.
*Prior to the battle with Dark Beast Ganon in {{BotW|-}}, Princess Zelda states that Ganon has given up on reincarnation to fight in his "pure, enraged form."<ref name="Pure" /> However, the dialogue in the Japanese version appears to contradict this, as Zelda says that the form was born from his obsessive refusal to give up on revival.<ref>{{Cite Web|quote= This form was born from his obsessive refusal to give up on revival… |published= August 11, 2017 |retrieved= February 22, 2018 |url= https://legendsoflocalization.com/breath-of-the-wilds-ganon-in-english-japanese/ |title= How Ganon’s Motivation Changed in Breath of the Wild’s English Translation }}</ref>
**In order for both passages to be consistent, it can be assumed that Ganon has an obsessive refusal to give up on revival, but has given up on reincarnating into a more sane, sapient form, and instead continues to revive himself in the form of a "purely enraged being".
*Interestingly, in {{OoT|-}}, while Link and Princess Zelda have an obvious change in appearance between the seven-year time periods, Ganondorf himself is rather more subtle. When he becomes the Evil King upon obtaining the Triforce of Power, aside from having his long red royal cape his beast form wears in early media, his eyes become bloodshot, has earrings, his ears are more pointy, and now has slightly longer hair as well. Surprisingly, his in-game model also depicts an increase in height as well, possibly by the Triforce's influence.[[File:SMG Megaleg fight.webp|thumb|288x288px|The original idea for Ganon's final battle against Link in {{OoT|-}} was used for Megaleg in the ''Mario'' series]]
*The iconic final battle of {{OoT|-}} was originally planned to have Ganon at a gigantic scale, and Link must climb onto the monster and attack him on various parts of his body. However, during development, due to the hardware limitations of the [[Nintendo 64]] - being a cartridge-based console from 1996, it resulted in the framerate being reduced severely and the player would not be able to see Ganon himself if Link were to be on top of him, rendering it unplayable. Ultimately, the developers scrapped this idea and reworked the entire Final Boss battle to become how it is in the final release. [[Nintendo]] would, however, successfully reuse this concept in ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_Galaxy|Super Mario Galaxy]]'', released on the CD-based [[Wii]] in 2007, for the [[mariowiki:Megaleg|Megaleg]] boss fight.
**Ironically, the battle against the [[Demon Dragon]] in ''Tears of the Kingdom'' had Link be on top of an unusually huge boss in order to defeat this form of Ganondorf.
*{{aLBW}} in 2013 marks the first time in the longtime series where Ganon's original appearance is displayed in full 3D graphics, 15 years after his first overall in {{OoT|-}}.
*Excluding ''Breath of the Wild'' where he is incomplete, Ganon's final battle in his beast form, from ''The Legend of Zelda'', is often considered to be the easiest to defeat, as when Link is equipped with the [[Magical Sword]] alongside the Red Ring for defense, it only takes four blows to stun him and then vanquish him with the Silver Arrow. However, if he remained with the starting Sword, it requires 15 hits, making the duel significantly more challenging, especially if Link were to fight his archenemy with just his standard tunic.

*According to ''Hyrule Historia'', if the legendary [[Hero of Time]], the Link from the Nintendo 64 titles, were to be ultimately killed by Ganon regardless of his human or beast form in the final battle and the player consequently receives a Game Over, the [[The Legend of Zelda timeline|Downfall Timeline]] would occur story-wise, making himself the only villain in the entire franchise to have canonically won.[[File:HW Ganon as player.png|thumb|311x311px|Gameplay screenshot of a playable Ganon in ''Hyrule Warriors'']]

*During the final battle in {{OoT|-}}, if Link attempts to strike Ganon with [[Deku Nut|Deku Nuts]] or [[Din's Fire]] while in front of the evil beast, the [https://noproblo.dayjo.org/zeldasounds/OOT/OOT_Hookshot_Clang.wav sound effect] of the [[Hookshot]] fired directly into a wall can be heard. Although rather odd, since the main antagonist does not wear anything metal (excluding his large swords) and the items themselves are ineffective in spite of Ganon being briefly stunned by shaking his head unless hit by the tail, this is likely due to the sheer durability of his body granted by the Triforce of Power.
*Ganondorf's maniacal laugh in {{TWW|-}} before the final battle is often considered by fans to be one of the greatest villain laughs in video game history, due to the Gerudo warlock having nothing to lose after ultimately failing his goal when he is notably close to achieving it compared to most other ''Zelda'' games.
*When compared to his original incarnation Demise, Ganon appears to be less inherently evil, as Demise's goal in {{SS|-}} was to wipe out all life, while the franchise's main antagonist simply wants to conquer Hyrule in most games (with the only possible exception being in ''Tears of the Kingdom'' where he was shown planning omnicide as part of his plans to reshape the kingdom), even though Ganon himself is no stranger to murdering individuals or his own minions. For similar reasons, he may have also been less inherently evil compared to [[Majora's Mask (Item)|Majora]] in {{MM|-}} and [[Null]] in {{EoW|-}}, whose antagonists sought to reduce all of existence for fun or into the primordial void, respectively.
*Although they are technically the same character, some fans in the ''Zelda'' community and even a few games of the series treat Ganon and Ganondorf as separate entities. This is especially true in spin-off titles such as the ''Super Smash Bros.'' series and ''Hyrule Warriors''.
*Noticeably, in {{OoT|-}}, when Ganondorf takes damage by Link, his royal red cape can be seen gaining small rips, becoming more numerous upon taking more hits.
*{{MM}}, ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Four Swords|Four Swords]]'', {{TMC}}, {{ST}}, {{SS}}, and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tri Force Heroes|Tri Force Heroes]]'' are the only mainline games in {{TLoZ|-}} series so far that do not feature Ganon/Ganondorf in any way.

*Due to Ganon's appearance in {{OoT|-}} and its remake, there is a well-known strategy to quickly reach his weak point, his tail, by simply forward rolling under his legs in order for Link to easily strike him with certain weapons he is vulnerable to, including Deku Nuts and [[Arrow|Arrows]]. *Perhaps comically, in ''Tears of the Kingdom'', despite the Master Sword's important role of defeating him in most games of the series and the sacred blade itself was [[Decayed Master Sword|damaged]] by his [[Gloom]], if Link is wielding the Gibdo Claymore weapon with the correct gear, with a powerful rating of 178 (the sacred blade is 30 to 60 in comparison), he can inflict very high damage onto the King of Evil when thrown, defeating Ganondorf in just a few blows even in his second phase (multiple must be kept, as they will instantly break after each hit). It is also quite effective against [[Bosses in Tears of the Kingdom|previous bosses]].[[File:SSBB Ganondorf and Bowser.png|thumb|341x341px|Ganondorf and Bowser together, as seen in ''Super Smash Bros. *Due to {{TLoZ|-}} and ''Super Mario'' franchises known to have many parallels between each other (as they were originally created by the same development team), Ganon can be considered a mirror to Bowser. Both main antagonists are hideous and powerful bipedal monsters based on a real-world animal (boar and turtle/ox, respectively) with an inconsistent yet large size and have a very similar longtime goal of taking over a kingdom (Hyrule/[[mariowiki:Mushroom_Kingdom|Mushroom Kingdom]], respectively) that is constantly thwarted by a hero alongside a princess normally as a damsel-in-distress (Link/Mario and Princess Zelda/[[mariowiki:Princess_Peach|Peach]], respectively). The two iconic villains have met each other in the ''Super Smash Bros.'' series since ''[[Super Smash Bros. Melee|Melee]]'', and although there are few cases of Mario teaming up with Bowser to face a greater enemy (such as [[mariowiki:Smithy|Smithy]]), this had never occurred for Link with Ganon or Ganondorf in the mainline ''Zelda'' games (despite they can join together in ''Hyrule Warriors'', and the story mode for ''Brawl'' and to a lesser extent ''[[Super Smash Bros. Ultimate|Ultimate]]'' had Ganondorf working alongside Link and Zelda to stop a greater threat). So far, there has not yet been any official media by Nintendo that explicitly depicts Ganon in beast form teaming up with Bowser, in spite of fans wanting to see this happen for many years.
*The cutscene of Ganon knocking the Master Sword out of Link's hands during the final battle of {{OoT|-}} and forcing to use another item to fight the villain marks the first time in the series where the main protagonist loses a [[Sword]].
*There is a wide misconception among the ''Zelda'' fanbase that Ganon in {{aLttP|-}} wields and fully possesses the power of the complete [[Triforce]] during the final battle (and therefore considering the Hero of Legend to be the [[Controversy in The Legend of Zelda series|ultimate Link]] in the series). Contrary to popular belief, Ganon, although powerful, does '''not''' have any fragment of the Triforce within him during this encounter, including his usual piece the [[Triforce of Power]], as the villain himself, although he did indeed wished to rule the [[Sacred Realm|Golden Land]] prior to the game's events and plans to do it again for the [[Light World]] (Hyrule), he was sealed in the [[Dark World]] by the [[Sage|Seven Wise Men]] and cannot escape with his own power, and consequently requires Agahnim to weaken his seal with the seven [[Maiden|Maidens]], explained in-game, the instruction manual and even [[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past — Nintendo Player's Guide|the official strategy guide]]. The erroneous belief is likely based on the fact that the full Triforce does appear following Ganon's ultimate defeat. However, the golden relic is seen as its component parts from its own pedestal and not absorbed by anyone before Link makes his peaceful wish with it. This would technically be similar to what was seen later in the ''Wind Waker'' when Ganondorf separates the pieces from their respective hosts to reform the Triforce in order to make a wish but fails to do so.
**Ironically, in ''Hyrule Warriors'', in spite of being a spin-off title, Ganon does indeed possess the full Triforce during the Final Boss battle of this game, and when he is beaten by Link, the final cutscene shows golden relic can be seen coming out of his body and then split apart and go into its rightful bearers (though [[Lana]] doesn't absorb the Triforce of Power), before they reform it again to defeat him entirely.
*The official artwork of Ganondorf fighting Link from {{OoT|-}} shows the villain fighting the hero using only his fists and Link himself attempting to defend with his [[Hylian Shield]]. However, in-game, the final battle is a [[Dead Man's Volley]] match. Despite this, Ganondorf can perform an Earthquake Punch if Link were to stand directly under him and this move cannot be blocked.
*[[Yuga Ganon]], the form Yuga becomes when merging with Ganon, has the latter's Triforce of Power on his left hand rather than the right hand, much like Link with the [[Triforce of Courage]]. This is likely due to it being Yuga's dominant hand.
*Ganon's [https://noproblo.dayjo.org/zeldasounds/OOT/OOT_Ganon_Scream.wav vocal effects] in the final battle of {{OoT|-}} were reused from [[Queen Gohma]] and [[Volvagia]]'s [https://noproblo.dayjo.org/zeldasounds/OOT/OOT_Gohma_Scream.wav own] [https://noproblo.dayjo.org/zeldasounds/OOT/OOT_Volvagia_Scream_Battle.wav vocals], though slightly edited. *Ganondorf in ''Tears of the Kingdom'', in spite of his one-sided dark character, his standard appearance has been seen by some fans as surprisingly more handsome than in previous games, enough that he is called "charming and charismatic" rather than a typical villain.[[File:ALTTP Ganon death.png|thumb|223x223px|Ganon dying by Link's Silver Arrows in {{aLttP|-}}, an extremely rare case of the boar-like monster being wiped out in the core series rather than thrown into a seal]]
*Although Ganon/Ganondorf's defeats in the majority of the games of {{TLoZ|-}} series has himself merely wounded and/or sealed away, some titles have had the main antagonist outright killed, as seen in ''Tears of the Kingdom'', {{TP|-}}, {{TWW|-}}, the [[Linked Game|linked ''Oracle'' game]], {{aLttP|-}}, and the original {{TLoZ|-}}. In the latter two of the games, as Ganon, he was eliminated by the Silver Arrows which simply reduced him into ashes, and in the ''Oracle'' finale battle, as he was not fully revived, the villain was vanquished upon his defeat by Link's Sword. In the case of {{TWW|-}}, the Master Sword did a fatal blow by Link performing a powerful [[Down Thrust]] via [[Parry Attack]] directly onto Ganondorf's head for his final attack, causing him to turn into stone. In {{TP|-}}, similar to the aforementioned game, Ganondorf was impaled on his chest via an [[Ending Blow]] and struggled to get up before ultimately succumbing while standing upright, with it being heavily implied that a major contributing factor in his death being the loss of the [[Triforce of Power]] as well as [[Zant]] refusing revival. In ''Tears of the Kingdom'', he was slayed in his [[Demon Dragon]] form by hitting a weak spot on his forehead with the sacred sword, resulting in him exploding. {{TWW|-}}, {{TP|-}}, and ''Tears of the Kingdom'' are the only instances so far where the King of Evil dies by the Blade of Evil's Bane, despite not being Ganon in those final battles. In the spin-off ''Hyrule Warriors'', Ganon can be seen being destroyed by the full Triforce's beam of light upon his defeat.
*As Ganon is well-known as the king of demons that constantly attack Hyrule, the [[King of Hyrule]] can be considered his opposite.
*Although Ganon is known to be tremendously strong in raw physical strength none can match due to the Triforce of Power, in {{OoT|-}}, however, the Hero of Time can rival the archvillain's with the [[Golden Gauntlets]], as when worn, Link can lift and throw black, giant pillars of granite great distances. Despite this, like other strength-enhancement items, they are not used for combat.
*Ganon inflicts an extreme amount of damage to Link in {{aLttP|-}} if the young hero were to fight him in the final battle without any defensive upgrades; If Link were to make contact with the Demon King while only in his standard [[Green Clothes|Green Tunic]], he will take a staggering ''eight'' [[Heart Container|Hearts]] worth of damage, making it almost impossible to survive even if he has the maximum 20 Heart Containers. For comparison, the [[Blue Mail|Blue]] and [[Red Mail|Red]] mails would cost six and three Hearts when hit, respectively, therefore making the latter essential. This also applies for ''[[BS The Legend of Zelda: Ancient Stone Tablets|BS Ancient Stone Tablets]]''. The [[Cane of Byrna]] and [[Magic Cape]] makes Link invulnerable, which can be an advantage, though his [[Magic Meter]] will drain rapidly.
**On a similar note, in {{TLoZ|-}}, Link would lose four Heart Containers if he makes contact with his archenemy in his normal tunic, and with the game's own max number of Heart Containers being 16, the young hero will die and result a [[Game Over]] in only four hits. With the [[Blue Ring|Blue]] and [[Red Ring]], he takes three or two Hearts in damage, respectively.
**In {{OoT|-}}, Ganon inflicts four Hearts of damage onto Link and will be thrown back some distance if he is hit by his large golden swords, similar to [[Iron Knuckle (Boss)|Iron Knuckles]]. However, Ganondorf's special charging move during his Dead Man's Volley duel, although slow to execute and can be stopped quickly by shooting a Light Arrow at him, will cost 5 Hearts. Therefore, the main antagonist is especially dangerous in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time 3D|Ocarina of Time 3D]]''<nowiki/>'s ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Master Quest|Master Quest]]'', where the damage is doubled, making the defense upgrade by the [[Great Fairy of Courage]] to reduce it to half or using the [[Nayru's Love]] spell essential.
***Despite all of this, the Demon King does not have the highest damage output of any vulnerable [[enemy]] in the franchise. [[Shadow Link]] and [[Gramps]] in {{aLBW|-}}, when given the opportunity to use the [[Purple Potion]] during their [[StreetPass]] battles to inflict double damage, they will deal 8 Hearts, but in [[Hero Mode]] where it's increased four times, it can reach an incredible '''32''', making their attacks deadly and always resulting in an instant defeat even with the Red Mail.
*In an interview made during the time {{OoT|-}} was undergoing development, script director Toru Osawa indicated that Ganondorf had some redeemable traits despite being pure evil, similar to the character [[w:c:hokuto:Raoh|Raoh]] from the anime and manga ''[[w:c:hokuto:Hokuto no Ken|Hokuto no Ken]]'' (''Fist of the North Star'').<ref>http://www.angelfire.com/games5/makzelda/interviews/kiootcomments.html</ref>[[File:OoT Ganon's Defeat.png|thumb|276x276px|The original release of {{OoT|-}} featured a violent scene of the ultimately defeated Ganon spewing actual blood while Link also stabbed him directly into his mouth, before being censored in later versions]]
*In the initial v1.0 and v1.1 NTSC builds of the original Nintendo 64 version of {{OoT|-}}, Ganondorf can be seen coughing red blood when defeated, as well as during when Link viciously slashes his face then shoving the Master Sword straight into the mouth for the finishing blow in his beast form (stained blood can also be seen in the [[Bottom of the Well]] and the [[Shadow Temple]]). In the later v.1.2 build, PAL releases, and subsequent ports, due to [[Controversy in The Legend of Zelda Series|controversy]] of the rather brutal level of violence by Nintendo's own standards for the ''Zelda'' franchise or their games in general, it was quickly changed to green (except in aforementioned dungeons as they remain red), and in the 3DS remake, the Master Sword now hits Ganon in the forehead, to tone down the cutscenes and Nintendo specifically wanting to avoid the risk of the original game being re-rated to "T" (Teen), as it was in fact "E" (Everyone) at the N64 release. The remake, however, has ''Ocarina of Time 3D'' under the "E10+" (Everyone 10+) [[wikipedia:Entertainment_Software_Rating_Board|ESRB]] rating.
*The dangerous Iron Knuckle [[Sub-Boss|Mini-Bosses]] seen in {{OoT|-}} and {{MM|-}}, including the one with [[Nabooru]] as such in the former, [https://noproblo.dayjo.org/zeldasounds/OOT/OOT_IronKnuckle_Attack.wav share Ganondorf's voice], though they are supposed to be [[Gerudo]]s inside the armor. Ironically, Ganondorf himself is a Gerudo - a male, as the king of their race.
*Ganondorf's English voice actor in {{TotK}}, Matthew Mercer, unofficially portrayed the character in the fan film ''There Will Be Brawl''.
*In the ''Super Smash Bros.'' series starting in ''Brawl'', Ganondorf's [[Final Smash]] has himself transforming into Ganon based on {{TP|-}} where he stuns opponents in front of him before charging forward for a mighty attack. In ''Ultimate'', Ganon based on {{OoT|-}} appears as a boss.
*Ganondorf's slap onto Princess Zelda's face (though technically [[Tetra]]) in {{TWW|-}} marks the first time in {{TLoZ|-}} series Ganon in any form were to directly attack the princess of Hyrule. It itself also became an infamous meme among the fanbase due to the way he stylishly performed it in retaliation after the Gerudo warlock was struck by one of her Light Arrows, and the fact in some cultures men hitting women or girls is seen as an inappropriate act.[[File:ALBW Ganon Model.png|thumb|274x274px|Ganon's classic appearance is known to be similar to that of Moblins, as seen in {{aLBW|-}}]]
*Ganon's early design in some installments of the franchise shares a parallel to Bowser in that they resemble a giant, significantly more ferocious version of a certain common enemy the two villains command in most games ([[Moblin]] and [[mariowiki:Koopa_Troopa|Koopa Troopa]], respectively). Despite this similarity, however, it should be noted that while Bowser is indeed a [[mariowiki:Koopa|Koopa]] himself, Ganon is not technically a Moblin, but rather a [[Demon]].
*In the original instruction manual for ''The Legend of Zelda'', Ganon was represented by a question mark instead of being shown directly unlike other characters, which was meant to subtly encourage the player to play through the game due to himself only appearing at the end of the final dungeon like Princess Zelda. A similar situation was used for Princess Peach in the manual for ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_Bros.|Super Mario Bros.]]'' in the ''Mario'' series.
*Ganon's boar-like appearance was based on [[wikipedia:Zhu Bajie|Zhu Bajie]], one of the characters from the Chinese novel ''Journey to the West''. In fact, during development of {{TLoZ|-}}, Ganon was originally going to be named "Hakkai", the Japanese transliteration of Zhu Bajie.<ref>https://archive.today/20120629063241/http://www.nintendo.co.uk/NOE/en_GB/news/iwata/iwata_asks_-_zelda_handheld_history_15603_15604.html</ref>
*{{OoT|-}} and {{TP|-}} are the only games in the main series where both of Ganon's beast and human forms are fought by Link, while the former is also the only title so far where they're used for the Final Boss battle altogether. Although {{TWW|-}} has [[Puppet Ganon]] and this particular boss is possibly a form of Ganondorf, it is not actually Ganon himself.
*Ganondorf's iconic voice actor in {{OoT|-}} and {{TWW|-}}, [[List of Voice Actors|Takashi Nagasako]], is formerly the voice of [[mariowiki:Donkey_Kong_(character)|Donkey Kong]] of the ''Mario'' franchise as well as the Japanese voice of [[w:c:sonic:Big the Cat|Big the Cat]] for Sega's ''[[w:c:sonic:Sonic the Hedgehog series|Sonic the Hedgehog]]'' series.
*The act of Ganon's minions using Link's blood to sprinkle into his ashes to revive the villain during the Game Over sequence in {{TAoL}} is a reference to the ''Dracula'' franchise, where the main antagonist, [[wikipedia:Count_Dracula|Count Dracula]] himself, was often resurrected by blood dripping into his remains. This makes sense, as Ganon himself is a powerful demon emperor who often appears [[Ganondorf|as a man]] like Dracula does.
**The ''Death Battle'' web series has one episode pinning Ganon against [https://castlevania.fandom.com/wiki/Dracula Count Dracula from the ''Castlevania'' series], which resulted in himself losing.
*Ganon's final battle in {{BotW|-}} as [[Dark Beast Ganon]] has been criticized as one of the most anticlimactic and straightforward Final Boss fights in the entire series among fans, as the monster's very slow movement and the glowing targets by Princess Zelda leaves him extremely open to Link's retaliation, and his attack via a large energy beam from his mouth often doesn't aim for him and rarely causes high damage. Because of this, the more difficult duel with [[Calamity Ganon]] is believed to be the true final battle of the game, while Dark Beast Ganon is more of a cinematic ending fight.
**On the other hand, the final battles in {{OoT|-}}, {{TWW|-}}, {{TP|-}}, and ''Tears of the Kingdom'' are widely considered among the most climactic fights and defeats of Ganon/Ganondorf in the franchise.
[[File:Final Battle in Hyrule.jpg|thumb|223x223px|Artwork of the final battle of {{TWW|-}}, the most famous case where Ganon only fights as his human form Ganondorf in the game's climax]]
*There are only two mainline games so far in the longtime series where Ganondorf does not transform into his true form, Ganon, during the final battle, those being {{TWW|-}} and ''Tears of the Kingdom''. In the latter game, he instead transforms into a different monstrous form called the Demon Dragon on his apparent defeat.
**Ironically, the designs of both Ganondorf incarnations are similar.
**In addition, there are only three games where Ganon expresses no known interest in the Triforce whatsoever, those being ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Four Swords Adventures|Four Swords Adventures]]'', ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Oracle of Seasons|Oracle of Seasons]]''/''[[The Legend of Zelda: Oracle of Ages|Oracle of Ages]]'', and ''Tears of the Kingdom''.
*In ''Tears of the Kingdom'', the secret stone of Ganondorf is etched with "闇（暗)" which means "Darkness" in Chinese after he transforms into the Demon King.
**In the Japanese version for the game, Ganondorf's motivations for his actions, including the theft of Sonia's secret stone, were to set the clock back to before Rauru brought all the tribes together to form peace, with it being implied he hated the Zonai due to "ruining courage" (implied to be referring to the usage of Zonai devices during battle). This was not made apparent in the English localization.
*During early development of {{OoT|-}}, according to an interview of [[Shigeru Miyamoto]], Ganondorf was originally going to change into Ganon slowly as the story progresses, gradually becoming more grotesque, but was scrapped when in the final release he remained as Ganondorf until the final battle where he simply transforms into his true form using the Triforce of Power.[[File:Gannon 010.jpg|thumb|265x265px|Ganon's physical model used in ''Zelda's Adventure.'']]
*In one of the unsuccessful [[Philips CD-i]] titles, ''[[Zelda's Adventure]]'', Ganon is depicted in an unusually sinister manner unlike the main games of {{TLoZ|-}} series. Although the archvillain himself is indeed a demon, in this game, instead of a boar, his appearance shown to be much closer to that of a pure evil mythical creature - based on [[wikipedia:Demon|actual demons]] described in [[wikipedia:Christianity|Christianity]], as Ganon has red eyes, a muscular and grotesque bipedal body and long claws, giving a rather Satanic look. Ironically enough, he is named Demon Ganon in-game.
*Aside from the Demon King form's obvious resemblance to Demise by Ganondorf in ''Tears of the Kingdom'', it also bears a slight resemblance to an [[wikipedia:Oni|Oni]], a Japanese mythological creature that is often used as a villain in various Japanese stories including ''Momotarou''. In particular, the similarity extends to Ganondorfs horns in this form, and to a lesser extent his hairstyle. The similarity is further reinforced with his usage of the [[Gloom Club]], which was modeled after a Kanabō, a spiked club weapon often used by Oni.
**Ironically, Link's infamous Fierce Deity form is often called "Oni Link" due to a mistranslation.
*Ganon is indirectly alluded to in the Sow-phisticated [[Nintendo eShop]] sale via {{EoW}} being one of the pig-themed games being emphasized.
