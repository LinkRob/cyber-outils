'''Yusuke Nakano''' is an illustrator who works for [[Nintendo]].

==Biography==
Yusuke Nakano joined [[Nintendo]] as an artist in 1992, starting as the package artist for ''{{Smw|Wario Land: Super Mario Land 3}}''.<ref name="A&A 413">{{Cite Book|quote= |book= E |publisher= |page= 413}}</ref> The first game in {{TLoZ|Series}} that Yusuke Nakano contributed towards was {{OoT}}. After being disappointed by the results from outsourcing the artwork of {{OoT|-}} to an external studio, Nakano pushed for Nintendo to develop its own 3D graphical renders for the game.<ref name="A&A 411">{{Cite Book|quote= |book= A&A |publisher= |page= 411}}</ref> Afterwards, Nakano approached {{OoT|-}}{{'}}s Character Designer, [[Yoshiaki Koizumi]], and proposed to do all of the artwork himself.<ref name="A&A 411"/> After drawing a [[:File:OoT Link Portrait.png|bust shot of {{Term|OoT|Link}} as an adult]] and presenting it to Koizumi, Nakano received his approval and officially began work on {{OoT|-}}'s artworks.<ref name="A&A 412">{{Cite Book|quote= |book= A&A |publisher= |page= 412}}</ref> When designing adult {{Term|OoT|Link}}, Nakano incorporated Koizumi's 3D model of {{Term|OoT|Link}} and designed his face after a "world-famous Hollywood actor".<ref name="A&A 412"/> Yusuke Nakano is also responsible for designing the {{Term|Series|Hylian Crest|link}}, which joined the {{Wp|cross}} from the {{Plural|Series|Shield|link}} in {{TLoZ}} and {{TAoL}} and the feathers from the {{Term|ALttP|Fighter's Shield|link}} in {{ALttP}}.<ref name="A&A 414">{{Cite Book|quote= |book= A&A |publisher= |page= 414}}</ref> At the time of its creation, the {{Term|Series|Hylian Crest}} had no deeper meaning, but later inspired the {{Plural|SS|Loftwing|link}} from {{SS}}.<ref name="A&A 414"/> The {{Term|Series|Hylian Crest}} was also joined with the {{Term|Series|Triforce}} to create the {{Term|Series|Wingcrest|link}}, which was adopted as the {{Term|Series|Symbol|link}} for {{Term|Series|Hyrule|link}}.<ref name="A&A 414"/>

While illustrating for {{MM}}, Yusuke Nakano was inspired by the {{Wp|Hellboy}} comics and incorporated a gritty art style with hard shadows to match the dark atmosphere of the game.<ref name="A&A 414"/>{{Fact}}<!-- Nintendo Power August 2005 --> Using this motif, Nakano illustrated [[:File:MM Artwork.png|a piece]] in preparation for [[Nintendo Space World '99]].<ref name="A&A 414"/> This artwork lacked many of the final details of the game, such as the {{Term|MM|Moon|link}}'s face, the presence of the {{Term|MM|Clock Tower|link}}, and the final design for the {{Term|MM|Kokiri Sword|link}}.<ref name="A&A 415">{{Cite Book|quote= |book= A&A |publisher= |page= 415}}</ref> After contributing to {{MM|-}}, Nakano worked closely with [[Capcom]] staff to design {{Term|OoS|Link}} in a manner that harmonized with the finished sprite work of the game and the artistic style of {{ALttP|-}} and {{LA}}.<ref name="A&A 415"/><ref name="E 255">{{Cite Book|quote= |book= E |publisher= |page= 255}}</ref> He also drew from {{Term|OoT|Link}}'s {{OoT|-}} design, giving the new {{Term|OoS|Link}} a shorter nose and white tights.<ref name="A&A 415"/><ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= A&A |publisher= |page= 416}}</ref> Nakano struggled with communicating {{Term|OoA|Nayru (Oracle)|link}}'s beauty and wisdom in her design, taking half a year to finalize it.<ref name="E 255"/>

Nakano created all of the the illustrations for {{TWW|-}} by using a ''{{Wp|Washi}}'' texture after being shown [[Yoshiki Haruhana]]'s [[:File:TWW Link Concept Artwork.png|cartoonish concept art of {{Term|TWW|Link}}]].<ref name="A&A 417">{{Cite Book|quote= |book= A&A |publisher= |page= 417}}</ref> At the request of [[Eiji Aonuma]], Nakano also illustrated the {{Term|TWW|Scroll of the Hero of Time|link}} for the demo of {{TWW|-}}.<ref name="A&A 418">{{Cite Book|quote= |book= E |publisher= |page= 418}}</ref> Its distinct woodblock texture was created by printing the art onto paper and using {{Wp|cellophane tape}} to partially remove ink.<ref name="A&A 418"/>

In anticipation for its large-scale development, [[Satoru Takizawa]] tasked Yusuke Nakano with designing {{Term|TP|Link}} for {{TP}} to assist external artists.<ref name="A&A 419">{{Cite Book|quote= |book= E |publisher= |page= 419}}</ref> To match expectations for a realistic title, Nakano took great care into meticulously detailing {{Term|TP|Link}}'s attire.<ref name="A&A 420">{{Cite Book|quote= |book= A&A |publisher= |page= 420}}</ref> Nakano's [[:File:TP Link Mature Concept Artwork 5.png|initial designs]] for {{Term|TP|Link}} depicted him as an adult in his late 20s, but he later revised it to more closely resemble the {{Term|OoT|Link}} from {{OoT|-}} after receiving feedback from [[Nintendo of America]].<ref name="A&A 420"/> Using his new designs, Nakano prepared [[:File:TP Link Princess Zelda Artwork.png|an illustration of {{Term|TP|Link}} and {{Term|TP|Princess Zelda}}]] for [[E3 2004]].<ref name="A&A 419"/> After the concept of {{Term|TP|Link}} transforming into a {{Term|TP|Wolf|link}} was introduced, Nakano attempted to incorporate the "wolf cut" hairstyle into {{Term|TP|Link}}'s design, but was ultimately unable to make him look "wild" enough.<ref name="A&A 420"/> When designing the {{Plural|TPHD|Fused Shadow|link}} from {{TP}}, Nakano incorporated themes from bronze artifacts from the {{Wp|Shang dynasty}}.<ref name="A&A 420"/> Nakano designed {{Term|TP|Zant|link}}'s {{Term|TP|Zant's Helmet|link|display=Helmet}} by using the themes from the bronze artifacts and sketching shapes that reminded him of Southeast Asia.<ref name="A&A 420"/> As Nakano was deeply invested in {{TP|-}}{{'}}s character design, he later approached Satoru Takizawa and insisted that he be the one to illustrate for {{TPHD}}.<ref name="A&A 420"/>

Because of Nakano's work on {{TWW|-}}{{'}}s woodblock-style opening, his team was tasked with creating the opening sequences for {{PH}} and {{ST}}.<ref name="A&A 422">{{Cite Book|quote= |book= A&A |publisher= |page= 422}}</ref> Under Nakano's direction, [[Yuri Adachi]] created the artwork for {{Term|ST|Niko the Pirate's Picture Show|link}}.<ref name="A&A 422"/> Nakano is also responsible for {{Term|SS|Fi|link}}'s design in {{SS}}, which he submitted for an internal art contest at Nintendo.<ref name="A&A 422"/> His winning sketch was used as promotional material for the then-unnamed {{SS|-}} at [[E3 2009]].<ref name="A&A 422"/> According to Nakano, the similiarity in design between {{Term|SS|Fi}} and the {{Term|TWW|Fairy Queen|link}} was purely coincidental, and the design of both arose from attempting to create a character with metallic features.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= A&A |publisher= |page= 423}}</ref> Yusuke Nakano assisted [[Takumi Wada]] in drafting the latter's [[:File:Link SS.png|first illustration]] for {{SS|-}}, which was unveiled at [[E3 2010]]. At Nakano's request, Wada's designs for {{Term|SS|Link}} specifically featured a detailed set of lower eyelashes.

Yusuke Nakano assumed the role of artist for both {{ALBW}} and {{TFH}}. He was also tasked with creating the "Everyone is Here!" artwork for {{SSBU}}, which was originally a piece featuring 66 {{Plural|SSBU|Fighter|link}} made for [[E3 2018]].
