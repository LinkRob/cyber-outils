The {{Term/Store|Credits|Credits|Series}} are a recurring interface in {{TLoZ|Series}}.{{Name Ref}}

The credits are the ending sequence of a video game, where the names of a title's development team is revealed to the player. It is most often considered the player ultimately winning the game; In most [[Nintendo]] titles and other media, it is seen when the main protagonist fully accomplishes their mission, usually after defeating the [[Final Boss]], and a cinematic cutscene is played showing the world returning to peace once more. Though this mechanic generally means a positive conclusion to the game, occasionally, if multiple different paths are possible, there are cases where the credits are played when the player "completes" the quest story-wise but did not technically beat the game the way it should be done, even showing negative results. These are known as Bad Endings. In some games, earning the credits unlocks post-game content - bonus objectives or rewards in order for the player to continue playing the game after finishing the main storyline.

In the ''Zelda'' franchise, almost always the credits are seen when [[Link]] defeats his archenemy [[Ganon]] or other lead antagonists and rescues [[Princess Zelda]]. Although the credits' ending cutscenes varies from game-to-game, due to the heroic nature of the longtime series, since {{aLttP}}, they are famously known by the fanbase to have exciting, touching, or sometimes emotional scenes/music before and after the development team's names are shown. In some installments, Link is shown putting the [[Master Sword]] back into its [[Pedestal of the Master Sword|pedestal]].

==Overview==
==={{TLoZ|-}}===
{{Stub|TLoZ}}
In {{TLoZ}}, after Link defeats [[Ganon]] with the [[Silver Arrow]] and then rescues [[Princess Zelda]] at [[Level 9|Level-9]], the princess herself congratulates him for becoming the hero of [[Hyrule]]. The two respectively use the restored [[Triforce of Wisdom]] and [[Triforce of Power]] to restore peace with rapid flashes on the screen (virtually none in subsequent releases to prevent epilepsy), then the credits appear. Following this, the [[Second Quest]] message will be seen, allowing the player to continue. Once the more difficult replay mode is completed, a unique message shows congratulating the player for having great wisdom and power, even saying "End of The Legend of Zelda 1".

== Trivia ==

* The player receiving a [[Game Over]] is often considered the opposite of gaining the credits.
** Ironically, the credits theme in {{TLoZ}} is an extended version of its Game Over theme.
* In {{MM}}, if [[Link]] does not have certain [[Mask|Masks]], a part of the ending cutscene corresponding to that mask is not shown to the player until they earn it the next time they play the game. This has encouraged gamers to do a [[100% Completion]] in order to fully see the credits.
* Although {{OoT}} technically does have a Bad Ending via the [[Hero of Time]] being ultimately defeated by [[Ganon]], according to ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Hyrule Historia|Hyrule Historia]]'' explaining the [[The Legend of Zelda timeline|Downfall Timeline]], the game itself doesn't have credits or at least a cinematic cutscene specifically for it, as this was never planned during development.
