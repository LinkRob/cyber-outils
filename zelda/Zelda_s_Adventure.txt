'''''Zelda's Adventure''''' is a video game developed by Viridis and released for the [[Philips CD-i]] in 1995. It is the third {{TLoZ|Series}} game released for the console, following the release of ''[[Link: The Faces of Evil]]'' and ''[[Zelda: The Wand of Gamelon]]'' a year prior, which were developed separately by [[Animation Magic]]. {{ZA|-}} boasts an entirely unique design in comparison to the previous CD-i ''Zelda'' titles, emphasizing the difference in production between Viridis and the developer of its predecessors.

A product of a compromise between [[Nintendo]] and {{wp|Philips}} due to their failure to release a CD-ROM based add-on to the [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System]],<ref name="Sony Philips">[http://www.nytimes.com/1991/06/03/business/nintendo-philips-deal-is-a-slap-at-sony.html Nintendo-Philips Deal Is a Slap at Sony - NYTimes.com]</ref> {{ZA|-}}, alongside the other two, are the only licensed {{TLoZ|-}} games developed for and released on a non-Nintendo system. The games have been subject to much criticism, and Nintendo does not recognize them as canon to {{TLoZ|-}} series.<ref>[http://multiplayerblog.mtv.com/2013/09/19/eiji-aonuma-addresses-those-horrible-zelda-cd-i-games/ Eiji Aonuma Addresses Those Horrible 'Zelda' CD-i Games | MTV Multiplayer]</ref>

==Story==
[[File:ZA Gaspra Intro.png|thumb|left|An in-game cutscene|269x269px]]
The lands of [[Hyrule]] and [[Tolemac]], a region in southeastern Hyrule,<ref>{{Cite manual|quote=Zelda must set out on her journey deep into the uncharted southeastern region of Hyrule, known as Tolemac.|game=ZA|page=2}}</ref> are in the middle of an Age of Darkness. [[Ganon]] (referred to in-game as "[[Ganon#Names|Gannon]]") has captured [[Link]] and began exerting his influence over the land.<ref>{{Cite manual|quote=It is the Age of Darkness. The evil Gannon has captured the young hero Link.|game=ZA|page=2}}</ref> He had stolen the seven [[Celestial Sign]]s and hidden them away in Tolemac's Shrines, guarded by his followers, the Shrine Keepers.<ref>{{Cite|Ah, the scroll of Shurmak, bearer of sad news these many years ago. A brave warrior will have to be found to face this evil monster.|Gaspra|ZA}}</ref> In need of a brave warrior to save the kingdom, the astronomer [[Gaspra]] summons [[Princess Zelda]] as his champion to undertake the dangerous task of relocating the Celestial Signs, to rescue Link, and to ultimately defeat Ganon and return peace to Tolemac.<ref>{{Cite|And so I found this champion of strength and courage, it is you, Princess Zelda. Along the way, Zelda is guided by Gaspra and [[Shurmak]],<ref>{{Cite|I am Shurmak your guide. I have known you since you were a child. You were given a difficult challenge. Zelda journeys through Tolemac as she confronts the Shrine Keepers and takes back the Celestial Signs, while meeting many other helpful and tricky characters along the way. She eventually finds the final Celestial Sign, but she is stopped by Ganon and is sent falling down an opening at the [[Vision Henge]].<ref>{{Cite|Zelda, life is always one order more horrifying than you expect. She defeats him, and he disappears in a twister as his realm shatters. With Ganon's defeat, Link is rescued and peace is restored to Hyrule and Tolemac as the kingdom enters an "{{Term|ZA|Age of Lightness|link}}".<ref>{{Cite manual|quote=Once she succeeds, she gains the knowledge and strength to rescue Link and brings the magical land of Hyrule into the Age of Lightness.|game=ZA|page=2}}</ref><ref>{{Cite|Princess Zelda, you've succeeded. ==Gameplay==
[[File:ZA Gameplay.png|thumb|left|An in-game screenshot|274x274px]]
Princess Zelda serves as the game's protagonist, making {{ZA|-}} the second game in the series where the eponymous princess is playable. Unlike the previous two CD-i ''Zelda'' games, which are platforming side-scrolling titles similar to {{TAoL}}, {{ZA|-}} incorporates a similar top-down view reminiscent of most conventional 2D ''Zelda'' games. The land of Tolemac and its Shrines are divided into individual screens, in a very similar vein to the overworld and dungeons in {{TLoZ}}. Instead of a [[Sword]], Zelda uses a [[Items in Zelda's Adventure#Wand|Wand]] to combat enemies with. This Wand can acquire a plethora of [[Spells of Zelda's Adventure|Spells]] for added offense, some of which are required to defeat certain enemies and bosses. Casting these Spells costs a certain number of [[Rupee]]s per use, similarly to the items in the previous CD-i ''Zelda'' games.

{{ZA|-}} also differs from the other two games in that it has dungeons, which are notably absent from {{TFoE|-}} and {{TWoG|-}}. Zelda must locate the Shrines where the Celestial Signs are guarded, which are to be completed in a specific order. The entrances to the dungeons will be barricaded otherwise should Zelda try to visit them out of order. [[Map]]s and [[Compass]]es can also be found in these Shrines to navigate them, akin to the dungeons in canon ''Zelda'' games.

As with the other two games, {{Wp|full motion video}} cutscenes are used to provide story. These cutscenes are used much less extensively than in the previous games, and are filmed live-action as opposed to animation. Character interaction is mainly achieved through the use of voice acting, as many characters will verbally speak to Zelda when approached on the overworld.

==Development==
{{ZA|-}}, along with the previous titles {{TFoE|-}} and {{TWoG|-}}, were the result of a compromise between Nintendo and Philips. After an attempt to produce a CD-ROM based add-on for the Super Nintendo Entertainment System with Philips failed, Nintendo reached a compromise and gave Philips the license to five of their franchises' characters for use for the Philips CD-i.<ref>{{Cite Web|quote=In a tribute to Nintendo's drawing power, Philips N.V. of the Netherlands has reached an agreement for Nintendo to provide its games for Philips's new interactive compact disk player, which lets users manipulate characters on a television screen. The arrangement is expected to give the Philips machine an edge over competing products.|author=Eben Shapiro|published=June 1, 1991|retrieved=September 15, 2015|url=http://www.nytimes.com/1991/06/01/business/nintendo-goal-bigger-game-hunters.html|title=Nintendo Goal: Bigger-Game Hunters|site=NYTimes.com}}</ref> {{ZA|-}} was developed separately from the other two titles by Viridis Corporation.

As Viridis was given very little budget to work with, the development team had to get creative. Unlike the previous ''Zelda'' CD-i games, the idea was to build a game using top-down aesthetics similar to {{TLoZ|-}} with realistic-looking graphics. However, this goal faced numerous difficulties and challenges with the team's limited budget as well as the CD-i's memory limitations.<ref name="Develop1">[http://cdii.blogspot.ca/2007/02/philips-viridis-zelda.html Interactive Dreams: Philips + Viridis = Zelda]</ref> One issue was the top-down motion capture necessary for all of the human characters, as Viridis could not afford to rent a studio. The team instead resorted to hanging a mirror on the office ceiling and have a camera on the floor point up to it to record the actors.<ref name="Develop1"/> The human characters were all played by the in-office staff.<ref name="Actors">{{Cite Web|quote=Then Viridis was contracted to make Zelda’s Adventure for Phillips CD-i. We all wore many different hats when working on this game. They would use everyone in the office as characters in the game. The receptionist Diane Burns ended up being Zelda in the cut scenes and I ended up being casted as Gaspra.|author=Mark Andrade|published=September 3, 2014|retrieved=March 19, 2018 |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170808052537/http://sputnikgames.com/blog/gaspra-zeldas-adventure-story|title=Zelda's Adventure - My First Video Game Development Job|site=Sputnik Games|type=Archive}}</ref> The walking animations were achieved by having the actors walk on a motorized treadmill. For the FMVs, one of the walls in the office was painted a bright blue to achieve the use of blue screen.<ref name="Develop1"/> The indoor backgrounds and enemy sprites were created from clay models and props made by Jason Bakutis, which were then filmed on a blue screen.<ref name="Develop2">[http://www.nintendoplayer.com/interview/zeldas-adventure-jason-bakutis/ Interview with Zelda's Adventure {Philips CD-i) Model and Prosthetic Maker Jason Bakutis - Nintendo Player]</ref> The background seen in Gaspra's FMVs were one of the clay props used.<ref name="Develop3">[http://cdii.blogspot.ca/2007/03/zelda-voyeur-and-man-who-worked-on-both.html Interactive Dreams: Zelda, Voyeur, and a man who worked on both CD-i projects...]</ref>

The overworld background terrains were taken from a combination of photo shoots of the Los Angeles area, photos of Hawaii taken by helicopter prior to the start of the project, as well as holiday vacation photos taken from other members of staff. A few of the artists were also sent with cameras to take macro photos of various textures for the in-game scenery.<ref name="Develop3"/> The game's music was composed by in-house composer Mark Andrade, who also played Gaspra in the live-action FMVs. The character was instead voiced by Hal Smith. Zelda in the opening cutscene was played by office receptionist Diane Burns.<ref name="Actors"/>

Due to the console's limited memory and other hardware issues, the development team faced many frustrating difficulties with putting the game together. The highly-detailed backgrounds and sprites had to be reduced in size and color<ref name="Develop2"/> and, at one point, the game's music and sound effects had also took up extra kilobytes of RAM.<ref name="Develop3"/> These issues became a contributing factor as to why the game loads slowly when moving between screens.<ref name="Develop3"/>

The game took two years of testing at Philips, longer than it took to develop, before finally being released.<ref name="Develop3"/> Philips had stopped publishing CD-i games in North America by the time {{ZA|-}} was finished, and as a result the game was exclusively released in Europe.<ref name="ReleaseDate"/>

==Trivia==
*''Zelda's Adventure'', along with the [[TWoG|previous]] [[TFoE|two]] [[Philips CD-i|CD-i]] games, were the first ''Zelda'' titles to have extensive voice acting and dialogue, which wouldn't happen again until {{BotW}} was released in 2017.
*This game is likely a precursor to ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Echoes of Wisdom|Echoes of Wisdom]]'', which was released in 2024, as it features [[Princess Zelda]] as the protagonist rather than [[Link]], who seems to have been captured by [[Ganon]].
*Ganon, rather than a boar-like beast, is depicted much like that of a [[wikipedia:Demon|real-life demon]] described in [[wikipedia:Christianity|Christian religious beliefs]], despite being ironically [[Demon|such an evil creature]].
*Ganon's name is misspelled as "Gannon" on the scroll in the first cutscene, similar to the earliest games of the [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|core series]].
*Tolemac Is just Camelot spelled backwards.
*This is the only ''The Legend of Zelda'' game on the CD-i to not feature [[King Harkinian]].
*The tutorial has several errors. It features several screens that never appear in game and enemies that only appear in Shrines on the overworld, [[Rupee|Rupees]] are incorrectly called "Rubies" like in the original release of ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]'', and it claims Zelda will come across many locked doors but in the game there are only a few and only in one Shrine. The inventory screen also has a different font compared to in-game and it counts items with a single digit number instead of the double digit number. It shows 4 keys in the inventory, which is impossible since there are only two keys in the game. It is possible that these are leftovers from earlier builds.
*''Zelda's Adventure'' is infamous for having slow loading times, lasting several seconds. It would take 30 minutes to transition to every screen just once.
*This game marks the final acting role of Hal Smith who voiced [[Gaspra]], despite not being listed in the credits.
*The Skateboarder seen in the game was meant to be foreshadowing Viridis Corporation's next game,{{Exp|What was the game's name?}} but it was cancelled.
*This is one of the few games in the series where the [[Triforce]] neither appears, nor is even mentioned.
