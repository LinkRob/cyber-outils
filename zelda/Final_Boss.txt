'''[[wikipedia:Boss_(video_games)#Final_boss|Final Bosses]]''', also known as the '''Last Boss''', '''End Boss''', or, rarely, '''Boss of the Game''', are a recurring mechanic in [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' series]].

== Characteristcs ==
In most video games, the Final Boss is a special type of [[boss]] that specifically serves as the last and toughest obstacle standing in the way of the main protagonist's mission. As they appear at the end of the game via the final stage and are often the primary goal, the defeat of this very powerful [[enemy]] or non-player character results in the positive conclusion of the main storyline, and therefore rewarding the [[credits]] for the player (and in some cases, post-game or special content). Usually, but not always, the Final Boss is the main antagonist and mastermind behind the plot of the game, much like the role [[Ganon]] recurringly plays in [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|{{TLoZ|-}} series]], and the final showdown is that antagonist's ultimate chance to destroy the protagonist and vice versa for the fate of the game's story (which is more explicit in titles with multiple endings based on whoever is victorious). They can be distinguished by having a grander introduction and a unique, more climactic battle theme as opposed to the common bosses (especially in older games). Despite in terms of gameplay they can be quickly defeated by an expert player, these characters, story-wise, are often equal or sometimes stronger than the main protagonist in raw strength, making themselves fierce opponents and prove highly difficult for an unprepared or inexperienced player battling them the first time. However, in rare cases, the complete opposite can occur, with the Final Boss character being weaker in comparison but still rather cunning enough to be able to hold their ground against the protagonist (eg. [https://kirby.fandom.com/wiki/Kirby Kirby] against [https://kirby.fandom.com/wiki/King_Dedede King Dedede] in the ''[https://kirby.fandom.com/wiki/Kirby_(series) Kirby]'' series). Due to their important role, considerable challenge, and more often than not intense atmosphere of the battlefield, the final battle is often considered by fans to be arguably the most exciting point of many video games, similar to literature and movies.
[[File:SWHD Link vs Demise.png|left|thumb|272x272px|Gameplay screenshot of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Skyward Sword HD|Skyward Sword HD]]''<nowiki/>'s final battle against [[Demise]], one of the few cases in the series where the Final Boss is not Ganon (although this character is technically his original incarnation).]]
In the ''Zelda'' franchise, whether their plan is to take over the [[Universe|world]], gain the true force of the [[Triforce]], or rid the land of peace and serenity, it is [[Link]]'s main objective to stop and defeat this character before the villain succeeds their goal. Ganon is by far the most popular Final Boss of the series, but several games have seen a variety of different lead antagonists such as [[Majora's Mask (Item)|Majora's Mask]], [[Vaati]], or [[Demise]]. The Final Boss often has a puppet, or minion who precedes him, like [[Zant]] in {{TP}} or [[Agahnim]] in {{aLttP}}, who appeared to be the primary villain of the game until the plot took an unexpected turn story-wise, revealing the main antagonist to be a larger, more influential figure, such as Ganon himself. In nearly all cases throughout the long-running series, the Final Bosses who Link ultimately fights at the games' climax are ferocious, deadly monsters - some being [[Demon|demons]], and in [[Zelda II: The Adventure of Link|one game]], [[Dark Link|even himself]]. Link normally battles such foes alone, but some installments have the hero being assisted by other allies, often [[Princess Zelda]], to help defeat them, starting in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker|The Wind Waker]]'' (though she attempted to do so in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]''). Expectedly, although not necessarily required in most games, as with standard bosses, it is rather recommended for Link (or other playable protagonists) to obtain both offensive and defensive [[Upgrading|upgrades]] as well as healing [[Item|items]] ([[Potion|Potions]], [[Fairy|Fairies]], etc.) to reduce the risk of a [[Game Over]], due to their high damage output, strong defensive capabilities or health, and ability to counter certain attacks. In rare cases, the young hero cannot defeat or even harm the Final Boss without having a certain key item serving as its weakness ([[Master Sword]], [[Light Arrow]], [[Silver Arrow]], etc.) or performing the correct fighting technique such as the [[Spin Attack]].  
[[File:OOT3D Link defeating Ganon gif.gif|thumb|269x269px|Link's final blow in defeating Ganon in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time 3D|Ocarina of Time 3D]]'', credited as one of the most famous finishing blows]]
Famously, unlike lesser enemies, in certain 3D games since ''Ocarina of Time'', whenever the Final Boss is successfully defeated, Link delivers a climactic final blow with the Master Sword (or similar weapons) to finish them off. This itself is one of the main contributing factors of the franchise's massive success alongside the popularity of Link himself. 

==Recurring Final Bosses==
===Ganon===
{{Main|Ganon}}
[[File:ALttP Ganon Artwork.png|right|frameless|283x283px]]
'''Ganon''' is the most frequently recurring Final Boss of the series, namely because as he is the main villain of {{TLoZ|-}} franchise and the archnemesis of [[Link]] and [[Princess Zelda]], his ties to the [[Triforce]] and to [[Hyrule]] itself outweigh any other antagonist in every other ''Zelda'' title. As the wielder of the [[Triforce of Power]], Ganon assumes an incredible amount of strength, not only physically, but magically as well; his ability to transform the land and its atmosphere to suit his dark desires is a power used in a more positive light by his representative goddess, [[Din (Goddess)|Din]]. Throughout the longtime series and other media, Ganon exists in [[Gerudo]] form as [[Ganondorf]], his standard pig form (wielding a [[Trident]] in some games), and even in [[Dark Beast Ganon|beast]] form in {{TP}}, using different aspects of his Triforce of Power to morph into various shapes and raw strength, though it is often implied his actual demon form is more powerful than his human counterpart.

===Vaati===
[[File:FS Vaati Artwork.png|right|200px]] {{Main|Vaati}}
'''Vaati''' is the second-most recurring major antagonist in the ''Zelda'' series, only after Ganon, appearing in a total of three games. Vaati is a [[Minish]] wind mage who uses his dark magic to get his way, in his search for the [[Light Force]] of Hyrule through selfish motives. Like Ganon, Vaati also can change form, last of which being his intimidating "Wrath" phase. Vaati only appears as an antagonist, however, whenever Link is forced to pull the [[Four Sword]] from its pedestal in the [[Elemental Sanctuary]], releasing his spirit, only to be sealed back into the sword during the game's finale, with the exception of ''The Minish Cap'', where he is ultimately destroyed.

Vaati is the Final Boss in:
*{{FS}}
*''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Minish Cap|The Minish Cap]]''

== Trivia ==

* Although most Final Bosses throughout [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|{{TLoZ|-}} series]] have themselves utterly killed by [[Link]]'s hand upon their ultimate defeat, very few such [[Dark Link]], [[Vaati]], and especially [[Ganon]] himself have returned to reappear in a later game, due to those villains being revived from being sealed away or resurrected. In the case of Ganon, often as [[Ganondorf]], this is because he is in fact the endless reincarnation of [[Demise]]'s hatred.
* The Final Bosses of the ''Zelda'' series can be seen as a parallel to the [[mariowiki:Category:Final_bosses|ones in the ''Super'' ''Mario'' franchise]], as in both cases, the games' main antagonist is usually Ganon/[[Bowser]] in different forms, respectively, but others have foes that appear from other worlds and have twisted appearances or personalities.
* {{TAoL}} is the first game in the series to have unique themes for both the Final Boss and regular bosses, as the original {{TLoZ}} did not have such music whatsoever.
* Due to Link's rather unique sword attack moves in order to defeat the lead antagonists in various 3D installments, which involves slashing the foe harder than usual or even impaling them to reflect the medieval-like setting (therefore depicting more violence than most other [[Nintendo]] games), some fans in the series' community have played the games solely just to face the Final Boss and see the finishing blow. This is especially true for {{OoT}}, {{TWW}}, {{TP}}, {{SS}}, and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom|Tears of the Kingdom]]''.[[File:HW Ganon as player.png|thumb|254x254px|Ganon controlled by the player in ''Hyrule Warriors'', a rare case of a Final Boss character being playable]]
* Though an uncommon mechanic for most video games, a character known as the Final Boss can be playable when unlocked by special means or glitches, usually in fighting games, giving the player major, if not broken, advantages compared to standard characters. As with normal bosses, this is never a case in the core series of the ''Zelda'' franchise for obvious reasons. However, in spin-offs, specifically ''[[Hyrule Warriors]]'', where he is indeed its final opponent in the main story, Ganon can be played in the "[[Ganon's Fury]]" DLC Challenge Mode. Here, he actually fights against his [[Enemies in Hyrule Warriors|own minions]], including ones that are otherwise bosses for Link or other Warriors, and eventually battles against a shadowy version of himself named [[Dark Ganon]]. In its [[Hyrule Warriors: Age of Calamity|sequel]], [[Calamity Ganon]] can be unlocked by completing the "[[Versus Calamity Ganon]]" mission.
* Usually but not always, [[Hyrule Castle]] or similar locations is the setting of Final Boss battles throughout the series.
* Although popularized by {{OoT|-}}, the last boss having a special defeat/death animation compared to lower enemies have existed as early as {{TLoZ|-}}, where in the [[Nintendo Entertainment System|NES]] game, Ganon can be seen physically exploding into dust after being hit by a [[Silver Arrow]] to finish him off.
* Rarely, a recurring character originally depicted as the Final Boss can appear as a [[Boss|standard boss]] or even a [[Sub-Boss]] in a later game. For example, Dark Link debuted as the final opponent of {{TAoL|-}}, but became a mini-boss in ''Ocarina of Time''. Even Ganon was set as a usual boss in {{TP|-}} and {{EoW}} (though in the latter case it was an [[Echo Ganon|Echo]] by [[Null]] and not the actual character).
* Some Final Bosses such as [[Majora's Mask (item)|Majora]], Vaati, [[Onox]], [[Yuga Ganon]], [[Lady Maud]], [[Null]], and Ganon himself have two or three increasingly more powerful forms as they take enough damage between their battles, with the last being their true state.[[File:OoT Ganon's Defeat.png|thumb|244x244px|The final blow on Ganon in {{OoT|-}} is widely considered one of the greatest Final Boss endings in the series, but it is also controversially violent in the original release]]
* The final battle with Ganon in {{OoT|-}}, in both his [[Gerudo]] and [[Demon]] forms, and the destruction of [[Ganon's Castle|his castle]] between their phases, is arguably considered the most climactic boss battle in not only the long-running franchise, but video game history among critics and fans alike. However, it is not without [[Controversy in The Legend of Zelda Series|controversy]], as unlike in any other ''Zelda'' game, the final blow features real blood via stabbing into the villain's mouth that would ultimately be censored to green in later versions and the [[Master Sword]] hitting Ganon's forehead in its [[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time 3D|Nintendo 3DS remake]]. Ganondorf also pukes blood when defeated.
** On the other hand, [[Dark Beast Ganon]], the second phase of the final battle against Calamity Ganon in {{BotW}} is met with more criticism than any other Final Boss, and is called an anticlimactic fight due to the gigantic boar-like monster being shockingly easy to damage and dodge its attacks, as it rarely and slowly turns toward Link's direction and takes only a few hits from the [[Bow of Light]] to defeat it.

* According to the [[The Legend of Zelda timeline|Zelda Timeline]], in {{OoT|-}}, if the [[Hero of Time]] were to be ultimately defeated at any point in the final battle and the player consequently receives a [[Game Over]], the "Downfall Timeline" branch would occur. This makes Ganon the only Final Boss in the entire series to have canonically won. However, there is no unique cutscene or cinematic for this event in-game (as it was never planned during development).
* Three Final Bosses so far are [[Demon King|Demon Kings]]. They are Demise, Malladus, and of course, Ganon himself.
* Final Bosses in ''The Legend of Zelda'' series, not unlike other gaming franchises, are a major subject of spoilers by fans on social media by an upcoming or relatively new game's release, which is met with controversy.[[File:ALBW Gramps Model.png|thumb|208x208px|Gramps from ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link Between Worlds|A Link to Between Worlds]]'' is considered by fans to be arguably the hardest boss in the franchise]]
* Despite their famous role of being the final opponent of the game and defeating them often rewards the ending credits, contrary to popular belief, the Final Boss is not always the most powerful foe. There is a rare type of boss known as a "[[wikipedia:Boss_(video_games)#Superboss|Superboss]]", where such an enemy has extreme strength and/or health that is notoriously difficult to defeat even for skilled players and is actually stronger than the Final Boss, though unlike the traditional boss, they are entirely optional and therefore not required to be defeated in order to complete the game. {{TLoZ|-}} series does not use such a category, but very few major opponents such as Dark Link, [[Shadow Link]], and especially [[Gramps]] are considered by some fans to be in the level of a Superboss due to their high level of challenge and mirroring Link's skills. Gramps is rather unique in that he is not an antagonist.
* For video games in general, although the Final Boss is normally the main protagonist's archenemy, as seen with Ganon, Bowser, [[metroidwiki:Mother_Brain|Mother Brain]], [https://donkeykong.fandom.com/wiki/King_K._Rool King K. Rool], [[starfox:Andross|Andross]], and so forth in various [[Nintendo]] franchises as well as other media, there are rare exceptions where the character to ultimately defeat is not a villain, but rather another good-alignment individual who simply serves as the game's final opponent before completing the main campaign (in which their defeats do not result in their demise); A popular example of this case are the [[pokemon:Pokémon_Champion|Pokémon Champions]] from the [[pokemon:Pokémon_(franchise)|''Pokémon'']] series. Occasionally these characters even help the protagonist stop threats before the final battle.
* Although the phrase "final boss" technically means the last major opponent in a video game's main storyline, it is also used as a slang term in the real-world or other media, where it refers to an abnormally powerful person or dangerous task that proves to be an extremely difficult obstacle to overcome compared to lesser problems and can easily result in injures or even death (e.g. an average man boxing against a professional champion).
