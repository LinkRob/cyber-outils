'''Madame Grusha''' is a character in {{TCT}}.{{Name Ref|TCT}} 

==Biography==
{{Term|TCT|Madame Grusha}} is a {{Term|TCT|Fortune Teller}} who resides in the {{Term|TCT|Town of Ruto|link}}. When {{Term|TCT|Princess Zelda|link}} arrives to the {{Term|TCT|Town of Ruto}}, she decides she could use some advice and enters the fortune-teller's parlor.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= 'I could use some advice,' Zelda says. She pushes aside the curtain and steps into the fortune-teller's parlor. |book= TCT |publisher= Archway |page= 116}}</ref> Inside, a wrinkled old woman known as {{Term|TCT|Madame Grusha}} greets her and mentions that {{Term|TCT|Princess Zelda}} has traveled far from {{Term|TCT|Midoro Palace|link}}, which surprises {{Term|TCT|Princess Zelda}} as to how the old woman knew.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= 'You have traveled far,' the fortune-teller says, taking one of Zelda's hands in her own. 'From Midoro Palace.' 'You know?' Zelda shivers. |book= TCT |publisher= Archway |page= 116}}</ref> {{Term|TCT|Madame Grusha|display=Grusha}} goes on to say that the {{Term|TCT|Princess Zelda|display=Princess}} is seeking three things and although {{Term|TCT|Madame Grusha|display=Grusha}} knows how to help {{Term|TCT|Princess Zelda}}, she cannot speak of it in their current location.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= 'Grusha can see far. You are seeking three things.' 'That's right!' Zelda cries. 'I can help you,' Grusha whispers. 'But I can't speak of it here. Come into my inner sanctum.' She starts to lead Zelda into the other room. |book= TCT |publisher= Archway |page= 116}}</ref> As such, she asks that {{Term|TCT|Princess Zelda}} follow her into her inner sanctum; however, before {{Term|TCT|Princess Zelda}} follows her into the other room, the {{Term|TCT|Princess Zelda|display=Princess}} begins to question whether she should trust the fortune-teller or not.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= 'What's in there?' Zelda thinks. 'Grusha seems kind--but should I trust her?' |book= TCT |publisher= Archway |page= 17}}</ref>

If Zelda follows {{Term|TCT|Madame Grusha}} into the other room, she will promptly get surrounded by {{Plural|TCT|Moblin|link}} who quickly attack her.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= But as soon as she passes through the curtain, she's surrounded by moblin! Short, scaly fingers grab Zelda's arms and legs. Sharp claws bite into her like iron nails. She's trapped. |book= TCT |publisher= Archway |page= 12}}</ref> Just then, {{Term|TCT|Madame Grusha|display=Grusha}} reveals herself to be a {{Term|TCT|Wizzrobe}} in disguise and the book ends with a Game Over as Zelda wishes she had been more careful.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= The old fortune-teller whirls about and her silk robe flies off. Instead of the face of a kindly old woman, Zelda looks into the distorted features of a wizzrobe! |book= TCT |publisher= Archway |page= 12}}</ref> If, however, Zelda chooses not to follow {{Term|TCT|Madame Grusha}}, Zelda will back away from the other room and take out her bow, and just then the fortune-teller commands her to not leave.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= She stops at the entrance to the other room and backs away, quickly unslinging her bow. The old woman turns and the friendly light is gone from her eyes. [...] 'I think I've had enough fortune telling for today.' Zelda backs toward the door. 'No!' commands the old woman. |book= TCT |publisher= Archway |page= 26}}</ref> {{Term|TCT|Madame Grusha|display=Grusha}}'s shape changes into a {{Term|TCT|Wizzrobe}}, who orders {{Plural|TCT|Moblin}} to attack the princess; however, Zelda easily defeats them with the use of arrows.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= Before Zelda's eyes, Madame Grusha changes shape. The silk robes fall away and the bent back straightens. Instead of the kindly face of an old woman, Zelda is now looking at the evil mask of a wizzrobe! |book= TCT |publisher= Archway |page= 26}}</ref> The {{Term|TCT|Wizzrobe}} then throws a casting spell at her, but the princess reflects the attack by grabbing a nearby mirror, defeating both the {{Term|TCT|Wizzrobe}} and the remaining {{Term|TCT|Moblin}}.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= 'He's going to cast a spell!' Zelda guesses. She grabs a mirror off the wall and holds it up as a shield just as the wizzrobe lowers his staff. There's a flash of red light. The spell hits the mirror--and bounces back across the room. The wizzrobe and the moblin freeze where they stand. The spell has turned them to stone! |book= TCT |publisher= Archway |page= 27}}</ref>
