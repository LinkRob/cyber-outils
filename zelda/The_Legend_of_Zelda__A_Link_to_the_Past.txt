'''''The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past''''' is the third main installment of {{TLoZ|Series}}, and the first and only title for the [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System]], released in November 21, 1991 in Japan and April 13, 1992 in the United States, and few months later in United Kingdom & Ireland. After the [[Controversy in The Legend of Zelda series|controversial]] side-scrolling and RPG-like gameplay of {{TAoL}}, {{ALttP|-}} was a return to the overhead view and gameplay style of {{TLoZ}}. One of the most influential titles of the series, this game introduced major new items to the franchise such as the [[Master Sword]], and the concept of two different worlds ([[Light World]] and [[Dark World]]), which was revisited to an extent in {{OoT}} with two separate time periods rather than the light and dark worlds. {{ALttP|-}} was [[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past & Four Swords|re-released]] in 2002, on the [[Game Boy Advance]], sharing a new multiplayer game called {{FS}}. The Japanese version of the game is known as '''''The Legend of Zelda: Triforce of the Gods'''''.

''[[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening]]'' on the [[Game Boy]] is the game's direct sequel, while ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link Between Worlds|A Link Between Worlds]]'' on the [[Nintendo 3DS]] is the indirect equivalent.
===The Imprisoning War===
{{Main|Imprisoning War}}[[File:LTTP Title Screen.png|left|251x251px|thumb|The title screen]]
One day, a band of evil thieves managed to open the gateway to the [[Sacred Realm|Golden Land]], where the mystical [[Triforce]] was hidden. Upon finding the sacred golden relic, the leader of the thieves, [[Ganondorf]], slew his followers and claimed it as his own.<ref>{{Cite Manual|quote=In a long running battle, the leader of the thieves fought his way past his followers in a lust for the Golden Power. After vanquishing his own followers, the leader stood triumphant over the Triforce and grasped it with his blood- stained hands.|game=ALttP|page=5}}</ref> Before long, dark power began to flow forth from the Sacred Realm. People were drawn into this darkness, and never heard from again. As a result, the [[Royal Family of Hyrule#A Link to the Past|King of Hyrule]] ordered the [[Sage|Seven Wise Men]] to seal the entrance to the Sacred Realm, now known as the Dark World. A great battle ensued—monsters poured into the [[Light World]] ([[Hyrule]]) from the sacred land and attacked the castle. The [[Knights of Hyrule]] defended the Sages during the great battle against evil, and, though most of them perished in the struggle, the sages were able to cast their seal, stopping the flow of darkness and trapping the evil demon king [[Ganon]] within.<ref>{{Cite Manual|quote=The Knights took the full brunt of the fierce attack, and although they fought courageously many a brave soul was lost that day, However, their lives were not lost in vain, for they bought precious time for the Seven Wise Men to magically seal Ganon in the Golden land.|game=ALttP|page=6}}</ref> This battle became known as the [[Imprisoning War]].

===The Wizard===
[[File:ALttP Agahnim Artwork.png|thumb|220x220px|Agahnim, the antagonistic wizard bent to conquer Hyrule]]
Once the conflicts against Ganon had ceased, [[Hyrule]] entered a time of peace which lasted for centuries, until one day, when unexplained catastrophes began to occur. Pestilence and drought ravaged the land. Thinking the recent disasters plaguing Hyrule were somehow linked to the sages' seal, the king investigated it, but found it to be intact. Desperate for answers, he offered rewards for anyone who could find the source of Hyrule's troubles.

In response, a stranger named [[Agahnim]] appeared as if from nowhere and stayed the catastrophes with mighty magic. As a reward, the king gave him a new position as chief adviser to the throne, and the common folk proclaimed him their hero. Once more, peace appeared to have returned to Hyrule. Yet, all was not well. Agahnim began to govern Hyrule in place of the king and abuse his political power as he saw fit. Rumors spread saying that Agahnim planned to kill the king and take the crown for himself (which he did in-game), and that strange magical experiments were taking place in the castle tower at night. He cast spells on the [[Soldier|soldiers]] and kidnapped the young [[maiden]]s descended from the seven Wise Men, using their powers in an attempt to break the seal placed on the Sacred Realm to free Ganon.<ref>{{Cite|Link, listen carefully. The wizard is magically controlling all the soldiers in the castle.|Princess Zelda|ALttP}}</ref><ref>{{Cite|A mysterious wizard known as Agahnim came to Hyrule to release the seal. He eliminated the good King of Hyrule... Through evil magic, he began to make descendants of the seven wise men vanish, one after another.|N/A|ALttP}}</ref> He manages to successfully send six of the seven into the Dark World, and the [[Princess Zelda|last]] is in desperate need of help.
===Link's Quest===
[[File:ALttP Zelda Telepathy Artwork.png|thumb|right|Link hears Princess Zelda's telepathic plea in his sleep|239x239px]]
The game starts with a boy in green named [[Link]] being awakened in the middle of a stormy night by a telepathic plea from [[Princess Zelda]], the princess of Hyrule and the seventh and final maiden herself, who tells him that she is being held prisoner in the dungeon of the [[Hyrule Castle|castle]] and needs help. Now wide awake, Link finds his [[Link's Uncle|uncle]] with a [[sword]] and [[shield]] in hand. He tells Link not to leave the house, then sets off for the castle. Link ignores his uncle's warning and follows him, only to arrive at the castle to find him gravely wounded. He gives Link his weapons and entrusts him with the fate of Princess Zelda. Link proceeds to rescue Zelda himself, and the two escape the castle through its sewer system to the [[sanctuary]] just north of the castle.

There, the [[priest]] tells Link that the only weapon powerful enough to defeat Agahnim was the sacred [[Master Sword]].<ref>{{Cite|I sense that a mighty evil force guides the wizard's actions and augments his magical power. The only weapon potent enough to defeat the wizard is the legendary Master Sword.|Loyal Sage|ALttP}}</ref> He then sends him to find the elder, [[Sahasrahla]], a descendant of the Seven Wise Men, who explains that not just anyone could wield the legendary blade, and that a hero must appear from the descendants of the [[Knights of Hyrule]]. To prove himself worthy of wielding the Blade of Evil's Bane, Link would need to acquire the three [[Pendants of Virtue]], scattered in three [[Dungeon|dungeons]].<ref>{{Cite|Link, I am surprised a young man like you is searching for the sword of evil's bane. Not just anyone can use that weapon. Legends say only the Hero who has won the three Pendants can wield the sword.|Sahasrahla|ALttP}}</ref>
[[File:ALttP Sacred Grove Artwork.png|left|thumb|257x257px|The Master Sword at its pedestal in the Lost Woods]]
After successfully obtaining the pendants and withdrawing the magical sword from its [[Pedestal of the Master Sword|pedestal]] in the [[Lost Woods]], Zelda suddenly calls out to Link yet again, this time warning that soldiers have invaded the sanctuary and is under attack. Link hurries off to the sanctuary only to find that he was a moment too late. The priest tells the young hero that the soldiers had already taken the princess off to Hyrule Castle before dying.<ref>{{Cite|Link! You are a second too late. I have failed... Zelda... The soldiers have abducted her. They have taken her to the castle. You must find her before the wizard works his magic.|Loyal Sage|ALttP}}</ref> Once again, Link hurries to her rescue, fighting enemies along the way, and once again arrives too late, as Agahnim was already in the process of sending Princess Zelda to the [[Dark World]] to finish his ordeal. Upon completing this ritual, at last banishing all seven maidens into the Dark World, little time was needed before the Sages' seal was completely broken.<ref>{{Cite|Only a short time remains until the gate at the castle linking the worlds opens completely.|Maiden|ALttP}}</ref> Link goes on to fight Agahnim himself in a [[Dead Man's Volley]] duel with the power of the Master Sword. Although the young hero manages to injure him, the dark wizard also suddenly draws Link into the Dark World, saying he is not truly defeated, yet.
[[File:Ganon Battle ALTTP.jpg|thumb|306x306px|Link vs. Ganon, inside the Pyramid of Power]]
There, Link is contacted telepathically by Sahasrahla, who tells him that the world he is standing in is actually what was once the Golden Land, but it was transformed by Ganon's evil wish by the Triforce to conquer the world to become the Dark World.<ref>{{Cite|Link, it is I, Sahasrahla. I am communicating to you across the void through telepathy... The place where you now stand was the Golden Land, but evil power turned it into the Dark World.|Sahasrahla|ALttP}}</ref><ref>{{Cite|Ganon's wish was to conquer the world. That wish changed the Golden Land to the Dark World.|Essence of the Triforce|ALttP}}</ref> He then commissions Link with the task of rescuing the 7 imprisoned maidens from the dungeons scattered across this twisted, parallel land.<ref>{{Cite|In order to save this half of the world, the Light World, you must win back the Golden Power. You must also rescue the seven maidens who Agahnim sent to the Dark World. As members of the blood-line of the seven wise men, they have power that will surely help you.|Sahasrahla|ALttP}}</ref> Although the Dark World is much fiercer than the Light World counterpart, upon eventually freeing them all through [[Crystal|crystals]], including Princess Zelda, Link travels to [[Ganon's Tower (A Link to the Past)|Ganon's Tower]], where the seven maidens use their combined power to dispel the powerful barrier sealing off the entrance. Link proceeds to traverse the tower and then defeat Agahnim a second time, this time killing him for good. Upon his defeat, the spirit of Ganon himself rises from the body, turns into a bat, and flies off into the [[Pyramid of Power]], where Link chases after it, and then faces off against the Dark Beast for the final battle. With the power of the [[Silver Arrow]]s and the Master Sword (or, optionally, the [[Master Sword Lv2|Tempered]]/[[Master Sword Lv3|Golden Sword]]), Link ultimately vanquishes Ganon. The young hero soon recovers the full Triforce, and by making a peaceful wish, reverts the effects of Ganon's evil reign over Hyrule, and ultimately restores the land to its former glory. Link himself, as the savior of Hyrule, would become known as the Hero of Legend.<ref name="E 19">{{Cite Book|quote= |book= E |publisher= |page= 19}}</ref>

==Gameplay==
===Commonplace Mechanics===
[[File:LttP bigcannonball.png|thumb|right|Screenshot of gameplay, at the [[Eastern Palace]]|258x258px]]
{{ALttP|-}} returns various gameplay elements from the [[The Legend of Zelda|original NES game]], among them the top-view perspective; [[Link]] can collect [[Rupee|Rupees]] once more and use items by assigning them to a button, as well as recovering his [[Life Gauge|life energy]] with hearts. Elements retained from {{TAoL}} is the ability to use [[Magic Meter|Magic]] to perform spells and to make special items functional, as well as a major interaction with non-playable characters to obtain vital information regarding the quest.

A returning mechanic from both games is the availability of dungeons, which must be conquered in order to archive success in Link's adventure. Each dungeon has a [[map]] and a [[compass]], the former shows the layout of the dungeon in particular, and the latter locates the boss's whereabouts; keys must be collected in order to open locked doors, puzzles must be solved to progress, and enemies must be defeated if they are interrupting the exploration. The dungeons are multi-leveled, ranging in number from [[Desert Palace|two]] to [[Ice Palace|ten]], and [[Big Key]]s are needed both to open major chests (containing important items that increase Link's inventory and help him progress through his adventure) and to open [[boss]] rooms.

Similar to the [[Bomb]]s in ''The Legend of Zelda'' and the [[Magic Meter]] in ''The Adventure of Link'', various items in {{ALttP|-}} can be upgraded either in capacity or by being turned into more advanced versions, including the [[Master Sword]]. This can be done through completing sidequests or entering certain special places. Optional weapons and items are hidden throughout [[Hyrule]] as well. 

===Light and Dark World===
[[File:Light and Dark World.png|thumb|The same location in both the Light World and the Dark World|320x320px]]
''A Link to the Past'' introduces a unique and very important mechanic: The [[Light World]]/[[Dark World]] dichotomy. Light World is simply normal Hyrule, while the Dark World is a twisted, desolate, more dangerous parallel version under [[Ganon]]'s evil reign that was once the [[Sacred Realm|Golden Land]]. The game has actually two separate maps of Hyrule, which are related to each other in several ways; for example, by switching from the Dark World to the Light World via the [[Magic Mirror]] while standing in an apparently empty dead end, Link can find in the latter world a secret cave or a passage that was otherwise inaccessible, leading to secret prizes. By doing the opposite (switching from the Light World to the Dark World), Link can gain access to new dungeons, which is important to the success of the quest. {{ALttP|-}} is praised for cleverly using this gameplay device, which in some ways has been reused in some subsequent ''Zelda'' games such as ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]''.

This installment is also the first title in [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' series]] to have a more developed storyline, which reflects for the first time the dungeon's different purposes. The first three house the sacred pendants that give [[Link]] access to the Master Sword, the fourth has to do with a failed attempt to rescue [[Princess Zelda]], the next seven dungeons house the imprisoned [[Maiden|maidens]], who (once freed) help Link break the seal of the final dungeon's entrance. This style of story progression was used in various later ''Zelda'' games, among them ''Ocarina of Time'' and {{TWW}}, although the latter added various twists and subversions to the concept (by adding the [[Triforce]] sub-quest, lacking a dungeon for the [[Nayru's Pearl|third pearl]], etc.).

==Game Information==
===Regional Differences===
{{Main|A Link to the Past Translations}}
The Japanese version of the game is titled {{Romanize|ゼルダの伝説 神々のトライフォース|Zeruda no Densetsu: Kamigami no Toraifōsu|The Legend of Zelda: Triforce of the Gods|2}}. [[Nintendo of America]] changed the original name to {{ALttP|-}} due to their censorship guidelines discouraged religious content (oddly, later games still use the word "Gods" such as ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker|The Wind Waker]]''<nowiki/>'s [[Tower of the Gods]]).{{Fact}} Nintendo of Europe used the American name. The Japanese name, however, was translated faithfully in the subsequent Chinese and Korean localizations.

Nintendo of America modified meanings in the game's text for the same reason. {{Term|ALttP|Agahnim|link}} is a priest in the original Japanese version,{{Fact|ALttP}} but a wizard in the international release. The {{Term|ALttP|Cursed Fairy|link}} was originally {{Romanize|女神|Megami|Goddess}}. The {{Romanize|教会|Kyōkai|Church|2}} became the {{Term|ALttP|Sanctuary|link}} and the {{Romanize|神父様|Shinpu-sama|Priest|2}} became the {{Term|ALttP|Loyal Sage|link}}. Nintendo of Europe's localizations follow the American one in naming the Fat Fairy but are inconsistent with respect to the {{Term|ALttP|Loyal Sage}} and the {{Term|ALttP|Sanctuary}}, sometimes favoring the Japanese meaning and other times the American.
[[File:ALttP Egyptian hieroglyphics.png|thumb|235x235px|The untranslated Mudora language seen in the Japanese version of the game]]
In the Japanese version, the [[Hylian (Language)|Hylian]] symbols translated by the {{Term|ALttP|Book of Mudora|link}} are Egyptian hieroglyphs. The symbols are smaller and slightly jumbled in the international version (and the text is not centered). In the [[Eastern Palace]], a symbol within a room contains what appears to be the Star of David in the Japanese release, which was changed to a more generic one internationally.<ref>{{Cite Web|retrieved= January 23, 2020|url= https://tcrf.net/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_A_Link_to_the_Past#Regional_Differences|title= The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past|site= The Cutting Room Floor}}</ref>

Nintendo of America censored the game's text but did not change its religious visuals. The {{Term|ALttP|Sanctuary}} retains its church pews and stained-glass windows. When Link makes a wish to enter {{Term|ALttP|Desert Palace|link}}, he looks skyward and appears to cross himself before joining his hands together in prayer.

Although never seen in gameplay, an artwork exists of Link knelling to a crucifix in prayer inside the Sanctuary, itself a clear reference to Christianity (earlier games did depict [[Shield|Shields]] with the Christian cross and the [[Book of Magic]] in the original game is known as the Bible in the Japanese version).

===Logo===
The North American and European versions of the game both use a new logo for the title screen and box art, designed by the Seattle-based Girvin design firm.<ref>{{Cite Web|quote= The Legend of Zelda logo design, which we created for Nintendo.|author= Tim Girvin|published= October 21, 2013|retrieved= March 1, 2019|url= https://www.girvin.com/blog/game-on-a-legacy-of-gaming-design/|title= Game On <nowiki>|</nowiki> A Legacy Of Gaming Design|site= Girving.com|type= }}</ref> This logo has been featured with slight variations in every subsequent {{TLoZ|-}} game in North American and Europe (identical in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening|Link's Awakening]]''), and in the Japanese versions starting with ''Ocarina of Time'', with the exception of {{BotW}} and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom|Tears of the Kingdom]]''.

===Graphics and Audio===
The game made use of the 16-bit [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System|Super NES]] capabilities to offer more realistic graphics over the 8-bit [[Nintendo Entertainment System|NES]] titles, not only in regards of the areas' textures and colors, but also to add new visual effects, such as the mist and the upper leaves' shadows in [[Lost Woods]], the thunderstorm in the [[Dark World]] equivalent of [[Death Mountain]], and so on.

The game's audio, which is greatly improved over previous games, retains the iconic [[The Legend of Zelda (Theme)|overworld tune]] that debuted in the original ''The Legend of'' ''Zelda'' game, although it is only heard while [[Link]] is in the [[Light World]]. Several famous music in the series that would be heard in some later titles, such as [[Kakariko Village]]'s theme, debuted in this game. [[Hyrule Castle]]'s background theme, [[Ganon]]'s theme. [[Princess Zelda]]'s theme, known as "[[Zelda's Lullaby]]" in {{OoT|-}}, also makes its first appearance. The Dark World's themes are unique in all regards (overworld, dungeons, etc.), meaning that the overall soundtrack of the game is far more diverse than that of the first two titles in the series.

===Setting===
Like its predecessors (and, for that matter, most ''Zelda'' games in general), {{ALttP|-}} is set in the fictional land of [[Hyrule]]; notably, the territory of the land is larger than in the first game, but smaller than in {{TAoL}}.

However, a [[Dark World]] counterpart - the [[Sacred Realm]] in a courrpted state by Ganon - is now included and explorable. In the Light World, familiar zones like [[Lost Woods]] and [[Death Mountain]] are present, while [[Kakariko Village]] and [[Lake Hylia]] are introduced here. In the Dark World, every location is different in name and presentation; for example, the Lost Woods is replaced by the [[Skeleton Forest]], Kakariko Village is the [[Village of Outcasts]], and the southwestern desert is the [[Swamp of Evil]]. The replacements also affect the dungeons which, instead of housing the [[Pendants of Virtue]], hold captive the Maidens who were supposed to break the seal protecting [[Ganon's Tower (A Link to the Past)|Ganon's Tower]].

===Timeline Placement===
According to the [[Zelda Timeline|chronology of ''The Legend of Zelda'' series]], {{ALttP|-}} is the first title placed in the "Downfall" split timeline. This timeline branch starts with [[Ganondorf]] (or Ganon) successfully [[Game Over|defeating Link]], the [[Hero of Time]], in {{OoT}} and obtaining the complete [[Triforce]]. Despite the tragic loss of their great Hero, the [[Sage|Six Sages]] and Princess Zelda seal the villain immediately within the corrupted [[Sacred Realm]] (similar to the [[Nintendo 64]] game's actual ending). Years later, greedy people enter the [[Dark World]] seeking the Triforce, turning into monsters and becoming part of Ganon's army. In the war that ensues, the [[Knights of Hyrule]] protect the Seven Wise Men from Ganon's minions, while they cast a seal to close off the entrance to the Dark World. Ages later, when Ganon attempts to escape from the Dark World in order to conquer Hyrule, the events of {{ALttP|-}} take place.

According to {{E}}, the game's immediate sequel is ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening|Link's Awakening,]]'' which is eventually followed by {{OoS}} and {{OoA}}. The English version of the {{E|-}} states that all four games feature the same Link, while the original Japanese lists the Link in the ''Oracle'' titles as a separate incarnation. Sometime after defeating Ganon at the end of this game, Link leaves Hyrule by boat when he is shipwrecked on [[Koholint Island]], leading to the events of {{LA|-}}.

During an interview around the release of ''Ocarina of Time'', series' creator [[Shigeru Miyamoto]] stated that {{ALttP|-}} occurred after {{TAoL}}.<ref>{{Cite Person|quote= Ocarina of Time is the first story, then the original Legend of Zelda, then Zelda II: The Adventure of Link, and finally A Link to the Past. It's not very clear where Link's Awakening fits in—it could be anytime after Ocarina of Time.|name=Shigeru Miyamoto |url=http://zeldalegends.net/index.php?n=interviews&id=1998-11-13-np-miya&m=html |title=Nintendo Power Vol. 116: Interview with Mr. Miyamoto}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Sales===
{{ALttP|-}} was commercially successful, selling 4.61 million copies worldwide, making it the 7th best-selling game in the series as well as the [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System]] itself.<ref>[https://nintendo.fandom.com/wiki/List_of_best-selling_Zelda_games List of best-selling ''Zelda'' games]</ref>

===Reviews===
{{ALttP|-}} received a near-perfect score of 39/40 from Japanese magazine Famitsu.<ref>[http://firsthour.net/editorial/the-famitsu-40-40-list-a-review The famitsu 40/40 - A Review]</ref>

Both the [[Game Boy Advance]] and the [[Wii#Virtual Console|Virtual Console]] versions of ''A Link to the Past'' gained IGN's and GameSpot's recommendation, with praises in matter of presentation, graphics, sound, gameplay and lasting appeal.<ref>[http://www.gamespot.com/gba/rpg/legendofzeldaalinkttp/review.html The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past Review for Gamespot (GBA)]</ref><ref>[http://www.gamespot.com/wii/rpg/legendofzeldaalinkttpsnes/review.html The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past Review for Gamespot (VC)]</ref><ref>{{Cite Web|quote= |author= Craig Harris|published= |retrieved= February 2, 2020|url= https://web.archive.org/web/20100316155808/http://gameboy.ign.com/articles/379/379258p2.html|title= The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past w/ the Four Swords Game Boy Advance Review at IGN|site= IGN.com|type= }}</ref><ref name="IGN">{{Cite Web|quote= |author= Lucas M. Thomas|published= January 22, 2007|retrieved= February 2, 2020|url= http://wii.ign.com/articles/757/757573p1.html|title= The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past Wii review at IGN|site= IGN.com|type= }}</ref> Criticism on the GBA version focused on the possibility that ''Zelda'' fans, at that time, would complain for the lack of [[The Legend of Zelda: The Minish Cap|a new game for the handheld console]], while the Virtual Console version was recommended only for those who didn't play the game in any of the past releases already.

{{ALttP|-}} is one of the highest-ranked games for the Super NES, and frequently rivals other highly praised ''Zelda'' games like {{OoT}} and {{TP}} for being the best in the series.<ref name="IGN"/> Nintendo Power ranked it second place in their list of best ''The Legend of Zelda'' games (behind ''Ocarina of Time''), citing the game's improvements over its NES predecessors.<ref>{{Cite Magazine|quote= |magazine= Nintendo Power|issue= 248|publisher= |date= December 2009|page= 73}}</ref> Video Game Canon ranks {{ALttP|-}} as one of the best games of all time.<ref>[https://www.videogamecanon.com/ Video Game Canon]</ref>

==Ports and Remakes==
[[File:Four Swords Box.png|thumb|right|255x255px|Box art for the Game Boy Advance version]]
===Game Boy Advance===
{{Main|The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past & Four Swords}}
{{ALttP|-}} was ported for the [[Game Boy Advance]] in 2002 as ''The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past & Four Swords'', introducing {{FS}} into the same cartridge, the first multiplayer title of the franchise. There were numerous alterations to the game, including a more accurate translation, additional shops and enemies, changing [[Link]]'s voice to that of the young [[Hero of Time]] from ''Ocarina of Time/[[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask|Majora's Mask]]'' (which had left minor confusion among fans today, as the incarnations between the SNES and N64 titles are different, and this would repeat for ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Minish Cap|The Minish Cap]]''), and the addition of an exclusive[[Palace of the Four Sword|dungeon]] and new [[Lumberjack's Riddles|quest]] and [[Hurricane Spin|attack]] unlocked only by playing through {{FS|-}}.

===Virtual Console===
The original SNES version was also ported to the [[Wii]]'s Virtual Console on January 22, 2007, and later onto the [[Wii U]] Virtual Console on January 30, 2014, and [[Nintendo 3DS]] Virtual Console on April 14, 2016. The re-release was virtually unchanged from the original. However, the full, bright screen flashing effect that was used on the title screen in the international release, when [[Agahnim]] or [[Vitreous]] attacks with lightning or when Link uses the [[Ether Medallion]], was toned down significantly, to prevent the dangerous effect of {{Wp|epilepsy}}.

===Satellaview===
{{Main|Satellaview}}
{{Lacking Sources}}
[[File:AST Mole's Treasure Cave.png|thumb|231x231px|The ''Ancient Stone Tablets'' Satellaview game, something which is not seen very often in the world]]
The game's engine and features were used in the later released broadcast service, the [[Satellaview]]. In 1997, ''[[BS The Legend of Zelda: Ancient Stone Tablets]]'' was released exclusively in Japan via the Satellaview. Although it is not canonical to the main series, it was presented as a sequel taking place six years after the events of ''A Link to the Past'' (like the real-world Japanese release time gap), seemingly like a unique [[Second Quest]]. Like ''[[BS The Legend of Zelda]]'', this particular ''Zelda'' game is extremely rare and not well-known overseas. 

{{ALttP|-}} itself was also released through the Satellaview; it only has a few minor changes from the original retail version, such as the save system. Unlike the other ''Zelda'' games broadcast over the Satellaview service, it could be downloaded and played at the player's convenience. Officially, the Satellaview port does not have a differentiating title, but fans often refer to the game as ''BS The Legend of Zelda: Triforce of the Gods'', despite this prefix being reserved for {{wp|Satellaview#SoundLink|SoundLink}}-compatible titles.

The games were released exclusively in Japan for a brief period of time. However, like ''BS The Legend of Zelda'', fans have managed to mostly translate ''BS The Legend of Zelda: Ancient Stone Tablets'' to English and compiled a fully playable version in a resurrection project for emulators today.

==Legacy==
Like its predecessors, {{ALttP|-}} introduced many elements that became mainstream for the ''Zelda'' franchise, including:

*{{ALttP|-}} is the first game that introduced a different [[Link]], as opposed to ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]'' and ''[[Zelda II: The Adventure of Link|The Adventure of Link]]'' prior which featured the same incarnation of the hero (according to [[The Legend of Zelda: Hyrule Historia|''Hyrule Historia'']] and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Encyclopedia|Zelda Encyclopedia]]'').<ref name="E 19"/>
*{{ALttP|-}} is also the first game in the series to include multiple, parallel overworlds.[[File:ALttP Sacred Grove Artwork.png|thumb|261x261px|Official artwork used for the Japanese box art, which shows the Master Sword in its [[Pedestal of the Master Sword|pedestal]] in the [[Lost Woods]]]]
*The iconic [[Master Sword]] made its debut in this game (prior games had its precursor the [[Magical Sword]]), which would become an important and often essential item in Link's quest in almost all later installments of the long-running franchise as well as his trademark weapon in other appearances. Few games like {{OoT}} and {{TP}} feature it with a similar function and purpose as in the Super NES game.
*Although they have technically appeared in the previous NES games, {{ALttP|-}} introduces the [[Sage]]s, who would play similar roles in {{OoT|-}} and {{ALBW}}.
*The [[Dungeon]]s became more thematically distinctive, reflecting different architectures and purposes. Dungeons based on forest, water and ice, for instance, are played for the first time here.
*This installment is the first where [[Princess Zelda]] plays a proper role in the series.
*This is the first game in the series to have [[Piece of Heart|Pieces of Heart]], leading to more [[Side Quest|sidequests]].[[File:ALttP English Logo 2.png|thumb|The famous franchise logo makes its debut in this game's international release]]
*{{ALttP|-}} was the first game in the series to use the now standardized {{TLoZ|-}} logo in Western releases. Japan would later use this logo by the release of {{OoT|-}}.
*[[Hyrule Castle]] makes its first on-screen appearance in the series.
*The game introduces [[Cucco]]s, as well as the infamous [[Cucco Revenge Squad]], where a flock of Cuccos will attack Link if he himself attacks one continuously.
*[[Kakariko Village]] and [[Lake Hylia]] made their first appearance in this title.
*This is the first ''Zelda'' game where a member of Link's family is shown, in this case his uncle. The only other games that do this are {{TWW}} and {{TMC}}.
*The notion of Link freeing Maidens from [[crystal]] prisons was reused with Princess Zelda in {{OoT|-}} and {{SS}}, and with [[Din (Oracle)|Din]] in {{OoS}}.
*This is the first game to give the [[Moblin]]s enemy their pig-like appearance, which would become a series staple after {{OoT|-}}.
*This is the first game where the [[Agahnim|seemingly main antagonist]] is actually just a minion of Ganon.
*Many [[Recurring Music in The Legend of Zelda Series|recurring musical themes]], such as the Fairy Fountain/file select theme, "[[Zelda's Lullaby]]", the Hyrule Castle theme, and Ganon's theme, were first introduced in this game.
*This is the first ''Zelda'' game where Link swings his sword horizontally and introduces the iconic [[Spin Attack]] technique.
*The name '[[Ganondorf]]' is introduced, heard only once by one of the Maidens, though Ganon's human form would not make his first appearance until ''Ocarina of Time''.
*This title marks the first appearance of the [[Sacred Realm]] and also remains the only time it is a playable [[overworld]] as the [[Dark World]].

==Trivia==
*Coincidentally, the Japanese version's release date, November 21st, is the same as the original release of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]'' as well as the [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System|Super Famicom]] (SNES), seven years before and one year later, respectively.
*With a total of thirteen [[Dungeon]]s, {{ALttP|-}} has more Dungeons than any other official ''Zelda'' game (not including [[Second Quest]]s). However, [[Hyrule Castle]] is technically two Dungeons, as it has different maps between rescuing [[Princess Zelda]] and encountering [[Agahnim]].[[File:ALttP The Return of the King Credits Scene.png|thumb|216x216px|Zelda with a gold crown at the end of the game]]
*Although easily missed by players, Princess Zelda wears a golden crown instead of her usual white one during the ending cutscene when she is shown with her revived father the [[King of Hyrule]] alongside the other six Maidens.
*''A Link to the Past'' is the first and so far only game in [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' series]] where the [[Master Sword]] cannot defeat [[Ganon]], as the [[Silver Arrow|Silver Arrows]] are required to destroy the Demon King like in ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]''. Despite this, it is still useful during the final battle.
*An [[Cameos in The Legend of Zelda|Easter Egg]] for the game, the portraits on the walls inside many houses in [[Kakariko Village]], which occasionally dispense [[Rupee|Rupees]] when pulled by Link, appears to be [[Mario]], the main protagonist of the [[mariowiki:Mario_(franchise)|''Mario'' franchise]] and [[Nintendo]]'s mascot. The theme that plays when [[Soldier|soldiers]] chase Link in the village is actually a remix of the ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_Bros._2|Super Mario Bros. 2]]'' boss theme.
**The seventh Dark World Dungeon, [[Turtle Rock]], contains [[Chain Chomp|Chain Chomps]], a dangerous enemy from the ''Mario'' series that was originally planned for ''The Legend of Zelda''.
*In ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past & Four Swords]]'', getting the Master Sword will completely restore Link's [[Life Gauge]] and [[Magic Meter]], similar to after defeating [[Bosses in A Link to the Past|bosses]].{{Verify}}
*The [[cave]] theme of this game is heard in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask|Majora's Mask]]'' played by [[Tijo]] of [[The Indigo-Go's]] [[Zora]] band, as an Easter Egg.[[File:ALttP ganon with other nintendo characters.jpg|thumb|235x235px|''A Link to the Past'' concept art of the game's main characters with several from ''Mario'']]
*An artwork of the game exists showing Link, Princess Zelda, Ganon, and few other key characters next to some ''Super Mario'' characters at a size scale. Interestingly, certain ''Zelda'' characters are depicted much differently than what is seen in-game. For example, Agahnim is known to be large, but he is only half the size of the rather enormous Ganon in the concept art. Link and Zelda, despite their sprites clearly show them as children, are both taller than [[Princess Peach]] and [[Luigi]]. It is unknown if the two were supposed to be at the same age as in ''[[Zelda II: The Adventure of Link|The Adventure of Link]]''.
*''A Link to the Past'' is the first of only two times in the series so far where the Master Sword can be upgraded to a [[Master Sword Lv2|more]] [[Master Sword Lv3|powerful]] form, alongside ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link Between Worlds|A Link Between Worlds]]'' (as opposed to some later games having the sacred blade powered down such as ''The Wind Waker'', ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild|Breath of the Wild]]'' or ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom|Tears of the Kingdom]]''). However, the first game to have a separate sword stronger than the Master Sword is ''Ocarina of Time'', with [[Giant's Knife]]/[[Biggoron's Sword]].[[File:ALttP Essence of the Triforce.png|thumb|225x225px|Link encountering the essence of the Triforce after Ganon's defeat]]
*There is a popular belief by longtime fans that Ganon in the [[Final Boss]] battle of ''A Link to the Past'' wields and fully possess the power of the completed [[Triforce]] (therefore [[Controversy in The Legend of Zelda series|considering the Hero of Legend to be the ultimate Link]]). However, on the contrary, as explained in-game, the instruction manual, and the [[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past — Nintendo Player's Guide|official strategy guide]], the main antagonist, although powerful, does '''not''' have it or any fragment thereof within him during this encounter. While Ganon indeed managed to obtain it prior to the game's events and made an evil wish to conquer the Golden Land and plans to do it again for the Light World, he was sealed into the Dark World by the Seven Wise Men and cannot escape with his own power, which itself explains why he needs the help from Agahnim. The misconception likely comes from the fact that the Triforce's symbol is visible on the floor during the final battle and all three scared triangles appear following Ganon's ultimate defeat, before Link makes his own wish with it as golden relic reforms itself. This would be technically similar to ''The Wind Waker'' when Ganondorf separates the three pieces from their respective hosts to reform the Triforce in order to make a wish (but fails to do so when [[Daphnes Nohansen Hyrule|King Daphnes]] intervened).
**On the other hand, and ironically, Ganon does possess the full Triforce during the final battle of the spin-off title ''[[Hyrule Warriors]]'', as the relic can be seen coming out of his body upon his ultimate defeat.
