The {{Term/Store|Hero's Spirit||Series, TP, TPHD}}<ref>[[The Legend of Zelda: Encyclopedia|Encyclopedia]], Dark Horse Books, pg. 276</ref>, also known as the '''Hero’s Shade''', is a character appearing in {{TP}}. He is a mysterious skeletal specter who can assume the form of the '''White [[Wolf]]''', with a personal goal to teach [[Link]] his own seven special sword techniques to make the young hero become a more skillful combatant.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= E |publisher= |page= 277}}</ref>{{Note|The White Wolf is also known as the '''Golden Wolf''' both in-game and in {{Guide|TP|Nintendo}}, as well as the '''Spirit Wolf''' in {{Guide|TP|Prima}}.<ref>{{Cite Guide|quote= |game= TP|publisher= Nintendo|page= 192}}</ref><ref>{{Cite Guide|quote= |game= TP|publisher= Prima|page= 447}}</ref> However, as these names contradict its name in {{E}}, they are not considered canon.}}

== Biography ==
==={{TP|-}}===
[[File:TP Golden Wolf Icon.png|left]]
Although appearing to be a mere ghost in extremely old knightly armor closely resembling a [[Stalfos]] or [[Darknut]], the Hero's Spirit is actually the '''[[Hero of Time]]''' - the '''[[Link]]''' from {{OoT}} and {{MM}}, in the form of such due to his death and is restless, filled with serious regrets from being unable to pass on the "lessons of his life to those who came after him" after he was sent back in time to his childhood by the [[Princess Zelda]] of his time at the end of the former [[Nintendo 64]] game. He also lamented the fact that he was not remembered as a legendary hero.<ref name=":0">The spirit of Link’s ancestor, the Hero of Time, teaches him his secrets. Ever since returning to the Child Era, the swordsman has lamented the fact that he was not remembered as a hero. This is the reason he passes down the proof of his courage and his secret techniques to the Link of this era, addressing him as “son.” — {{HH|-}}, Dark Horse Books, pg. 118</ref>
[[File:TP Hero's Spirit Sparring.jpg|thumb|253x253px|Link and the Hero's Shade touch swords as the former begins his training with the latter, as seen in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess HD|Twilight Princess HD]]'']]
In order to ease his regrets and finally pass on his long-lost masterful knowledge of the arts of the sword to keep their bloodline of the [[Knight of Hyrule|Knights of Hyrule]] remaining in honor, the Hero's Spirit teaches the highly advanced seven [[Hidden Skill|Hidden Skills]] to his descendant, Link, the [[Link#Twilight Princess|Hero of Twilight]] from {{TP|-}}.<ref>{{Cite|Although I accepted life as the hero, I could not convey the lessons of that life to those that came after. At last, I have eased my regrets.|Hero's Spirit|TP}}</ref>The seven techniques, in respective order, are the [[Ending Blow]], [[Parry|Shield Attack]] (Parry), [[Back Slice]], [[Helm Splitter]], [[Mortal Draw]], [[Jump Strike]], and [[Great Spin]]. In [[Hyrule]], he appears as the White Wolf, similar to the Hero of Twilight's [[Wolf Link|wolf form]], who can be summoned by finding a [[Howling Stone]], with the exception of the first time he is encountered, in which he appears to Link directly. Once a Howling Stone is found, the song has to be howled along with the Spirit. He then tells Link to take "sword in hand" and find him,<ref>{{Cite|Let teachings of old pass to you... Take sword in hand and find me.|Hero's Spirit|TP}}</ref> implying that Link should return to human form before searching for the Wolf. The White Wolf will then appear on Link's [[Map]] indicating his current location. When Link confronts the Wolf, it leaps through Link, transporting him into another world.<ref>{{Cite|In that case, return to your world and train yourself further. When you are prepared, come visit me again.|Hero's Spirit|TP}}</ref> The White Wolf then transforms into its true shape - a tall ghostly knight, the Hero's Spirit, who then teaches Link one of the seven Hidden Skills, which would ultimately prove handy in order to defeat powerful foes and [[Ganondorf]].
	
The Hero's Spirit is mostly transparent save for his armor, which appears to be filled with a ghostly pale blue apparition, rather than flesh, with a seemingly decaying body and mostly skeletal face. He carries a rusting sword and shield and wears armor, which is covered with leaves in sections. He carries a scabbard belted to his waist. Similarly to his White Wolf form, his left eye glows red while his right eye is missing.

== Trivia ==
[[File:SSB Link intro.png|thumb|271x271px|The Hero's Spirit being a decayed Stalfos/golden wolf-like ghost of the legendary [[Hero of Time]] has met with outrage among fans]]
*Although the Hero's Shade was not directly revealed as Link the Hero of Time in-game, ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Hyrule Historia|Hyrule Historia]]'' confirmed it as such in its release.<ref name=":0" />
**The Hero's Spirit being Link the Hero of Time is not without [[Controversy in The Legend of Zelda Series|controversy]], as the character is this rather iconic [[Link]] in a sad, horrific state.
**As he is indeed the Hero of Time iteration of the [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|franchise]]'s main protagonist, his fighting stance is similar to how did in the [[Nintendo 64|N64]] games {{OoT}} and {{MM}} via [[Targeting|Z-Targeting]], although the Hero's Spirit held his sword upright rather than sideways.
*In the Japanese version, the Hero's Spirit, alongside Golden Wolf, is also known as the '''Skeleton Warrior'''.
**In Spanish localization, he is called '''The Spirit of the Hero''', which is similar to how Link in all games possesses the spirit of the [[Hylia's Chosen Hero|Legendary Hero]], the very first incarnation of the main character, story-wise.
*The Hero's Shade missing his right eye as seen in both his forms implies a serious injury the Hero of Time once took in the past before his death.
**Oddly, his profile icon in ''My Nintendo Picross: Twilight Princess'' shows both the Shade's eyes open. This also applies to the [[The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess (Himekawa)|{{TP|-}} manga adaptation]] by [[Akira Himekawa]].
*The Hero's Spirit talking to Link in {{TP|-}} marks the first time in {{TLoZ|-}} series where two different incarnations of the main protagonist meet in a mainline game as well as the Link himself being able to fully speak conversations on his own rather than being mostly silent.
**The second case of Link meeting himself in the franchise is the hero from {{TP|-}}, in his [[Wolf Link|wolf form]], can be summoned to the Hero of the Wild incarnation in {{BotW}} via [[amiibo]] to assist his counterpart.
*In his White Wolf form, when he howls with the Link of {{TP|-}} in his own wolf form, he can be heard singing the "[[Prelude of Light]]", "[[Requiem of Spirit]]", and even "[[Song of Healing]]", three songs from the Hero of Time's adventures via the [[Ocarina of Time (Item)|Ocarina of Time]].
*Due to his [[Stalfos]]-like appearance, the Hero's Spirit stands at a much taller height than when the Hero of Time was alive, which is identical to the Link of {{TP|-}}.
*Despite any incarnation of the main protagonist within the franchise technically can die upon receiving a [[Game Over]], the Hero's Spirit - the Hero of Time, is the first and so far only time where a dead Link is canon.
*When the Hero's Spirit is teaching Link the [[Parry]] and [[Great Spin]], he can be seen shooting [[Sword Beam|energy balls]] with his sword and is even able to split himself into multiple images, respectively.
*Although only the first Hidden Skill, the [[Ending Blow]], is required to complete {{TP|-}}, if Link manages to learn all of the seven by the Shade, the Hero's Spirit will acknowledge the Hero of Twilight as his worthy successor (even calling him his "son") and tells him he has what it takes to be a true hero.
**Because the Ending Blow technique is the actual move used to ultimately defeat Ganondorf during the [[Final Boss]] battle, this would make the Hero of Time indirectly responsible for the archvillain's death, unlike what he experienced at the end of {{OoT|-}} (as [[Ganon]] survived the finishing blow via brutally stabbed in the mouth with the [[Master Sword]] in that game).
[[File:Hero's Spirit scabbard.png|thumb|270x270px|The hidden scabbard on the Hero's Spirit's side]]
*If one looks closely, when the Hero's Spirit teaches Link the seven Hidden Skills, a [[scabbard]] can be briefly seen on his hip. It appears to be a worn-down version of the Master Sword's, the sacred weapon he himself formerly used, though the actual sword the skeletal ghost uses is clearly not.
**Although Link himself is famously known to have his scabbard on his back for carrying his swords (and [[Shield]]) to make him be instantly recognized according to [[Nintendo]], this is not always realistic and the Shade is actually using such equipment correctly.
*As most of the Hidden Skills were never used by the Hero of Time in his main or spin-off appearances prior to {{TP|-}}, it is likely that after the events of {{MM|-}} but before becoming the Hero's Spirit, he managed to establish his already exceptional skills even further via training, according to [[The Legend of Zelda: Encyclopedia|''Zelda Encyclopedia'']].
*In the {{TP|-}} manga, the training of the Hero of Twilight by the Hero's Spirit is depicted much more intensely than seen in the game. He even assists Link in facing Ganondorf.
*The Hero's Shade being the Hero of Time filled with major regrets after his death and wanted to pass his fighting spirit to his descendant is a mirror to how [[Darmani III]] and [[Mikau]], themselves [[Goron]] and [[Zora]] heroes, respectively, both met a similar fate and Link himself gaining [[Goron Link|their]] [[Zora Link|powers]] as [[transformation]] [[Mask|masks]] in {{MM|-}}.
*Due to his appearance as a ghost in old swordsman armor with leaves on him, fans have widely speculated that the Hero of Time possibly died in a very intense war in [[Hyrule]] as a [[Knight of Hyrule]] or turned into an actual Stalfos while searching for [[Navi]] in the [[Lost Woods]].
**Ironically, according to ''Hyrule Historia'', he was possibly such a knight in his life.
