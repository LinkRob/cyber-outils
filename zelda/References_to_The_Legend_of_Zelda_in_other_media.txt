{{DISPLAYTITLE: References to {{TLoZ|-}} in other media}}

{{TLoZ|Series}} has been referenced numerous times in other media. Elements and characters from the series have appeared in a number of [[Nintendo]] games and other licensed, related media.

This listing is not to be confused with cameos, which are appearances with little to no effect on the game itself, and may not always be immediately obvious to the player. For instances of {{TLoZ|Series}} cameos in other media, see [[Cameos of The Legend of Zelda in other media|Cameos of {{TLoZ|-}} in other media]]. For unlicensed parodies and homages in other media, see [[The Legend of Zelda in Popular Culture|{{TLoZ|-}} in popular culture]].

==Nintendo franchises==
===''Animal Crossing'' franchise===
{{Main|Animal Crossing}}
[[File:ACNL Zelda Items.jpg|thumb|right|250px|''Zelda'' items in ''Animal Crossing: New Leaf'']]
Throughout the ''Animal Crossing'' series, a number of objects and characters from {{TLoZ|-}} series make cameos in the form of furniture, clothing and accessories. Some of the Villagers also tend to make references to {{TLoZ|-}} series, either from an [[Iconic Quotes from The Legend of Zelda|iconic quote]] or mentioning a {{TLoZ|-}} character by name.

===''Donkey Kong'' franchise===
[[File:Cranky'sVideoGameHeroes.png|frame|right|Cranky's Video Game Heroes in ''Donkey Kong Country 2'']]
====''{{Smw|Donkey Kong Country 3: Dixie Kong's Double Trouble}}''====
* If the main characters ask about the castle in Bazaar's Shop (available after buying the shell), and refuse to pay for the story, Bazaar will recount how a boy also asked about the castle and had paid 500 Rupees for it. This reference was removed in the Game Boy Advance remake.

====''{{Smw|Donkey Konga}}'' series====
* In the ''{{Smw|Donkey Konga}}'' series, {{TLoZ|-}} theme is featured in a number of games, including the original, {{Smw|Donkey Konga 2: Hit Song Parade|''Hit Song Parade''}}, and ''{{Smw|Donkey Konga 3}}''.
* In the original ''Donkey Konga'', the player can buy a {{TLoZ|-}}-themed bongo sound set.

===''Kirby'' franchises===
[[File:SwordKirbyscreen.png|thumb|right|200px|Sword Kirby firing a Sword Beam in ''Kirby Super Star'']]
* In several games, starting with ''{{Wk|Kirby Super Star}}'', Kirby's {{Wk|Sword}} ability is a reference to Link, most notably as the hat Kirby wears is based on Link's cap. The Sword Kirby uses also resembles the Master Sword. In some games, the ability grants Kirby with sword techniques based on those of Link's, such as the [[Sword Beam]], [[Spin Attack]], [[Down Thrust]], and the [[Skyward Strike]] (called "Sky Energy Strike" in ''{{Wk|Kirby: Triple Deluxe}}'').

====''Kirby Super Star''====
* In [[w:c:kirby:The Great Cave Offensive|The Great Cave Offensive]], one of the treasures to collect is the Triforce. According to Nintendo, the reason for this and the references to other Nintendo games are because the developers wanted to repay the favor of having [[Anti-Kirby|Kirby]] appear in {{LA}}.<ref>{{Cite Web|quote= In The Great Cave Offensive in the Kirby Super Star game, Kirby can find treasures based on classic games like Super Mario Bros., EarthBound, and The Legend of Zelda. Developers wanted to return the favor, since Kirby got to make an appearance in The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening!|author= Nintendo|published= 2012|retrieved= September 18, 2012|url= http://kirby.nintendo.com/sneakpeek/|title= Kirby's 20th Anniversary - Sneak Peek at Kirby secrets and tips|site= Nintendo.com}}</ref>

====''Kirby: Triple Deluxe''====
* A "Master Sword" {{Wk|Keychain}} can be collected. It is based on the {{Wk|Master|Master Copy Ability}} from ''{{Wk|Kirby & The Amazing Mirror}}'' and is likely a {{TLoZ|-}} reference in name only.

====''{{Smw|Mario & Luigi: Superstar Saga}}''====

Gadd and the items slightly renamed and redesigned. *In the [[Nintendo 3DS]] remake, ''{{Smw|Mario & Luigi: Superstar Saga + Bowser's Minions}}'', the Great Force is redesigned, now bearing a closer resemblance to the Triforce. ====''{{Smw|Super Paper Mario}}''====
* Throughout the game, Mario is guided by a hint-giving Pixl named Tippi. Her fairy-like appearance and role is likely a reference to Navi.
* When Mimi insists that Mario pays for a vase he broke, she will not accept coins, as she prefers to use octagonal gems called "{{Smw|Rubees}}", a reference to Rupees. The {{Smw|Flipside Token}} also strongly resembles a Rupee.

====''{{Smw|Super Mario Galaxy}}''====
* In the third mission of the {{Smw|Good Egg Galaxy}}, Mario fights giant creatures that resemble [[Deku Scrub]]s firing cannonballs at him.
* Game director Yoshiaki Koizumi said in an interview with Nintendo Power (issue #225) that the battle strategy against the boss {{Smw|Megaleg}} was inspired by what was originally going to be used for the final battle against [[Ganon]] in {{OoT|-}}, which was scrapped due to difficulty on camera control.

====''{{Smw|Super Mario Galaxy 2}}''====
* In one of the galaxies, a Gearmo will ask Mario for a Goomba. After the plumber gives him the Goomba, he will hold it up in the air exactly like Link does when he gets an item.

====''{{Smw|Super Mario 3D Land}}''====
* {{Smw|World 5-2 (Super Mario 3D Land)|World 5-2}} is heavily based on {{TLoZ|-}} series and was created to honor the game's 25th anniversary. The course is played predominantly from a top-down perspective and involves Mario moving from room to room in a similar manner to a traditional ''Zelda'' dungeon. A puzzle to obtain a Star Coin involves lighting torches using Fire Mario, which causes the ''Zelda'' "puzzle solved" jingle to play. The course also includes traps that mimic those that appear in ''Zelda'' dungeons, such as [[Guruguru Bar]]s and [[Rolling Spike Trap]]s.

====''{{Smw|Mario & Luigi: Dream Team}}''====
* One of the Luiginoid moves is called {{Smw|Luiginoid Generator#Hookshot|Hookshot}}, which is a reference to the [[Hookshot|item of the same name]]. This Luiginoid move works in a similar manner to the aforementioned item, causing multiple Luigis to stretch out in a chain to latch onto targets and pull Mario and the remaining Luiginoids to it.

====''{{Smw|Super Mario 3D World}}''====
*A secret area in {{Smw|Rainbow Run}} takes Mario to an area composed of black blocks that form an 8-bit Link sprite from {{TLoZ}}. Lighting them all up to reveal the sprite causes the "item found" refrain from {{OoT}} to play, and a Green Star to appear. After this, the music will change to a remix of part of the main theme of the ''Zelda'' series until Mario leaves.

====''Super Mario Maker 2''====
{{main|Super Mario Maker 2}}
[[File:SMM2 Link Transform.jpg|thumb|Link transformation from ''Super Mario Maker 2'']]
In the Ver. 2.0.0 update, promoted as ''A Legendary Update'',<ref>{{Cite Web|author= Nintendo|published= Dec. 2, 2019|retrieved= Nov. 25, 2021|url= https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x66vguSN8Rk|title= Super Mario Maker 2 - A Legendary Update - Nintendo Switch|site= YouTube}}</ref> Link was added as a playable character through the use of the [[Master Sword]] power-up.

In the game's Story Mode, several course creators are references to other Nintendo characters. Two of these are "Doctor Gizmo" and "Youthful Researcher", who appear to reference [[Robbie]] and [[Purah]].

====''{{Smw|Paper Mario: The Origami King}}''====
The fourth chapter in the game has Mario sailing across an ocean called {{Term|TWWHD|Great Sea|link|display=The Great Sea}} on a boat with similar mechanics to the {{Term|TWWHD|King of Red Lions|link}} from {{TWW|-}}. In order to unlock a dungeon called the Sea Tower, which looks similar to the {{Term|TWWHD|Tower of the Gods|link}} from the outside, Mario must travel to Diamond Island and complete the Trials of Power, Courage and Wisdom to collect the three sacred orbs, which is a reference to the {{Plural|TWWHD|Golden Goddess|link}} and the {{Plural|TWWHD|Goddess Pearl|link}}. When arriving on Diamond Island, {{Smw|Captain T. Ode}} remarks on the scenery by saying "It seems so familiar. I would swear I've heard a legend about this place. I won't try to force a name upon it."

A reference to {{BotW|-}} can be found earlier in the game, where a Toad in Autumn Mountain complains he doesn't have enough stamina to climb a wall, before commenting that at least it isn't raining.

In the Japanese version, the Whispering Woods area is known as {{Romanize|デクの山|Deku no yama|Deku Mountain|2}}. One of its inhabitants, an elderly tree stump named {{Smw|Ol' Grandsappy}}, is called {{Romanize|デクじい|Deku jī|Grandfather Deku|2}}. His face has similarities to the {{Term|OoT3D|The Great Deku Tree|link}} from {{OoT|-}}.

===''Splatoon'' series===
====''Splatoon 3''====
* A {{Ink|Power vs. Wisdom. vs Courage|Splatfest}} event was announced to promote the release of {{TotK}}. Players were prompted to make a choice between Power, Wisdom, or Courage, represented by silhouettes of Ganondorf, Zelda, and Link respectively, as well as the symbols of corresponding to their respective pieces of the [[Triforce]]. The event was announced on April 14, 2023, and it ran between May 5 to May 7. 
** The {{Ink|Tricolor Turf War}} {{Ink|Stage|stage}} was Scorch Gorge, which resembles the Gerudo Highlands. The stage had a custom layout unique to this specific Splatfest, being the first to utilize a three-point symmetrical layout. The layout of the stage layout resembled the [[Triforce]], with markings of it seen at the center of the stage.
** T-shirts were also announced to be releasing in celebration of the Splatfest, bearing the designs of the main Splatfest poster.
