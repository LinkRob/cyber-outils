'''''The Legend of Zelda: The Minish Cap''''' is the twelfth main installment of {{TLoZ|Series}}. It was released for the Game Boy Advance in 2004.

Like most other titles in the series, {{TMC|-}} features the fully explorable land of {{Term|TMC|Hyrule|link}}, although it can be viewed from the eyes of a human or the eyes of a {{Term|TMC|Minish|link}}, a race of tiny people and an [[Transformation|alternate form]] that {{Term|TMC|Link|link}} can transform into. The game is the first in the story surrounding the {{Term|TMC|Four Sword|link}} and features {{Term|TMC|Vaati|link}} as the game's main villain. However, unlike the multiplayer focus of the other games in the series, {{TMC|-}} retains the original form of exploration and dungeons as seen in {{ALttP}}, {{OoS}}, and {{OoA}}, as well as returning characters and game mechanics such as {{Term|TMC|Malon|link}} and the {{Term|TMC|Spin Attack|link}}. New features include fusing {{Plural|TMC|Kinstone|link}} and shrinking to the size of a {{Term|TMC|Minish}}.

==Backstory==
[[File:TMC Link Artwork 12.png|thumb|left]]
Evil spirits appeared in the land of {{Term|TMC|Hyrule|link}}, and just when it seemed that the world was to be cast into the darkest of shadows, the tiny {{Term|TMC|Minish|link|display=Picori}} descended from the skies and bestowed on a courageous human the golden {{Term|TMC|Light Force|link}} and the {{Term|TMC|Picori Blade|link}}. This {{Term|TMC|Hero of Men|link}} used his wisdom and courage, the golden light, and his sword to fight off the demons and restore peace to the world.

The people were so grateful to the {{Term|TMC|Minish|display=Picori}} that they held an annual celebration in their honor. Whoever won the sword competition held at the festival would earn the honor of touching the sacred blade. The tradition of the {{Term|TMC|Picori Festival|link}} continued, but over the generations, the {{Term|TMC|Minish|display=Picori}} themselves passed into legend. The legend of the {{Term|TMC|Minish|display=Picori}} tells of a mystic doorway that opens just once every hundred years. When the door is open, the {{Term|TMC|Minish|display=Picori}} can cross from their world into {{Term|TMC|Hyrule}}.

==Plot==
One year, in celebration of when the {{Term|TMC|Minish|display=Picori}} last came to {{Term|TMC|Hyrule}} 100 years ago to the day, the festival was unusually grand. {{Term|TMC|Hyrule Castle|link}} was hosting a tournament of sword-fighting skill that calls to mind the heroes of legend. That year’s champion was a mysterious man by the name of {{Term|TMC|Vaati|link}}. He won the tournament with an almost magical ease, and all of {{Term|TMC|Hyrule}} was abuzz with rumors about this shadowy figure. The young {{Term|TMC|Link|link}}, who attends the festival that year with his childhood friend, {{Term|TMC|Princess Zelda|link}}, is charged by his grandfather, {{Term|TMC|Smith|link}}, with delivering the sword that will be presented to the champion.

During the ceremony following the sword competition, {{Term|TMC|Vaati}} shatters the sacred {{Term|TMC|Picori Blade}} and opens the {{Term|TMC|Bound Chest|link}}, which, for an age, had been sealed by the {{Term|TMC|Picori Blade}}, trapping evil monsters within. {{Term|TMC|Vaati}} is after the {{Term|TMC|Light Force}}, the golden light that was once wielded by the {{Term|TMC|Hero of Men}} mentioned in the legends of the {{Term|TMC|Minish|display=Picori}}. He subdues the castle guards and turns {{Term|TMC|Princess Zelda}} to stone before departing. {{Term|TMC|Vaati}} was once a {{Term|TMC|Minish}}, an apprentice of the {{Term|TMC|Minish}} sage {{Term|TMC|Ezlo|link}}. {{Term|TMC|Ezlo}} had made a magical {{Term|TMC|Mage's Cap|link|display=Cap}} with the power to grant wishes, but {{Term|TMC|Vaati}} had stolen it and used it to become a powerful sorcerer. He had transformed {{Term|TMC|Ezlo}} into a hat himself, then proceeded to hunt down the {{Term|TMC|Light Force}}. {{Term|TMC|Link}} is asked by {{Term|TMC|King Daltus|link}} to seek out the {{Term|TMC|Minish|link|display=Picori}} and ask them to reforge the sacred blade, and he travels to the {{Term|TMC|Minish Woods|link}} with the help of his {{Term|TMC|Map|link}}.

There he meets {{Term|TMC|Ezlo}}, a curious creature whom he rescues from a group of {{Plural|TMC|Octorok|link}}. {{Term|TMC|Ezlo}} hops onto his head and offers to help him find the {{Term|TMC|Minish|display=Picori}}. He also helps {{Term|TMC|Link}} shrink down to a minuscule size — the size of the {{Term|TMC|Minish|display=Picori}} people, who call themselves the {{Term|TMC|Minish}}. {{Term|TMC|Link}} meets with {{Term|TMC|Gentari|link}}, the elder of the {{Term|TMC|Minish Village|link}}, who tells him that in order to reforge the sacred {{Term|TMC|Picori Blade}}, he will need the {{Term|TMC|Four Elements|link}}, the embodiment of the sacred powers of earth, fire, water, and wind. {{Term|TMC|Vaati}} had learned from the legend of the {{Term|TMC|Wind Tribe|link}} that when the {{Term|TMC|Four Elements}} were collected, the path to the {{Term|TMC|Light Force}} will open. Knowing that {{Term|TMC|Link}} is collecting them, {{Term|TMC|Vaati}} decides to wait until the task is complete.

Once {{Term|TMC|Link}} has gathered the {{Term|TMC|Four Elements}}, he brings the {{Term|TMC|Picori Blade}} — renamed the {{Term|TMC|White Sword|link}} by {{Term|TMC|Melari|link}}, a {{Term|TMC|Minish}} sword-smith — to the {{Term|TMC|Elemental Sanctuary|link}} hidden within the courtyard of {{Term|TMC|Hyrule Castle}}. There he infuses the {{Term|TMC|White Sword}} with the {{Term|TMC|Four Elements}}, fully restoring the sacred {{Term|TMC|Four Sword|link}} — a legendary blade with the power to split one warrior into four. {{Term|TMC|Link}} then gains access to a chamber with stained glass, where the location of the {{Term|TMC|Light Force}} is revealed to be within {{Term|TMC|Princess Zelda}}.

Meanwhile, {{Term|TMC|Vaati}} has brainwashed {{Term|TMC|King Daltus}} and shrouded {{Term|TMC|Hyrule Castle}} in darkness. He discovers from the stained glass of the chamber inside the {{Term|TMC|Elemental Sanctuary}} that the sacred {{Term|TMC|Light Force}} had actually been passed down through {{Term|TMC|Hyrule}}’s princesses, and so he prepares to extract it from {{Term|TMC|Princess Zelda}}. {{Term|TMC|Link}} rushes to her rescue, and, after vanquishing {{Term|TMC|Vaati}}, uses the {{Term|TMC|Four Sword}}’s evil-banishing power to restore {{Term|TMC|Princess Zelda}}.

With {{Term|TMC|Vaati}} defeated, the {{Term|TMC|Mage's Cap|link}} could be used to restore {{Term|TMC|Hyrule|link}}. {{Term|TMC|Princess Zelda}} puts on the cap and wishes with all her heart that {{Term|TMC|Vaati}}’s cruelty might be undone. With her righteous wish and the power of the {{Term|TMC|Light Force}} that still dwelt within her, many miracles happen as a result.

Now that the land of the humans had been saved, {{Term|TMC|Ezlo}} ventures back into the world of the {{Term|TMC|Minish}} through the {{Term|TMC|Minish Door|link}}, not to return until perhaps the next time, one hundred years later, when the doorway to their world would open once more.

==Game Information==
===Timeline Placement===
{{Main|Zelda Timeline}}
Telling the origin of {{Term|Series|Vaati}} and the {{Term|Series|Four Sword|link}}, {{TMC|-}} takes place many years before {{FS}}, making it the first story in the {{FS|-}} trilogy.

The official timeline released in {{HH}} places {{TMC|-}} and {{FS}} between {{SS}} and {{OoT}}. {{FSA}}, which concludes the {{FS|-}} trilogy, is an indirect sequel to {{TP}} and currently the third member (along with {{MM}} and {{TP}}) of the "Child Timeline".

===Ports and Remakes===
{{TMC|-}} was one of the ten [[Game Boy Advance]] games available on the [[Nintendo 3DS|3DS]] Virtual Console as part of the Ambassador Program.<ref>{{Cite Web|url=http://www.linkshideaway.com/2011/12/14-3ds-ambassador-games-revealed-minish-cap-confirmed/|title=3DS Ambassador Games Revealed - Minish Cap Confirmed|site=Link's Hideaway}}</ref> It was later released on the [[Wii U]]'s Virtual Console on June 5th, 2014 in North America.<ref>{{Cite Web|url=http://www.nintendolife.com/news/2014/05/the_legend_of_zelda_minish_cap_will_be_sizing_up_the_wii_u_eshop_soon/|title=The Legend Of Zelda: Minish Cap Will Be Sizing Up The Wii U eShop Soon|site=Nintendo Life}}</ref>

===Limited Editions===
[[File:GBA SP Minish Cap 1.png|thumb|180px|Outside]]
[[File:GBA SP Minish Cap 2.png|thumb|left|180px|Inside]]
A special limited edition ''Zelda''-themed [[Game Boy Advance SP]] bundle was also released. Limited to 25,000 copies, it featured a golden console with the [[Triforce]] outside and the Royal Crest inside, it also included a copy of {{TMC|-}} (without box). It was available for sale only in Europe and Australia.

A limited edition adventure set was also released. Limited to 300 copies, it included the limited edition Game Boy Advance SP, a copy of {{TMC|-}} (without box), a shirt, a knit cap and a certificate of authenticity.

==Trivia==
[[File:Minishdemo.jpg|100px|thumb|right|All three {{TMC|-}} cartridges]]
*{{TMC|-}} is the only Game Boy Advance game with two different "Not For Resale" cartridges which were used in store kiosks. One says "Not For Resale" and contains the full version of the game. The other says "Not For Resale Demo," where there are three playable areas to explore. Subsequent ''Zelda'' games like {{PH}} and {{ST}} also had demo carts with selectable stages.
*The logo for the game featured in pre-release material and some commercials featured the {{Term|Series|Master Sword|link}} rather than the {{Term|TMC|Four Sword}}. The {{Term|Series|Master Sword}} does not appear in the final version of the game.
*There have been three existing official logos for the game. These include one with {{Term|TMC|Ezlo}} next to the name, and one with the {{Term|Series|Master Sword}} next to the name. Some commercials incorporate some of these alternate logos.
*There are several {{smw|Super Mario (series)|''Super Mario''}} references in {{TMC|-}}. These include enemies such as {{Plural|TMC|Bob-omb|link}}, {{Plural|TMC|Spiked Beetle|link|display=Spinies}}, and {{Plural|TMC|Lakitu|link}}. In the {{Term|TMC|Palace of Winds|link}}, chain-link platforms with spinning gates such as those found in ''{{smw|Super Mario World}}'' and ''{{smw|Super Mario Sunshine}}'' appear. In {{Term|TMC|Mount Crenel|link}}, small beanstalks use the same sound effect from ''{{smw|Super Mario Bros.}}'' when growing.
*The game over music is an excerpt from the opening demo music in {{ALttP}}.
*{{TMC|-}} is the only main ''Zelda'' game to feature a sound test mode.
