The '''Munchoid''' is an enemy in [[Donkey Kong Bananza|''Donkey Kong Bananza'']].

== Description ==
Munchoids are green centipede-like enemies that have grey heads with sharp-toothed jaws and drill horns at the sides of the head. They have yellow eyes.

== Behaviour ==
Munchoids spawn from small structures when Donkey Kong gets close enough to their territory. A provoked Munchoid will pursue Donkey Kong, destroying terrain. It can only extend to a certain length before being dazed for a few seconds and then retracting back inside its structure. Munchoids can be stunned by hitting their heads with terrain chunks, and Donkey Kong can defeat Munchoids by destroying the terrain body, which can be done by hitting them with any of his attacks, or redirecting their bite attacks against their own body. Outside of Battle Challenges, Munchoids can infinitely respawn from their structures; inside Battle Challenges, any structures they spawn from are destroyed instantly.

== In ''Donkey Kong Bananza'' ==
Munchoids make their debut in the Forest Layer in [[Donkey Kong Bananza|''Donkey Kong Bananza'']]. They also appear in two of that layer's Battle Challenges, specifically the fourth and sixth ones, where four apiece are found.

== Behind the Scenes ==
Munchoids are based on centipedes, and their name comes from "munch" (referencing how they can eat terrain). The -oid suffix denotes similarity to something, and also referenes their creators, the [[VoidCo|Void Company]].

== Nomenclature ==
In Japanese, Munchoids are called "''Munchī'',"  referencing their munching attacks. Their name becomes ''meoncho'' (written 먼초) in Korean, from English "munch." Spanish refers to them as ''Masticóide'', from ''masticar'' ("to chew") and the suffix -''óide'' (which denotes similarity to something). In Portuguese, the enemy is called ''Mastigóide'', from ''mastigar'' ("to chew") and retaining the suffix -''óide''. Italian translates their name as ''Masticóide'' (from ''masticare'' ("to chew") and the suffix -''óide'' (which denotes likeness to something). In German, they are called ''Mampfolgoid'', from ''mampf'' ("chew"), ''folgen'' ("to follow"), and the -''oid'' suffix denoting similarity to something.
