A '''checkpoint''' is a game mechanic denoting an object in a [[level]], stage, or other area that marks the point where the player's character can continue from after losing a life. Checkpoints mostly appear in the platforming games, such as the {{series|Super Mario}}. Their designs vary between games, but they typically take the form of flags.

== History ==

=== In the [[Super Mario (series)|''Super Mario'']] series ===
Checkpoints are unmarked in the first ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]'', ''[[Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels]]'' (''Super Mario Bros. 2'' in Japan), ''[[Super Mario Land]]'', and the first ''[[Super Mario Galaxy]]''. In ''[[Super Mario Bros. 2]]'', [[Door|Doors]] serve as a type of checkpoint, as do [[Rocket|Rockets]]. ''[[Super Mario Bros. 3]]''<nowiki/>'s levels lack checkpoints due to their short length, and ''[[Super Mario 64]]'' and ''[[Super Mario Sunshine]]'' also lack checkpoints in most of their levels due to their sandbox structure.

''[[Super Mario World]]'' is the first game to have an object as a checkpoint, that being a gate that looks like the [[Goal Gate]], albeit shorter. Other objects have been used, like a bell in ''[[Super Mario Land 2: 6 Golden Coins]]'' and a [[Middle Ring]] in ''[[Super Mario World 2: Yoshi's Island]]''. Since the release of ''[[New Super Mario Bros. DS]]'', flags have been employed as the checkpoint marker.{{stub}}
