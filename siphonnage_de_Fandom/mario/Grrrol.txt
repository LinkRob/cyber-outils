'''Grrrols''' are small, spiky [[Thwomp]]s that appear in ''[[New Super Mario Bros. U]]'' and ''[[New Super Luigi U]]''. They appear in the levels such as [[Underground Grrrols]] and [[Grinding Stone Tower]]. They attack simply by rolling in a direction and changing directions when they hit a block or obstacle similar to a [[Spiked Ball]].

== Description ==
Grrrols have round bodies with red rolling eyes and a big grin with a single tooth missing. Their bodies have twelve grey spikes that subtly alternate between lighter and darker shades.

==Appearances==

=== In ''New Super Mario Bros. u'' ===
Grrrols make their debut appearance in ''[[New Super Mario Bros. U]]'' and ''[[New Super Luigi U]]'', where [[Mario (character)|Mario]] and company encounter them in a secret room in the [[Stoneslide Tower]] of the [[Layer-Cake Desert]] as well as the main obstacle in the [[Rock-Candy Mines]]' [[Grinding-Stone Tower]] in the former game and as the main obstacle of [[Underground Grrrols]] (again, in the [[Layer-Cake Desert]]), [[Soda Jungle]]'s [[Stone-Snake Tower]], and the [[Rock-Candy Mines]]' [[Smashing-Stone Tower]]. Not unlike [[Thwomp|Thwomps]], Grrrols are immune to most damage sources, with only the [[Super Star]] and hitting an [[Mystery Block|Item Block]] or [[Brick Block]] that the Grrrol is on being able to destroy them. Certain pipe-like walls can spawn Grrrols, being able to make infinite units. Grrrols can also defeat any enemies caught in their path. They also have a larger variation called [[Mega Grrrol]] which is around twice as large.

=== In the ''Mario Party'' franchise ===
Grrrols appear in several roles in the [[Mario Party (series)|''Mario Party'']] franchise.

==== ''Mario Party 10'' ====
Grrrols appear in ''[[Mario Party 10]]'', where they show up as an obstacle that must be avoided by the soloist's rivals in [[Hop, Drop, and Roll]]. They periodically spawn rom a device that hangs on the ceiling, with said device moving across the screen which changes where the Grrrols land. Upon landing, Grrrols move toward the more distant ledge from their spawn point. Any "rival" character who touches them will be eliminated as though they touched the [[Bullet Bill|Bullet Bills]].

==== ''Super Mario Party'' ====
A Grrrol returns in ''[[Super Mario Party]]'' as an obstacle in [[Get Over It]] where it may roll across the bridge that the characters are standing on. If the Grrrol hits character, that character dies and is eliminated. Grrrols also appear in [[Follow the Money]] with their larger cousins, where characters must swerve around them in their pursuit of [[Coin|Coins]]. Grrrols also appear in [[Rattle and Hmmm]] where their rolling causes the player's [[Nintendo Switch]] Joy-Con to rumble with a steady rumble. They can also appear in [[Absent Minded]] where they may show up on the images that [[Toadette]] shows on her monitor.

==== ''Super Mario Party Jamboree'' ====
Grrrols make a return in [[Super Mario Party Jamboree|''Super Mario Party Jamboree'']] where they return in the 1-versus-rivals minigame [[On-Again, Off-Again]] as the soloist's weapons to eliminate rivals, and their movements have to be redirected using an [[ON/OFF Switch]]. The Showdown minigame [[Jr.'s Jauntlet]] may have them appear in a possible event where players must avoid them to earn a point.

=== ''Mario Kart World'' ===
A tire-like variation of Grrrol can be found in [[Mario Kart World|''Mario Kart World'']]. They look the same as regular Grrrols, but have tire treads on their outer rims rather than spikes. Racers who drive into them will spin out, though this also destroys this new Grrrol sub-species. Regular Grrrols make cameo appearances on stickers.{{stub}}

== Behind The Scenes ==

=== Etymology ===
The name of the enemy comes from the growling or grinding sound ("grrr") and "roll."

== Nomenclature ==
In Japanese, Grrrols are referred to as ''Gorō'' (normally written 五郎, but formatted in Katakana (ゴロー) here, as a play on the Japanese rolling onomatopoeia (''gorogoro''; written ゴロゴロ)). Their Chinese name, ''Cì Gǔnlún'' (written 刺滾輪), literally means "spiked roller." Korean translates their name as ''Degureu'' (written 데구르), from ''gureuda'' ("to roll"; written 구르다). Spanish refers to them as ''Cróncher'', derived from English "cruncher." In Portuguese, the enemy is called ''Grrroda'', from ''roda'' ("wheel") and the English growling onomatopoeia ("grr"). It has its name translated to ''Grollo'' in Italian, from said growling onomatopoeia, ''trollo'' ("troll," or perhaps the English word itself), and ''rullo'' ("roller"). French refers to it as ''Mabroule'', from ''maboul'' ("crazy") and ''roule'' ("he rolls"). German translates the Grrrols' name to ''Grrrolle'', from the growling onomatopoeia and ''Rolle'' ("roller"). In Russian, their name is ''Shipokat'' (written Шипокат), from ''ship'' ("thorn"; written шип) and ''pokatit'' ("to roll"; written покатить). They go by ''Grɔɔlll'' in Thai (written กรอลลล), with the /l/ sound elongated instead of the /r/ sound.
