The '''Spikely''' is an enemy from ''[[Mario & Luigi: Brothership]]''. They commonly appear on Merrygo Island, but can also be found at the Great Lighthouse of Lushgreen Sea in smaller size.

== Description ==
Spikelies are indigo-colored ball-shaped monsters supported by three legs. The top of their body contains spikes that [[Spiny (Enemy type)|protect them from jumps]]. There are two size variations of the creature, with the larger version of them being called Big Spikely. Their design bears a resemblance to that of the [[Thorb]] from ''[[Mario & Luigi: Dream Team]]'', though it is unknown if the resemblance is intentional.

== Behaviour ==
In the field, a Spikely that notices [[Mario (character)|Mario]] and/or [[Luigi]] will charge at them. Spikelies are always seen in small form in the overworld and only appear in the field in the [[Lushgreen Sea]]'s [[Great Lighthouse Islands|Great Lighthouse Island]], though these small specimina can be brought in by a [[Snaptor]] on [[Desolatt Island]], [[Merrygo Island]], [[Lottacoins Island]], and [[Color-Full Sea#Islets|Minmin Islet]], and bigger ones can be encountered during combat against a [[Krode]].

Spikelies [[Spiny (Enemy type)|cannot be jumped on due to their spikes]], but as a tradeoff always take increased damage from regular (or combined) [[hammer]] attacks using the Blunt Hammer or if the player applies an Anti-Spike Specialist [[Battle Plug]].

Spikelies attack their prey with three methods. Their first attack has them jabbing their victim with spikes by jumping onto their prey, or run in a circle around their prey before charging into their target. Mario and Luigi can block these two moves with their hammers. In the third attack, they can spin and trace a circular path, not unlike an [[Oho Jee]]. This attack must be dodged by jumping. How easily Spikelies can be avoided during this attack depends on their size, with smaller specimina being easier to dodge than larger ones.

[[Capnap|Capnaps]] can be fought alongside small Spikelies at the Great Lighthouse of Lushgreen Sea, with the larger Spikelies on Merrygo Island sometimes being encountered alongside a [[Krode]] or [[Snaptor]].

Additionally, the [[Gobblick]] can spit out Spikelies, which must be jumped over.

== Statistics ==
Spikelies have differing stats based on their size. Smaller Spikelies vary in their stats, with the ones at the [[Great Lighthouse Islands|Great Lighthouse Island]] of the [[Lushgreen Sea]] being level 10 monsters with 52 hit points, an attack power of 36, a defense of 33 and a speed of 33, giving 25 experience and dropping 13 coins along with possibly a Refreshing Herb (3%) or Syrup (1%) when slain, the ones on [[Merrygo Island]] being Level 13 with 72 hit points, an attack power of 46, a defense of 44, and a speed of 41 giving 40 experience and 19 coins when slain, and the ones on [[Lottacoins Island]] being Level 15 with 86 hit points, an attack power of 53, a defense of 53, and a speed of 47, giving 57 experience and 23 coins when slain. The larger Merrygo Island specimina are Level 15 with 129 hit points, an attack power of 54, a defense of 61, and a speed of 63, giving 86 experience and 35 coins when slain along with the possibility of a [[Syrup Jar]] (5%), a [[1-Up Mushroom]] (2%), or a [[Crafting Materials (Mario & Luigi: Brothership)|Merrygo Bolt]] (23%).

== Variants ==

=== Glohm Spikely ===
In the Glohm Gobblick sidequest, the Spikelies in the Lushgreen Sea's Great Lighthouse will contract the bond-destroying virus-esque substance [[Glohm]]. This enemy is of Level 34 with 302 hit points, giving 334 experience and 65 coins when destroyed.

After [[Reclusa]] wakes up, all Spikelies contract Glohm, regardless of size and location. Smaller Glohm Spikelies have their level raised to 39, with a maximum health of 404, giving 794 experience and 120 coins when destroyed. The bigger ones are of Level 40 with 780 hit points, giving 1271 Experience and 189 Coins when destroyed.

== Behind the Scenes ==

=== Etymology ===
Spikelies take their name from "spiky," due to them having spikes at the top of their body.

== Nomenclature ==
In Japanese, Spikelies are known as ''Togebon'', from ''toge'' ("spike"; written 刺 in the Kanji script, とげ in the Hiragana script, and トゲ in the Katakana script) with ''bon'' (an affectionate term for boys, written 坊 in the Kanji script, ぼん in the Hiragana script, and ボン in the Katakana script) added to the end. Their Chinese name, ''Jiāncì'' ''Bǎo'', comes from ''jiāncì'' ("spike," written 尖刺) and ''bǎo'' (an affectionate term, written 宝). Their Spanish name, ''Puelectrín'', comes from ''pua'' ("spike") merged with ''eléctrico'' ("electric"), with C changed to N as a reference to ''puercoespín'' ("porcupine"). Portuguese refers to them as ''Espinhuço'', from ''espinha'' (an Old Galician-Portuguese term meaning "spike" which was replaced in that niche by ''espinho'', which could be another ancestor) with the suffix -''uço'' ("a lot"). Their Italian name, ''Spinotto'', comes from ''spina'' ("spike") and the diminutive suffix -''otto'', synonymous with "electric plug." ''Bouléspine'' serves as their French name, from ''boule'' ("ball") and ''espine'' ("spike"), not unlike how [[Thorb|Thorbs]] got their French name, ''Boulapik'' from ''boule'' ("ball") and ''pique'' ("lance"). Spikelies retain their English name in the Dutch dub for unknown reasons. In Korean, they have the name ''Gasidoryeong'', which comes from ''gasi'' ("spike"; written 가시) and ''doryeong'' (an affectionate term for a young, unmarried man; written 도령).
