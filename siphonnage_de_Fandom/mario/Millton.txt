The '''Millton''' is an enemy from ''[[Mario & Luigi: Brothership]]''. They are encountered on [[Offandon Island]].

==Description==
Milltons are brownish-gold in colour and have toothy frowns. Their head has a face at the front with a permanent toothy frown and expressive eyes, a spike on top, horns on the side, and a socket to attach to the body. Their body is composed of three to five segments with a red symbol on one side and a green symbol on the other.

==Behaviour==
In the field, Milltons cannot move from their spot, but intruding upon their territory will cause them to spit rocks at the intruder.

=== Combat===
In battle, [[Spiny (Enemy type)|Milltons cannot be jumped on safely due to the spike on their head]]. How the Milltons' segments line up determines what attack will be used by them, giving the creatures three attacks. The segments determine the target by their colour: red for Mario and green for Luigi.

*If it fails to line its segments up properly, the Millton sends each of its segments at the Bros. one by one before it calls them back. The segments get sent out starting from the bottom.
*If their segments line up properly but do not match in colour, Milltons spit rocks at the brothers one by one. The target is determined by the segments, starting with the top segment and going down. Mario and Luigi have to use their hammers to repel these rocks.
*If the Milltons line up the same colored symbol on their segments, a giant prize ball hangs above the target, who must repel a giant spiked ball that pops out with a hammer strike. Sometimes, [[Mushroom|Mushrooms]] appear instead.

==== Statistics ====
Milltons have an attack power of 118 and a defense of 125. To calculate their hit points, use the following formula, where hp is their health maximum and s is equal to the number of segments making up their body, not counting the head:

<math>hp = 27 * (s)^2 - 79 * (s) + 432</math>

The number of segments making up the Millton's body also impacts their speed, which is calculated with this formula:

<math>speed = 5 * (s)^2 - 63 * (s) + 264</math>

Milltons give 590 experience upon defeat, with their loot drop consisting of 78 coins, with the possibility of a 1-Up Mushroom (7% chance), the Gamble Hammer (1 in 50 chance), and/or an Offandon Brick (1 in 4 chance).

==Variant: Glohm Millton==
Later in the game, Milltons may become infected with [[Glohm]]. In this state, the creatures appear red with glowing crimson eyes and shadowy particle effects, and their behaviour is more aggressive with faster attacks and higher stats. A Glohm Millton also has the ability to increase its stats each turn. It is of Level 41, and starts with an attack power of 192 and a defense of 266, as well as the following values for health and speed:

<math>hp = 64.5 * (s)^2 - 193.55 * (s) + 1032</math>

<math>speed = 7.5 * (s)^2 - 99.5 * (s) + 485</math>

When destroyed, a Glohm Millton gives 1808 experience and 263 coins along with a 1-Up Mushroom (10%), a Gamble Hammer DX (3%), an Offandon Brick (25%), a Glohm Crystal (17%), a Glohm Fragment (15%), or some Glohm Dust (12%).

==Behind the Scenes==
Milltons take their design from totem poles, with their rotating segments being similar to slot machines. Their name seems to come from the given name "Milton," with the duplicated L referencing millstones.

== Nomenclature==
In Japanese, Milltons are known as ''Usurotto'' (written ウスロット), from ''usu'' ("mortar"; written 臼) and "slot" transliterated into Japanese, resulting in ''surotto'' (written スロット). Their Chinese name, ''Zhuànlún Jiù'' (written 转轮臼), means "rotating mortar." In Korean, they are known as ''Rulles Beullok Taweo'' (written 룰렛 블록 타워), a botched transliteration of "Roulette Block Tower." (A proper transliteration would have been ''Rullet Beullok Taweo'' (written 룰렡블롴타워).) Spanish translates their name as ''Morterotem'', from ''mortero'' ("mortar") and ''totem'' ("totem"), with possibly ''rotar'' ("to rotate," alluding to them spinning their segments) mixed in. Portuguese calls them ''Pilãotra'', from ''pilão'' ("pestle") and ''pilantra'' ("crook"). In Italian, their name is ''Maciniere'', from ''màcina'' ("millstone") and ''-iere'' (a performing suffix). They are known as ''Meulard'' in French, from ''meule'' ("millstone") and the familiar suffix -''ard''. Milltons are called ''Drehstrotem'' in German, from ''drehstrom'' ("rotary current") and the word "totem."
