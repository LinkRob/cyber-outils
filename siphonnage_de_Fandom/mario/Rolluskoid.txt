The '''Rolluskoid''' is an enemy in [[Donkey Kong Bananza|''Donkey Kong Bananza'']]. They first appear in the Freezer Layer.

== Description ==
Rolluskoids resemble ammonites and nautili in spherical shells. The material in the shell matches where they are found.

== Behaviour ==
Rolluskoids attack by rolling at their prey to steamroll it; [[Pauline]] will warn [[Donkey Kong (character)|Donkey Kong]] of the steamrolling Rolluskoid if she is present. If Donkey Kong and Pauline dodge, the Rolluskoid will become dizzy, rendering them vulnerable to attack from the heroic duo. A Rolluskoid that takes enough damage will lose its shell, at which point it can be destroyed and will clear out most types of terrain it travels across.

== Battle Challenges ==
Rolluskoids appear in the first and fourth Battle Challenges (three and four specimina apiece) of the Freezer Layer and the fifth Battle Challenge (five) of the Tempest Layer.

== Behind the Scenes ==
The Rolluskoid's design is based on an ammonite, with the name coming from "roll" (referencing how it rolls around to move and attempts to steamroll its prey to attack) and "mollusk" (the phylum that ammonites belong to, referencing the enemy's design).

== Nomenclature ==
In Japanese, Rolluskoids are known as ''nōchiro'' (written ノーチロ), a portmanteau of "nautilus" (though they resemble ammonites) and the Japanese onomatopoeia ''gorogoro'' (written ごろごろ in Hiragana). In Spanish, they are known as ''Giroluscoide'', a portmanteau of ''girar'' ("to spin") and ''molusco'' ("mollusk"), complete with the suffix -''oide'' (which denotes similarity to something). In Portuguese, the enemies have their name translated as ''Roluscoide'', from ''rolar'' ("to roll," referencing their rolling attack), and ''molusco'' ("mollusk"), complete with the suffix ''-oide'' (which, like Spanish ''-oide'', denotes similarity to something.) Italian refers to them as ''Rotuloide'', from ''ruota'' ("wheel," referring to how the enemy rolls like one) and either ''mítilo'' (referring to animals of the family ''Mytilidae'') or ''naútilo'' ("nautilus"), complete with the suffix ''-oide'' which denotes similarity to something. In German, the name is untranslated for some reason.
