'''Bullet Bills''' are recurring enemies in the ''[[Mario (franchise)|Mario]]'' franchise. They first appear in ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]'' While Bullet Bill have been primarily portrayed as obstacles, some games depict them as individual characters.

==Profile==
===Physical description===
Bullet Bills are black anthropomorphic {{wp|missile}}-like creatures. They have eyes with black pupils/irises and engines at their backsides, thus allowing them to travel in the air. In most games, they can be defeated by simply [[jump]]ing on them, though other games make them more resilient. Like other enemies, Bullet Bills have had varying appearances. In the earlier ''Super Mario'' games, Bullet Bills had a slightly brown hue, though this was due to color limitations. Bullet Bills were inspired by shark mouth {{wp|nose art}} on fighter planes<ref>[https://www.ndw.jp/mario-220913-2/ "Shigeru Miyamoto on the origin of the names of Mario and his friends"]. ''[[Nintendo DREAM]]''. February 2010 volume. Retrieved April 22, 2023.</ref> and older artwork shows them with both arms and mouths, which are depicted variously in early sprites and models. To date, they appear with both arms and a visible mouth in the Game & Watch ''Super Mario Bros.'', the DIC Entertainment cartoons, ''Super Mario Bros. Print World'', the PC release of ''Mario's Time Machine'', ''Yoshi's Safari'', ''Super Mario RPG: Legend of the Seven Stars'', and ''Mario & Luigi: Superstar Saga''. In ''Super Mario Kart'', ''Super Mario 64'', and ''Yoshi's Story'', they lack arms but have an open mouth with sharp teeth and an angry expression, resembling [[Banzai Bill]]s. In ''Super Mario Land 2: 6 Golden Coins'', ''Super Mario World 2: Yoshi's Island'', and ''Yoshi's Island DS'', Bullet Bills display a grin instead, as well as a green color scheme in the second of these. In ''Super Mario Sunshine'', they have a slimmer shape in the vein of [[Torpedo Ted]]s, in addition to having a smile, fuller eyes, and rocket-like fins in the back.

===Powers and abilities===
In most games, Bullet Bills simply fly around without targeting a victim, usually being launched from [[Bill Blaster]]s horizontally, coupled with a firing sound effect to indicate their presence. Even if Bullet Bills approach from off-screen without the presence of a Bill Blaster, a firing sound effect is still heard.

The ''Super Mario Galaxy'' games give Bullet Bills the ability to home in on their opponents, while their eyes turn red. In the ''Super Smash Bros.'' games, Bullet Bills can also home in on their opponents, though only a few of them do this. In some cases, they can spin while flying. In ''Super Mario Odyssey'', Bullet Bills appear as [[Capture|capturable]] enemies; this game reveals that, Bullet Bills can fly at three different speeds, and can self-destruct after a certain period of time. In the ''Paper Mario'' games, Bullet Bills use a kamikaze attack, thus resulting in explosions. In ''Puzzles and Dragons: Super Mario Bros. Edition'', Bullet Bills are associated with the Dark attribute, boast higher ATK, and attack on every turn. The ''Mario & Sonic'' games depict Bullet Bill (and more specifically, [[Big Bullet Bill]]), as a Speed rival. This is supported by the ''Mario Kart'' series, which temporarily turns the user into a Bullet Bill, a high-speed item with automatic turning capabilities. A billboard advertisement in ''Mario Kart Wii''{{'}}s [[Moonview Highway]] course addresses Bullet Bill as the "King of Speed".

Due to their metallic shell, Bullet Bills are invulnerable to [[fireball]]s.

In the ''Nintendo Comics System'' and ''Mario Party Advance'', Bullet Bills are shown to be sentient and possess the ability to speak.

==Main appearances==
===''Super Mario Bros.''===
[[File:SMB Bullet Bill sprite.png|frame|left]]
[[File:SMB Bullet Bill Artwork.jpg|thumb|Artwork of a Bullet Bill from ''Super Mario Bros.'']]
[[Image:Bullet Bill SMB Booklet.png|thumb|left|A Bullet Bill's image in the ''Super Mario Bros.'' instruction booklet]]
Bullet Bills first appear in [[World 5-1 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 5-1]] of ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]'' as the last enemy introduced in the game. Bullet Bills travel endlessly in a straightforward horizontal direction. They are usually shot from a [[Bill Blaster|Turtle Cannon]], though are sometimes shot from off-screen, and fly in a straight line, through walls. A Bullet Bill can typically be defeated from a [[stomp]]. In rare cases, when a [[Star|Starman]] is available nearby, [[Mario]] or [[Luigi]] can defeat a Bullet Bill by touching it while [[Invincible Mario|invincible]]. A kicked [[Koopa Shell]] or [[Buzzy Shell]] can defeat Bullet Bills that come from off-screen but harmlessly passes through ones shot from the Turtle Cannons. They are invulnerable to [[fireball]]s from [[Fire Mario|Fiery Mario]]. Stomping on a Bullet Bill is always worth 200 [[point]]s as the "point combo" rule for stomping from one Goomba, Koopa Troopa, or Buzzy Beetle onto another does not apply to Bullet Bills.

If Mario touches the [[Goal Pole|flagpole]] while a Bullet Bill is still onscreen, the Bullet Bill immediately disappears; this would later inspire how in later games, certain enemies near the end of a level immediately disappear and turn into [[coin]]s if the level is completed while they are still onscreen.

Bullet Bills and [[Piranha Plant]]s are the only two enemies to not have their sprites flipped upside-down upon defeat, the former remaining right side-up and the latter simply disappearing.

Bullet Bills share the same color palette as Goombas, which is mainly evident from how Bullet Bills appear gray in [[World 6-3 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 6-3]]. This color distinction is not retained in the ''[[Super Mario All-Stars]]'' remake.

===''Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels''===
[[Image:Bullet Bill Hard.jpg|thumb|right|A Bullet Bill]]
Bullet Bills retain the same behavior and sprites as in ''Super Mario Bros.'' Because of the game's increased difficulty, Bullet Bills often have a use for going across wide [[pit]]s.

In [[World 9-1 (Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels)|World 9-1]], there are Turtle Cannons that do not fire out any Bullet Bills. The level is uniquely a swimming level with overworld graphics. Bullet Bills also share a palette with [[Blooper]]s, so had they been launched by Turtle Cannons, Bullet Bills would have possibly been depicted as black with gray details. 

In the ''[[Super Mario All-Stars]]'' remake, there is a Bullet Bill disguised as a [[Fake Bowser]] in [[World C-4]]. Bullet Bills still share a palette with Goombas, so Bullet Bills have a teal color in [[World A-2]]; the teal palette for Bullet Bills originally went unused in ''Super Mario Bros.'' because the game does not feature them in any underground level.
{{br}}

===''Super Mario Bros. 3 / Super Mario Advance 4: Super Mario Bros. 3''===
[[Image:Bullet Bill 3.gif|thumb|left]]
In ''[[Super Mario Bros. 3]]'', Bullet Bills are commonly encountered on [[airship]]s and later levels. If Mario or [[Luigi]] stand right next to a Bill Blaster, a Bullet Bill does not fire out of it. Just as before, Bullet Bills can be defeated by being jumped on or using a [[Starman]], but a Bullet Bill can alternatively be defeated by either spinning [[Raccoon Mario]] or [[Tanooki Mario (power-up)|Tanooki Mario]]'s tail onto it, by throwing a [[hammer]] at it as [[Hammer Mario]], or by hitting it with a Koopa Shell. In certain levels, a Bullet Bill can also be defeated by an exploding [[Bob-omb]]. Bullet Bills reward a slightly lower number of 100 points upon being defeated.

A variant of Bullet Bills was introduced, [[Missile Bill]], which turns around and flies back towards Mario or Luigi if it misses him the first time.

Reaching the [[Goal (Super Mario Bros. 3)|goal]] defeats any enemy onscreen, similar to ''Super Mario Bros.'', in which touching a flagpole causes a Bullet Bill to disappear, but later 2D ''Super Mario'' installments retain the feature of defeating all enemies onscreen when reaching the goal at the end.

In the remake ''[[Super Mario Advance 4: Super Mario Bros. 3]]'', diagonally-fired Bullet Bills that would later be featured in ''Super Mario World'' appear in the [[World-e]] level [[Wild Ride in the Sky]].

===''Super Mario Land''===
{{main|Bullet Biff}}
Although the Bullet Bill does not appear in ''[[Super Mario Land]]'', a variant named Bullet Biff does appear.

===''Super Mario World'' / ''Super Mario World: Super Mario Advance 2''===
[[File:Bullet Bill SMW sprite.png|frame|left]]
In ''[[Super Mario World]]'', Bullet Bills retain their behavior of traveling in a single direction upon being launched, but the game also introduces Bullet Bills that are fired diagonally from four corners and vertically shot Bullet Bills.

Bullet Bills are first encountered in [[Vanilla Dome 4]] where they are fired off-screen. In [[Cheese Bridge Area]], they are encountered in a zone led from a Warp Pipe. Bullet Bills emitted from Turtle Cannons are first encountered in [[Sunken Ghost Ship]], in addition to the only underwater level that features Bullet Bills. The [[Special Zone]] level [[Outrageous]] prominently features many Turtle Canons that emit Bullet Bills.

Some Bullet Bill variants also make their debut, most notably the larger [[Banzai Bill]]s, which are introduced in one of the first levels, [[Yoshi's Island 1]], a few [[world]]s before Mario or Luigi encounter any Bullet Bills. There are also [[Torpedo Ted]]s, which are launched from [[Skull Box]]es underwater. Additionally, when the Special Zone is completed, horizontal-moving Bullet Bills turn into [[Pidgit Bill]]s, which function identically but look like flying [[Pidgit]]s. In ''[[Super Mario World: Super Mario Advance 2]]'', vertically and diagonally-fired Bullet Bills are also changed to this design, and they flap their wings rather than maintain a static image like ordinary Bullet Bills.

===''Super Mario Land 2: 6 Golden Coins''===
[[File:Bullet Bill SML2 sprite.png|frame|left]]
Bullet Bills are commonly found in the [[Mario Zone]] in ''[[Super Mario Land 2: 6 Golden Coins]]''. While Bullet Bills have a different design consisting of a grinning face and no arms, they behave identically, traveling in a straight direction after being shot from Turtle Cannons. Mario can get rid of a Bullet Bill by either stomping it or just dodging it, letting the Bullet Bill fly off-screen.
{{br}}

===''Super Mario 64''===
[[File:Bullet Bill 64.png|thumb|left|A Bullet Bill in ''Super Mario 64'', where it resembles a Banzai Bill.]]
''[[Super Mario 64]]'' marks the first appearance of Bullet Bills in a 3D game. Their design resembles that of a Banzai Bill, not only in size but especially their shark-like row of teeth. Bullet Bills appear in only a single course, [[Whomp's Fortress]], starting in the second [[mission]], [[To the Top of the Fortress]], after Mario defeats the [[Whomp King]]. The Bullet Bills are shot out of a Turtle Cannon and home in on Mario, similarly to Missile Bills in other games, and spin backward upon contact with him. Bullet Bills explode after hitting a wall or reaching their maximum distance. Bullet Bills cannot be defeated, and if Mario gets hit by one, he loses three wedges of [[Health Meter|health]]. It is the first game of the ''[[Super Mario (series)|Super Mario]]'' series where Bullet Bills cannot be jumped on.
{{br}}

===''Super Mario Sunshine''===
[[Image:Bullet Bill Beach.png|thumb|A Bullet Bill being fired out of a cannon as seen in ''Super Mario Sunshine''.]]
Bullet Bills return as enemies in ''[[Super Mario Sunshine]]''. They have received some major changes to their design, having a more robotic appearance, including a metallic exterior, google-eyes, and a quick radar beeping as they fly. Bullet Bills smile in a manner akin to [[Torpedo Ted]]s. A Bullet Bill constantly makes a beeping sound, and they follow Mario until either exploding or crashing into a surface.

The other Bullet Bill variants also appear: [[Gold Bullet Bill]], [[Guided Bullet Bill]], and [[Missile Bill]]s (which have been recolored purple and have therefore been renamed "Purple Bullet Bills" specifically for this appearance). Each type of Bullet Bill can be defeated by having [[F.L.U.D.D.|FLUDD]] squirt [[water]] at it, releasing two [[coin]]s as a result, except Golden Bullet Bills, which release eight coins. In the game's demo reel, viewable by waiting on the "press start" screen, both Bullet Bills and Purple Bullet Bills are shown with blinking red noses as well as solid red eyes with white pupils, suggesting the design was changed slightly very late in development.

Bullet Bills are first encountered in [[Pinna Park]], specifically the first [[Mission|episode]] [[Mecha-Bowser Appears!]], in which [[Mecha-Bowser]] launches Bullet Bills from four cannons in his chest. Bullet Bills travel along the roller coaster tracks throughout the battle, approaching Mario from either the front or back. During the fight, FLUDD alerts Mario whenever a Bullet Bill is approaching from behind. In the second episode, [[The Beach Cannon's Secret]], the [[Monty Mole]] shoots Bullet Bills from its [[Cannon (Super Mario Sunshine)|cannon]], as well as Purple and Gold Bullet Bills. Every Bullet Bill fired from the cannon travels in a long, downward arc before eventually exploding upon hitting the ground. An upside-down [[basket]] containing a [[Blue Coin]] can only be opened by luring a Bullet Bill to crash into it.

In [[Noki Bay]]'s first episode, [[Uncork the Waterfall]], a Bullet Bill can be found dormant on the platform connecting the cannon to the giant cork. Unlike in Pinna Park, the Monty Mole shoots out [[Glorpedo]]s instead of Bullet Bills.

Both Purple and Guided Bullet Bills are used during the final boss battle against [[Bowser]] and [[Bowser Jr.]] in [[Corona Mountain]]. Bowser Jr. shoots [[Missile Bill]]s which come in black and blue, the former having a blinking nosecone (aside than the first one launched) and the latter being solid-colored. These ones follow Mario more persistently when they get a lock on him, though due to the amount that are launched at once as the fight goes on, each has a fairly short and narrow line-of-sight, and they can also change their altitude to get on his level. These can be defeated by jumping on them, with the blue ones giving a [[1-Up Mushroom]] or [[Water Bottle]] if beaten this way. They are also immediately destroyed by Bowser's [[Fire Breath]].

===''Super Mario 64 DS''===
[[File:Bullet Bill DS.png|thumb|A Bullet Bill in ''Super Mario 64 DS'']]
In ''[[Super Mario 64 DS]]'', Bullet Bills retain much of their role from the original ''Super Mario 64''. They have also been redesigned to a standardized appearance, featuring arms and no visible mouth. Aside from the arms being colored gray instead of white, this design would become their typical design in following games. Bullet Bills can now be defeated by a well-timed kick or a [[Super Mushroom|Mushroom]], which sends them spinning backwards to eventually explode.

Bullet Bills appear in a few more areas. They can now appear at the new base of Whomp's Fortress, directly opposite of where Mario starts. Bullet Bills also appear in [[Dire, Dire Docks]], where they are seen at the submarine dock until the completion of [[Bowser in the Fire Sea]].

===''New Super Mario Bros.''===
[[File:6-1 NSMB.png|200px|thumb|left|[[Luigi]] defeating Bullet Bills in World 6-1 of ''New Super Mario Bros.'']]
In ''[[New Super Mario Bros.]]'', Bullet Bills appear in various levels and are shot out of not only [[Bill Blaster]]s but also [[Bill Blaster Turret]]s. Banzai Bills also return, now being fired from their own [[Banzai Bill Cannon|cannon]] for the first time. Bullet Bills can be defeated with a stomp as before, which is usually needed for reaching high ledges. Some alternative methods to defeat a Bullet Bill include hitting it with a [[Koopa Shell|Shell]] (which includes [[Shell Dash|dashing]] into it as [[Shell Mario]]) or using a [[Star|Starman]] or [[Mega Mushroom]]. Bullet Bills retain their invulnerability to fireballs.

Bullet Bills first appear in {{world-link|2|castlen|World 2-Castle (New Super Mario Bros.)}}. They are later also encountered in [[World 5-4 (New Super Mario Bros.)|World 5-4]] (where they travel from offscreen instead of from Bill Blasters), [[World 6-1 (New Super Mario Bros.)|World 6-1]] (where they are shot from Bill Blast Turrets), {{world-link|6|castlen|World 6-Castle (New Super Mario Bros.)}} (where they are fired by [[Monty Mole]] from his cannon), [[World 7-5 (New Super Mario Bros.)|World 7-5]], and lastly [[World 8-1 (New Super Mario Bros.)|World 8-1]].

===''Super Mario Galaxy''===
[[Image:Bullet Bill is on Your Back.png|thumb|Mario luring a Bullet Bill to break open a cage with a Power Star in ''Super Mario Galaxy''.]]
[[File:SMG Megaleg Head Bullet Bills.png|thumb|left|A Bullet Bill chases Mario in ''Super Mario Galaxy'']]
Bullet Bills reappear in ''[[Super Mario Galaxy]]'' as one of the most common enemies, appearing in several [[galaxy|galaxies]]. After Bullet Bills have been fired, if they spot the player, their eyes turn red and they home in on Mario until they hit him, an obstacle, or an enemy. Bullet Bills can often be used to break open [[cage]]s, which can contain useful items or objects such as [[Star Bit]]s, a [[Rainbow Star]], a [[Launch Star]], a [[Sling Star]], or sometimes even a [[Power Star]]. In [[Bowser Jr.'s Robot Reactor]] the robotic boss [[Megaleg]] can be attacked and eventually defeated upon being hit by Bullet Bills. If Bullet Bills hit Mario, he is knocked down for a few seconds and loses a wedge of health from his [[Health Meter]].

Both Bullet Bill variants introduced in ''Super Mario World'' make a reappearance. [[Torpedo Ted]]s can be used the same way as Bullet Bills, although they make a specific radar sound to relay their closeness in proximity. [[Banzai Bill]]s also appear on [[Bowser's Galaxy Reactor]], shot from [[Banzai Bill Cannon]]s, but do not home in on Mario or Luigi.

In two-player mode, Bullet Bills are among the enemies that can be stunned by the second player via the [[Star Pointer]].

The Bullet Bills were named '''Cosmic Bullet Bills''' in beta builds, suggesting that Bullet Bills would have originally been a standalone variant of Bullet Bills.
{{br}}

===''New Super Mario Bros. Wii''===
[[File:NSMBW Bullet Bills Screenshot.png|thumb|left|Bullet Bills in World 7-4 of ''New Super Mario Bros. Wii'']]
Bullet Bills also appear in ''[[New Super Mario Bros. Wii]]'', retaining their behavior from ''New Super Mario Bros.'', although they now spin whenever a [[bah]] plays in the background music. Just as before, Bullet Bills can be defeated with a stomp or from the effects of a [[Starman]]. While Bullet Bills retain their invulnerability to fireballs, they can now be frozen by having a character shoot an [[Ice Ball]] at them in either their [[Ice Mario|Ice]] form or [[Penguin Mario|Penguin]] form. When frozen, the Bullet Bill becomes a stationary platform that can help the character either get across a [[pit]] or reach a higher ledge. Unlike other flying enemies, a frozen Bullet Bill can be picked up and thrown. Jumping from Bullet Bills is used more often in this game to reach ledges with secrets.

Bullet Bills are first encountered in [[World 3-2 (New Super Mario Bros. Wii)|World 3-2]], but they are also encountered on the map of [[World 6 (New Super Mario Bros. Wii)|World 6]] as [[Enemy Course]]s. Bullet Bills also appear in several levels of [[World 7 (New Super Mario Bros. Wii)|World 7]]. In [[World 5-5 (New Super Mario Bros. Wii)|World 5-5]], Bullet Bills fly out from somewhere off-screen instead of being fired from Bill Blasters.

Two of Bullet Bills variants also make a reappearance, Banzai Bills and [[Missile Bill]]s. Banzai Bills similarly received their own heat-seeking variant, [[Missile Banzai Bill]]s. Bullet Bills receive another variant, [[King Bill]]s, which are much bigger than Banzai Bills, taking up a majority of the screen's height, and can smash dense blocks of stone into oblivion.{{br}}

===''Super Mario Galaxy 2''===
[[Image:Mighty Malletoid Bullet Bills.jpg|thumb|[[Megahammer]] launching several Bullet Bills at Mario and Yoshi in ''Super Mario Galaxy 2''.]]
Bullet Bills return as enemies in ''[[Super Mario Galaxy 2]]''. They can be found in various galaxies, such as [[Bowser Jr.'s Fearsome Fleet]] and [[Bowser Jr.'s Boom Bunker]], both of which feature certain Bullet Bills that follow a set path like in the 2D ''Super Mario'' games. Many of them retain their homing capabilities in this game, though some Bullet Bills in [[Bowser Jr.'s Fearsome Fleet]] and [[Bowser's Galaxy Generator]] fly straight and do not follow Mario whatsoever. Banzai Bills also return from ''Super Mario Galaxy'', although Torpedo Teds do not return.

In certain galaxies, [[Yoshi (character)|Yoshi]] can use his tongue to capture a Bullet Bill in his mouth for a short time, and then spit it back out at other incoming enemies or bosses. This plays an important role in the defeat of [[Megahammer]] in Bowser Jr.'s Fearsome Fleet, as the giant robot continuously fires multiple Bullet Bills at Mario every few seconds. Yoshi must spit Bullet Bills at the glass circles on Megahammer a total of eight times in order to defeat it. Yoshi can also spit a Bullet Bill out at certain objects that cannot be destroyed otherwise, but if Yoshi carries a Bullet Bill in his mouth for too long, the Bullet Bill explodes, dealing damage.
{{br}}

===''Super Mario 3D Land''===
[[Image:Bullet Bills 3D.jpg|thumb|[[Tanooki Mario]] flying by some Bullet Bills in ''Super Mario 3D Land''.]]
Bullet Bills return as enemies in ''[[Super Mario 3D Land]]''. This is the first 3D ''Super Mario'' game in which they travel only in a straight line, like in the 2D games, rather than homing in on Mario or Luigi. Bullet Bills are commonly found on [[airship]]s, similar to ''Super Mario Bros. 3'', but can occasionally be found in other [[level]]s. Aside from being stomped or touched by Mario or Luigi while he is invincible, a Bullet Bill can alternatively be defeated by having Mario or Luigi throw a [[boomerang]] at it in his [[Boomerang Mario|Boomerang]] form. If Mario or Luigi performs a tail spin on a Bullet Bill while in his [[Raccoon Mario|Raccoon]] or [[Tanooki Mario (power-up)|Tanooki]] form, the Bullet Bill starts to travel in a different direction.

Bullet Bills received a variant named [[Tail Bullet Bill]], which act like Bullet Bills, but sometimes swing their tail to try and swipe at Mario or Luigi.
{{br}}

===''New Super Mario Bros. 2''===
[[File:Bullet Bill Shooters.png|thumb|left|Several Bullet Bills near a [[Star Coin]] in [[World 5-5 (New Super Mario Bros. 2)|World 5-5]] of ''New Super Mario Bros. 2'']]
Bullet Bills are featured as enemies again in ''[[New Super Mario Bros. 2]]'', behaving exactly as they did in ''New Super Mario Bros. Wii''. They can once again be defeated with stomps, allowing Mario or Luigi to reach certain items that are otherwise out of reach, or from the effects of a [[Mega Mushroom]], [[Star|Starman]], or the [[Gold Flower]].

Contributing to the gold theme of the game, Bullet Bills are among the enemies to receive a gold variant, [[Gold Bullet Bill]]s, which they transform into while the effects of a [[Gold Ring (New Super Mario Bros. 2)|Gold Ring]] are active. Banzai Bills also return and can actually destroy Bullet Bills upon contact.

Diagonally flying Bullet Bills from ''Super Mario World'' can be found in the [[Coin Rush]]-exclusive course packs [[Coin Challenge Pack B]] and [[Platform Panic Pack]].
{{br}}

===''New Super Mario Bros. U''===
[[File:Bullet Bill Hill.jpg|thumb|Bullet Bills in [[Switchback Hill]] in ''New Super Mario Bros. U'']]
Bullet Bills appear in ''[[New Super Mario Bros. U]]''. They act just like they did in past installments. However, the Bullet Bills produce a red smoky flare, similar to their behavior in ''Super Mario 3D Land''. In ''New Super Mario Bros. Wii'', the Bullet Bills only ejected puffy cloud smoke. Bullet Bills also appear in the battle against [[Roy Koopa]]. He holds a Bill Blaster and shoots Bullet Bills at the players as his battle strategy. [[Torpedo Ted]]s from ''Super Mario World'' also reappear. They now have a sub-species under the name [[Targeting Ted]]s.

===''Super Mario 3D World'' / ''Super Mario 3D World + Bowser's Fury''===
[[Image:Bullet Bill 3D New.jpg|thumb|left|Bullet Bills from ''Super Mario 3D World''.]]
Bullet Bills make a reappearance in ''[[Super Mario 3D World]]'', although they much less common than before in favor of a new variant, the [[Cat Bullet Bill]], which chase after Mario. Upon being fired, a Cat Bullet Bill targets Mario or another playable character similar to how regular Bullet Bills do in earlier 3D ''Super Mario'' games. Bullet Bills can be defeated by jumping on it or from either the effects of a [[Super Leaf]], [[Invincibility Leaf]], [[Super Bell]], [[Invincibility Bell]], or a [[Starman]].
{{br}}

===''Super Mario Maker'' / ''Super Mario Maker for Nintendo 3DS''===
[[Image:Bullet Bill Makers.jpg|thumb|right|The Bullet Bills from ''Super Mario Maker''.]]
In ''[[Super Mario Maker]]'' and its remake ''[[Super Mario Maker for Nintendo 3DS]]'', although Bullet Bills are not placeable enemies, Bill Blasters are placeable objects, and Bullet Bills can be shot from them, among other objects. Bullet Bills can be defeated from either a stomp or the invincibility effects of a [[Star|Starman]]. [[Missile Bill]]s also reappear, and they are fired from [[Red Bill Blaster]]s.
{{br}}

===''Super Mario Run''===
[[Image:Bullet Bill Run Run Run.jpg|thumb|x200px|Bullet Bills as seen in ''Super Mario Run''.]]
In ''[[Super Mario Run]]'', Bullet Bills act the same as in earlier 2D entries of the ''Super Mario'' series. They appear in the levels [[Sky-High Lifts and Leaps!]] and [[Bullet Bill Barrage]], where they are fired from offscreen. Bullet Bills are required to reach higher collectibles, and due to the nature of the gameplay, Mario or another playable character have to time their stomps on a Bullet Bill.

===''Super Mario Odyssey''===
[[Image:Bullet Bill Flyers.jpg|thumb|left|Mario as a Bullet Bill outrunning some regular Bullet Bills on his way to the next [[Power Moon]].]]
Bullet Bills and Bill Blasters reappear in ''[[Super Mario Odyssey]]''. They appear in the [[Metro Kingdom]] and the [[Sand Kingdom]], where they can be seen wearing visors. They also appear in the Underground Caverns in the [[Moon Kingdom]], where they wear white top hats. Mario can throw [[Cappy]] at a Bullet Bill to knock its hat off, allowing it to be [[capture]]d. When Bullet Bills are fired from a Bill Blaster, they chase after Mario, exploding after a little while. As a Bullet Bill, Mario can travel across pits, and the player can shake the [[Joy-Con]]s to make him travel even faster. The Bullet Bill explodes if it hits a wall or if Mario controls it for too long.

Bullet Bills that use their sprites from ''Super Mario Bros.'' can be found in two-dimensional section through murals, where they are fired from Bill Blasters. These Bullet Bills travel in straight lines instead of following Mario. If a two-dimensional part ends before the wall it is on does so, a Bullet Bill which has not been defeated leaves the mural, transforms into its three-dimensional counterpart and starts chasing Mario.
{{br}}
===''Super Mario Maker 2''===
In ''[[Super Mario Maker 2]]'', Bullet Bills appear in a similar capacity as the game's predecessor. In the ''Super Mario 3D World'' style, Bullet Bills can defeat enemies that they run into, and are defeated when they hit a wall. In version 3.0.0, a new wearable item exclusively for the ''Super Mario 3D World'' style based on the Bullet Bills was introduced called the [[Bullet Bill Mask]].

===''Super Mario Bros. Wonder''===
Bullet Bills return in ''[[Super Mario Bros. Wonder]]''. The game introduces some variants of Bullet Bill: [[Missile Meg]]s, which bear a vicious grimace and a more elongated shape resembling a missile and can be stood upon; and [[Seeker Bullet Bill]]s, which stick onto surfaces and fly towards the players on contact.
{{br}}
