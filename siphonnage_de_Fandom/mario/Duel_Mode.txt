'''Duel Mode''' is a gameplay mode exclusive to [[Mario Party 3]], in which two players compete head-to-head under a distinct set of rules. Unlike the standard [[Party Mode]], Duel Mode features its own dedicated board maps, unique [[space]]s, and mechanics centered around [[Duel Mini-game]]s.

==Overview==
Upon entering Duel Mode, the player first chooses a character. Since each character begins with a different starting partner, this choice has strategic implications. After selecting a character, the player chooses a board map and sets the desired number of turns. The match then begins.

Prior to gameplay, Tumble appears in the Star Lift segment to describe the selected board and its unique mechanic. The [[Fake Millennium Star|Millennium Star]] then grants each player ten coins and determines turn order by rotating itself—heads awards the first turn to Player 1, while tails gives it to Player 2.

Each player begins the match with a default partner (determined by their chosen character) and five hit points. The central goal is to reduce the opponent's HP to zero by utilizing one's partner to attack. Every partner has individual stats for attack power, HP, and salary cost, and some possess additional special abilities. At the start of each turn, players must pay their partner's salary to retain them; failure to do so causes the partner to leave. Players earn coins primarily through duel mini-games, which award ten coins to the winner. A duel mini-game triggers whenever two players occupy the same space or when [[Belltop]]'s counter reaches zero.

When a partner reaches the opposing player, it may strike either the opponent directly or the opponent's partner. A direct hit on the opponent reduces their HP, while attacking the opponent's partner causes that partner to take damage instead, with any difference in power reflected as HP loss to the opponent. Each time a player completes a full lap of the board, they receive ten coins and gain access to a roulette that assigns them a new partner. If the roulette lands on the same partner already in use, that partner's stats are doubled—including their salary. The match concludes when one player's HP is fully depleted. If both players retain HP after the final turn, the player with more HP wins. In the event of a tie in HP, victory goes to the player with more coins. Should both HP and coin totals be equal, the match ends in a draw.

The boards in Duel Mode feature an exclusive character named [[Belltop]]. It resembles a mechanical figure topped with a numbered yellow hat and a wind-up key that turns as the count decreases. Each time a player passes Belltop, its counter decrements from five toward zero. When the counter reaches zero, Belltop strikes its head with drumsticks to signal the start of a duel mini-game. Notably, winning a mini-game triggered by Belltop awards 20 or 30 coins, compared to only 10 coins for mini-games initiated by landing on a Mini-Game Space. After the mini-game concludes, Belltop's counter resets to five. Belltop is typically located at key intersections or near a board's central feature—on the [[Blowhard]] board, for instance, it can be found beside the fan at the center of the map.

==Board maps==
The board maps used in Duel Mode are entirely separate from those in Party Mode. Each duel board has a signature mechanic that distinguishes it from the others, though all share the same types of spaces and supporting characters.

*[[Gate Guy]] – Two gate-like characters are positioned in the northern and southern sections of the board. Players may pass through a Gate Guy by paying five coins.
*[[Arrowhead]] – Cone-shaped figures with arrow-like arms appear at certain junctions, pointing toward different routes. Players must choose a path based on the directions indicated by these Arrowheads.
*[[Pipesqueak]] – Warp pipe–like characters called Pipesqueaks are scattered across the board. Players can enter a Pipesqueak to be transported along alternate routes.
*[[Blowhard]] – A large fan character occupies the center of this board. Whenever a player steps on Blowhard, they are launched into the air, and the positions of their partners are swapped.
*[[Mr. Mover]] – A massive conveyor belt character sits at the board's center. The belt features blue and red sections; when a player lands on Mr. Mover, they are carried to a different part of the board depending on which color section they land on.
*[[Backtrack]] – This board includes a unique space that reverses a player's direction of travel upon landing. Directional arrows on the board indicate the player's current heading.

==Spaces==
Most spaces found in Duel Mode differ from those on standard Party Mode boards. While a few space types carry over, the majority are unique to this mode.

*[[Basic Space]] – The most frequently appearing space on duel boards, initially shown as a blank tile. When a player lands on one, their icon is placed on it, claiming ownership. Landing on a self-owned space awards bonus coins, while landing on a space owned by the opponent costs the player coins.
*[[Power-up Space]] – An uncommon space present on every duel board. Landing here doubles the player's partner's attack power as well as their salary.
*[[Back Space]] – Landing on this space forces the player to hit a Dice Block, which determines how many spaces they must move in reverse. On the Backtrack board specifically, this space instead swaps the positions of both partners.
*[[Mini-Game Space]] – Triggers a duel mini-game when a player lands on it.
*[[Happening Space]] – Functions similarly to its Party Mode counterpart. A roulette is spun to select a random event that alters the state of play on the board.
*[[Game Guy Space]] – Landing here initiates a Game Guy mini-game. All of the player's coins are wagered during the challenge.
