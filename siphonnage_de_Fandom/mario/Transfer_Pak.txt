The '''Transfer Pak''' is an accessory for the [[Nintendo 64]] that allow for the transfer of certain data from a specific [[Nintendo 64]] game to a specific [[Game Boy Color]] game and/or vice versa.

The Transfer Pak was originally released in Japan in 1998 before released in overseas markets in 2000. It was most notably used for early ''[[w:c:pokemon:Pokémon (franchise)|Pokémon]]'' games, starting with ''[[w:c:pokemon:Pocket Monsters Stadium|Pocket Monsters Stadium]]'' and its international counterpart ''[[w:c:pokemon:Pokémon Stadium|Pokémon Stadium]]'', to transfer data both to and from the [[w:c:pokemon:Generation I|Generation I]] [[w:c:pokemon:Pokémon (core series)|core series]] games. The Transfer Pak was also used for ''[[w:c:pokemon:Pokémon Stadium 2|Pokémon Stadium 2]]'', which also featured compatibility with Generation I core series games, and with the addition of the [[w:c:pokemon:Generation II|Generation II]] core series games.

==Relation to ''[[Mario (franchise)|Mario]]''==
A few [[Camelot]]-produced ''Mario'' games made use of this feature, including ''[[Mario Golf (Nintendo 64)|Mario Golf]]'' and ''[[Mario Tennis (Nintendo 64)|Mario Tennis]]'' for the Nintendo 64, which could have their data transferred both to and from Game Boy Color games ''[[Mario Golf (Game Boy Color)|Mario Golf]]'' and ''[[Mario Tennis (Game Boy Color)|Mario Tennis]]'', respectively.

Two ''Mario'' games for the [[Nintendo 64DD]], ''[[Mario Artist: Paint Studio]]'' and ''[[Mario Artist: Talent Studio]]'', also made use of the Transfer Pak, which are compatible with Japanese [[Game Boy Camera]]s.
