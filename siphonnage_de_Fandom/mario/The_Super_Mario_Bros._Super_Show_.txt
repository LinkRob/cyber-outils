'''''The Super Mario Bros. Super Show!''''', also simply known as '''''Super Mario'''''<ref>[https://m.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLB_zcLWHIbyq5UxUAHAEswDaD7f1BEW4D "Super Mario in Spanish" YouTube playlist by Super Mario Spanish - WildBrain]</ref> and '''''Super Mario Brothers''''',<ref>[https://m.youtube.com/watch?v=XejH0WlLaFA Super Mario Brothers - DO YOU PRINCESS TOADSTOOL TAKE THIS KOOPA | Super Mario Bros | WildBrain]. ''YouTube''. Retrieved October 1, 2020.</ref> is a live-action/animated television series that originally aired from September 4 to December 1, 1989. In addition to being the first cartoon of [[DIC Entertainment]]'s ''[[Super Mario (series)|Super Mario]]'' trilogy, it was the only one to be produced directly for syndication. In the show's live-action segments, [[Mario (DiC cartoons)|Mario]] and [[Luigi (DiC cartoons)|Luigi]] (played by [[Lou Albano]] and [[Danny Wells]], respectively), living in [[Super Mario Bros. Plumbing|their basement workshop]] in [[Brooklyn]], are often visited by various celebrity guest stars. The show's cartoons, which are based on ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]'' and ''[[Super Mario Bros. 2]]'', feature the Mario Bros. joining [[Princess Toadstool (DiC cartoons)|Princess Toadstool]] and [[Toad (DiC cartoons)|Toad]] against [[King Koopa (DiC cartoons)|King Koopa]] and [[Bowser's Minions|his minions]] to save the many lands of the [[Mushroom Kingdom]]. The ''Super Mario Bros.'' cartoons were shown on Mondays through Thursdays only; on Fridays, the show aired cartoons based on the animated series ''[[w:c:zelda:The Legend of Zelda (television series)|The Legend of Zelda]]''.

==Overview==
[[File:SMBSS title card.png|thumb|left|Title card for the show]]
Each episode begins with [[Mario's face|Mario's floating head]] [[Fourth wall|welcoming the viewer to the show]] followed by the first part of "[[Mario Brothers Rap|The Mario Rap]]," the opening theme for the show. After "The Mario Rap," the show then cuts to a live-action segment starring [[Mario (DiC cartoons)|Mario]] (portrayed by the late {{wp|World Wrestling Entertainment}} Hall of Famer "Captain" [[Lou Albano]]) and [[Luigi (DiC cartoons)|Luigi]] (the late [[Danny Wells]]) living in [[Brooklyn]], where they are often visited by a celebrity guest star either playing themselves or another character at [[Super Mario Bros. Plumbing|Mario Brothers Plumbing]], a plumbing business which doubles as their home.

The live-action segment is followed by the second part of "The Mario Rap," showing Mario and Luigi unclogging the drain of a bathtub and being [[warp]]ed to the [[Mushroom Kingdom]]. It is then followed by a cartoon based on the ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]'' and ''[[Super Mario Bros. 2]]'' video games, where Mario, Luigi, [[Princess Toadstool (DiC cartoons)|Princess Toadstool]] (Peach), and [[Toad (DiC cartoons)|Toad]] battle against [[King Koopa (DiC cartoons)|King Koopa]] (Bowser) throughout the many lands of the Mushroom Kingdom, often in a book, film, or historical parody. [[Mouser]], [[Tryclyde (DiC cartoons)|Tryclyde]], [[Fryguy]], and a single, unnamed [[Koopa Troopa]] often work closely with King Koopa, serving as his henchmen. In line with these parodies, Koopa usually takes on various [[King Koopa's alter egos|alter egos]]. He usually has a different outfit for each alter ego, and usually assumes a different alias to go along with the alter ego. For example, in "[[Rolling Down the River]]," King Koopa is "Captain Koopa" and wears a pirate outfit, while in "[[The Provolone Ranger]]", he goes by "Billy the Koopa" and wears a cowboy outfit. In many episodes, King Koopa's minions often dress up in outfits as well to go along with Koopa's themed costumes. In some episodes, King Koopa goes without an alter ego or a costume, such as "Jungle Fever" and "Mario of the Apes." The only episode where King Koopa does not appear is "[[Love 'Em and Leave 'Em]]." At the beginning of each cartoon segment, Mario quotes a brief entry from the [[Plumber's Log]], a unseen logbook that he uses to record his adventures throughout the show.

[[Wart]], the main antagonist of ''Super Mario Bros. 2'', does not appear in the show, though most of his minions appear as members of the [[Bowser's Minions|Koopa Pack]]; King Koopa is also partially designed after him, featuring a mainly green color scheme with a crown on his head. Like most 1980s cartoons, King Koopa prolongs the series' run by escaping from his adversaries, which he does through the use of a [[Magical Potion|Magic Potion]]. Also similar to most 1980s cartoons, ''The Super Mario Bros. Super Show!'' mainly follows an episodic format, having little continuity from episode to episode and ending with no clear series finale.

[[File:Plumber Rap SMBSS.png|thumb|The opening sequence of the show, where Mario and Luigi are pulled into a Warp Pipe that leads to the Mushroom Kingdom]]
The basis of the storyline, introduced at the beginning of every animated episode during the second part of "The Mario Rap," is that Mario and Luigi are working on unclogging the drain of a bathtub, which is a [[Warp Zone]] to the Mushroom Kingdom, and Mario and Luigi travel down the drain and end up in the Mushroom Kingdom rescuing Toad and Princess Toadstool, who are cornered on the ledge of a cliff by Koopa and his [[Koopa Troopa]]s. Having rescued Toad and the Princess, the Mario Bros. focus on returning to Brooklyn, while stopping King Koopa whenever they can. Lou Albano and Danny Wells also voice Mario and Luigi for the animated segment. According to the [[Shout! Factory]] release of the ''[[The Super Mario Bros. Super Show! Volume 1]]'' DVD, the live-action segments take place before the Mario Bros. were sucked down the Warp Pipe to the Mushroom Kingdom. The story "[[Im Pilzewunderland]]" in the German book ''[[Zur Fernseh-Serie Super Mario Bros. - Spiele und Geschichten]]'' features an alternate premise for the show, featuring the Princess being locked in the cage in the [[Peach's Castle|Mushroom Castle]] and Mario and Luigi cooperating with Toad to free the Princess.

Following the cartoon is the third part of the episode, which continues the story that the live-action segment set up in the beginning. Towards the end, the second part of the live-action segment is interrupted with scenes from that week's upcoming episode of ''The Legend of Zelda''. The show ends with "[[Do the Mario]]," the ending theme for the show, where Mario (portrayed by Lou Albano) performs a dance and sings a tune similar to the [[Ground BGM (Super Mario Bros.)|Ground Theme]] from ''Super Mario Bros.''

The ''Super Mario Bros.'' cartoon was shown on Mondays through Thursdays only. On Fridays, the show aired ''[[w:c:zelda:The Legend of Zelda (television series)|The Legend of Zelda]]'' cartoons, based on [[w:c:zelda:The Legend of Zelda|the game of the same title]]. However, a live-action segment of ''The Super Mario Bros. Super Show!'' aired with the ''Zelda'' episodes.

==Broadcast history==
The show was originally meant to start September 11 and end December 7 instead of September 9 to November 30.<ref>TheUltiMarioFan (July 22, 2020). [https://mobile.twitter.com/TheUltiMarioFan/status/1285972652885147650 ''Twitter''] Retrieved July 23, 2020.</ref>

When the series was distributed and broadcasted internationally outside the United States, all ''The Legend of Zelda'' previews were removed. Some of these prints can be found on DVDs released by [[Maximum Entertainment]] from 2004 to 2008 in the [[United Kingdom]]. Most of the episodes in these prints (including foreign variations of the series) have the Saban International logo whereas some episodes have both the DIC and Saban logos.<ref>https://www.avid.wiki/DIC_Entertainment#3rd_Logo_(September_12,_1987-2005)</ref>

After DIC's ''Super Mario'' cartoons ended, the show was aired in reruns on the Family Channel (currently known as {{wp|Freeform (TV channel)|Freeform}}).<ref name="Freeform">[https://archive.org/details/1993-fam-tv-disc-1-merged 1993 Fam TV Disc 1 Merged]</ref> Like the international prints of the series, the Family Channel's reruns of the series removed ''The Legend of Zelda'' previews and the scenes that led into them from the live-action segments, and unlike the international prints, these reruns also slowed down the episodes to bring them back to their original length. They also changed the placement of the commercial breaks, placing them during scene dissolves in the animated segments. (For unknown reasons, the Family Channel version of "[[King Mario of Cramalot]]" and "[[Day of the Orphan]]" is the version used in subsequent DVD and digital releases.) Also, from that point onward, the song covers were removed, similar to season one of ''[[Captain N: The Game Master]]'', and were replaced with instrumentals of seven songs from ''[[The Adventures of Super Mario Bros. 3]]'' and one song from ''[[Super Mario World (television series)|Super Mario World]]''. This was done for licensing reasons as the lyrics of the song covers are copyrighted. In the DVD and digital releases of the former episode mentioned, the 1987 DIC logo is replaced with the 1990 DIC logo.

In the UK, this series was featured as part of Jungle Fun on {{wp|TV-am}} in 1991. Later, only the cartoon segments aired on {{wp|GMTV}} in 1993.<ref name="GMTV">https://youtu.be/y1egXITX1hI?t=5759</ref> However, the series also aired as part of {{wp|List of GMTV programmes#Parkin's In / Fun In The Sun|Parkin's In}} on the former channel during holidays.<ref>https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mgBbdVMM3C8</ref>

In France, the series is aired via Pluto TV Kids Gaming on {{wp|Pluto TV}} alongside ''[[w:c:sonic:Adventures of Sonic the Hedgehog|Adventures of Sonic the Hedgehog]]''.{{ref needed}}

In Australia, New Zealand, Philippines, Singapore, the United States, United Kingdom, and Thailand, the series is currently airing via the [[Super Mario Bros. (FAST channel)|Super Mario Bros.]] FAST channel on LG Channels, {{wp|The Roku Channel}} and {{wp|Samsung TV Plus}} alongside ''The Adventures of Super Mario Bros. 3'' and ''[[Super Mario World (television series)|Super Mario World]]''.

===''Club Mario''===
[[File:Club Mario Logo.png|thumb|Title card for ''[[Club Mario]]'']]
[[File:Tommy Treehugger.jpg|thumb|left|[[Tommy Treehugger]] and [[Co-MC]], the co-hosts of ''Club Mario'']]
On May 4, 1990, the show was reworked and aired under the name ''[[Club Mario]]''.<ref>https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-All-BC/Broadcasting-Magazine/BC-1990/BC-1990-05-28.pdf</ref> This iteration of the show replaced the original live-action segments featuring Mario and Luigi (played by Lou Albano and Danny Wells, respectively) with new live-action segments featuring two ''Super Mario''-loving fans named [[Tommy Treehugger]] (played by [[Chris Coombs]]) and [[Co-MC]] (played by [[Michael Anthony Rawlins]]), serving as the self-proclaimed co-hosts for the show. This iteration also replaced the original opening theme of the show with [[Club Mario (song)|a new opening theme]], played at the beginning of every episode of this iteration of the show; an instrumental of the opening theme was used for this iteration's closing theme.

In addition to Tommy and Co-MC, the live-action segments also featured several recurring guests, including the [[Dude Brothers]], a duo of {{wp|List of Muppets|Muppet}}-esque puppets who share an interest in beaches and surfing, [[Evil Eric]] (played by Michael Anthony Rawlins), Co-MC's evil twin brother who causes trouble for Tommy whenever Co-MC is absent, and [[Tammy Treehugger]], the younger sister of Tommy who is often seen putting on a puppet show.

An episode of ''Club Mario'' starts with the opening theme of the show, followed by a live-action segment usually beginning with Tommy and Co-MC welcoming the viewer to the show. In most episodes, the two hosts then proceed to read a synopsis of the cartoon episode featured for the show, followed by a segment which usually takes elements from that episode. For example, the [[Club Mario (Pirates of Koopa)|''Club Mario'' episode]] for the episode "[[Pirates of Koopa]]" features Co-MC and Tommy dressing up as a pirate and a parrot, respectively.

The second part of a ''Club Mario'' episode features the cartoon episode mentioned at the beginning of the live-action segment. The cartoon episodes are left entirely unmodified in this iteration of the show, keeping the second part of "The Mario Rap" as the introduction for this segment of the show.

The third part of a ''Club Mario'' episode continues the live-action segment started at the beginning of the episode. Like the original iteration, the second half of the live-action segment is interrupted by a preview scenes showing scenes of the week's ''The Legend of Zelda''. Each episode ends with Tommy and Co-MC bidding farewell to the viewer.

In addition to the main live-action segment featured in each ''Club Mario'' episode, ''Club Mario'' also featured several smaller, self-contained recurring segments during the iteration's airing. Segments include "[[Are You Bogus?]]", where Tommy and Co-MC interview a guest and ask them questions to determine if they are who they claim to be, "Celluloid Rating," where the hosts watch popular films and rate them based on how they felt about the film, ''Spaced Out Theater'', a show hosted by a green alien woman named [[Princess Centauri]] who bookends another show produced by [[DIC Entertainment]] called ''{{wp|Photon (TV series)|Photon}}'', and ''Big Kid Theater'', hosted by its titular character [[Big Kid]] (played by [[Jeff Rose]]) who shows clips of old black-and-white films, such as ''{{wp|Tarzan the Ape Man (1932 film)|Tarzan the Ape Man}}''.

This iteration of the show was apparently not as popular as the original iteration, and most later airings of ''The Super Mario Bros. Super Show!'' used the original iteration of the show with the original live-action segments.<ref name="Freeform"/> As a result, after the ''Club Mario'' iteration was replaced by the original iteration after 1990,<ref>[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=63Bf1D0eTaw&list=PL6g25LhRf6_ZbOSypSdrdkLZ93ZA3hFAp&index=36 <nowiki>(November 14, 1990) Club Mario with commercials (partial) [FOX WPXT 51 Portland] [60fps]</nowiki>]</ref> the ''Club Mario'' ceased to be aired on television. Outside of streaming releases and the YouTube upload by Wildbrain of the episode "[[The Unzappables]]," the series has not been reproduced in any format, and as such most available segments are from homemade recordings of the segments during the iteration's airing.

==Production==
{{quote|The bible was written by Bruce and Reed Shelly. Reading it, you could tell that they were still struggling to get a handle on the show. I mean, the core problem was obvious: There are no real characters or stories in a Nintendo game, so how do you turn one into a TV series? [...] There was little indication about the kinds of adventures our heroes would have, and a lot of unanswered questions about how we would incorporate elements of the game. I had no clue how to solve those problem and didn’t see how that show was going to work at all! But DIC had an order for 52 episodes and deadlines were looming. We had to make some decisions fast or fall behind schedule, which would be a disaster. So at the beginning there was a lot of urgency to solve those problems and get on with it.|Perry Martin<ref name="Perry">Brett Homenick (September 11, 2018). [https://vantagepointinterviews.com/2018/09/11/do-the-mario-perry-martin-on-scripting-the-cartoon-adaptations-of-the-super-mario-bros/ DO THE MARIO! Perry Martin on Scripting the Cartoon Adaptations of the Super Mario Bros.!]. ''Vantage Point Interviews''. Retrieved October 05 2018.</ref>}}

Strong from its multiple animated shows based on pre-existing properties, [[DIC Entertainment]] approached [[Nintendo]] with an offer to make a cartoon based on the ''[[Super Mario (franchise)|Super Mario]]'' franchise. Nintendo initially declined, but later signed a deal after DIC put together a creative team they liked.<ref name="Canoe">[http://slam.canoe.ca/Slam/Wrestling/Movies/2013/01/11/20490741.html Canoe: Super Mario Bros. Super Show hit a high score]</ref> Nintendo required DIC to pay extensive royalties, an unusual arrangement for children's programming at the time.

[[File:EarlySSPoster.jpg|thumb|left|Preview image from [[Nintendo Power issue 7|''Nintendo Power'' issue 7]] featuring King Koopa resembling his ''Super Mario Bros.'' sprite design, while Mushroom Retainers resemble normal mushrooms]]
[[File:SMBSSHotdogPoster.jpg|thumb|A different early poster of concept artwork featuring a vastly different interpretation than the final product]]
A few pieces of conceptual artwork have surfaced from early design phases of the show. Some of them were somewhat more accurate to the games, notably in King Koopa's design, while others were far more loosely based on official designs. One such piece is a poster featuring a much different depiction of Mario and Luigi (the latter of which is using his in-game clothing colors for ''Super Mario Bros.'') who are brandishing a plunger and a wrench as weapons, a yellow-capped Toad, two large, grotesque [[Trouter]]s, a flying green [[Birdo]] being ridden by a [[Snifit]] holding two [[Beezo]] spears, a large purple frog creature with a necklace (possibly [[Wart]]) with a [[Hammer Bro]]ther in tow, a flying yellow [[Pidgit]], two [[Hoopster]]s with distinct heads (one of which has a worried expression), a blue [[Tryclyde (DiC cartoons)|Tryclyde]], a pelican-like [[Albatoss]] holding a muscular red [[Bob-omb|Bob-Omb]] by the fuse, a giant red multi-eyed octopus with blue arms (possibly a complete reinterpretation of [[Blooper|Bloober]]), two tube worm-like creatures with sharp teeth and long tongues (possibly [[Piranha Plant]]s), and some goggle-wearing, long-tongued aliens atop spacecrafts with vaguely face-like fronts (possibly intended to be [[Lakitu]]s). King Koopa appears in the background and mostly looks as he does in the finished product but with more exaggerated proportions, while Princess Toadstool, the Shyguy, the Snifit, and the Beezo are fairly accurate to their artwork. The poster also shows a helmet-wearing skull mounted to a "Go Back!" sign, a Sphinx, a sea serpent, and some prehistoric reptiles. This loose, heavily abstracted depiction of game elements closely resembles that of their later show ''[[Captain N: The Game Master]]''.

According to [[Danny Wells]], he and co-star [[Lou Albano]] recorded the show on a six-day schedule, where they would first film the live-action segments and then drive to another studio in order to record voices for the animated segments.{{ref needed}}

In a 2018 interview, freelance writer [[Perry Martin]] explained that the show's focus on parodies came from Andy Heyward, as the production team had struggled to make much material from the thin story present in the games.<ref name="Perry"/> Writers would first submit a one-page premise of the story to the show's editors Bruce and Reed Shelly, then spend two days on a four-page outline and finally a week to create the final script.

There was initially an episode titled "{{wp|Ali Baba and the Forty Thieves|Ali Koopa and His Forty Goombas}}" and a cover song in every episode including "{{wp|Gimme Shelter}}", "{{wp|Love Potion No. 9 (song)|Love Potion Number Nine}}", and "{{wp|Ain't No Mountain High Enough}}".<ref>TheUltiMarioFan (June 12, 2020). [https://mobile.twitter.com/TheUltiMarioFan/status/1271519848603037698 ''Twitter''] Retrieved June 21, 2020.</ref>
Additionally, every episode without a cover song contains a unique piece of music suggesting that DIC replaced the last copyrighted songs with original ones.

In an interview about DIC's history in adapting video games to television, DIC executive Robby London stated that video games such as ''Super Mario Bros.'' were DIC's favorite type of media to adapt because "[...] the videogames themselves were colorful, imaginative, hip and more than a little bizarre – in the best sense" and that their sparse lores and simple characters allowed more creativity than when adapting material from other media.<ref name="GamesTM">''GamesTM''. "[https://www.gamestm.co.uk/uncategorised/from-pixels-to-primetime-the-making-of-mario-and-sonics-cartoon-careers/ From Captain N to Sonic Underground: Behind videogames' earliest cartoons]. Retrieved September 13, 2016</ref> London also spoke positively of Nintendo's involvement in the show, stating "[...] Nintendo was reasonable, professional and good to deal with. Their America office seemed quite capable of speaking definitively on behalf of their Japanese owners, and I don’t remember any problematic disputes with Nintendo [...]" and contrasting it with DIC's more turbulent partnership with [[Sega]] for its three ''[[Sonic]]'' series.<ref name="GamesTM"/>

The series was a rating success and was widely syndicated.<ref name="Canoe"/> Also, according to Wells, the guest stars actively asked to be part of the live-action segments due to the popularity of ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]'' with their children.<ref name="Canoe"/> However, despite its success, Nintendo had little interest in continuing the show beyond the initial package, leading to its cancellation.

==Staff==
{{main|The Super Mario Bros. Super Show!/Staff}}
The ''Super Show'' was executive produced by [[Andy Heyward]], directed by Dan Riba and produced by [[John Grusd]], who also produced and directed the two subsequent ''Super Mario'' cartoons by DIC. Animation was provided by [[Sei Young Animation Co., Ltd.]] The live-action sequences were co-produced with Saban Productions.

==Differences from the games==
*Several characters have very different appearances from what became their standard character models in later years, mostly owing to being based on sprites and/or character artwork from ''[[Donkey Kong (game)|Donkey Kong]]'', ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]'' and ''[[Super Mario Bros. 2]]''.
**King Koopa's appearance was loosely based on his sprite from ''Super Mario Bros.'' His main skin color is green instead of orange-yellow; his ribbed stomach is a deeper yellow than its game color; he has a crocodilian snout that matches the rest of his skin in color; he has two spike-bands instead of five (worn on his wrists only), which are dark green with gold spikes instead of black with white spikes; his shell has a bright-green lining and fewer spikes than in the games; his horn- and spike-rings are green like his skin; he has a crown instead of a mane and no eyebrows; his tongue is reptilian instead of human-like; and his eyes are yellow instead of white and lack their red irises. In various pieces of concept art for the show, King Koopa retains his original color palette.
**Princess Toadstool's model portrays her as a redhead instead of a blonde, resembling her sprites from the first two ''Super Mario Bros.'' games. She also lacks her gloves and crown jewels, and her brooch, earrings, and eye color are green rather than blue (though some episodes do depict her earrings and irises as blue later on within animation).
**Mario and Luigi have overalls matching their cap colors, with blue shirts, and also have black hair instead of brown, along the lines of their early appearances in sprites and artwork. However, in the later DIC cartoons, Mario's hair color is changed to brown, as seen in his current design, while Luigi's hair stays black.
**Toad's appearance is based on his sprite from ''Super Mario Bros.'', with the waistcoat being red instead of blue. In the first three episodes, all instances of white and red in his appearance are inverted, but this is fixed from the fourth episode onwards, although his shoes are recolored purple instead of the red from the original sprite, which is seen in the opening in every episode. However, Toad's original inverted color scheme is reused for his [[Fire Mario|super form]] in the episode "[[The Fire of Hercufleas]]" and on VHS and DVD covers.
*In addition to not having his more fantastical design from the games, King Koopa does not have the ability to [[Fire Breath|shoot fire]]; he instead uses his [[Magic Wand|magic wands]] or [[Koopa Troop|minions]] for long-range attacks.
*In several episodes, Birdos are shown to be able to fly, a trait not seen in any ''Super Mario'' game.
*Rather than becoming [[Fire Mario]], unlike in the games, Mario and Luigi become "Super Mario" and "Super Luigi" upon touching either a [[Fire Flower]], [[Star|Starman]], or some other source of great power. As Super Mario, Mario can hurl [[fireball]]s, gains strength, and, on a few rare occasions, can even fly. Although Mario can lose his powers by taking a hit (similar to the 2D ''Super Mario'' side-scrollers), it is also possible for them to wear off after a while.
*Mario's super form is based on his fire form sprites from ''Super Mario Bros.''; Luigi's variant of this form replaces the red with his defining color, green. This resembles what would be their standard fire form color schemes from ''Super Mario World'' onwards, but with their shirt and overall colors swapped.
*During Mario's transformation into a "Super Mario", Mario's color scheme from the Japanese cover of ''[[Mario Bros. (game)|Mario Bros.]]'' can be seen.
*During Luigi's transformation into a "Super Luigi", he is shown in one frame with a green shirt and blue overalls, which would later become his current color scheme in the franchise, starting with ''[[Super Mario Bros. 3]]'', though his cap is colored blue instead of green.
*Although Mario's eyes are the established blue, Luigi's are green instead. However, their game color is used on the cover of the ''[[The Super Mario Bros. Super Show! Volume 1]]'' DVD set.
*[[Trouter]]s are portrayed as being more territorial than they are in ''Super Mario Bros. 2'', and pursue anybody who comes near them. Their appearance more closely resembles that of [[Cheep Cheep|Cheep-Cheep]]s, and they may be only a merger of the two enemies.

==Differences from the other series==
*The voices of Mario and Luigi in this show most resemble their current voices, whereas their voices in ''The Adventures of Super Mario Bros. 3'' and ''Super Mario World'' television series (voiced by [[Walker Boone]] and [[Tony Rosato]], respectively) are lower and raspier for Mario and higher and softer for Luigi, instead of Mario having a higher voice and Luigi having a lower voice like [[Charles Martinet]] does for the two's voices, who made neither of them any raspier than Mario and Luigi's voice actors in this show.
*This is the only English DIC show where there is a live-action segment. However, the Danish dub of ''[[The Adventures of Super Mario Bros. 3]]'' features live-action segments of Mario and Luigi (played by [[Lennart Johannessen]] and [[Jørn Rosenville]], respectively) in Mario Brothers Plumbing, like the original live-action segments of ''The Super Mario Bros. Super Show!''
*There is the [[Plumber's Log]], whose numbered quote in every cartoon episode is a reference to the Captain's Log quote from ''{{wp|Star Trek}}''. The Plumber's Log is also featured in ''Zur Fernseh-Serie Super Mario Bros. - Spiele und Geschichten'' and the German version of the ''Super Mario Bros.'' comics by Valiant Comics, where unlike the show, where the log is only quoted at the beginning of every cartoon episode, the log entries shown in these works are shown in their entirety.

===''King Koopa's Kool Kartoons''===
{{main|King Koopa's Kool Kartoons}}
[[File:KoopaLogo.jpg|thumb|Title card for ''[[King Koopa's Kool Kartoons]]'']]
''King Koopa's Kool Kartoons'' was a live-action children's show created as a spinoff of ''The Super Mario Bros. Super Show!'', first aired on September 11, 1989.<ref>[https://cocatalog.loc.gov/cgi-bin/Pwebrecon.cgi?v1=3&ti=1,3&Search%5FArg=King%20Koopa%27s%20Kool%20Kartoons&Search%5FCode=TALL&CNT=25&PID=EKSFnTaBcyyUotCYTplrRKdM0_LF&SEQ=20250305220131&SID=1 King Koopa's kool kartoons : opening theme, King Koopa's song.]</ref> The show stars King Koopa, played by [[Christopher Collins]] (later [[Patrick Pinney]]), who acts as the host for the show, and the show's format is comparable to {{wp|Bozo the Clown}}'s television show. Each episode of the show followed a set format: the introduction, which shows King Koopa, his pet [[Ratso]], and his [[Koopa Troopa]]s strutting down a street in [[Los Angeles]] towards a television while the show's theme song is played, the studio, where Koopa sat behind a desk in front of his Koopa Troopas, and the credits, which appeared in front of pre-recorded segment. King Koopa read fan mail and hosted quizzes during the studio segment, and at the end of the episode, he gave away prizes such as a {{wp|Power Glove}}. Koopa also played cartoons from the 1920s to 1940s that were in the public domain, such as the ''{{wp|Rainbow Parade}}'' series. 

The show was nominated for a [[Los Angeles]] {{wp|Emmy Awards|Emmy Award}} in the category "best children/youth program."<ref>Bernstein, Sharon (April 20, 1990). "[https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1990-04-20-ca-1436-story.html News story]. LA Times. Retrieved July 7, 2022.</ref> The show was canceled due to a letter from the then-president of {{wp|The Walt Disney Company}}, {{wp|Michael Eisner}} to the then-president of {{wp|20th Century Studios}}, {{wp|Billy Diller}}, which allegedly stated that the show "undermined the morals of ite live, youthful audiences."<ref>Thomas Game Docs (July 3, 2022). "[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OtQ9BafnBik The Mario TV Show that made Parents Angry]". YouTube. Retrieved September 14, 2022.</ref>

==Translations==
[[File:TSMBSS Korean Mario and Luigi.png|thumb|Mario and Luigi in the Korean live-action segments of ''The Super Mario Bros. Super Show!'']]
The show received 21 translations, including {{wp|Danish language|Danish}}, {{wp|Dutch language|Dutch}}, {{wp|Finnish language|Finnish}}, {{wp|French language|French}}, {{wp|German language|German}}, {{wp|Greek language|Greek}}, {{wp|Hebrew language|Hebrew}}, {{wp|Icelandic language|Icelandic}}, {{wp|Indonesian language|Indonesian}}, {{wp|Italian language|Italian}}, {{wp|Korean language|Korean}}, {{wp|Mandarin Chinese}}, {{wp|Taiwanese Mandarin}}, {{wp|Norwegian language|Norwegian}}, {{wp|Brazilian Portuguese}}, {{wp|Russian language|Russian}}, {{wp|Spanish language in the Americas|Latin American Spanish}}, {{wp|Peninsular Spanish|European Spanish}}, {{wp|Swedish language|Swedish}}, {{wp|Thai language|Thai}}, and {{wp|Turkish language|Turkish}}, along with {{wp|Bulgarian language|Bulgarian}}, {{wp|Polish language|Polish}}, and Russian voiceovers, with several differences between them.
*While most translations dub both the live-action and animated segments, the Dutch, Finnish, Hebrew, Icelandic, Korean, and Norwegian dubs do not dub the live-action segments, instead being subtitled. The Turkish dub removes the live-action segments entirely.
*The French, German, Italian, and Russian dubs are the only ones to completely dub the "Mario Rap," whereas the Icelandic, Brazilian Portuguese, Spanish, and Swedish dubs use the English version and dub only the animated Mario head introducing the show. The remaining translations leave the "Mario Rap" completely intact.
**On a related note, the German and Italian versions use dubs of "Do the Mario" for the closing credits.
*The original translations in Spain and Italy use an entirely different extended theme song: "[[Super Mario (song)|Super Mario]]."
*The Korean version uses a slightly modified lyrical version of the original ''Super Mario'' theme music and replaces the live-action skits with different ones starring domestic actors. These skits feature original plots, sets, and costume designs, rather than simply remaking the Albano/Wells skits; among other changes, Mario and Luigi's mustaches are stereotypical handlebar mustaches (as opposed to Albano and Wells' natural facial hair), the set design is significantly more sterile, and the brothers' outfits more closely resemble those of their game counterparts.<ref>[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3YC111rmkGU (reup) Korean Super Mario Bros. Super Show Live-Action Segment (with English subtitles)]</ref>
*The Dutch, French, German, Hebrew, Italian, Norwegian, Brazilian Portuguese, Russian, and Swedish dubs cut the ''Zelda'' previews.
**The Italian and Brazilian Portuguese dubs also cut the Friday live-action skits and the ''Zelda'' episodes. Later, these animated episodes were broadcasted in their own series: ''Un regno incantato per Zelda'' (''An enchanted kingdom for Zelda'') in Italian and simply ''Zelda'' in Brazilian Portuguese.
*In the Chinese, Danish, Finnish, Brazilian Portuguese, Russian, and Latin American Spanish dubs, a narrator reads the episode title's translation as it appears, usually starting with "today we present" followed by the episode name (this was a common practice for cartoon dubs).
**In the Dutch, French, Hebrew, Icelandic, Korean, Brazilian Portuguese, and Russian dubs, the title is visually translated instead.
*In the French dub, the [[Plumber's Log]] is not used, leaving the intro without any dialogue in some instances. The Plumber's Log is also not used in the first four episodes of the Norwegian and Swedish VHS dubs, with "[[Rolling Down the River]]" being the first episode to use it. It is additionally not used in the first episode of the German dub, with "[[King Mario of Cramalot]]" being the first episode to use it. However, in both of these cases, Mario still gives a narration at the beginning of the episode.
*The Latin American Spanish dub does not refer to [[Super Mario Bros. Plumbing|Mario Brothers Plumbing]] under a consistent name, with names including ''Hermanos Mario Fontaneros'' ("Mario Brothers Plumbers"), ''Fontanería Hermanos Mario'' ("Mario Brothers Plumbing"), ''Mario Hermanos Fontaneros'' ("Mario Brothers Plumbers"), ''Fontanería de los Marios'' ("Marios Plumbing"), and ''Fontaneros Mario'' ("Mario Plumbers").

===Lost translations===
Despite the show's success and wide syndication, many translations of the show have become partially or entirely lost. Information on missing translations is as follows:
*Bulgarian: Only the majority of the episode "[[Koopa Klaus]]" and the first half of its accompanying live-action segment "[[Little Marios]]" have been found. The final thirty seconds of the episode is lost.
*Danish: Only the first seven episodes of the dub have been uploaded online. However, home media releases also include episodes 8–12, as well as "[[Pirates of Koopa]]" and "[[Two Plumbers and a Baby]]."
*French: Most French dubs of the live-action segments are lost, with only five having been recovered to date: "[[Neatness Counts]]," "[[All Steamed Up]]," "[[The Mario Monster Mash]]," "[[Bonkers From Yonkers]]," and "[[Santa Claus is Coming to Flatbush]]." However, the Canadian VHSes ''[[Super Mario Bros.: Volume 2]]'' and ''[[Super Mario Bros.: Volume 4]]'' also contain the segments "[[Day of the Orphan]]," "[[Marianne & Luigeena]]," "[[Bats in the Basement]]," and "[[Will the Real Elvis Please Shut Up!]]"
*Greek: Only "[[The Great BMX Race]]" and "Pirates of Koopa" have been found.
*Hebrew: Only a small clip of the {{wp|Channel 6 (Israel)|Channel 6}} version of the dub has been found.
*Icelandic: Only the first six episodes of the dub have been found.
*Indonesian: Only four episodes of the dub have been found: "[[Do You Princess Toadstool Take this Koopa...?]]," "[[Mighty McMario and the Pot of Gold]]," "[[Mario and the Red Baron Koopa]]," and "[[The Mark of Zero]]." "[[On Her Majesty's Sewer Service]]" and "[[Bad Rap]]" had also found, but were lost again when the YouTube channel hosting these two episodes was taken down.<ref>[https://web.archive.org/web/20241207082227/https://www.youtube.com/watch?app=desktop&v=GHj5mkZD6qA Super Mario Bros Super Show Indonesia Dub]</ref>
*Italian: Almost half of the episodes of this dub are lost. Of the 28 found, only 12 have their accompanying live-action segments also found. The episodes "[[The Pied Koopa]]," "[[The Unzappables]]," "[[The Trojan Koopa]]," and "[[Karate Koopa]]" only have the audio of the dub found, with the visuals using the original version of the episode, resulting in their title cards being lost. The episodes "[[The Provolone Ranger]]" and "[[Plummers Academy]]" are missing a large portion of the episode, with three to four minutes missing from the episode respectively. The episodes "[[Raiders of the Lost Mushroom]]," "[[Little Red Riding Princess]]," and "[[Crocodile Mario]]" are also missing portions of the episode, but not as much as the aforementioned two episodes, with the former missing the first fifty seconds of the episode and the latter two missing the last thirty seconds of the episode.
*Korean: Only the episodes "[[Stars in Their Eyes]]," and "Pirates of Koopa," with three live-action segments corresponding to unknown episodes also being found. However, eight VHSes of the dub are known to exist, which contain "[[The Bird! The Bird!]]," "[[Butch Mario & The Luigi Kid]]," "[[Mario and the Beanstalk]]," and "[[Love 'Em and Leave 'Em]]."
*Mandarin Chinese: The episodes "[[King Mario of Cramalot]]," "Little Red Riding Princess," "[[Escape from Koopatraz]]," and "[[Flatbush Koopa]]" are lost.
*Taiwanese Mandarin: Only the episodes "The Unzappables," "The Trojan Koopa," "[[Quest for Pizza]]," "Karate Koopa," "Raiders of the Lost Mushroom," and "[[Star Koopa]]" have been uploaded online. However, ten VHSes of the dub are known to exist, which contain at least nine more episodes that have yet to have surfaced on the Internet.
*Norwegian: Only the first twelve episodes of the {{wp|Fox Kids}} dub have been found.
*Polish: Only the {{wp|Top Kids}} voiceovers of "[[Rolling Down the River]]," "Mario and the Beanstalk," and "The Great BMX Race" have been found. The {{wp|Polsat}} voiceover is completely lost.
*Brazilian Portuguese: 45 of the 52 episodes of this dub have been found, making it one of the more complete dubs of the lost dubs based on episode count. However, the completeness of the actual episodes varies, from being entirely complete to being mostly lost with only the first half of the episode's live-action segment being recovered. The episodes in this dub that are completely lost include "[[Koopenstein]]," "Mario and the Red Baron Koopa," "[[The Koopas are Coming! The Koopas are Coming!]]," "Little Red Riding Princess," "Escape from Koopatraz," "Crocodile Mario," and "Star Koopa."
*Russian: Only the dubs of the first eight episodes and "[[Mario Meets Koop-zilla]]" have been found.
*European Spanish: Only the dub's [[Super Mario (song)|theme song]] has been found.
*Thai: 2 hours and 58 seconds of the dub have been found. However, the video containing the dub is inaccessible outside of Thailand.
*Turkish: Outside of a bootleg VCD,<ref>[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w2zUx_CCrlY Süper Gladyatör Mario | Türkçe Çizgi Film (+ başka bir çizgi film)]</ref> the entirety of the Turkish dub is lost, with the only proof of its existence being an entry on a blog.
