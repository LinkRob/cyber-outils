The '''Nintendo 3DS XL''' (titled '''Nintendo 3DS LL''' in Japan and '''iQue 3DS XL''' in China) is the second version of the [[Nintendo 3DS]] handheld. It was announced by [[Satoru Iwata]] in a ''[[Nintendo Direct]]'' video on June 22, 2012. The handheld was released in Japan and Europe on July 28, 2012, in North America on August 19, 2012, and in Oceania (bundled with an AC adapter) on August 23, 2012.<ref>Arktus (July 19, 2012). "[http://web.archive.org/web/20120829204451/http://www.aussie-nintendo.com/news/ac-adaptor-to-be-bundled-with-3ds-xl-in-aunz AC adaptors to be bundled with 3DS XL in AU/NZ]". aussie-nintendo (Wayback Machine). Retrieved October 17, 2023.</ref> In Japan and Europe, the Nintendo 3DS XL is not included with a battery charger, but it can still use the [[Nintendo 3DS AC Adapter]]. The handheld has its own charger in North America,<ref name=ign/> but it does not include the charging cradle packaged with the Nintendo 3DS. A few years after the Nintendo 3DS XL's release, another "XL" hardware revision to the Nintendo 3DS was made, the [[New Nintendo 3DS XL]], specifically for the [[New Nintendo 3DS]] model.

The Nintendo 3DS XL is based on the [[Nintendo DSi XL]] because, aside from sharing an "XL" prefix ("LL" in Japan), their main difference from the original Nintendo DSi and Nintendo 3DS models is a larger screen size, respectively. A Nintendo 3DS XL's upper screen measures 124 mm (4.88 inches) while the lower one measures 106 mm (4.18 inches), both being 90% larger than the standard Nintendo 3DS screens, making it tied to the New Nintendo 3DS XL as the biggest screen on a Nintendo handheld so far. Even though the screen is larger, the internal resolution is identical to the Nintendo 3DS's.

Besides a larger screen size, the Nintendo 3DS XL has some other differences aside from original Nintendo 3DS units. The Nintendo 3DS XL was given rounded corners and digital {{button|3ds|home}}, {{button|3ds|select}}, and {{button|3ds|start}} buttons instead of the embedded ones found on the Nintendo 3DS. The handheld also sports a single color job instead of the dual color job of its predecessor (black in the interior of the lid and a second main color overall), although it may also come with dual colors. The Nintendo 3DS XL is packaged with a 4 GB [[SD card]]. The Nintendo 3DS XL's battery has been optimized to last longer, as it lasts about 3.5 to six hours (283 to 485 mA) when playing Nintendo 3DS games, compared to the three to five hours (260 to 433 mA) of play on the Nintendo 3DS. The [[stylus]] was also changed to more closely resemble the stylus used for Nintendo DS systems, being a single, solid color and removing the extension feature on the normal model. The stylus for these versions is stored on the side of the handheld, similarly to the Nintendo DS Lite and Nintendo DSi.

The Nintendo 3DS XL received various themed models, such as a ''[[Mario (franchise)|Mario]]'' one styled after [[The Year of Luigi]], exclusively released in Japan.

==Events==
In late 2014, Nintendo of Japan announced their plan to discontinue the Nintendo 3DS XL model in Japan in 2015.<ref>Sahdev, Ishaan (November 30, 2014). "[https://www.siliconera.com/nintendo-discontinue-old-nintendo-3ds-xl-model-japan/ Nintendo To Discontinue Old Nintendo 3DS XL Model In Japan]". Siliconera. Retrieved October 17, 2023.</ref>

On February 27, 2021, Nintendo announced that it would no longer repair Nintendo 3DS and Nintendo 3DS XL consoles after March 31, 2021. However, on March 10, 2021, repairs ended early in Japan due to a shortage of parts.<ref>Stockdale, Henry (March 9, 2021). [https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2021/03/nintendo_will_stop_repairing_original_3ds_and_3ds_xl_consoles_next_month_due_to_parts_shortage Nintendo Will Stop Repairing Original 3DS and 3DS XL Consoles Next Month Due To Parts Shortage] ''Nintendo Life''. Retrieved October 17, 2023.</ref>
