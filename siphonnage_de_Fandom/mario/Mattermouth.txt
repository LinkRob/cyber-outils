{{Nihongo|'''Mattermouths'''|ガツガツ|Gatsugatsu|Gatsugatsu}} are enemies which first appear in ''[[Super Mario Galaxy 2]]''.

== Profile ==
Mattermouths resemble a [[Dry Bones]]' skull as well as that of a [[Bone Dragon]]. They are about as big as [[Mario]]. Mattermouths have jaws with square teeth, and inside their mouth is a purple void. Mattermouths have black eyes with yellow pupils.

==Appearances==
===''Super Mario Galaxy 2''===
[[File:SMG2 Haunty Halls Mattermouth Path.png|thumb|left|Mattermouths in Haunty Halls Galaxy in ''Super Mario Galaxy 2'']]
Mattermouths made their debut in ''[[Super Mario Galaxy 2]]''. They appear in the [[Haunty Halls Galaxy]] and [[Bowser's Lava Lair]]. If Mario falls into the hole Mattermouth creates as he destroys the ground, Mario will lose a life. Mattermouth will turn around if you stomp on his head as he is travelling, though this is risky. Mario will also lose some of his health if he touches these enemies elsewhere, such as getting caught in their path.
{{br|left}}
===''[[Mario Party: Island Tour]]''===
[[File:Mattermouth MPIT.png|thumb|left|Mattermouths in Tragic Carpet Ride in ''Mario Party: Island Tour'']]
Mattermouths appear in the minigame [[Tragic Carpet Ride]] in ''[[Mario Party: Island Tour]]''. It involves these enemies tearing away at a carpet with their feasting, hence the game's name. The players have to stand on the carpet's remains to score points (1 point per second). If they fall, they stop earning points. Whoever has the most points at the end (that is, the one who survives the longest) wins. Mattermouth will also stun anyone he collides with in this game.

==Behind the scenes==
===Etymology===
''Mattermouth'' is a portmanteau of ''matter'' and ''mouth''. It is also a pun of ''motormouth''. ''Matter'' is a physical substance that is made out of matter, it can be found anywhere on earth. ''Matter'' derives from Middle English by way of Old French from Latin. ''Materia'' is Latin for ''timber'' and ''substance''. ''Mater'' means ''mother'' in Latin. ''Mouth'' is the cavity of the lower part of the human face. It derives from the Old English word ''mūth'' of a Germanic origin, Dutch word ''mond'' and German word ''Mund''.
