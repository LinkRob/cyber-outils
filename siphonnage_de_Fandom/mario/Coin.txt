'''Coins''' (also called '''Mushroom coins''' in the [[Beanbean Kingdom]]) are the main currency of the [[Mushroom Kingdom]]. They can be collected in most [[Mario (franchise)|''Mario'' games]]. They first appear in ''[[Mario Bros. (video game)|Mario Bros.]]'' but their main and recurring role would be codified in ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]''

==Description==
[[File:SM64 Yellow Coin art.jpg|thumb|100px|A Yellow Coin]]
Coins have varying effects depending on the game type: in platformer games, they increase a player's score and grant [[extra life|extra lives]]; in racing games, they increase speed and recovery times; and in role-playing games, they can be used to purchase items, all among other uses.

Yellow Coins are the main types of coins found in the [[Mario (franchise)|''Mario'' franchise]]. They are found everywhere, ranging from floating in the air, acquiring them through blocks, or by defeating enemies. Generally in ''Super Mario'' side-scrolling games, they are worth 50, 100 or 200 points. Collecting either 50 or 100 in certain games will make the player earn an extra life. Yellow Coins are the primary normal coins in some games.

==Original appearances==
Coins have appeared in a few earlier series of the ''Mario'' franchise that predates their role in the {{series|Super Mario}}.

===''Mario Bros.'' series===
====''Mario Bros.''====
[[File:MB Coin sprite.gif|frame|left]]
Coins first appear in ''[[Mario Bros. (game)|Mario Bros.]]'' After Mario or [[Luigi]] defeat an enemy, the coins emerge the top pipes and slide along surfaces until they reach a bottom pipe. If obtained by either touching them or hitting them through the [[floor (Mario Bros.)|floor]], they give Mario or Luigi 800 points. They also appear in bonus games between several phases, in which ten midair ones are littered throughout the stage and can be reached with precise [[jump]]ing within a [[time limit]]. Collecting all ten results in a perfect bonus multiplier. A dollar sign ($) briefly appears whenever a coin is collected.

====''Mario Clash''====
In ''[[Mario Clash]]'', Coins appear in Bonus Chance, where collecting them is worth 300 [[point]]s each. If the player manages to collect every coin, they earn an extra life.

===''Wrecking Crew'' series===
====''VS. Wrecking Crew''====
[[File:Race for the Coin.png|thumb|250px|An event where Mario and Foreman Spike have to race for the coin in ''Wrecking Crew'']]
In the [[Bonus Stage (Wrecking Crew series)|bonus stage]]s of the arcade game ''[[VS. Wrecking Crew]]'', Mario must smash walls to find a hidden coin before the time runs out. In two-player mode, players compete in searching for the coin, and collecting it is required to complete the challenge.

====''Wrecking Crew''====
In the [[Nintendo Entertainment System]] port ''[[Wrecking Crew]]'', a CPU-controlled character called [[Foreman Spike]] competes against Mario during the bonus round, where Mario has to make it to the Coin before Foreman Spike in order to win the championship.

==Main appearances==
===''Super Mario Bros.''===
[[File:Coin SMB sprite.png|frame|left]]
[[File:Coins NES.png|thumb|Mario collecting Coins in a Coin Heaven in [[World 2-1 (Super Mario Bros.)|World 2-1]] of ''Super Mario Bros.'']]
In ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]'', coins are the most common items. They are found all over the terrain in every level, appearing not only in midair but also spawning from certain [[? Block]]s and [[Brick Block]]s. A dedicated type of Brick Block known as a [[Coin Block]] releases up to ten coins in succession, depending on how many times [[Mario]] or [[Luigi]] jumps at it. Each coin is worth 200 points when collected, and collecting 100 coins grants Mario or Luigi an extra life. The game also features [[sub-area]]s known as [[Coin Heaven]]s, which are filled with coins.

===''Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels''===
[[File:Coins NES Hard.png|thumb|left|Coins on a platform amidst the blowing [[wind]] in [[World 7-3 (Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels)|World 7-3]] in ''Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels''.]]
Coins return in ''[[Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels]]'', serving the same function as in ''Super Mario Bros.'' Since the game was designed to have higher difficulty than ''Super Mario Bros.'', Coins can be more scarce in latter points of the game and some are more trickier to obtain.
{{br|left}}
===''Super Mario Bros. 2''===
[[File:Coin SMB2 sprite.png|frame|left]]
[[File:Coin Find Dark.png|thumb|200px|Mario discovering a Coin in Subspace in ''Super Mario Bros. 2'']]
In ''[[Super Mario Bros. 2]]'', coins have a much different role than in ''Super Mario Bros.'' and the ''Super Mario'' series at large. Coins appear only in [[Subspace]], where the player character (Mario, Luigi, [[Princess Peach|Princess Toadstool]], or [[Toad (character)|Toad]]) can pluck a patch of [[grass]] to obtain a coin. However, the player character has to use a [[Magic Potion]] wherever there is grass and not pluck it outside of Subspace, otherwise they cannot collect the coin.

The functional purpose of coins is that they are used in the [[Bonus Chance]] after every [[level]], which gives the player a chance to win [[extra life|extra lives]] depending on how many slots they line up. Each coin forces the player to play a round of the Bonus Chance.

''[[Super Mario Advance]]'' introduces a new type of giant red coin called an [[Ace Coin]].
{{br}}
===''Super Mario Bros. 3''===
[[File:SMB3 Coin Sprite.gif|frame]]
[[File:Smb3 coin-heaven.png|thumb|left|Mario looking at coins in a Coin Heaven in ''Super Mario Bros. 3''.]]
Coins reappear in ''[[Super Mario Bros. 3]]''. Like in ''Super Mario Bros.'' and ''Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels'', Coins can be found in midair, ? Blocks, Brick Blocks, and Coin Heavens, and they reward Mario or Luigi an extra life when 100 of them are collected. Each coin is worth 50 points when collected, lower than in previous games. Another difference from before is that killing certain enemies with the effects of a [[Fire Flower]] can also cause them to drop a Coin as well. Coins can also be obtained in the [[Matching Game]].

In the player's status panel at the bottom of the screen, a dollar sign ($) is used to represent coins. This was changed to the ''[[Super Mario Land]]'' coin sprite in the [[reissue|remakes]].

[[P Switch|Switch Block]]s, introduced in this game, can turn [[Brick Block]]s into coins and vice versa; some Switch Blocks also cause a new type of coin, [[Silver Coin|Blue Coin]]s, to appear.

Coins are also found in the 2-Player [[Battle Game]], where a player must collect five coins to win.

===''Super Mario Land''===
[[File:Coin SML sprite.png|28px|left]]
[[File:Black and White Coins.png|thumb|200px|Mario collecting coins in a sub-area in ''Super Mario Land''.]]
In ''[[Super Mario Land]]'', Coins return as collectible items. Several of them are commonly found in secret areas in stashes, but they are also in the main levels as well, either on the main path or in ? Blocks. Each Coin is worth 100 points, slightly higher than in ''Super Mario Bros. 3'' but still slightly lower than in ''Super Mario Bros.''.

Coins have a different sound effect compared to the rest of the entire franchise, which is reminiscent of the [[Game Boy]] start-up jingle.
{{br}}

===''Super Mario World''===
{{quote|YOU ARE A SUPER PLAYER!!|Coins message in [[Funky (level)|Funky]]|Super Mario World}}
[[File:CoinSMW.gif|frame]]
[[File:Mario And Yoshi Coin Flight.png|thumb|Mario and Yoshi collecting coins in ''Super Mario World''.]]
In ''[[Super Mario World]]'', coins act in nearly the same manner as in the previous games. If a coin is collected from a ? Block, it is worth 10 points.

[[Fire Mario]] can turn enemies into coins by defeating them with [[fireball]]s, and the Coins move along the ground. These enemies can respawn offscreen if the coin is not collected. Jumping on a yellow [[Koopa Troopa]] forces a coin from its [[Koopa Shell|shell]]. Everything that [[Yoshi]] swallows also counts toward Mario's coin total.

If [[Mario]] or [[Luigi]] jumps on a [[Gray P Switch]], some types of enemies will turn into [[Gray Coin]]s. In addition to counting as regular coins, each coin collected gives multiplied points, up to 3-UP in the original game, or 5-UP in the [[Game Boy Advance]] port.

If Yoshi eats two [[Pink Berry|Pink Berries]] in the same level, he lays an [[Yoshi Egg|egg]] that hatches a [[Yoshi Cloud]], which throws smiley-faced [[Bonus Coin]]s. Bonus Coins can fall through the floor after bouncing once, making them slightly tricky to catch. If all ten Bonus Coins are collected, the Yoshi Cloud throws a [[1-Up Mushroom]] before flying away.

The game also introduces [[Dragon Coin]]s, which are large coins with an oval shape and a profile of Yoshi on them. If Mario collects all of them in a level, he gets an extra life. In ''[[Super Mario World: Super Mario Advance 2]]'', Dragon Coins appear in every level, and if Mario collects them all, the Dragon Coins are replaced by [[Peach Coin]]s, which serve the same function.

===''Super Mario Land 2: 6 Golden Coins''===
[[File:Coin SML2 sprite.png|26px|thumb|left]]
[[File:Coins Gray.png|thumb|200px|Coins in ''Super Mario Land 2: 6 Golden Coins'']]
In ''[[Super Mario Land 2: 6 Golden Coins]]'', Coins are collected through the usual means of either [[? Block]]s, [[Brick Block]]s, or in midair. Coins have a different purpose now, being used to purchase extra lives and power-ups through a [[Casino|gambling minigame]], with stats that can be changed depending on how many coins Mario has spent.
{{br}}
===''Super Mario 64''===
[[File:Yellow Coin SM64.gif|left|thumb]]
[[File:Coin Flight.png|thumb|Mario flying toward a ring of coins in the Tower of the Wing Cap in ''Super Mario 64''.]]
Coins, also named '''Yellow Coins''',<ref>''Super Mario 64'' English instruction booklet, page 18.</ref><ref>''Nintendo Official Magazine'' (UK) issue 54, page 22.</ref> once again appear commonly throughout ''[[Super Mario 64]]'' and its remake, ''[[Super Mario 64 DS]]''. Unlike in its preceding games, coins have a star-shaped symbol. Coins restore Mario's lost health as well as his oxygen when he [[swim]]s underwater. Unlike in previous games, Mario gains an extra life for every 50 coins he collects each time he completes a level, but only up to three lives (no more than 150 coins). Collecting 100 coins in one of the 15 main courses grants Mario a [[Power Star]]. The score screen also determines the highest coin total for the 15 main courses.

Most enemies drop coins when defeated. Weak enemies, such as [[Goomba]]s and [[Bob-omb]]s, only grant one Coin. [[Scuttlebug]]s and [[Mr. Blizzard]]s grant three Coins. Stronger enemies, such as [[Chuckya]]s, [[Spindrift]]s, and [[Moneybag]]s, will grant five Coins. Besides enemies, coins can also be obtained by smashing [[Brick Block]]s and crates.

Some coins are arranged in the shape of rings and usually contain a coin within the center. In [[Bob-omb Battlefield]], collecting the coin in the center of each of the five coin rings is required to reveal the [[Power Star]] of the [[mission]] [[Mario Wings to the Sky]].

[[Red Coin]]s also appear in this game and are worth two Yellow Coins, and collecting eight of them in a course spawns a Power Star. [[Blue Coin]]s are also found in the courses; these are much rarer as they are worth five Yellow Coins.
{{br}}

===''Super Mario Sunshine''===
[[File:SMS Coin Artwork.png|thumb|left|200px|A Yellow Coin from ''Super Mario Sunshine'']]
[[File:Coins Underwater.png|thumb|Mario swimming to some coins underwater in ''Super Mario Sunshine''.]]
Coins are seen again in ''[[Super Mario Sunshine]]'', where they are found all over the game's many courses. Once again, they heal [[Mario]]'s health or air, and Mario earns an [[extra life]] with every 50 Coins he collects. If Mario obtains 100 Coins in one of the game's seven main courses or [[Delfino Plaza]], he can obtain a secret [[Shine Sprite]]. Also, just as in ''Super Mario 64'', top coin scores are once again tracked for each course, but with a Shine Sprite icon next to the count if Mario succeeded in reaching the 100-coin mark and collected the Shine Sprite that comes with it.

Certain enemies also give Coins upon defeat, with some yielding either one, two, or three. Enemies such as [[Pokey Sprout]]s have two different Coin awards depending on how they are defeated: if they are stomped, they only give one Coin, but if they are sprayed into a wall with [[F.L.U.D.D.|FLUDD]], they grant three Coins. Additionally, the sand on the beaches of [[Pinna Park]]'s entrance reveals Coins whenever sprayed in certain locations.

[[Red Coin]]s also return in this game, making a [[Shine Sprite]] appear once eight are collected, though they do not add to the overall Coin count this time. [[Blue Coin]]s also return, but their role is much different and more essential than in ''Super Mario 64'', because Mario has to regularly trade ten of them at the [[Boathouse]] for a Shine Sprite.

===''Super Mario Advance 4: Super Mario Bros. 3''===
[[File:SMA4 Animated Coin Sprite.gif|frame|left]]
In ''[[Super Mario Advance 4: Super Mario Bros. 3]]'', coins have the same role as in the original ''Super Mario Bros. 3''. However, there is an [[Orange Switch]] e-Reader card that, when activated, turns enemies into moving coins if hit by Fire Mario's fireballs, a mechanic ported from ''Super Mario World'' and its Game Boy Advance remake. Also, thrown objects (like shells or [[Ice Block]]s) can pick up coins, being worth double if obtained this way. Exclusive to [[World-e]] levels are [[Advance Coin]]s, which work like the Dragon Coins in ''Super Mario World''.

===''Super Mario 64 DS''===
[[File:CoinSM64DS.png|thumb|left|A coin in ''Super Mario 64 DS'']]
[[File:Coin Secrets.png|thumb|Mario approaching a ring of coins in Hazy Maze Cave in ''Super Mario 64 DS'']]
In ''[[Super Mario 64 DS]]'', Coins are functionally similar as in the original ''Super Mario 64'', but their largest difference is with [[Yoshi (character)|Yoshi]], introduced as a player character. If Yoshi [[swallow]]s certain enemies, he can turn them into [[Yoshi Egg|eggs]] which break into a Coin upon impact. The Coin that appears is equal to how many Coins the enemy would have provided if defeated as usual. The eggs home in and ricochet off nearby enemies, granting more Coins. Therefore, if Yoshi swallows an enemy that provides a high number of Coins (such as a [[Moneybag]]) and tosses it at a group of other enemies, he can collect Coins from not only the enemies hit by the egg but also the Coin reward equal to the Moneybag's value each time, which can heavily multiply the total Coin amount. Smaller enemies that cannot be made into eggs, such as [[Micro Goomba]]s, [[Swooper]]s, and [[Fly Guy]]s, immediately provide a Coin or two upon being swallowed by Yoshi. Yoshi can also grab Coins by sticking his tongue out.

Instead of being animated 2D sprites like in ''Super Mario 64'', Coins are now fully modeled in 3D and look octagonal as a result.

Coins also have a role in some of the minigames not part of the main adventure, like [[Coincentration]].

===''New Super Mario Bros.''===
[[File:Coin Spinning NSMB.gif|frame|left]]
[[File:Coin Lines.png|thumb|Mario near some Coins and a Starman in ''New Super Mario Bros.'']]
In ''[[New Super Mario Bros.]]'', Coins are featured in many of the same ways as before. They appear in several locations, including along the levels, inside [[Question Block]]s and [[Brick Block]]s, and in [[Coin Heaven]]s. Each Coin collected earns the player 100 points, the same as in ''[[Super Mario Land]]''. As usual, collecting 100 Coins rewards [[Mario]] or [[Luigi]] with an extra life. Defeating enemies while under the effects of a [[Fire Flower]] can also cause them to drop Coins. [[P Switch]] turn all Brick Blocks in the area into Coins and vice versa, as usual, and [[Silver Coin]]s can also appear in some cases.

Coins are also featured in a few additional capacities. [[Moneybag]]s drop Coins each time they are hit. In underwater levels, some Coins are also seen floating around in [[bubble]]s. It is also the first game outside of the {{franchise|Yoshi}} to feature [[Hidden Coin]]s, which appear as outlines of coins until touched, becoming regular coins afterward.

''New Super Mario Bros.'' is the first 2D game to feature [[Red Coin]]s, with a set of eight appearing temporarily from [[Red Ring]]s. Collecting all eight spawns a power-up item or a [[1-Up Mushroom]]. The game also introduces a required collectible, [[Star Coin]], of which there are three to collect in every level.
{{br}}

===''Super Mario Galaxy''===
[[File:Battlerock Galaxy coins SMG1.png|thumb|left|Mario collecting coins in [[Battlerock Galaxy]] from ''Super Mario Galaxy'']]
In ''[[Super Mario Galaxy]]'', coins appear throughout the various different [[Galaxy|galaxies]]. Unlike past games, Coins have become slightly more rare due to being relegated as a secondary collectible, with [[Star Bit]]s being the main collectibles instead.

Coins can be found in several ways: inside [[Item Crystal]]s, [[Question Block]]s, and by shooting Star Bits at various ground-based objects such as [[lantern]]s and [[Flower (Super Mario series)|flowers]]. They can also be found by defeating certain enemies with stomps, such as [[Goomba]]s, [[Electrogoomba]]s, and [[Piranha Plant]]s. Other enemies—including [[Micro Goomba]]s, [[Topman]], [[Brrr Bit]]s, [[Burn Bit]]s, [[Mechakoopa]]s, and [[Preying Manta]]s—always give Coins upon defeat.

Like in previous 3D titles, Coins can heal [[Mario]] or [[Luigi]] if they take damage as well as replenish their air supply while underwater. Collecting Coins will update the high score, with every 50 collected awarding an extra life. Unlike previous 3D ''Mario'' games, collecting 100 Coins does not reveal a secret [[Power Star]].

The top coin scores in each course are tracked on the Score screen, as in ''Super Mario 64'' and ''Super Mario Sunshine'', but also the top coin scores for each individual Power Star are tracked on the Star select screen for each course. If Mario or Luigi lose a life in a [[mission]], the Coin count resets to zero.

Coins have an added function in which they give [[Bee Mario]]/[[Bee Luigi]] more flight time.

''Super Mario Galaxy'' introduces two types of coins. The [[? Coin]]s are larger and cause miscellaneous effects, such as spawning a series of [[Musical Note]]s or producing a trail of Star Bits. There is also [[Purple Coin]]s, and some missions involve having to collect 100 of them to obtain a [[Power Star]], a role that regular coins had in earlier 3D ''Super Mario'' titles.

===''New Super Mario Bros. Wii''===
[[File:NSMBW Coin Screenshot.png|thumb|Mini Mario collecting coins in ''New Super Mario Bros. Wii'']]
[[File:NSMBW Coin Sprite.png|frame|left]]
In ''[[New Super Mario Bros. Wii]]'', coins return as collectible items. They have much of the same role as in ''New Super Mario Bros.'', being not only spread throughout the levels and Coin Heavens but also inside [[? Block]]s and [[Brick Block]]s. Just as before, underwater levels have some Coins floating around in [[bubble]]s.

Coins are spawned when enemies are defeated by the effects of a [[Fire Flower]], or when enemies frozen by the effects of an [[Ice Flower]] or [[Penguin Suit]] are shattered. Some enemies that self-destruct, such as [[Ghost Block]]s and [[Ghost Vase]]s, also reveal Coins upon shattering. In some cases, Coins can be made to appear by standing in certain locations (mostly in niches).

Whenever a [[P Switch]] is stood on, some Brick Blocks may become Coins and vice versa, just as before. Spinning next to a [[Flower (Super Mario series)|flower]] can also cause a Coin to spring from it. During [[Kamek]]'s boss fight, he may also use his magic to turn a platform into coins. Additionally, [[Lakitu]]s spawned from [[Cloud Block]]s will toss Coins down instead of the hazardous [[Spiny Egg]]s. Coins can also be attained during the credits, and these Coins go towards a score that can be topped at any time.

Coins also play a more central role in [[Coin Battle]] mode. In this game mode, four players battle to collect the most Coins in a level, and whoever finishes a round with the most is declared the winner.

===''Super Mario Galaxy 2''===
[[File:CoinSMG2.png|thumb|left|Mario collecting coins in ''Super Mario Galaxy 2''.]]
[[File:Giant Coin What.png|thumb|The Giant Coin in the Supermassive Galaxy in ''Super Mario Galaxy 2'']]
Coins are a lot more common in ''[[Super Mario Galaxy 2]]'' than in the first game, now appearing all over the various galaxies. The methods for obtaining coins are otherwise similar to: inside [[Item Crystal]]s, [[Question Block]]s, by stomping on certain enemies, and by shooting Star Bits at a Lantern. Enemies such as [[Mandibug]]s, [[Topman]], and [[Preying Manta]]s also always reward Coins upon defeat.

Just as before, Coins can be used to restore lost health points and restore air while underwater. They also allow for longer flight time while under the effects of a [[Bee Mushroom]]. Just as before, the Coin count resets to zero if the plumbers die.

Unlike the previous game, every 100 Coins is required to earn an extra life rather than 50. The Coin scores, and the extra life per every 50 coins, are no longer tracked individually. Instead, the Coins that Mario or Luigi collect during missions are added to their total on [[Starship Mario]] alongside Star Bits. On Starship Mario, Mario can feed 100 Coins to the second [[Lumalee]] for a shot at spinning five [[Chance Cube]]s. One of the possible turnouts on a Chance Cube is a coin, and if it lands with its coin side face-up, several coins appear for Mario or Luigi to collect.

Coins can also be used to feed [[Coin Luma]]s, a coin-craving variety of the [[Hungry Luma]] that transforms into a planet leading to a hidden [[Power Star]] upon being fed the required Coins.

In addition to the returning ? Coins and Purple Coins, the game also introduces another type of coin, [[Comet Medal]]s, which cause comets to appear in galaxies when collected. There is also a giant Coin near the start of the [[Supermassive Galaxy]] that Mario must [[Wall Jump|wall-jump]] off of to reach the top of a [[Mega ? Block]]. The giant coin still spins like a regular coin, except slower. The giant coin cannot be collected, but various pre-release builds of ''Super Mario Galaxy 2'' actually did include larger Coins in Supermassive Galaxy that could be collected.

===''Super Mario 3D Land''===
[[File:3D Coin Heaven.png|thumb|Invincible Mario collecting coins in a Coin Heaven in ''Super Mario 3D Land''.]]
Coins reappear in ''[[Super Mario 3D Land]]'' once again as a common collectible. Like many other elements, coins retain their core functions from the 2D titles of the ''Super Mario'' series. They are often located around the overworld, in ? Blocks, and Brick Blocks. 100 Coins now granting [[Mario]] or [[Luigi]] an extra life.

Usually, when an enemy is defeated, Mario or Luigi collects the coin automatically. If enemies are hit with a projectile, they drop the Coin, which Mario or Luigi have to collect themselves. Enemies that spawn indefinitely, such as those from [[Baddie Box]]es, stop giving out Coins after three enemies from the supply are defeated. [[Coin Coffer]]s also make their first appearance, and they provide many Coins upon defeat. Coins can be obtained through the use of [[Coin Box]]es, which provide Coins as Mario or Luigi run while wearing one.

Coins can commonly be found in formations. In some cases, grabbing a suspiciously lone Coin will cause a path of other Coins to appear, which typically leads to a secret. Coins can also be spawned in this manner by hitting a [[P Switch]]. The game also introduces [[Coin Ring]]s, which give 10 Coins when jumped through.

Coins are also the functional replacement of points from earlier 2D ''Super Mario'' titles. For level clear rewards, tagging the [[Flagpole]] anywhere below the top will give Mario or Luigi a range of 1–10 Coins, depending on how high they grabbed on. At the end of every level except for {{world-link|8|bowser|World 8-Bowser: Part 2}} 2, one coin is gained for every 10 seconds remaining on the [[Time Limit]] counter.

The game introduces another type of coin called [[Star Medal]]s, which are required collectibles for unlocking levels. [[Red Coin]]s also return, but only five of them are spawned from each [[Red Ring]].

===''New Super Mario Bros. 2''===
[[File:NSMB2Coin.gif|frame|left]]
Coins are seen again in ''[[New Super Mario Bros. 2]]'', where they are heavily emphasized and much more abundant than in other games. Aside from several additions to their role, Coins still have largely the same function as before. 100 Coins once again grant [[Mario]] or [[Luigi]] an extra life. Mario or Luigi can still obtain coins through all the usual methods: all over the terrain, inside [[Question Block]]s and [[Brick Block]]s, in [[Coin Heaven]]s, and being dropped by enemies hit by one of [[Fire Mario]]'s [[fireball]]s. In addition, hitting a [[P Switch]] turns most Brick Blocks into Coins and vice versa, while also making [[Silver Coin|Blue Coin]]s appear. Dash Coins, Red Coins, and Star Coins all return with the same functions as in ''New Super Mario Bros.'' Coins can still appear inside of bubbles in underwater levels.

The game also introduces several special collection methods. [[Gold Block]]s appear whenever Mario or Luigi successfully hits a Coin Block in full. If he jumps into a Gold Block, he wears it as headgear that grants him Coins as he runs and moves, nearly matching the effect of the [[Coin Box]] in ''Super Mario 3D Land'', except Gold Blocks can only release up to 100 coins before disappearing. If a Gold Block is ground-pounded, the coins fly out rapidly instead. [[Gold Ring]]s also appear, temporarily transforming all enemies into golden versions of themselves, which provide Coins in different ways depending on the enemy type. [[Roulette Coin Block]]s release a number of Coins matching the number displayed at the time of being hit, including 5, 10, 30, and 50. Hitting one of the latter three numbers releases coins in denominations of [[10-Coin|10]]. There is also the rare [[100-Coin]], which can be released from larger Roulette Coin Blocks, although the larger variety can still land on a 10 or a 50 instead. A [[Flying Gold Block]] follows Mario or Luigi around on the world map, appearing in the next level they select for use with every 500 Coins collected. Coins, or more directly [[Star Coin]]s, also have a [[Moon Coin]] variety, found exclusively in [[World Star (New Super Mario Bros. 2)|World Star]].

The game's main debuting powered up form, [[Gold Mario]], have the ability to shoot Gold Fireballs, which explode on impact and turn any Brick Blocks hit into Coins. Any enemies caught within the Gold Fireball's blast radius also get destroyed and provide multiple Coins, the amount of which multiplies the more enemies caught in the blast.
[[File:Its Raining Coins.png|thumb|Several coins raining down to Luigi in ''New Super Mario Bros. 2''.]]
Coins can also burst from the ground and from [[Pipe]]s in fountain-like supplies by triggering certain events, such as collecting a [[Star Coin]] or hitting a [[Question Switch]]. Furthermore, Coin masses can also burst from the ground when standing in certain spots, such as niches.

The game features a prominent coin counter that tracks all Coins collected across all save files and game modes, which increases even when dying after collecting them (as opposed to only upon level completion). One primary goal is collect 1,000,000 coins total, which awards the player with a gold Mario statue in the title screen surrounded by Coins. Upon collecting 9,999,999 Coins, this changes again to a Gold Raccoon Mario statue. When playing a level, a separate counter tracks the Coins collected since the player last entered, and each level also tracks the highest number of Coins collected in a single run, similar to ''[[Super Mario 64]]''.

Finally, the Coin Rush mode challenges the player to collect the most Coins in three levels without dying. This mode offers multiplied rewards: every Star Coin or Moon Coin collected adds to the Coin count (10 Coins for the first, 20 Coins for the second, and 50 Coins for the third). Additionally, getting the top of the [[Goal Pole]] (as well as beating a boss) doubles the current Coin amount. Players also earn bonus Coins for the time remaining upon the level's completion. Contributing further to the mode's coin theme, 1-Up Mushrooms are replaced with [[Gold Mushroom]]s, which provide 50 bonus Coins.

===''New Super Mario Bros. U''===
[[File:NSMBUCoin.gif|thumb]]
[[File:The Coin Trail Sky.png|thumb|left|Mario seeing a Coin trail in a sub-area of [[Acorn Plains Way]] in ''New Super Mario Bros. U''.]]
Coins reappear in ''[[New Super Mario Bros. U]]''. As usual, coins appear throughout the games' many levels, as well as inside [[? Block]]s and [[Brick Block]]s, in [[Coin Heaven]]s, and from the effect of activating a P Switch. The game still retains the usual mechanic of collecting 100 Coins for an [[extra life]]. P Switches can still turn Coins into Brick Blocks and vice versa. Just as before, killing an enemy while under the effects of a [[Fire Flower]] causes it to drop a Coin, while shattering an enemy frozen by the effects of an [[Ice Flower]] or [[Penguin Suit]] also causes it to drop one.

While Coins can also appear when standing in a niche, they can also occasionally burst from [[Pipe]]s, the ceilings, or the ground. Coins can also spring out of [[Flower (Super Mario series)|flowers]] if the player character spins next to them. Coins can also be found within [[bubble]]s outside of underwater levels. Coins are sometimes arranged in such a way that requires [[Flying Squirrel Mario]] to [[glide]] to have a chance at collecting every coin. Some coins fall while attached to [[parachute]]s, similar to some of the Red Coins from ''New Super Mario Bros. 2'', and are found in groups of up to three. Coins also appear during the minigames featured in the [[1-Up Mushroom]] [[Toad House]]s.

Red Coins, Silver Coins, Dash Coins, and Star Coins also make an appearance, retaining their roles from earlier ''New Super Mario Bros.'' titles. The game introduces the [[Green Coin]] variety, spawned from [[Green Ring]]s, and collecting all five sets of Green Coins spawns a power-up item, making them essentially the same as Red Coins. 

===''Super Mario 3D World''===
[[File:Coin Jump 3D World.png|thumb|Coins appear in ''Super Mario 3D World'']]
Coins reappear in ''[[Super Mario 3D World]]'' once again as a common collectible, found all over the mainland, as well as inside [[? Block]]s and [[Brick Block]]s. Furthermore, [[point]]s are generally reinstated, as in the ''New Super Mario Bros.'' games. Collecting 100 Coins yields an [[extra life]], continuing the precedent set in ''Super Mario Bros.'' The game also contains invisible Coins that can be revealed by walking amidst them, similarly to Dash Coins, or by using the [[Wii U GamePad]].

Similar to ''Super Mario 3D Land'', defeating an enemy yields a Coin that is automatically collected. However, if the enemy is hit by a projectile, the Coin gets dropped and must be collected in the traditional way. [[Coin Coffer]]s also return and provide many Coins upon defeat.

There are also large stacks of coins known as [[Coin Pile]]s, and collecting these yields the corresponding number of Coins. For special collection objects, [[Coin Ring]]s make a reappearance but this time only give three Coins. [[Gold Shell]]s also appear in this game, and these release Coins that are automatically collected as the shells slide.

There is also a secret level in [[World 5 (Super Mario 3D World)|World 5]] called [[Coin Express]], which is filled with Coins and Coin Piles, but players only get one attempt at this level. [[Lucky House]]s offer opportunities to win Coins depending on the number of rows matched: no matches yields 10 Coins, one match yields 100 Coins, two matches yield 200 Coins, three matches yield 300 Coins, and matching all icons yields a whopping 777 Coins.

For special coin varieties, [[Red Ring]]s return, once again causing eight [[Red Coin]]s to appear. Collecting all eight yields either a power-up item or a [[1-Up Mushroom]]. [[P-Switch]]es also return in this game, once again spawning [[Blue Coin]]s. In addition, [[Green Star Ring]]s also appear, which cause [[Green Star Coin]]s to appear, and collecting all of them awards a [[Green Star]].

===''Super Mario Maker'' / ''Super Mario Maker for Nintendo 3DS''===
[[File:Coin Maker.png|thumb|Mario collecting Coins in the ''[[Super Mario Bros.]]'' style.]]
In ''[[Super Mario Maker]]'' and ''[[Super Mario Maker for Nintendo 3DS]]'', Coins are a placeable object and can be applied to any theme or style. Coins can be placed along the terrain, in the air, or inside [[Question Block]]s and [[Brick Block]]s. They can also be placed inside [[Bill Blaster]]s and [[Pipe]]s, in which case the two endlessly shoot out Coins. [[Lakitu]]s can also be made to throw down Coins by dragging a Coin onto a Lakitu. [[Parachute]]s can also be applied to Coins. Defeating enemies with the effects of a [[Fire Flower]] also cause them to drop a Coin.

As of the software update released on March 9, 2016, the player can shake a Coin to turn it into a [[Pink Coin (Super Mario Maker)|Pink Coin]].

===''Super Mario Run''===
[[File:Mario Coin Run.png|thumb|left|250px|Mario magnetically attracts the Coins while under the effects of a [[Starman]].]]
In ''[[Super Mario Run]]'', Coins can be found all over the levels, inside [[Question Block]]s, [[Brick Block]]s, and more. Beating enemies also yields Coins (an amount which can increase the more enemies are cleared in a row). While [[Mario]] is under the effects of the [[Starman]], he magnetically attracts any nearby Coins. Upon a level's completion, Coins are awarded based on how high up the [[Flagpole]] [[Mario]] jumps (1–10 Coins) and how much time was remaining on the clock. Coins can be used to buy items in the shop, such as houses, characters, and decor for [[Kingdom Builder]]. Coins are also counted, alongside [[Toad (species)|Toads]], to determine the winner of a game of [[Toad Rally]]. There have been multiple events that have multiplied the victor's Coins by 1.5 at the end of the round.

In each level, five [[Pink Coin]]s can be found. The Pink Coins take up the role of the [[Star Coin]]s found throughout the game. Once five are collected, [[Purple Coin]]s appear; Purple Coins are typically trickier to obtain than Pink Coins, and some are found inside [[Question Block]]s. Once these are found, [[Black Coin]]s appear. These are the hardest to find/obtain as they are usually in tricky locations, but collecting all of them will fully complete a level 100%.

In [[Remix 10]], a bubbled item appear for every 100 Coins collected (a [[Super Mushroom]] if [[Mario]] is [[Small Mario]], and a [[Super Star]] if he is [[Super Mario (form)|Super Mario]]).

===''Super Mario Odyssey''===
[[File:Bowser Castle Treasure Vault SMO.png|thumb|Stacks of coins in the [[Bowser's Kingdom#Bonus areas|Treasure Vault]] of [[Bowser's Castle]].]]
In ''[[Super Mario Odyssey]]'', Coins are a common collectible found all over the various kingdoms and inside [[Question Block]]s. Their main role acknowledges them being a currency, as rather than granting [[Mario]] extra lives (since lives are entirely absent from this game), Coins are primarily used as currency for purchasing items, costumes, [[Power Moon]]s, and other items in the [[Crazy Cap]] stores. Coins no longer refill any lost [[Health Meter|health]], a role that has gone to the [[Heart (item)|Heart]]s, further cementing coins having the role of currency.
[[File:Coin Dash Odyssey.png|thumb|left|Mario dashes to collect coins in ''Super Mario Odyssey'']]
Mario can obtain coins by grabbing them himself, by using the [[Cappy]] capture ability, and by beating enemies. [[Coin Ring]]s and Coin Piles also return. Additionally, invisible Coins return, which can be revealed by walking amidst them or by using Cappy. [[Coin Coffer]]s also make a return, once again providing heaps of coins upon defeat. If Mario [[capture]]s a Coin Coffer, he collects coins as he moves and can also spit coins at other enemies as a projectile, although this does subtract one Coin from his total.

If Mario dies in this game, instead of losing a life, he loses 10 coins. However, Mario can still collect the coins in the immediate area where he died before they disappear, but if Mario died in [[lava]], a [[pit]], or [[Poisoned Water]], he cannot obtain the coins again.

This game also introduces [[regional coin]]s, which are purple and can only be spent in the kingdoms in which they are found.

===''Super Mario Maker 2''===
In ''[[Super Mario Maker 2]]'', coins and Pink Coins reappear, and [[10-Coin]]s, [[30-Coin]]s, and [[50-Coin]]s are introduced as usable course elements. Coins are also used to progress in [[Story Mode]], as they fund the reconstruction of [[Peach's Castle]]. As of the version 2.0 update, coins can now be [[Frozen Coin|encased in ice]], being solid blocks until they are heated with fire-based attacks or enemies.

===''Super Mario Bros. 35''===
In ''[[Super Mario Bros. 35]]'', Coins could be collected and used for the Item Roulette.

===''Super Mario 3D World + Bowser's Fury''===
In ''[[Super Mario 3D World + Bowser's Fury]]'', aside from retaining their role in the main adventure, Coins also appear in ''[[Bowser's Fury]]'', where collecting 100 of them grants the player a random [[List of power-ups|power-up]] instead of an extra life (since lives are not present in the campaign). These power-ups include the [[Super Bell]], [[Super Mushroom]], [[Boomerang Flower]], [[Fire Flower]], [[Super Leaf]], and [[Super Star]]. As of version 1.1.0, collecting 100 coins in the introduction before leaving [[Fur Step Island]] only rewards a Super Mushroom.

===''Super Mario Bros. Wonder''===
In ''[[Super Mario Bros. Wonder]]'', Coins act as they do in previous games. Their choppier spinning animation is similar to those found in older games, like ''[[Super Mario Bros. 3]]'' and ''[[Super Mario World]]''.
