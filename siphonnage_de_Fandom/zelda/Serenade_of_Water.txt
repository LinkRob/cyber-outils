The "{{Term/Store|Serenade of Water||Series, OoT, OoT3D}}" is a recurring {{Term|Series|Song|link}} in {{TLoZ|Series}}.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= E |publisher= |page= 96 {{Exp Game|OoT}}}}</ref>

==Overview==
==={{OoT|-}}===
[[File:OoT3D Serenade of Water Icon.png|left]]
The "Serenade of Water" is one of the [[warping]] songs [[Link]] learns as an adult in {{OoT}}. It is taught to him by [[Sheik]] after obtaining the [[Iron Boots]] in the {{Term|OoT3D|Ice Cavern|link}}, and enables him to easily warp to [[Lake Hylia]], where the {{Term|OoT3D|Water Temple|link}} is located.

==={{MM|-}}===
In {{MM}}, the "Serenade of Water"'s notes sometimes appear on a wall in the northern part of [[Termina Field]], where Link can play a song for [[Rupee]]s. 

==={{TP|-}}===
In {{TP}}, the serenade can be heard as the background music for the two instances when Link encounters the ghost of the deceased [[Rutela]]. The aforementioned two moments are when the throne of [[Zora's Domain]] is found totally frozen, and after escorting [[Ralis]] from [[Hyrule Castle Town]] to [[Kakariko Village]]. It also plays briefly after Ralis gives Link the [[Coral Earring]] and states his decision to return to Zora's Domain.

==Other Appearances==
{{Noncanon|Start}}
==={{SSBB|-}}===
The "Serenade of Water" can be heard at the end of the "[[Song of Storms]]" track alongside with [[Ganondorf]]'s theme in {{SSBB}}.

==={{HW|-}}===
Sheik can play the "Serenade of Water" with her [[Harp]] Weapon to summon a barrier of water to protect her from damage in {{HW}}.

==={{SSB4|-}}===
The "Serenade of Water" can be heard near the end of the "Song of Storms" track in {{SSB4}}<!-- Composed by Tsukasa Masuko -->.

==={{SSBU|-}}===
The "Serenade of Water" appears as part of the"Song of Storms" track in {{SSBU}}.
{{Noncanon|End}}

==Nomenclature==
A serenade, or "serenata" is a musical composition or performance in someone's honor. Serenades are typically calm, light music. The word Serenade is derived from the word "sereno," which means calm, making it fitting to the smooth and relaxing tempo of the "Serenade of Water."
