'''Kotake''' and '''Koume''',<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= E |publisher= |page= 239,{{Exp Game|OoT}} 244,{{Exp Game|MM}} 250,{{Exp Game|OoS}} & 254 {{Exp Game|OoA}}}}</ref> collectively known as {{Term/Store|Twinrova||Series, OoT, OoT3D, OoS, OoA}},<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= E |page= 210 {{Exp Game|OoT, OoT3D, OoS, OoA}}}}</ref> are a recurring pair of [[Gerudo]] twin witches in {{TLoZ|Series}}. They are known as the Sorceress of Ice and the Sorceress of Flame, respectively.<ref name="Sorceress of Flame"/><ref name="Sorceress of Ice"/> As the surrogate mothers of [[Ganondorf]], the king of the Gerudo race as well as the human form of [[Ganon]], they are among his more devoted, powerful servants.<ref>{{Cite|Maybe we should make her work for the great Ganondorf for a little while longer! Ho ho ho! Then we should brainwash her again! Hee hee hee!|Twinrova|OoT}}</ref> In ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]'', they serve as the [[boss]] of the [[Spirit Temple]]. They later appear as non-malicious market employees in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask|Majora's Mask]]'', but return as antagonists once again in the ''Oracle'' games.

In {{OoT}}, Kotake and Koume are a pair of 400-year-old twin witches who secretly lead the Gerudo tribe,<ref>{{Cite|But I'm only 400 years old!|Koume|OoT}}</ref><ref>{{Cite Book|quote=It could be said that the sisters, who have lived for over four hundred years, rule the tribe from behind the scenes.|book=HH|page=90}}</ref> and are the surrogate mothers of Ganondorf.<ref name="Sorceress of Flame"/><ref name="Sorceress of Ice"/> They maintain their long lives by using their powerful black magic.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote=Kotake and Koume, about four hundred years old at the time of Ganondorf's rise, are said to maintain their long lives by way of powerful magic.|book=E|publisher=Dark Horse Books|page=45}}</ref> They both have green, wrinkled skin and dress in black robes with white Gerudo patterns near the hems, with an obi-like belt around their waists. Kotake's association with ice is noted by a blue jewel on her forehead and blue slippers. The markings on her headband are in red with a blue border, whereas these distinctions are red on Koume to highlight her association with fire.

They first appear outside the [[Spirit Temple]], just after Child [[Link]] obtains the [[Silver Gauntlets]]. They kidnap [[Nabooru]], who they subsequently brainwash and dressed in an [[Iron Knuckle]] armor.<ref>{{Cite|Like I suspected, Nabooru was brainwashed by Ganondorf's followers...|Carpenter|OoT}}</ref> After Link defeats her as an adult, he fights Kotake and Koume inside the boss room of the Spirit Temple. After being defeated, halos appear over their heads and they are absorbed by a column of light,<ref>{{Cite|Hey, Koume, what is that above your head?|Kotake|OoT}}</ref> presumably passing to the afterlife.

Koume, likely the older twin sister, tends to act as the leader of the two, usually appearing first and deciding what their course of action should be. She is the first to introduce herself during the boss battle and is also the one who formulates the idea of combining with Kotake to transform into their combined more powerful form, Twinrova.

====Characteristics====
[[File:Twinrova battle.jpg|thumb|right|Link reflecting Kotake's ice magic attack using the Mirror Shield|281x281px]]
The boss room where Twinrova is fought has four small platforms surrounding a larger central platform. Link must climb the central platform in order to trigger the battle. Kotake and Koume will emerge from the smaller platforms before circling above him, then flying off in different directions.

Kotake and Koume fight using magic beams of the elements from which their respective powers are derived, with the powers from their brooms. If Link is struck by their attack, he will lose two [[Heart Container|Hearts]] alongside additional damage from the lava or ice on the ground. Using the [[Mirror Shield]], Link is able to redirect the attacks of one sister against the other. After taking a total of four hits, the pair will use their "Double Dynamite Attack" and merge into Twinrova, a large, elegant single being with the combined powers of both witches.<ref>{{Cite|OK, let's get serious now, Kotake!|Koume|OoT}}</ref><ref>{{Cite|Oh, OK, Koume.|Kotake|OoT}}</ref><ref>{{Cite|Kotake and Koume's Double Dynamite Attack!|Koume|OoT}}</ref><ref>{{Cite|Kotake and Koume's Double Dynamite Attack!|Kotake|OoT}}</ref>

After the transformation, the Mirror Shield remains essential in winning the battle. Link is able to use it to absorb the power of both the fire and ice spells. He is required to collect three consecutive charges of one type of spell, which allows him to release the spell back at Twinrova to stun her. However, since Twinrova frequently alternates between fire and ice attacks, care must be taken to only block attacks of one type at a time. Blocking a fire attack followed by an ice attack, or vice versa, will cause all power currently stored in the Mirror Shield to dissipate and the energy attack will consequently overlap and strike Link as a penalty, similar to using the [[Hylian Shield]].

Once Twinrova is injured by the reflected attack, she collapses on one of the four outlying platforms, allowing Link to attack with his [[Sword]]. After enough damage is dealt, Twinrova will be defeated, at which point she splits back into Kotake and Koume. The two witches intend to keep battling,<ref>{{Cite|Shoot! What a fresh kid! ====Characteristics====
[[File:Twinrovaoracles.png|left|90px]]
Twinrova casts magical projectiles at Link that he must deflect back at them. However, unlike in ''Ocarina of Time'', Link must use his Sword to deflect them rather than his [[Shield]]. The Mirror Shield, an upgrade that can only be attained in a Linked Game, can only protect him from their magic attacks. After the sisters have taken three hits, they fuse together and become Twinrova.

Twinrova alternates between two phases of attack, which affect the room around her and Link. In her cold phase, the room's floor is covered with slippery ice and Twinrova materializes spiked ice crystals that she launches at Link and which ricochet off the walls. In her fire phase, parts of the floor become lava pits and Twinrova launches fireballs. Link must strike Twinrova with his Sword several times to stun her, and once stunned, he must use a [[Mystery Seed]] to actually damage and defeat her.

==={{TotK|-}}===
[[File:TotK Twinrova.png|thumb|left|250px|Koume and Kotake behind Ganondorf]]
In {{TotK}}, Koume and Kotake appear behind {{Term|TotK|Ganondorf|link}} in the {{Term|TotK|Memory|link}} "{{Term|TotK|A Show of Fealty|link}}". They are significantly younger in this game than in any previous appearance, identical to the standard Gerudo. The twins have no dialogue and are not identified by name, but wear fabrics on their shoulders styled after Koume and Kotake's hairbands, with their names written on them in the [[Hylian (Language)#Era of the Hero of Time|Hylian]] writing from {{OoT}}, just as their names were written on their hairbands in that game. They also have red and blue gemstones on the foreheads of the masks they wear, and olive-green skin, unlike the brown skin of other Gerudo in {{TotK|-}}.

==={{HWAoI|-}}===
In {{HWAoI}}, their role was expanded slightly, confirming that, similar to their usual depictions, they were firmly in league with Ganondorf. They first appeared in [[Ardi]]'s flashback to when Ganondorf tasked her to have a squad of Gerudo attack Hyrule while he sent the Moldulga swarm as a distraction, with Koume and Kotake relaying the plan on his behalf. When Ardi expressed reluctance due to citing those among their group who wished for peaceful coexistence with Hyrule, one of the twins was going to cuss Ardi out for insubordination only for Ganondorf to calmly and nonverbally interfere. Their first in-person appearance occurred shortly after Ardi and her unit were driven out, where they use their flutes to summon spirits to attack Ardi and her unit, taking out most of them and making clear Ganondorf had a change in plans and ordered them to "clean up loose ends" by taking her out. However, they were ultimately forced to spare her and a few Gerudo after [[Bud|a construct]] alongside a Korok interfered. They then reported their failure to Ganondorf and begged for his forgiveness, though he ultimately let it slide, both because he already managed to curry favor with King Rauru enough from the earlier feint to get close to the Secret Stone and due to expressing interest in the construct's potential.

After Ganondorf was forced to create another Zelda doppelganger after his initial plan to awaken [[Forbidden Construct|a certain construct]] to attack Rauru resulted in the consumption of said doppelganger, Koume and Kotake reported the last known sighting of the construct as being at the [[Great Plateau]], though Ganondorf told them to do nothing but observe it, as he was interested in seeing the destructive potential behind it that nearly caused the Zonai to go extinct.

Koume and Kotake then had Rauru lured over to Ganondorf's encampment to see Ganondorf regarding mysterious attacks, although after it became apparent he wasn't there, they then activated their trap against the Hyrule King. They ultimately were driven off despite both their magical attacks and the usage of a Talus, although they nevertheless gloated that Ganondorf's rule will soon come. It's left ambiguous what happened to them after Ganondorf became the Demon King.


== Apperance ==

=== Kotake and Koume ===
Kotake and Koume are two extremely old [[Gerudo]] women, with wrinkled greenish skin and white hair; they have the typical appearance linked to witches - hunched bodies, bulging eyes (Kotake with blue eyes and Koume with orange eyes), and enormous, long aquiline noses. Both wear black tunics with white engravings on the sleeves and ornaments with jewels on their heads, with Kotake's being blue and Koume's, red.

=== Twinrova ===
When they fuse and become Twinrova, their appearance changes radically, now being the only Gerudo: a very tall woman with olive-green skin but much younger and exceptionally attractive, voluptuous and seductive with one blue eye and one red eye, red lipstick, and a sharp nose. Her hair is long and white, tied in two long high ponytails which are infused with elemental magic, with the right ponytail being ice and the left, fire. She wears a black blouse that reveals her cleavage, wide white pants, a headdress with two halves of a blue and red gem fused together, and yellow flat shoes.

== Abilities ==
As witches, Kotake and Koume's strength is based mainly on the mastery and control of [[magic]], especially elemental magic and black magic which they taught to Ganondorf, Kotake is the ice sorceress so her magic lies in cryokinesis that allows her to manipulate and create ice and frost while Koume is the fire sorceress and as such can create and manipulate fire at will, likewise they are able to fly and move in the air by means of magic brooms.

When fused into Twinrova, their two magics combine and increase enormously, being able to launch ice beams from the right staff and bursts of fire from the left side, and can also levitate in the air with great ease without needing a magic broom.

Kotake and Koume first appear alongside the Gerudo and an [[Iron Knuckle]] after Ganondorf realizes he was tricked with the [[Fairy Ocarina]] 7 years ago. Their first speaking roles are in the chapter "Shadow Guide:Shiek", where they convince Ganondorf to allow them to brainwash [[Ingo]] so they could lure Link into [[Lon Lon Ranch]] (as Ganondorf initially planned to have Ingo executed for his failure to keep [[Epona]] around long enough for Ganondorf to acquire her). They later witness Link slipping the crystal out of Ingo's ear, revealing the reason for the Gerudo tribe's brainwashing and hostile behavior in the Adult timeline. Later, they disguise themselves as fortune tellers when Link stocks up for the trek into Gerudo Desert telling him he would find the [[Zelda|one he seeks]] by entering the Spirit Temple. After a brief questioning of [[Shiek]]'s true loyalties to Ganondorf, they have Nabooru work with the Sheikah to capture Link and find out if he really is a traitor, which the Sage of the Temple agrees while one of the witches creates a sandstorm to make Link's journey into the desert more difficult. Afterwards, they confronted Shiek over "his" treachery (as he initially seemed to attack Link, only for him to save Link), making the ninja reveal he was never one of them and flees with Link on Epona fending off [[Lizalfos]] and [[Moblin]]s the Gerudo witches summon. Later when Shiek reveals himself to be Zelda, they attack and demand the Hero of Time to give them the Princess. Link defends Zelda with his [[Hylian Shield]] against Koume's flames, but almost gets burned to a crisp until Nabooru gives Link the Mirror Shield, allowing the Hero of Time to give the witches a taste of their own medicine. They die, while cursing their own loss and vow for the King of Evil and their surrogate son to avenge them.

==Trivia==
[[File:Twinrova Seals.png|thumb|264x264px|right|Twinrova's magic seals]]
*In ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]'', the magic seals out of which Kotake and Koume rise in the boss room interestingly display the [[Fire Medallion]] and [[Water Medallion]] symbols, respectively. The design for the Water Medallion was originally intended for the Ice Medallion, which was removed from the final release, but is still found in the game's code.<ref>{{Cite Web|quote=These two icons left inside the rom of Ocarina can be translated as Wind Medallion and Ice Medallion, and they are most likely the leftovers of two temples removed from the final game and probably scheduled for Zelda Ura.|url=http://www.unseen64.net/articles/zelda64-project-development/|title=Project Zelda 3D: The Development of Zelda 64|retrieved= May 14 2011|site=Unseen 64}}</ref>
*In the Japanese release, Twinrova is sometimes referred to as '''Twin Old Women''' or '''Sorcerers Twins'''.
*Twinrova can be considered a parallel to [[mariowiki:Kamek|Kamek]] from the ''[[Mario (franchise)|Mario]]'' series, as they are witches known to be the caretaker of the main antagonist [[Ganon]] as [[Ganondorf]] and are high-ranking minions themselves.
*If [[Link]] attempts to block one of the sisters' (or Twinrova) magic attacks with the [[Hylian Shield]], their beam will overlap the shield and hit the ground behind him, harming the hero. The same result will happen with the latter if blocking the incorrect magic with the Mirror Shield. It is possible to avoid damage by Link forward rolling the instant the beam strikes the floor or if he blocks while standing on the edge of the arena.
*If Link attempts to hit Koume or Kotake with any weapon (such as an [[Arrow]]) during the battle in ''Ocarina of Time'', they will spin around quickly on their broomsticks to deflect the attack. This also occurs after they combine into Twinrova, and she will move to a different platform immediately after spinning, without conjuring a spell first.
*Despite being villainous characters, when they were defeated in ''Ocarina of Time'', the twins' souls are surrounded in angelic halos and they ascend in a column of light, a Western depiction of rising to [[wikipedia:Heaven_in_Christianity|Heaven]].
*Although Kotake and Koume are identical twins, aside from their respective elements of fire and ice, their laughs are [https://noproblo.dayjo.org/zeldasounds/OOT/OOT_Twinrova_Kotake_Laugh.wav slightly] [https://noproblo.dayjo.org/zeldasounds/OOT/OOT_Twinrova_Koume_Laugh.wav different] to distinguish them in ''Ocarina of Time''.
**As their combined form, however, Twinrova's laugh is the same as the [[Gerudo Thief|Gerudo Thieves]] from [[Gerudo's Fortress]].
*In ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask|Majora's Mask]]'', Koume is certain that the [[Red Potion|Red]] (or [[Blue Potion|Blue]]) [[Potion]] given to her is Kotake's, even if it was purchased from the [[Trading Post]] in [[Clock Town]] or other means.<ref>{{Cite|Ah! That color! That smell... That's definitely Kotake's...|Koume|MM}}</ref> Therefore, Kotake may be supplying the Trading Post with its potions. As an alternative, it may simply be an oversight of the developers.
*In the [[Linked Game|linked ''Oracle'']] ending fight against Twinrova, their boss theme is a remix of the mini-boss theme from the game (as well as the boss theme from {{LA}}).
*In {{TWW}}, Koume and Kotake's names are engraved on Ganondorf's swords in {{Term|TWW|Hylian (Language)|link}}. Similarly, in {{TotK}}, the Gloom {{Term|TotK|Weapon|link}} set has their names engraved as well.
*The fight against Twinrova in ''Ocarina of Time'', due to mechanic of the [[Mirror Shield]] reflecting and absorbing their attacks, is often seen as one of the most unique [[boss]] battles in the game, if not [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' series]]. Despite this, some fans are rather disappointed as the special shield is specifically for this battle and otherwise has minimal use.
**This would be reused for the final battle against [[Majora's Mask (item)|Majora's Mask]] in ''Majora's Mask'' when it begins to shoot fire beams at Link with its eyes, which he can redirect the attack to itself or the Boss Remains. On a similar note, Majora's Incarnation and Wrath vocals were modified versions of Twinrova's vocal clips.

* In the [[The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom|''Tears of The Kingdom'']] “[[A Show of Fealty]]” [[memory]], the two Gerudo women standing behind Ganondorf bear sashes reading their names. Whether or not these are the actually younger versions of Kotake and Koume themselves from ''Ocarina of Time'' is unknown.

==Nomenclature==
'''Twinrova''' is a modified version of the characters' Japanese name {{Romanize|ツインローバ|Tsuinrōba|Twin Old Women}}.<br/>
Their individual names come from the Japanese mystery novel {{Romanize|八つ墓村|Yatsuhaka Mura|Village of Eight Gravestones|2}}. In it, a pair of elderly twin sisters bear the names Kotake and Koume.<ref>{{Cite Web|url=http://ho-lingnojikenbo.blogspot.com/2011/06/blog-post.html|title=「六つ墓村？八つ墓村じゃなくて？」 (in English)|site=The Case Files of Ho-Ling, June 1, 2011}}</ref><br/>
'''Kotake''' come from {{Romanize|小竹|Kotake}}: {{Romanize|小|ko|little}} and {{Romanize|竹|take|bamboo}}.<br/>
'''Koume''' comes from {{Romanize|小梅|Koume}}: {{Romanize|小|ko|little}} and {{Romanize|梅|ume|plum}}.
