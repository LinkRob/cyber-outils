[[File:Super Mario logo.svg|thumb|The current logo for the ''Super Mario'' series, often used to represent the ''Mario'' franchise as well|503x503px|center]]
The '''[[mariowiki:Mario_(franchise)|''Mario'' franchise]]''', also known as '''''Super Mario''''', is a long-running multi-billiondollar [[wikipedia:Media_franchise|media franchise]] created by [[Nintendo]]. Most often taking place in the fictional [[mariowiki:Mushroom_Kingdom|Mushroom Kingdom]], led by the elegant [[Princess Peach]], who is usually the damsel-in-distress (though she at times can be a protagonist), it stars the titular Italian plumber, [[Mario]], the main protagonist and longtime mascot of Nintendo, aided by his younger twin brother [[Luigi]]. The Mario Bros' mission in most games is to rescue the princess from the clutches of the evil Koopa King [[Bowser]], the main antagonist. Like [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' series]], some titles feature additional protagonists (such as [[Yoshi]]) and different lead antagonists (such as [[mariowiki:King_Boo|King Boo]] or [[mariowiki:Smithy|Smithy]]).
[[File:Super Mario Galaxy screenshot.webp|left|thumb|248x248px|An example of a ''Mario'' game, ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_Galaxy|Super Mario Galaxy]]'', a title for the [[Wii|Nintendo Wii]]]]
Debuted during the very early gaming era of the [[wikipedia:Atari_2600|Atari 2600]] in July 9, 1981 by [[Shigeru Miyamoto]], about five years before ''The Legend of Zelda'' in February 21, 1986, the ''Mario'' franchise is Nintendo's main flagship series. Although the very first game featuring the character Mario is the [[mariowiki:Donkey_Kong_(video_game)|''Donkey Kong'' arcade game]], marking his and [[mariowiki:Donkey_Kong_(character)|Donkey Kong]]'s grand debut, this particular title alongside ''[[mariowiki:Donkey_Kong_Jr._(video_game)|Donkey Kong Jr]].'' are technically part of the related [https://donkeykong.fandom.com/wiki/Donkey_Kong_(series) ''Donkey Kong'' series] (its parent franchise); the first actual installment is ''[[mariowiki:Mario_Bros._(video_game)|Mario Bros.]]'' in 1983, while its breakthrough success began with the [[Nintendo Entertainment System]] title ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_Bros.|Super Mario Bros.]]'' two years later, starting its own [[mariowiki:Super_Mario_(series)|''Super Mario'' series]]. It is also known for many spin-offs such as ''[[mariowiki:Mario_Party_(series)|Mario Party]]'', ''[[mariowiki:Mario_Kart_(series)|Mario Kart]]'', ''[[mariowiki:Mario_Kart_(series)|Mario Golf]]'', ''[[mariowiki:Mario_Tennis_(series)|Mario Tennis]]'', ''[[mariowiki:Mario_&_Luigi_(series)|Mario & Luigi]]'', ''[[Super Smash Bros. (series)|Super Smash Bros.]]'', and so on. ''Mario'' took the gaming industry by storm and became [[wikipedia:List_of_best-selling_video_game_franchises|the most successful video game franchise of all-time]], with over 250 titles released (20+ core installments) and more than 950 million copies sold as of 2025/2026 (in comparison, ''The Legend of Zelda'' series sold only 180+ million). Alongside ''Donkey Kong'', the ''[https://yoshi.fandom.com/wiki/Yoshi_(series) Yoshi]'' and ''[https://wario.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Games Wario]'' series are sister franchises.
[[File:MK8 Mario and Link trick.jpg|thumb|263x263px|Mario and Link together as seen in ''[[mariowiki:Mario_Kart_8|Mario Kart 8]]'']]
Although ''The Legend of Zelda'' is not technically a "sister" franchise to ''Mario'', as their settings are completely different despite being based on a kingdom and the gameplay were designed to be polar opposites (''Mario'' focuses on linearity while ''Zelda'' does not), it is still rather related, as both are widely known to have many parallels between them since their origins, coincidentally or intentionally, whether it is the main characters, events of the core games or even elements, often as [[Cameos in The Legend of Zelda|Easter]] [[Cameos of The Legend of Zelda in other media|Eggs]]. ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_Bros._3|Super Mario Bros. 3]]'' and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening|Link's Awakening]]'' are among the most famous titles to have references of them. This is because these iconic highly successful Nintendo franchises were originally created by the same development team at the exact same time and this continued into some later games of either series, especially the NES to [[Nintendo 64]] eras. In spite of never being stated outright by Nintendo itself, due to the many connections between ''Mario'' and ''Zelda'' compared to others by the company and the fact most major media were released nearly simultaneously (the former usually first), it is likely they are partner franchises and even possibly sharing the same universe, with the [[mariowiki:Mushroom_Kingdom|Mushroom Kingdom]] and [[Hyrule]] simply being extremely far away from each other. From the start, their main protagonists/mascots, Mario and [[Link]], have occasionally crossed paths, most prominently in merchandise, ''Super Smash Bros.'' and the latest ''Mario Kart'' installments.

Interestingly, according to their respective creator, Shigeru Miyamoto, while it is true that ''Super Mario Bros.'' and ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]'' were indeed co-developed, contrary to popular belief, the latter game was the first to be planned by Nintendo. But because it was originally for the [[Family Computer Disk System]] rather than the traditional Nintendo Entertainment System, he and his team decided to finish up the ''Mario'' game, first, explaining the reason why the [[wikipedia:Platformer|platformer]] title was released one year (or two internationally) before its [[wikipedia:Action-adventure_game|action-adventure]] counterpart.

==Relevance to ''The Legend of Zelda''==
[[File:TLoZ Manhandla Artwork.png|thumb|205x205px|The Manhandla boss enemy is possibly a massive Piranha Plant from the ''Mario'' universe]]
The ''Mario'' series has had quite a few connections to the ''Zelda'' franchise, as early as the original ''The Legend of Zelda'', which was developed concurrently with ''Super Mario Bros.'' by the same development team at Nintendo. ''The Legend of Zelda''<nowiki/>'s earliest evidence of a connection to ''Mario'' is in the Japanese instruction manual of the very first game, in which [[Manhandla]]s are said to be jumbo-sized [[Piranha Plant]]s.
[[File:HyruleWarriorsChainChomp.jpg|thumb|266x266px|A Chain Chomp being held by Link in ''[[Hyrule Warriors]]'', a ''Mario'' enemy meant for his home franchise]]
Two recurring elements of the ''Super'' ''Mario'' series, [[Chain Chomp]]s and [[Fire Bar]]s, were actually originally created for {{TLoZ|-}} series before they were given their prime association to the former.<ref>{{Cite Person|quote= We’d had concept art for Bow-Wow lying around for awhile. We’d put it aside thinking we might make use of it if we could, but someone discovered it and ended up using it for their own purposes.|name= Takashi Tezuka|url= http://www.glitterberri.com/a-link-to-the-past/the-men-who-made-zelda/|title= The Men Who Made Zelda – Staff Interview}}</ref><ref>{{Cite Web|quote=
'''Iwata:''' You mean the spinning bars of fire attached to the castle walls that you encounter as you make your way along the corridor to fight Bowser.<br/>
'''Nakago:''' Right. Those first appeared right in the centre of the screen in Zelda.<br/>
'''Iwata:''' ...Really!?<br/>
'''Nakago:''' Yes, that idea originally comes from Zelda. We thought it would work better in Mario, so we transferred it across. I think it was Miyamoto-san who suggested it. Or perhaps it was Tezuka-san...|url= https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/nsmb/1/3|title= Iwata Asks: New Super Mario Bros. Wii|site= Nintendo Official Website}}</ref> Nevertheless, both appear in the 1991 [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System|Super NES]] title ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past|A Link to the Past]]'', which is one of the earlier appearances of Chain Chomps in general.

Although uncommon in Japan, a lot of Western media in the late 1980s and early 1990s grouped ''Mario'' and {{TLoZ|-}} together. The most prominent instance of this was [[The Legend of Zelda (television series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' TV series]], which was a part of ''[[mariowiki:The Super Mario Bros. Super Show!|The Super Mario Bros. Super Show!]]'' syndication package, and would have new episodes of Fridays. Additionally, many of the live-action ''Mario'' segments of ''The Super Mario Bros. Super Show!'' would have a preview of a ''The Legend of Zelda'' episode. In ''[[mariowiki:Super Mario Bros. & Friends: When I Grow Up|Super Mario Bros. & Friends: When I Grow Up]]'', there is not only a career page dedicated to Link, but a few of the pages include a [[Bot]] in them. The ''Mario'' and {{TLoZ|-}} series were also licensed together for various [[Nintendo]]-themed merchandise, such as Nintendo Cereal System and the [[Nintendo Adventure Books]] series. Both the franchises' license were given to Philips at one point to produce a few [[Philips CD-i]] games each such as ''[[mariowiki:Hotel Mario|Hotel Mario]]'' or ''[[Zelda's Adventure]]'', which are infamously known to had poor development, little relation to the mainline games, and are consequently unsuccessful.
[[File:LA Kirby and Goomba.png|thumb|A Goomba and even [[Kirby]] from the ''Mario'' and ''Kirby'' franchises in ''Link's Awakening'' (despite the enemy for the latter is actually Anti-Kirby rather than the character)|207x207px]]
{{LA}} on the [[Game Boy]] in 1993 has guest appearances of various characters and enemies from other Nintendo franchises, especially ''Super'' ''Mario'', and has more representation of this series than any other ''The Legend of Zelda'' title. Since {{LA|-}}, [[Thwomp]]s have been a semi-recurring enemy in the ''Zelda'' franchise, and the game introduces two Thwomp variants that have not appeared within the ''Mario'' franchise—[[Mega Thwomp]]s and [[Stone Elevator]]s—not unlike ''[[mariowiki:Wario Land: Super Mario Land 3|Wario Land: Super Mario Land 3]]'' on the same handheld console, a platform game starring [[Wario]] that exclusively features another Thwomp variant, [[mariowiki:Pouncer|Pouncers]]. In ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Oracle of Seasons|Oracle of Seasons]]'' on the [[Game Boy Color]], one of the bosses is a Thwomp named [[Head Thwomp]], who has likewise never appeared in the ''Mario'' franchise. [[Bob-omb]]s and [[Cheep Cheep]]s are some other examples of ''Mario'' enemies that have appeared as foes for Link in more than one ''The Legend of Zelda'' game.

''The Legend of Zelda''<nowiki/>'s own enemy species have seldomly had their variants in the ''Mario'' franchise, but in ''[[mariowiki:Super Mario Galaxy|Super Mario Galaxy]]'' and ''[[mariowiki:Super Mario Galaxy 2|Super Mario Galaxy 2]]'' on the [[Wii]], there are [[mariowiki:Octoomba|Octoombas]], which are a variant of both [[Goomba]]s and [[Octorok]]s.
[[File:OoT Talon Artwork.png|thumb|197x197px|Talon, who himself bares a physical resemblance to Mario]]
Some characters in ''The Legend of Zelda'' series share a bit of resemblance to ''Mario''<nowiki/>'s own. The most well-known cases are [[Talon]] and [[Ingo]] in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]'', who themselves have similarities to [[Mario]] and [[Luigi]], respectively.
[[File:TFH Hammerwear Render.png|thumb|206x206px|The Hammerwear from ''Tri Force Heroes'' is similar to the Hammer Bro enemy from ''Mario'']]
Although actual items of either series are very rarely shared between each other, the most famous example is the [[Flute|Recorder]] from ''The Legend of Zelda'' was reused for ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_Bros._3|Super Mario Bros. 3]]'' to help Mario (or Luigi) warp between worlds. However, some ''Zelda'' games such as ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tri Force Heroes|Tri Force Heroes]]'' have references to ''Mario'' such as the [[Fire Gloves]] and [[Hammerwear]] [[Outfit|Outfits]], which are based on the [[mariowiki:Fire Flower|Fire Flower]] and [[mariowiki:Hammer Bro|Hammer Bro]], respectively.

Other than ''Super Mario Bros.'' and ''The Legend of Zelda'', Nintendo has often produced a mainline ''Super Mario'' and {{TLoZ|-}} game concurrently, such as ''[[mariowiki:Super Mario 64|Super Mario 64]]'' and ''Ocarina of Time'' on the [[Nintendo 64]] (the latter even uses a heavily modified ''Super Mario 64'' engine like the earlier NES titles) and ''[[mariowiki:Super Mario Odyssey|Super Mario Odyssey]]'' and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild|Breath of the Wild]]'' on the [[Nintendo Switch]].

Despite all the aforementioned information regarding the parallels between ''Mario'' and ''Zelda'', however, Nintendo has never yet produced a direct crossover game based on either franchise's main series.

== Trivia ==

* As of January 2025, although it is Nintendo's primary series, ''Mario'' is '''not''' the highest-grossing media video game franchise of all-time, as it was greatly surpassed by the company's own ''[[pokemon:Pokémon_(franchise)|Pokémon]]'' with $150 billion, making it the second in position, with $55 billion. ''[[The Legend of Zelda (series)|The Legend of Zelda]]'' in comparison, however, is far less in revenue, having only $3.8 billion. Nevertheless, all three of such franchises are hugely successful in their own right.
* ''Mario Kart 8/Deluxe'' of the ''Mario Kart'' series is the best-selling game of the ''Mario'' franchise, with over 73 million sold, combined.
** In terms of ''Super Mario'', ''Super Mario Bros.'' is its top with over 40 million sold.

* Aside from ''Mario'', Nintendo's other flagship franchises are ''The Legend of Zelda'' and ''Pokémon''.
* Much like how [[Link]], [[Princess Zelda]], and [[Ganon]] are the three main characters in ''The Legend of Zelda'' series, the ''Mario'' franchise's equivalent are [[Mario]], [[Princess Peach]], and [[Bowser]], which can be easily seen as a parallel. They even share the exact same longtime roles in their core games' plot.
* Although ''Donkey Kong'' is Nintendo's first major hit worldwide, it is not the first known title to be released. In 1979, an arcade shooter named ''[[wikipedia:Sheriff_(video_game)|Sheriff]]'' was popular in Japan. Its playable character appeared as an Assist Trophy in the ''Super Smash Bros.'' series starting in ''[[Super Smash Bros. for Nintendo 3DS / Wii U|SSB4]]''.
* Much like the original ''The Legend of Zelda'' and ''Ocarina of Time'' for ''Zelda'', ''Super Mario Bros''. and ''Super Mario 64'' are widely considered the most important installments of the ''Mario'' franchise based on their revolutionary influence in the 2D and 3D video game industry, respectively.
* Interestingly, although Mario is the main protagonist of the titular franchise, ''Donkey Kong Jr.'' has his role being the complete opposite, where he kidnaps [[mariowiki:Donkey_Kong_(character)|Donkey Kong]] (the first ever antagonist) as revenge for himself kidnapping his then-girlfriend [[mariowiki:Pauline|Pauline]] (originally named Lady).[[File:SMG Megaleg fight.webp|thumb|283x283px|The Megaleg boss fight in ''Super Mario Galaxy'' is actually a scrapped idea for Ganon in ''Ocarina of Time'']]
* Interestingly, in ''Super Mario Galaxy'', Mario's battle against the boss [[mariowiki:Megaleg|Megaleg]] was actually an idea that was originally planned for the iconic final battle against Ganon in ''Ocarina of Time''. During development, the Demon King was supposed to be gigantic and Link had to scale his archenemy and attack various parts of his body. However, due to the hardware limitations of the Nintendo 64 (as it is a cartridge console from 1996), the concept proved impossible to program, as it resulted in a significantly reduced framerate and Ganon himself cannot be seen if the [[Hero of Time]] were to be on top of him, rendering the game unplayable. Ultimately, [[Nintendo]] was forced to rework the [[Final Boss]] fight entirely for the final release. For the CD-based Wii game in the ''Mario'' franchise released in 2007, the development team found its newer hardware much easier to use for this idea and they themselves were happy about it.
** Ironically, the [[Demon Dragon]] in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom|Tears of the Kingdom]]'' had Link be on top of a very large boss in order to defeat this form of Ganondorf.
* Like Zelda in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Echoes of Wisdom|Echoes of Wisdom]]'', Peach has her own starring role as the main protagonist in a few games in the ''Mario'' franchise, beginning with ''[[mariowiki:Super_Princess_Peach|Super Princess Peach]]'' in 2005 on the [[Nintendo DS]], 19 years before her ''Zelda'' counterpart. However, she was already featured as a playable character as early as ''Super Mario Bros. 2'', in 1988.
* Prior to the debut of the ''Mario'' franchise to make Nintendo become the gaming giant ever since, it was once a nearly unknown toy/card manufacturer in its early history.
* One of the most famous examples of Link appearing in a ''Mario'' game as a [[Cameos of The Legend of Zelda in other media|cameo]] include the 1996 Super NES title ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_RPG:_Legend_of_the_Seven_Stars|Super Mario RPG: The Legend of the Seven Stars]]''.
* Similar to Link's guest appearance in the Namco fighting game ''[[SoulCalibur II]]'', Mario, and sometimes Luigi and Princess Peach, have been featured in non-Nintendo media as guests themselves. Famous examples include the Electronic Arts titles ''[[wikipedia:SSX_on_Tour|SSX on Tour]]'' and ''[[wikipedia:NBA_Street_V3|NBA Street V3]]''. Coincidentally, those games were released on the GameCube, and, for obvious reasons, must only appear in a Nintendo console in any case.[[File:Mario and Sonic shaking hands.png|thumb|251x251px|Nintendo's mascot Mario shaking hands with Sega's mascot, Sonic the Hedgehog, his former rival]]
* During the 1990s, especially in North America by the release of the Super NES and [[wikipedia:Sega_Genesis|Sega Genesis]] respectively, Nintendo had an infamous rivalry with [[wikipedia:Sega|Sega]], in which the two companies had the ''Mario'' and ''[https://sonic.fandom.com/wiki/Sonic_the_Hedgehog_series Sonic the Hedgehog]'' franchises directly compete with each other until the release of the GameCube (due to the latter transitioning into 3rd-party by the failure of the [[wikipedia:Dreamcast|Dreamcast]]). In recent years, their mascots Mario and [https://sonic.fandom.com/wiki/Sonic_the_Hedgehog Sonic the Hedgehog] are currently friends and are seen together in ''Super Smash Bros.'' as well as the crossover ''[[mariowiki:Mario_&_Sonic_(series)|Mario & Sonic at the Olympic Games]]'' (based on the real-world [[wikipedia:Olympic_Games|IOC Olympics]]).
* In the Wii U title ''Super Mario Maker'', alongside featuring Link, Zelda, [[Ganondorf]], and many other Nintendo-related characters, the game also included various popular guests from other media such as [https://vocaloid.fandom.com/wiki/Hatsune_Miku Hatsune Miku], [https://pacman.fandom.com/wiki/Pac-Man Pac-Man], [https://hellokitty.fandom.com/wiki/Hello_Kitty Hello Kitty], Sonic, and more, via [[mariowiki:Costume Mario|Costume Mario]] by the Mystery Mushroom item.
** Although this was scrapped in its sequel ''Super Mario Maker 2'' on the Switch for unknown reasons, Link himself is playable when Mario finds the [[Master Sword]] and transforms into the ''Zelda'' main protagonist.
