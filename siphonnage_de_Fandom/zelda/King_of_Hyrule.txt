The '''King of Hyrule''' is a recurring character of [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|{{TLoZ|-}} series]]. He is the reigning monarch of the [[Hyrule|Kingdom of Hyrule]] and normally the father of [[Princess Zelda]]. The King of Hyrule makes his debut in {{TAoL}} but was first seen in person in [[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past|{{aLttP|-}}]]. The King himself usually has a minor role in the franchise unlike Zelda, but he does play a major one in {{TWW}}, {{TMC}}, {{BotW}}, and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom|Tears of the Kingdom]]'' where he assists [[Link]]. As Hyrule's autocrat, he can be considered the opposite of [[Ganon]] the Demon King, the main antagonist. In certain games, the King's permission is usually required to climb [[Death Mountain]].

==Appearances==
==={{TAoL|-}}===
{{Main|King of Hyrule (The Adventure of Link)}}
In Hyrule's Golden Age, it was ruled by a King who was as wise and just as he was powerful, said power being due in no small part to his possession of the [[Triforce]], the sacred artifact left behind by the [[Golden Goddess]]es after the creation of the world.

Alas, for all his wisdom, justice, and power, the King was still only mortal. Upon learning of his imminent death, and finding none in Hyrule he could trust with the complete Triforce — not even [[Prince of Hyrule|his own son]] and heir — he had seven [[Palace#The Adventure of Link|Temples]] built: a [[Great Palace|Great Temple]] built in the depths of [[Valley of Death]] to house the [[Triforce of Courage]], and six others in which statues were placed, with hollows added into which [[Crystal]]s would be placed.

These statues would be enchanted so that when all six Crystals were inserted, the seal on the Great Temple would break, allowing entry. The King also filled the seven Temples with guardians and familiars to serve as a trial for the one who was worthy of retrieving the Triforce of Courage. Furthermore, he wrote a scroll for this worthy champion to read, instructing them on how to carry out this quest, and had it and the six Crystals placed into a small chest that he entrusted to the [[Sheikah]].

At last, he cast a spell on the entire kingdom, that when the worthy champion either finally proved himself or turned 16 years of age, whichever came second, the [[Mark of the Triforce]] would appear on his hand. At that moment, the Sheikah were instructed to give the champion the small chest and trust that the champion would see the quest through.

In addition to the Sheikah, the King also revealed all of this knowledge to his daughter [[Princess Zelda]] while on his deathbed.

Unfortunately, he underestimated his son's greed, or that the [[Magician]] he had once employed would reveal his deathbed confession to that same son, which would lead to the fateful Tragedy of Princess Zelda I.

Centuries later, in the events of {{TLoZ}}, a boy named [[Link]] would prove himself worthy by reassembling the [[Triforce of Wisdom]] and recovering the stolen [[Triforce of Power]] by defeating [[Ganon]]. But due to being well under the age of 16, the [[Mark of the Triforce]] would not appear on his hand until many years later. When Link's 16th birthday came, the Mark appeared, and [[Impa]] carried out the King's commands after showing him the slumbering Princess Zelda I, passing on both the King's hope that the Triforce would be whole once more by retrieving the [[Triforce of Courage]], and his penitent son's hope that his sister would be awakened. This is where the events of {{TAoL}} take place. The King himself is unseen in this game.

==={{aLttP|-}}===
{{aLttP}} is the first installment in the franchise where the King of Hyrule makes a physical appearance. When Hyrule is beset by plagues, the King searches far and wide for someone who can bring the plagues to an end, rewarding [[Agahnim]] when he does so by appointing the Dark Priest his chief advisor, and a hero to the kingdom.

Little did the King know, Agahnim had been behind the plagues all along, and now in a position of authority in the castle, he murdered the King and impersonated him, putting out a 100 [[Rupee]] bounty in his name for the delivery of any descendants of the [[Sage|Seven Wise Men]] to the castle.

[[File:ALttP The Return of the King Credits Scene.png|thumb|214x214px|The revived King of Hyrule and the seven Maidens in the ending of {{aLttP|-}}]]

When Agahnim and his master [[Ganon]] were defeated, Link wished on the [[Triforce]] for all the damage the two did to be undone. This led to the resurrections of the King as well as the [[Loyal Sage|Loyal Priest]], who was also killed by Agahnim and his minions.

In the game's intro cutscene, the King's dead body can be seen sitting on his throne on the left, as a skeleton but retaining his crown and royal outfit. When he is revived at the ending, he wears a cane.

==={{OoT|-}}===
The King does not appear physically in {{OoT}}, but is mentioned twice. When Princess Zelda tells him about her prophetic dreams and how [[Ganondorf]], the human form of Ganon, is connected to them, he does not believe her for reasons that can only be guessed, which she confesses to Link.

Later, when Ganondorf attacks [[Hyrule Castle]], he murders the King in order to usurp him. This is but one of many changes the legendary [[Hero of Time]] made to bring about the "Child" branch of [[The Legend of Zelda timeline|{{TLoZ|-}} timeline]], as in the ending of the game leading to {{MM}} and eventually {{TP}}, Link himself explains to Princess Zelda and the King about their archenemy's true motivations, gaining the [[Royal Family of Hyrule|Royal Family]]'s trust and have the [[Gerudo]] warlock [[Failed Execution of Ganondorf|executed]] for his crimes.

==={{TWW|-}}===
{{Main|Daphnes Nohansen Hyrule}}
When Hyrule [[Great Flood|falls]] in the Adult Timeline, it is during the reign of King Daphnes Nohansen Hyrule. In this era, King Daphnes would conduct the musical prayers of the Sages using the [[Wind Waker (Item)|Wind Waker]] baton, and had utilized the [[Gossip Stone]]s of old to create [[Pirate's Charm|communication stones]]. He also possessed a boat, the [[King of Red Lions]].

When Ganondorf returned from the corrupted [[Sacred Realm]] and tried to conquer Hyrule without a Hero to oppose him, King Daphnes prayed to the gods for relief, be it in the form of the Hero of Time's return, or some other miracle. It is unknown what reply, if any, he received, only that he then broke off a small piece of the [[Triforce of Wisdom]], entrusting the larger V-shaped piece to his daughter, and had as many people as possible evacuated to the tops of tall mountains, before being lost in the Great Flood.

Over the next several centuries, his spirit would inhabit his boat, the King of Red Lions, though he instead pretended he was the boat itself come to life. One day, he happened upon a young boy named Link who was floating unconscious on the [[Great Sea]] after being thrown from the [[Forsaken Fortress]] on Ganondorf's orders. After briefing Link on what was going on, the two sailed across the Great Sea on a journey that would see many crises resolved, Link becoming the Hero of Winds, Ganondorf petrified for good, and the King himself sadly lost with his kingdom.

[[File:Triforce TWW.jpg|thumb|205x205px|King Daphnes touching the Triforce before Ganondorf]]

Before he was lost, he stole from Ganondorf a wish on the completed Triforce, asking the gods for hope on behalf of Link and [[Tetra]] (as the new Princess Zelda), that they have a future, as well as for [[Hyrule]] itself to be washed away, as he realized his pining for the bygone age where Hyrule thrived made him little better than Ganondorf. His final words were to proudly acknowledge that he had "''scattered the seeds of the future...''"

==={{TMC|-}}===
{{Main|King Daltus}}
In {{TMC}}, when they were younger, King Daltus and [[Smith|Master Smith]] were veterans of the annual [[Picori Festival]]'s Swordfighting Tournament. Nowadays, however, Smith tends to his forge and raises his grandson Link, while King Daltus runs the kingdom, with help from [[Minister Potho]], and raises his daughter Princess Zelda, who is childhood friends with Link.

On a Picori Festval where the portal to the [[Minish]] World opened, a mysterious man named [[Vaati]] entered the tournament and won the whole thing in record time. But when it came time for the traditional ceremony, where he would be allowed to touch the [[Picori Blade]], Vaati instead threw the guards aside, broke the sacred blade, petrified Princess Zelda, and opened the [[Bound Chest]], expecting to find inside the [[Light Force]] entrusted to the Royal Family by the Minish long ago, but was disappointed, not caring about the horde of monsters he had just unleashed on Hyrule.

Later, after Link recovered, King Daltus entrusted Link with the task of contacting the Minish for aid in breaking the curse of stone on Zelda, giving the boy the broken Picori Blade, a map of Hyrule, and [[Smith's Sword|the sword]] that was meant to be Vaati's prize for winning the tournament.

Later still, Vaati returned, petrified Daltus, banished him to the dungeons, and then impersonated him, demanding the guards go out and bring him the Light Force, ordering for any objectors be imprisoned. Eventually, Vaati would abandon the disguise when Link discovered in the [[Elemental Sanctuary]] that the Light Force was passed down the Royal Family from queen to princess, meaning Zelda was in possession of the Light Force that Vaati coveted.

Link would find the real King Daltus in the dungeons after Vaati turned Hyrule Castle into [[Dark Hyrule Castle|a dark mockery of itself]]. The real King gave Link a key to reach the upper floors, but was forced to leave the matter of stopping Vaati to his old friend's grandson, though the boy did prevail in the end. After Vaati's death, Princess Zelda recovered the [[Mage's Cap]] and used it to undo Vaati's damage to the kingdom, including restoring Hyrule Castle to normal.

In addition to Daltus, there was another King of Hyrule involved with his quest: the spirit of the late [[Gustaf|King Gustaf]], who had enjoyed a deep friendship with the [[Wind Tribe]] in exchange for their knowledge of agriculture. King Gustaf would lead Link from the [[Temple of Droplets]] at [[Lake Hylia]] to his grave in the [[Royal Valley]], where he entrusted the young Hero with a [[Kinstone Piece]] that would open the way to climbing [[Veil Falls]], before returning to his rest.

==={{BotW|-}}===
{{Main|King Rhoam}}
The last known king of the Kingdom of Hyrule in {{BotW}} was named Rhoam Bosphoramous Hyrule. It was his reign that was ended with the kingdom's fall to [[Calamity Ganon]]'s army of [[Guardian]]s, led by the [[Divine Beast]]s, all corrupted by the Calamity to turn against their masters. Even King Rhoam was not spared, though it's not clear exactly when and where he lost his life.

[[File:BotW King and Guards.png|thumb|232x232px|King Roham in-game]]

100 years later, when Link awakens from the Slumber of Restoration, King Rhoam's spirit is present on the [[Great Plateau]], guiding Link under the guise of an old man in exile, not far off from his actual state. Once Link proves he is capable of undertaking the task of destroying the Calamity, the dead king reveals his identity, and directs Link to [[Kakariko Village]], where [[Impa]] will tell him more about his mission. After that, the spirit vanishes, but only until Link and Princess Zelda defeat [[Dark Beast Ganon]], where he watches with the other fallen [[Champions]] from Hyrule Castle, before they all pass on to the afterlife.

==={{TotK|-}}===
{{Main|King Rauru}}
Though the Kingdom of Hyrule is not re-established in the years between {{BotW|-}} and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom|Tears of the Kingdom]]'', a King of Hyrule does appear: Rauru, said to be the first King of Hyrule, having founded the kingdom alongside his wife [[Queen Sonia|Sonia]] in a bygone age, regardless of whether or not their reign predates even the previously-known earliest king, [[King Gustaf|Gustaf]].

It is during Rauru's reign that a mysterious girl appeared as from nowhere. Tended to by Rauru and Sonia, they discover the girl, named Zelda, possesses a blood connection to both of them, despite not being among their offspring, as they must surely have had by this point. Knowing the oddity would sow distrust and division, they pass Princess Zelda off as a distant relative of Sonia's.

Later, a [[Gerudo]] chief, a rare voe named [[Ganondorf]], attempted to attack Hyrule in his belief that the other races banding together made them weak. Despite being repelled by the awesome power that both Rauru, Sonia, and Zelda wielded as one, Ganondorf was undeterred, choosing instead to try and obtain a [[Secret Stone]] for himself, correctly guessing that they were how the three could wield such power.

Ganondorf would feign swearing fealty to Hyrule in order to get close to Rauru and Sonia, successfully murdering the latter while distracting her and Princess Zelda with a fake assassination attempt by a [[Phantom Ganon|phantom]] impersonating the latter. Claiming Sonia's Secret Stone, he transformed himself into the Demon King, creating the [[Blood Moon]] and summoning countless monsters, commanding them to destroy all of Hyrule and leave no survivors.

Rauru mustered the forces of not just the [[Hylian]]s, but the [[Goron]]s, [[Zora]], and [[Rito]] as well. Even the Gerudo joined his side, truthfully swearing the oath Ganondorf had done falsely, as even they were not exempt from the Demon King's bloodlust. Each sent a representative to aid Rauru in his war against Ganondorf, who would become Sages alongside him, Zelda, and his sister [[Mineru]].

Led by these Seven Sages, the [[Imprisoning War (Tears of the Kingdom)|Imprisoning War]] was waged, culminating in their collective defeat by the Demon King in a room that would come to be called the [[Imprisoning Chamber]] in Princess Zelda's era. In a desperation move, Zelda distracted Ganondorf, allowing Rauru to connect a strike that would seal both men in the chamber, bringing the war to a decisive end.

Eventually, Rauru would be succeeded by one of his and Sonia's children, in a line of descendants leading all the way to King Rhoam and Princess Zelda herself.

==={{EoW|-}}===
In {{EoW}}, the King of Hyrule is supported in his royal duties by [[Minister Lefte]] and [[General Wright]].

After Princess Zelda returns from her abduction by Ganon, a [[Rift]] opens beneath the throne, pulling the King and his attendants in. Just before he’s taken, he pushes Zelda out of harm’s way, saving her from being taken as well. [[Dark Echo|Fake versions]] of the King, Lefte, and Wright emerge and accuse Zelda of causing the Rift. To clear her name, Princess Zelda and [[Tri]] set out to rescue the real King from the [[Still World]], exploring Rifts in the [[Gerudo Desert]] and [[Jabul Waters]].

The King is eventually rescued by Zelda from the Rifts, along with Minister Lefte and General Wright. Once reunited, they work to support the princess in her mission to seal the Rifts that continue to appear across Hyrule.

==Spin-off appearances==
{{Noncanon|Start}}
==={{TLoZ|-}} (''TV Series'')===
[[King Harkinian]] is Hyrule's wise, if a bit scatterbrained, king. At least one of Ganon's schemes to conquer Hyrule involves kidnapping him, while on another occasion, Harkinian accidentally builds a water park over a fairy fountain, angering the fairies until the misunderstanding is discovered and cleared up.

==={{TLoZ|-}} (''Valiant Comics'')===
In the ''Valiant'' comics, King Harkinian resembles his appearance from the TV series, but is far less scatterbrained. He is an old friend of [[Queen Seline]] of [[Calatia]], and is protected by [[Captain Krin]] of the Royal Guard.

In "Missing in Action", the king informs an arriving Link of Zelda's intention to flee Hyrule with the Triforce of Wisdom, while in "It's Good to be the King", he willingly gives Link the crown to fulfill the Triforce of Wisdom's prophecy that he will be King of Hyrule on that day, as of the two, only Link could withstand a [[Wizzrobe]] spell specifically meant to target Hyrule's king, though the young hero quickly returns the crown when the battle is won, as adventuring is more his style.

==={{OoT}} Himekawa manga===
The King more or less has the same role as in the game, although his dismissal of Zelda's nightmares are elaborated on showing that he believed her nightmares were false due to Hyrule being in an era of peace. In addition, his death at the hands of Ganadorf is made more explicit when Zelda and Impa are cornered and Ganandorf declares that Zelda "shall sleep with her father for eternity in this burning castle."

==={{tFoE}} and {{tWoG}}===
King Harkinian is Hyrule's benevolent king, immediately wondering how to help [[Koridai]] when he learns it is under attack by Ganon. Later, when [[Gamelon]] is under attack by Ganon, he immediately sails there to aid his cousin [[Duke Onkled]], unaware that the duke has betrayed him to Ganon. When he is rescued by Zelda, he sentences Onkled to scrub all the floors in Hyrule.
{{Noncanon|End}}

{{Ambig|Start}}
==={{HWAoC}}===
King Rhoam appears in this alternate take on the return of Calamity Ganon after being sealed for 10,000 years. Thanks to the changes wrought by [[Terrako]], the Calamity is successfully defeated with significantly less loss of life, including the survival of King Rhoam himself.
{{Ambig|End}}
