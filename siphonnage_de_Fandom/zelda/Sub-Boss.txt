==Characteristics==
In most video games, Mini-Bosses are a special type of [[enemy]] or non-player character often found in the middle section of a stage. Although [[boss]]-like in nature, as they share a unique battle theme song like their upper counterparts and are sometimes established antagonists, they are not as powerful as the main boss found in the same level but can still be dangerous. Some are simply stronger versions of a normal enemy, Generally, in terms of rank in the enemy force, they are at the intermediate level - between the line of basic enemies and true bosses. In some cases, a previously defeated boss can later become a mini-boss or even a standard enemy if they are battled more than once in the main storyline and the protagonist is significantly stronger compared to their first encounter.
[[File:TWW Stalfos Model.png|thumb|167x167px|A [[Stalfos]] in ''The Wind Waker'', an example of a Sub-Boss later encountered as a normal enemy]]
In [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' series]], Sub-Bosses are larger monsters that usually protect an important [[item]] within a {{Term|Series|Dungeon|link}} or other locations. Most of the time, they must be defeated to proceed within a dungeon or area. A common characteristic that distinguishes a Sub-Boss from a regular enemy is a particular battle theme played during the encounter, which is different from the usual enemy theme. They may return later as common enemies, likely because [[Link]] (or other playable protagonists) will have new tools and weapons to defeat them more easily. They may come in groups, as in the case of common enemies. If they are fought in a room, the doors usually lock until they are defeated. In certain games of the franchise, most often 2D installments, some bosses are later fought again as mini-bosses, as they are no longer guarding [[Heart Container|Heart Containers]] or other essential items related to the storyline.

== Trivia ==

* Certian Mini-Bosses in the series are infamous among fans for having powerful abilities that can make themselves more difficult to defeat than even the main boss of the [[Dungeon]]. Examples include [[Dark Link]] and [[Iron Knuckle (Boss)|Iron Knuckles]] from ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]''. Ironically, the former was introduced as a [[Final Boss]] in ''[[Zelda II: The Adventure of Link|The Adventure of Link]]'' as [[Link's Shadow]].
* ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening|Link's Awakening]]'' is the first game in [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' series]] to introduce the battle theme for Sub-Bosses. However, it wasn't until ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker|The Wind Waker]]'' when unique themes occur.
* In ''The Wind Waker'', when [[Link]] is low on [[Life Gauge|health]], the music theme of the Sub-Boss begins to play faster until he recovers.
* The Mini-Boss theme of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Minish Cap|The Minish Cap]]'' is a remix of the regular enemy battle theme from the original Japanese version of ''The Adventure of Link'' released on the [[Family Computer Disk System]].
