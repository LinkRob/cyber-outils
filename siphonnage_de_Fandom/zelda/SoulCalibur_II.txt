'''''[https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soulcalibur_II SoulCalibur II]''''' is an [[wikipedia:Arcade_video_game|arcade]] [[wikipedia:Fighting_game|fighting game]] produced by [[wikipedia:Namco|Namco]] (now [[wikipedia:Bandai_Namco_Entertainment|Bandai Namco Entertainment]]), released on July 10, 2002 and was simultaneously given home ports the following year for the [[Nintendo GameCube]], [[wikipedia:PlayStation_2|Sony PlayStation 2]], and [[wikipedia:Xbox_(console)|Microsoft Xbox]]. It is the third installment of the [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_series ''Soul'' series]. The console versions were made distinct by including a different [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Guest_character guest character] from separate franchises unique to that platform; [[Link]] from [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' series]] in particular is expectedly unique to the GameCube release of the game, contributing to its popularity, while [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Heihachi_Mishima Heihachi Mishima], a recurring major antagonist from the ''[https://tekken.fandom.com/wiki/Tekken_(series) Tekken]'' series (another fighting series by Namco), is unique to the PlayStation 2 version, and [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Spawn Spawn], an anti-hero from [https://imagecomics.fandom.com/wiki/Image_Comics_Database Image Comics], being unique to the Xbox release.

''SoulCalibur II'' was met with critical acclaim and due to its considerable sales and reviews at the arcade and home ports, it is considered among the greatest fighting games of all-time. Unsurprisingly, despite all individual versions of the game are successful, the Nintendo GameCube release is the best-selling among the three home console ports (1.09 million copies sold), due to Link himself widely being a favorite pick in the ''Soul'' series community alongside ''Zelda'' fans into the game, as well as finding his rare inclusion in a third-party title as a playable guest rather an excellent choice for ''SoulCalibur II''<nowiki/>'s setting unlike Heihachi and Spawn, which, similarly to ''The Legend of Zelda'', is a fantasy based on early European ages in a never-ending clash between good and evil.

In 2013, a high-definition port based on the PS2 version was released [[wikipedia:PlayStation_3|PlayStation 3]] and [[wikipedia:Xbox_360|Xbox 360]], called '''''SoulCalibur II HD Online'''''. However, although the guest characters Heihachi and Spawn reappear together in this remaster, Link does not, due to the fact he is exclusively owned by [[Nintendo]]. Because of this, the GameCube version has become a collector's item among fans today.

''SoulCalibur II'', specifically the GameCube version, is available on the [[Nintendo Switch 2]]'s Nintendo Classics online feature, marking the first time in over two decades this port has an official re-release.

==Prologue==
[[File:SCII title screen (GameCube).png|thumb|296x296px|Title screen (GameCube ver.)]]
"''Transcending history, a tale of souls and swords, eternally retold.''"

-Narrator

The game is set four years after the end of ''[https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soulcalibur Soulcalibur]'', which based on calculations from a timeline released on the "Soul Archive" website, puts the game's story in the year 1591.

The wave of slaughters that terrorized Europe reached a sudden end. The knight in azure armor, [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Nightmare Nightmare], and his followers were successful in collecting enough souls and were about to start the restoring ceremony on the ruins of the once-proud Osthreinsburg Castle. But just as the ceremony was to start, three young warriors assaulted the castle. In a matter of time the clan was defeated and Nightmare stood in front of the young warriors wielding the Sacred Trinity. After an intense battle Nightmare fell, but then the evil soul inside [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Edge Soul Edge] sent the young warriors into a vortex of hellfire and stood to confront them. As a result of Soul Edge's evil aura, [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Krita-Yuga Krita-Yuga] revealed its true form: that of the Holy Sword, [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Calibur Soul Calibur]. 

The intense battle ended with the victory of the holy sword, but at the collapse of the vortex of [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Inferno Inferno], both swords along with the azure Nightmare were sucked in a void and expelled in another place. [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Siegfried Siegfried Schtauffen], who was until a moment before the Azure Knight, restored his mind. Recognizing his sins, he set on a journey of atonement. Still the evil blade held a strong bond, and every night it took control of the body and took souls of those nearby. The efforts made by the young knight were fruitless, and four years later the Azure Nightmare returned.

Around those times various warriors came into contact of the demonic blade's remaining fragments, revealing Soul Edge's ultimate survival after its defeat. Driven either to possess or destroy it, they join a new journey, while Nightmare starts his rampage once again, seeking souls to restore Soul Edge once again...

== Gameplay ==
[[File:SCII Link fighting Astaroth.webp|thumb|289x289px|Gameplay screenshot of the Nintendo GameCube version, showing Link delivering a horizontal slash blow against [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Astaroth Astaroth] in a player versus battle]]
Like most other traditional fighting games, the objective is to bring the opponent's [[Life Gauge]] (represented as a green bar resembling the [[Magic Meter]]) down to zero before the player's within a set amount of rounds, knocking them out. If the timer runs out, the fighter with the most health wins the round (if equal, a draw occurs, which earns both a won round). If the computer opponent wins the required rounds first, the [[Game Over|game is over]]. In the home release, however, there are several different modes such as Survival or Team Battle where the goal is to survive matches for as long as possible or battle multiple characters, respectively. The Extra mode allows characters to use unlockable weapons for a different gameplay experience (such as Link using the [[Biggoron's Sword]] rather than the [[Master Sword]]). The exclusive Weapon Master mode is essentially a story mode where the player, after choosing a character including guests, goes on an adventure to stop Soul Edge from an antagonist named Veral (playing as Nightmare) and encounter their rival from time-to-time.

In most modes, the end of the game involves the character fighting Inferno, the game's [[Final Boss]]. When ultimately defeated, an ending cutscene plays about the character's aftermath before the player returns back to the title screen. In Weapon Master, however, the credits will roll.

==Soul Edge==
[[File:SCII Restored Soul Edge.jpg|thumb|242x242px|The Soul Edge, with most of its fragments restored|left]]
The '''[https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Edge Soul Edge]''' is an ancient cursed sword in the ''Soul'' series and is its main antagonist. It is generally referred to as a male.

Many warriors in the ''Soul'' universe set out to destroy the deadly evil sword, with some wanting to have it for their own reasons, but almost none prevailed. Meanwhile, in [[Hyrule]], the sword began to control an evil wizard, who was defeated by Link. To prevent this from happening again, the young hero set out to destroy it once and for all in the other world where it came from. He managed to destroy a fragment of the sword via Inferno, ending the evil that threatened Hyrule. This is the strongest and most dominating sword Link has ever encountered, even compared to his best blades.

Soul Edge has been wielded by many. The infamous [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Edge_(Male) male] Soul Edge is wielded by Nightmare (its [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Edge_(Growth) growth] and [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Edge_(Complete) true] forms are unlockable), and the [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Edge_(Female) female version] is wielded by [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Cervantes Cervantes] (who is the main antagonist of the first game, ''[https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Edge_(game) Soul Edge]''). Link can actually buy this weapon for 7,800 Gold in the Castor Shop while playing the Weapon Master mode. [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Edge_(Complete)_Link His version] of Soul Edge causes his defense and offense to greatly increase to 135% and 140%, respectively; however, it drains his health by 2 HP every second.<ref>{{Cite|Fearsome demonic weapon said to excel in offense and defense....|N/A|Soulcalibur II}}</ref>

== Soul Calibur ==
[[File:Soul Calibur artwork.png|thumb|240x240px|The Soul Calibur|left]]
The titular '''[https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Calibur Soul Calibur]''' is an ancient spirit sword in the ''Soul'' series and is its main protagonist. It is generally referred to as a female. 

The polar opposite of Soul Edge, this sacred weapon was wielded only by a select few who are pure of heart, and warriors over the ages often needed Soul Calibur in order to defeat the demonic spirit within Soul Edge, Inferno. In ''SoulCalibur II'', as revealed in Weapon Master mode, the legendary blade was corrupted by Soul Edge's power to become an [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Calibur_(Evil) evil state], and the player is required to restore it. Link has not heard much about this sword unlike its counterpart when he entered the world from Hyrule, but soon discovers its holy powers and general purpose are comparable to that of his [[Master Sword]], as it too is explicitly designed to banish pure evil. In-game, it can change shape like Soul Edge does (with the Krita-Yuga being its base form) and is even used for the final blow against Inferno in a similar manner Link does with the Blade of Evil's Bane for his archenemy [[Ganon]].
[[File:SCII Soul Calibur sword near Link.png|thumb|214x214px|Soul Calibur on the ground near Link after the defeat of Inferno, in a coincidentally similar position as the Master Sword during the final battle from ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]'']]
The standard version of Soul Calibur is wielded by Xianghua as her unlockable Ultimate Weapon. [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Talim Talim], and, oddly enough, Nightmare (possibly due to Siegfried being the host for Soul Edge), also can have the holy sword in the shape of [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Calibur_(Elbow_Blades) their] [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Calibur_(Zweih%C3%A4nder) weapons]. Unlike Soul Edge, however, Soul Calibur is strangely unavailable for Link despite being famously known as a heroic character and often uses such sacred [[Item|items]] ([[Light Arrow|Light Arrows]], [[Four Sword]], [[Silver Arrow|Silver Arrows]], etc.) in most ''The Legend of Zelda'' games. Although Link himself is technically suitable for controlling the legendary weapon, this may be excusable as he already possesses the Master Sword alongside the [[Magical Sword]] and [[Great Fairy's Sword]], rendering it unnecessary.

Much like Soul Edge, although not seen in ''SoulCalibur II'', as she wasn't introduced until ''[https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soulcalibur_V SoulCailbur V]'', the Soul Calibur has a powerful spiritual will of its own, and its being known as [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Elysium Elysium]. Ironically, in that installment of the ''Soul'' series, she serves as its main antagonist, but returns to good ways in ''[https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soulcalibur_VI SoulCalibur VI]'', the latest game so far.

==[[Link]]==
[[File:SCII Link.jpg|thumb|Link's {{SCII|-}} render|227x227px]]
''Main article: [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Link Link (SoulCalibur II)]'' 

Link, the main protagonist/mascot of ''The Legend of Zelda'', appears as a guest character exclusively for the [[Nintendo GameCube]] version of the game, and the incarnation featured in ''SoulCalibur II'' is the legendary [[Hero of Time]] from ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]''. He is available by default. 

===Biography===
[[Hyrule]]'s tranquility was shattered by the arrival of several disasters, which brought opaque, ominous clouds with them. Eventually, it was discovered that an unknown magician was to blame for all that had happened. Just as the people were about to lose all hope, the sun miraculously returned as Link, having been summoned by [[Princess Zelda]] to find the disasters' source, successfully destroys the magician. The people of Hyrule rejoiced in the belief that eternal peace would rule; but it eventually became clear that the magician responsible for the disasters was in fact controlled by an evil sword from another world, Soul Edge, and that the fragments of Soul Edge had been gathering in the other world.<ref>{{Cite|Just as the people were about to lose all hope, the sun miraculously returned. Link, who was summoned by Zelda to find the cause of the disasters, successfully defeated the magician. The people of Hyrule rejoiced in the belief that eternal peace would rule. Unfortunately, this was not to be. It became clear that the magician responsible for the disasters was in fact controlled by an evil sword called Soul Edge, which existed in an another world.|N/A|SCII}}</ref>

Knowing that the resurrection of the evil blade must be prevented at all costs, Link chose to travel to the other world and destroy Soul Edge himself, taking his trusted [[Master Sword]] and {{Term|OoT3D|Ocarina of Time (Item)|link}}, and transporting himself off on a secret journey, to fully accomplish his mission of restoring peace to Hyrule.<ref>{{Cite|Knowing that the resurrection of the evil blade must be prevented at all costs, Link chose to travel to the other world and destroy Soul Edge. Link gathered his trusted Master Sword, Hylian Shield, and Ocarina, and set off on a secret journey.|N/A|SCII}}</ref> Through the completion of Arcade and most other modes, Link ultimately succeeds in sealing away the evil within the Soul Edge via defeating Inferno, saving both his homeland and the ''Soul'' universe, and quietly leaving the world he had come upon, leaving only a faint echo of his Ocarina.<ref>{{Cite|Link came to this world in order to defeat the evil which threatened Hyrule. After a long journey, Link finally succeeded in sealing away the terrible evil. In doing so, he not only saved his homeland from peril, but also maintained the tranquility of this world. Link then quietly left this world--a faint echo of his Ocarina was all that remained.|N/A|SCII}}</ref> It is unknown how he returned back to Hyrule, although it is possible he used the Ocarina of Time to warp to his destination.

===General Information===
*Link's Destined Battle in Arcade mode is against [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Raphael Raphael], the game's main protagonist, which is shared for the guest characters in general.
*The [[The Legend of Zelda (Theme)|main theme from ''The Legend of Zelda'' series]] is used in Link's profile viewer and Destined Battle theme. It is also played if Link was used in the Eurydice Shrine Gallery.
*Link's stage is the Ostrheinsburg Chapel and his alternate stage is Eurydice Shrine Gallery.

===Costumes===
Instead of having the standard two or three alternate costumes like every other character, Link has 4 different costumes based on appearances from other ''Zelda'' series games: green, red, blue, and pale violet. His two starter costumes are his traditional [[Green Clothes|green tunic]] and the [[Goron Tunic]]. To obtain the [[Zora Armor|Zora Tunic]], the player must buy it in the Castor Shop for 21,000 Gold, found in the Extra Chapter 1 of the Weapon Master mode. Only after buying the Zora Tunic can the player buy the [[Blue Ring]] Tunic from the original {{TLoZ}}, which is sold for the same price as the Zora Tunic.

===Fighting Style===
{{Main|Soulcalibur II/Command List}}
In {{SCII|-}}, Link is the only character with a ranged attack and is self-taught. His fighting style retains many of his signature moves from past games in the {{TLoZ|Series}} and {{SSB|Series}}. Some of these moves include the [[Spin Attack]], the [[Jump Attack]], the [[Down Thrust]], the [[Jump Thrust]], and the [[Dash Attack]]. His range attacks have Link use the [[Bow]], [[Bomb|Bombs]], and the [[Boomerang]]. He can also do swift kicks.

===Weapons===
Like all {{SCII|-}} characters, Link has a total of 11 weapons to choose from. His starting and most-balanced weapon is the traditional [[Master Sword]] and [[Hylian Shield]], and the rest of the weapons must be bought in shops in various cities in the Weapon Master Mode. Although most of his unlockable weapons are from ''Ocarina of Time'' and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask|Majora's Mask]]'', a few are from games predating the two [[Nintendo 64]] titles where the Hero of Time was playable, specifically the original ''The Legend of Zelda'' and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past|A Link to the Past]]'' on the [[Nintendo Entertainment System|NES]] and [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System|Super NES]], respectively. Two of such, the [[Armos]] Series and [[Soul Edge (Complete)|Link's version of Soul Edge]], are exclusive to the game. The majority of the weapons are paired with the Hylian Shield for defense.

Unlike all other characters including Heihachi Mishima and Spawn, Link's starting weapon, the Master Sword & Hylian Shield, does not have an alternate 2P appearance, as the set remains identical had he wear the Goron Tunic costume.

==Trivia==
*Bandai Namco Entertainment, formerly Namco, the video game company who produced the ''Soulcalibur'' series, is widely known for being the creator of ''[https://tekken.fandom.com/wiki/Tekken_(series) Tekken]'', ''[https://acecombat.fandom.com/wiki/Ace_Combat Ace Combat]'', ''[https://ridgeracer.fandom.com/wiki/Ridge_Racer_(series) Ridge Racer]'', ''[https://aselia.fandom.com/wiki/Tales_Series Tales]'', ''[https://darksouls.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Souls Dark Souls]'', and of course, ''[https://pacman.fandom.com/wiki/Pac-Man_(series) Pac-Man]'' - its flagship franchise and the [https://pacman.fandom.com/wiki/Pac-Man titular character] being its mascot.
**Pac-Man in particular is featured as a playable fighter in the [[smashwiki:Super_Smash_Bros._(series)|''Super Smash Bros.'' series]] since the [[Super Smash Bros. for Nintendo 3DS|3DS]]/[[Super Smash Bros. for Wii U|Wii U]] installments.
*''SoulCalibur II'' marks the first and so far, only time [[Link]] were to ever appear in a true fighting game, as well as being in a title not made by [[Nintendo]].
**Although he was previously featured in ''Super Smash Bros.'', specifically the [[Super Smash Bros.|original]] and ''[[Super Smash Bros. Melee|Melee]]'', that series used a completely different ruleset of battling on platforms, and therefore are considered distinct from conventional fighting games.
**It is also the second time Nintendo were to make Link have an official crossover video game appearance. The next occurrence, ''[https://www.mariowiki.com/Mario_Kart_8 Mario Kart 8]'', was released 11 years later.
**It is arguably the most popular case of any Nintendo character being featured in a series other than the multi-billiondollar company's.
*Interestingly, alongside having a unique guest character, each home port's box art of ''SoulCalibur II'' has a certain important weapon of the ''Soul'' series on the title logo and a different color theme, with the GameCube version having Soul Calibur (green), while the PlayStation 2 release shows the [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Calibur_(Evil) evil version of Soul Calibur] (blue) and the Xbox has Soul Edge (red).
*The GameCube release of ''SoulCalibur II'' is one of the best-selling 3rd-party titles for the console.
*Link's guest appearance in Namco's ''SoulCalibur II'' exclusively for the GameCube version can be considered a parallel to [[Mario]], [[Luigi]], and [[Princess Peach]] from the [[mariowiki:Mario_(franchise)|''Super Mario'' franchise]] later featured in sports games ''[[wikipedia:SSX_on_Tour|SSX on Tour]]'' and ''[[wikipedia:NBA_Street_V3|NBA Street V3]]'' by Electronic Arts (both were released in 2005), as they share the same rare case of being playable in a non-Nintendo game only for the console's port.
*The guest character of the PlayStation 2 version was originally going to be [https://finalfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Cloud_Strife Cloud Strife] from the ''[https://finalfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Final_Fantasy_series Final Fantasy]'' franchise, who himself is the main protagonist of ''[https://finalfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Final_Fantasy_VII Final Fantasy VII]''. However, his appearance in ''SoulCalibur II'' was canceled when the deal with Square Enix was struck, resulting in Namco using one of their own characters, [https://tekken.fandom.com/wiki/Heihachi_Mishima Heihachi Mishima], from the related ''Tekken'' series. This had left many fans disappointed, finding Heihachi's inclusion in the ''Soul'' series installment not unique, unlike Link and [https://spawn.fandom.com/wiki/Spawn Spawn].
*Link, alongside Spawn, was one of the first characters in the ''Soul'' series to use projectiles, in his case, [[Arrow|Arrows]], [[Bomb|Bombs]], and the [[Boomerang]].[[File:SCII Inferno up-close.webp|thumb|236x236px|A close-up view of Inferno, showing Soul Calibur (Evil) inside his body]]
*If one were to look closely at Inferno in this game, the demonic being of Soul Edge, he actually has [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Calibur_(Evil) Soul Calibur as its corrupted state] within the head and chest area of its skeletal body, likely as a power source. When defeated, the sacred blade seemingly comes out from his back, returning to normal, and then impales Inferno to ultimately make him explode into thin air.
**On a similar note, Inferno normally uses the weapon version of himself including when changing fighting styles, but on Extra modes, his set becomes random like [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Charade Charade]'s and it is possible for him to even wield the true Soul Calibur.
**Although not available under normal circumstances, Inferno is playable with the use of a cheating device. However, he is slightly unstable in the GameCube version and can potentially crash the game if the fiery monster were to use Ivy's fighting style.
*The character [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Necrid Necrid] is exclusive for the home console releases of the game. He himself shares the same creator as Spawn - Todd McFarlane.
*[https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Siegfried Siegfried Schtauffen] (who is Nightmare in this game), the franchise's protagonist, as well as [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Yoshimitsu Yoshimitsu], coincidentally share the same Japanese voice actor as Link, [[Nobuyuki Hiyama]]. ''SoulCalibur II'' is also the final game where Hiyama himself would reprise his role as Adult Link before his retirement for the Nintendo protagonist. Notably, the actor did not perform a deep voice for the Hero of Time like he did in ''Ocarina of Time'' or as [[Fierce Deity Link]] in ''Majora's Mask'', but rather in the same tone as Nightmare.{{Fact}}
**The Japanese voice actress for [[Young Link]], [[Voice Acting in The Legend of Zelda Series|Fujiko Takimoto]], voices the character {{Wp|Taki_(Soulcalibur)|Taki}} in this game for the same language as well.
*Aside from Charade (itself a fragment of Soul Edge), Link is the only character in the game to have his voice remain unchanged regardless of the dub option set to Japanese or English, due to not having any sentences to speak.
*Excluding guest characters, ''SoulCalibur II'' features a few crossover elements from older Namco franchises. Specifically, the characters [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Sophitia Sophitia] and [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Cassandra Cassandra] has a certain unlockable weapon, the [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Blue_Crystal_Rod Blue Crystal Rod] and [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Crystal_Rod_%26_Red_Line_Shield Red Crystal Rod], respectively, which are from the 1984 arcade ''[https://towerofdruaga.fandom.com/wiki/The_Tower_of_Druaga_(video_game) The Tower of Druaga]'' of the [[wikipedia:Babylonian_Castle_Saga|''Babylon Castle Saga'']] series and are essential items for its protagonist [https://towerofdruaga.fandom.com/wiki/Gilgamesh Gilgamesh] to obtain in order to defeat the main antagonist [https://towerofdruaga.fandom.com/wiki/Druaga Druaga] himself. Sophita's second alternate costume has herself resemble [https://towerofdruaga.fandom.com/wiki/Ki Ki], the deuteragonist.
**Interestingly, the challenging action role-playing maze game is considered a precursor of ''The Legend of Zelda''.
*In the game's demo sequence, the two characters' age, weapons, and origin are shown to the player halfway in the battle and when one wins. For Link, however, because his age is unknown, and often varies between the ''Zelda'' games and other media, it reads as "--".
*Link's fighting stance in ''SoulCalibur II'' is similar to his style via [[Targeting|Z-Targeting]] in ''Ocarina of Time'' and ''Majora's Mask''.
*Charade and Inferno, who randomly use various weapons and styles from every of the main characters, cannot wield Link's weapons (as well as the other guest characters). Story-wise, this is excusable, as Link does not originate in the ''Soul'' universe and [[Hyrule]] is beyond the villains' reach.
*For unknown reasons, whenever Link is equipped with his unlockable weapons, including the [[Mirror Shield]] where he wields the legendary blade, his [[Scabbard]] for carrying the Master Sword and [[Hylian Shield]] on his back is missing, possibly due to graphical limitations.
**Certain weapons such as the [[Megaton Hammer]], [[Biggoron's Sword]], or the [[Bug Net|Bug-Catching Net]] can be seen equipped on his back rather comically when using the Boomerang, [[Bow]], or in the Destined Battle cutscene against Raphael.
*The unlockable [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Magic_Sword_and_Magic_Shield Magic Sword and Magic Shield] in ''SoulCalibur II'' is actually the Magical Sword/[[Magical Shield|Shield]] from the original ''The Legend of Zelda'' and are in a completely different appearance in contrast to the NES game, with the sword having a rugged edge with a slight resemblance to the Master Sword, while the shield does not have the [[wikipedia:Christian_cross|Christian cross]] in the center and is mostly blue/silver rather than brown.
*If Link uses his Bow at full power, especially while having a [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Charge Soul Charge], its Arrow will become a [[Fire Arrow]] to inflict more damage.[[File:SCII Link Biggoron's Sword.png|thumb|285x285px|Link holding the hefty Biggoron's Sword, a two-handed blade, with just his left hand in ''SoulCalibur II'']]
*As opposed to their original source material in ''Ocarina of Time'', Link in this game is able to wield the Megaton Hammer and Biggoron's Sword, both known to be cumbersome weapons, in only one hand rather than two (implying great physical strength). Although the Great Fairy's Sword was originally a two-handed weapon in ''Majora's Mask'', the Hero of Time was a child in the latter Nintendo 64 game, and as an adult in ''SoulCalibur II'', he can use the powerful blade in the same manner as the Master Sword.
**Ironically, in these ''Zelda'' games, if Link were to equip them during cutscenes or in his idle animations, he can be seen wielding those weapons with one hand.
*Link's [[Dash Attack]] in ''SoulCalibur II'', in stark contrast to its in-game move name of "Hyper Dash Attack", behaves in a significantly different manner as opposed to the ''Zelda'' games, specifically ''A Link to the Past'' and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening|Link's Awakening]]''; Unlike its official source material, the hero is not wearing the [[Pegasus Boots]], simply runs toward his opponent for a short time rather than actually [[dashing]], and he uses his sword (or other weapons) with both of his hands like that of a spear weapon rather than one. However, he can still crash into walls, causing Link to briefly fall down to the ground.
**On a similar note, it is also possible for Link to side-jump from walls when cornered by opponents.
*Link's Bombs in this game have their time fuses function differently from the ''Zelda'' games and other spin-off appearances. Rather than flashing and pulse larger as they are about to explode, they catch on fire instead. The fuses themselves are also unlit when thrown.
*Link's [[Jump Thrust]] attack in this game, which goes by the name "Rocket Stab", unlike the ''Super Smash Bros.'' series, is accurate to how the technique was seen in ''[[Zelda II: The Adventure of Link|The Adventure of Link]]'' (its only mainline appearance in ''Zelda''), as he jumps up with one only hand on his sword rather than two.
*Although Link's non-green tunics, as seen from early games of the [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' series]], are known to give [[Defense|defensive upgrades]] and additional bonus protection against certain hazards, they do not serve such purpose in ''SoulCalibur II'', much like in the ''Super Smash Bros.'' series where they simply function as palette swaps of his [[Green Clothes|main costume]], as he still quickly takes damage when standing on lava in his second player alternate costume, the [[Goron Tunic]], on booby-trapped stages like other outfits (it should be noted that the tunic in ''Ocarina of Time'' does not provide complete immunity against magma or fire).
*Despite the weapon description of the Bug-Catching Net saying it has accompanied Link on "many adventures", the actual item has rarely appeared in the ''Zelda'' series. It so far existed only in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past|A Link to the Past]]'', ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Skyward Sword|Skyward Sword]]'', and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link Between Worlds|A Link Between Worlds]]''. Link's seemingly impractical use of the net as a sword in this game would become referenced in ''A Link Between Worlds'' from the [[Super Net]] upgrade, where it can damage enemies.[[File:SCII Link Arm Buster.png|thumb|280x280px|Link is shown to attack rather violently in this game in comparison to his home franchise or other appearances such as ''Super Smash Bros.'', as seen with himself twisting an opponent's arm for example, in spite of the character being well-known as a fighter in all cases]]
*Although ''SoulCalibur II'' is thought of as a "violent fighting game" and some fans find it strange to feature a Nintendo character like Link, as the company normally has a stance against violence (especially [[Nintendo of America]]) and mainly focuses on family-friendly content, ''The Legend of Zelda'' series creator [[Shigeru Miyamoto]] did not see a problem with Link being in the Namco title as a guest star, as the character himself had already<nowiki/> been established as a fighter in the first two ''Super Smash Bros.'' games by ''SoulCalibur II'''s release, plus he is clearly such in his home series. Ironically, the two different fighting series both had a Teen ("T") rating by the [[ESRB]] (though only ''Melee'' and ''[[Super Smash Bros. Brawl|Brawl]]'' obtain this level for the latter), and the ''Zelda'' franchise itself is no stranger, as ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess|Twilight Princess]]'', ''[[Link's Crossbow Training]]'', and the ''[[Hyrule Warriors]]'' games, were also rated Teen. In addition, ''Ocarina of Time'', despite being Everyone ("E"), also had brief moments of [[Controversy in The Legend of Zelda Series|controversial violence]] during its finale that risked a much higher rating.
**According to Namco, Link is the most difficult character to create for ''SoulCalibur II'', as everything about the character has to be strictly approved by Nintendo. There have been some instances during development where some of his attacks had to be toned down due to being "too violent for a Nintendo character" (easily seen with the Bomb throw animation). Despite this, Link's moveset is seen to be more brutal than in a typical 3D ''Zeld''<nowiki/>''a'' game and even the ''Super Smash Bros.'' series.
*Although the [[Cane of Byrna]] is by no means an actual weapon in ''A Link to the Past'' much like the Bug-Catching Net, in ''SoulCalibur II'', if Link were to perform a Soul Charge, it becomes his most powerful weapon in his arsenal, as its attack power goes up to 150%, surpassing the Biggoron's Sword, Megaton Hammer, Great Fairy's Sword, and even his version of Soul Edge.[[File:SCII Link vs Cassandra.jpg|thumb|249x249px|Link dueling against Cassandra, a fighter believed to be similar to himself]]
*The characters Sophitia and her younger sister Cassandra, as they are young swordswomen and the latter having an even more courageous personality, are seen by some fans as the female equivalents of Link. Cassandra in particular has also become a popular internet meme because of this.
*Link's blinking animation is slightly buggy, as at times he can b<nowiki/>e <nowiki/>seen blinking twice in a row.
*The [[Razor Sword]] and Megaton Hammer are depicted as more powerful than the Master Sword. This is an odd case, as in ''Majora's Mask'' for the former, the weapon is only double the strength of the [[Kokiri Sword]], which is identical to the Master Sword's power in ''Ocarina of Time'', and also how powerful the latter weapon in that game. However, the Megaton Hammer being stronger than the sacred blade in ''SoulCalibur II'' may be excusable, as aside from shorter reach, the key item from the [[Fire Temple]] designed to defeat [[Volvagia]] is able to smash large rocks and break certain walls.
*As Link is a very commonly chosen character by players due to his obvious popularity from ''The Legend of Zelda'' series, the fact he is featured in a non-Nintendo game (which is extremely rare in general), and the incarnation used is none other than the [[Hero of Time]], he is often claimed to be among the best playable fighters in ''SoulCalibur II'', especially for special guests. Contrary to popular belief, however, Link is actually frowned upon in competitive play; he is listed among the lowest-tier (similar to ''Super Smash Bros.''), most players resort to throws to achieve a Ring Out, and he is even banned in professional tournaments, not unlike fellow guests Heihachi and Spawn (except for ''HD Online'').
*If Link battles against the hidden character [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Lizardman/Generic Lizardman], the fight coincidentally resembles how the character faces the [[Lizalfos]] and [[Dynalfos]] enemies in the ''Zelda'' games.[[File:SCII Link Fighting Ivy.jpg|thumb|246x246px|Link is noticeably short in ''SoulCalibur II'', as seen with himself Guard Impacting [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Ivy Ivy], whose official height is 5'10 ½"]]
*As Link's official height is unknown, although the Hero of Time is of average size in ''Ocarina of Time,'' as he likely stands 1.7m (5'7") according to the corrected measuring graph in the [[Lakeside Laboratory]] in the [[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time 3D|3DS remake]], he strangely appears much shorter than nearly all of the characters in ''SoulCalibur II -'' including Heihachi and Spawn, who, according to their profiles, are often (but not all) between 5'6"-6'5", suggesting that Link is only about 5'0" in-game.
*Although not canon to either ''The Legend of Zelda'' nor ''Soul'' series in any way, Link's in-game story in ''SoulCalibur II'' is a reference to not only ''Ocarina of Time'', but also ''Majora's Mask'' and ''A Link to the Past'' as well. The Hero of Time, in the opening cutscene, pulls the Master Sword from its pedestal in the [[Lost Woods]] as opposed the [[Temple of Time]] and he took down an unseen evil magician to save [[Hyrule]], which could possibly be [[Agahnim]] or the [[Magician]] (despite this Link not actually being featured in the pre-N64 titles). As his journey in the game is in fact a ''second'' adventure and he leaves the ''Soul'' universe to return back to Hyrule after saving the day, this is a clear homage to his quest in [[Termina]]. In addition, alongside having various weapons and the [[Blue Ring]] tunic from ''A Link to the Past'' and earlier NES titles, many ''The Legend of Zelda'' series fans who played this game considered Link's appearance to be self-contained or [[The Legend of Zelda timeline|timeline]]-confused. 
*The unique [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Armos_Series Armos Series] unlockable weapon for Link in ''SoulCalibur II'', although the arms are based on the [[Armos]]' seen in ''Ocarina of Time'', the enemies never use them as they hop to attack. In addition, Link's version of the sword is notably larger with the head of such foe on its guard and the rock-like shield has minor damage, showing a crack on its front, which is accurate to the official artwork of the Armos in the N64 title.
*The Great Fairy's Sword and Bug-Catching Net serve as Link's [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Ultimate_weapon Ultimate] and [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Joke_Weapons Joke] Weapons, respectively, which can be identified with a unique jingle upon buying them in Weapon Master mode.
*Despite being a non-canon character in ''SoulCalibur II'' as with all other guests, Link's fighting style to an extent stayed within the ''Soul'' series for a brief time, possibly as a homage. For the game's sequel, ''[https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soulcalibur_III SoulCalibur III]'', the [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/BattleStyle:Sword_%26_Shield "Sword & Shield"] discipline retains a small bit of the Hero of Time's moveset, but any direct references to ''The Legend of Zelda'' franchise such as the Spin Attack, Dash Attack, Bow, etc., are removed for obvious reasons. However, unlike Link, no character in that title using this style were left-handed.[[File:MM Fierce Deity Link Artwork.png|thumb|Although Link can use the [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_Edge_(Complete) complete Soul Edge] in ''Soul Calibur II'', he is no stranger to darker weaponry or powers, as famously seen as his deadly Fierce Deity form in ''Majora's Mask'']]
*Despite Link, as the main protagonist of the long-ongoing ''Zelda'' franchise, is widely known for banishing evil and is pure of heart like the character [https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Talim Talim], himself able to optionally [[Soul Edge (Complete)|wield the very weapon he was set out to destroy - Soul Edge]], was not technically unheard of by the arcade's home release in 2003. On rare occasions since ''Majora's Mask'' (released three years prior), the young hero is capable of harnessing dark powers; the prime example is the infamous Fierce Deity Link transformation by the [[Fierce Deity Mask|Fierce Deity's Mask]].
*Although this applies to all characters and weapons, when Link performs a full Soul Charge and is wielding the Master Sword, the legendary blade will resemble how it appears when possessing the [[Power to Repel Evil]].
*According to an issue from ''Electronic Gaming Monthly'' around time of the game's release, Link's model in ''SoulCalibur II'' was depicted with visible genitals (which can barely be seen through his tights), which caused minor controversy due to his hailing from a more family-oriented franchise.
