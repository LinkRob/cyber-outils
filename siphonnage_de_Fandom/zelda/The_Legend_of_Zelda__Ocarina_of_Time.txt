'''''The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time''''' is the fifth main installment of {{TLoZ|Series}} and the first to be released for the [[Nintendo 64]]. It was one of the most highly anticipated games of its age,<ref>[http://www.gamesarefun.com/gamesdb/review.php?reviewid=130 Import Review: The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time - Games Are Fun]</ref> and is listed among the greatest video games ever created by numerous websites and magazines.<ref>[http://filibustercartoons.com/games.htm The Best Video Games in the History of Humanity - Fillbuster Cartoons]</ref> It was released in Japan on November 21, 1998, the United States on November 23, 1998, Europe on December 11, 1998, & Australia on December 18, 1998. It is the first game in the long-running franchise to display 3D graphics.

Widely considered to be a masterpiece, it most famously scored the first perfect 40/40 score in Famitsu Magazine - a feat that only 26 games (including {{TWW}}, {{SS}} and {{BotW}}) have ever achieved.<ref>[http://firsthour.net/editorial/the-famitsu-40-40-list-2011-perfect-score-update ''The Famitsu 40/40 List'', The First Hour]</ref> In addition, G4 television declared it "the #1 game of all time" as well as [[Nintendo Power]]. {{OoT|-}} is listed in Guinness World Records - Gamer's Edition 2008, as the highest ranked game of all time.<ref>http://gamers.guinnessworldrecords.com/records/nintendo.aspx</ref> In the 2010 Gamer's Edition, {{OoT|-}} was updated as "the most critically acclaimed game of all time."<ref>http://gamers.guinnessworldrecords.com/records/nintendo.aspx</ref> It is also the highest scoring game of all time on Metacritic, at 99/100.<ref name="metacritic">http://www.metacritic.com/browse/games/score/metascore/all/all?sort=desc Highest Scores - Metacritic</ref> It has also been ranked the [[wikipedia:List_of_video_games_considered_the_best|greatest video game of all time]] by dozens of other publications.{{Ref Cluster|<ref name="best_games"/><ref name=CVG2001/><ref name="edge100-2000"/><ref name="edge100-2007"/><ref name="edge-200-online"/><ref name="edge-top20"/><ref name="ign_top_2006"/><ref name="ign_top_2008"/><ref name="NP Top 200"/><ref name=NinPowerBestofBest/><ref name=NinPowerTop285/><ref name=GF2009/><ref name="gamestats"/><ref name="GamingBolt"/><ref name="videogamer"/><ref name="GI1999"/><ref name="Slant"/><ref name="FHM"/><ref name="Joystik"/><ref name=PALGN/>}} Therefore, like its contemporary ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_64|Super Mario 64]]'', ''Ocarina of Time'' is universally considered one of the most important and influential games of not only the ''Zelda'' franchise but the video game industry as a whole, and is essentially a repeat of ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]'' in terms of revolution for the [[wikipedia:Action-adventure_game|action-adventure]] genre but in 3D. With 7.6 million copies sold worldwide, for 19 years, this installment was the best-selling standalone title of the series until ''Breath of the Wild'' in 2017 - itself selling a whopping 35.3 million (primarily due to being a launch title for the [[Nintendo Switch]]).

An altered, more difficult version called ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Master Quest|Master Quest]]'' was released for the [[Nintendo GameCube]] through ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Collector's Edition|Collector's Edition]]''. At E3 2010, a remake of the game for [[Nintendo 3DS]] was announced, called {{OoT3D}}. The remake was released in June 2011 and it includes ''Master Quest'' with the addition of mirrored gameplay. As with most older ''Zelda'' games, the original version has been re-released multiple times through Virtual Console and [[Nintendo Switch Online]].

A second remake of the game titled "''Ocarina of Time''" was announced at the [[Nintendo Direct]] on June 9, 2026, and will be released later in the year for the [[Nintendo Switch 2]]. This makes ''Ocarina of Time'' the first and so far only installment in the franchise to have more than one official remake.

''[[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask]]'' is the game's direct sequel, while ''The Wind Waker'' is its indirect equivalent.

===Events leading up to {{OoT|-}}===
{{Main|Hyrulean Civil War}}
Approximately ten years before {{OoT|-}}'s story begins, there was a war known as the [[Hyrulean Civil War]].<ref>{{Cite|Some time ago, before the King of Hyrule unified this country, there was a fierce war in our world. One day, to escape from the fires of the war, a Hylian mother and her baby boy entered this forbidden forest. The mother was gravely injured... Her only choice was to entrust the child to the Deku Tree, the guardian spirit of the forest. The Deku Tree could sense that this was a child of destiny, whose fate would affect the entire world, so he took him into the forest. After the mother passed away, the baby was raised as a Kokiri.|Deku Tree Sprout|OoT}}</ref> This war explains the origins of several characters and provides extra backstory to their motives.

For as long as he can remember, Link has been ostracized by the Kokiri and has never quite fit in, except for one girl named [[Saria]].<ref>{{Cite|Hey you! "Mr. No Fairy!" What's your business with the Great Deku Tree? Without a fairy, you're not even a real man!|Mido|OoT}}</ref> One day, the Great Deku Tree sends [[Navi]] the fairy to summon the boy to him.<ref>{{Cite|{{Color|OoT3D Light Blue|Navi}}…go now! The fate of the forest, nay, the world, depends upon thee!|Great Deku Tree|OoT}}</ref> In order to test young Link's courage, the Great Deku Tree bids Link to venture inside his hollow and break the curse cast upon him by a "[[Ganondorf|wicked man dressed in black]]."<ref>{{Cite|I have been cursed... Link complies, but his efforts are for naught; the Great Deku Tree's death was sealed in the pages of fate before the hero's adventure had even begun.<ref>{{Cite|Though your valiant efforts to break the curse were successful, I was doomed before you started...|Great Deku Tree|OoT}}</ref> With his final breath, the Great Deku Tree bestows upon Link the [[Spiritual Stones|Spiritual Stone]] of the [[Forest]], the [[Kokiri's Emerald]], and entreats him to travel to [[Hyrule Castle]] and meet with Princess Zelda.<ref>{{Cite|Link... Go now to Hyrule Castle... There, thou will surely meet the Princess of Destiny... Take this stone with you. The stone that man wanted so much, that he cast the curse on me...|Great Deku Tree|OoT}}</ref>

[[File:MeetingPrincessZeldaOOT.jpg|thumb|right|Young Princess Zelda introducing herself to Link|272x272px]]
After traveling across [[Hyrule Field]] and passing through [[Hyrule Castle Town]], Link does not take long to realize that speaking to the princess of [[Hyrule]] will not be a simple matter; many [[Soldier|guard]]s stand watch, alert and ready to catch any trespassers. However, he manages to infiltrate the castle, bypassing the front gate and evading all of the guards in his path.<ref>{{Cite|Wha-ha-ha! What a crazy guy! Can you believe this guy was crazy enough to try to sneak into the castle to see Princess Zelda? All because of this idiot, they've tightened security at the castle. Wha-ha-hah!|Hylian|OoT}}</ref> When he reaches the castle itself, the raised drawbridge seems like an insurmountable obstacle. However, Link discovers an unguarded water drain by the castle moat and manages to crawl through the hole, emerging within the castle garden. Many more guards are on patrol here, but Link is able to dodge all of them and sneak into the [[Castle Courtyard]]. In this Courtyard, his fate converges with that of the young Princess of Hyrule.

Princess Zelda tells Link of her prophetic dreams, explaining that she had actually seen the young hero come from the forest and break through a veil of darkness, accompanied by a Fairy guide and bearing the [[Kokiri's Emerald]].<ref>{{Cite|I had a dream... In the dream, dark storm clouds were billowing over the land of Hyrule... But suddenly, a ray of light shot out of the forest, parted the clouds and lit up the ground... The light turned into a figure holding a green and shining stone, followed by a fairy... I know this is a prophecy that someone would come from the forest... Yes, I thought you might be the one...|Princess Zelda|OoT}}</ref> She also warns him about [[Ganondorf]], the man clad in black whom Zelda believes is symbolized by the dark clouds of her dreams,<ref>{{Cite|Can you see the man with the evil eyes? That is Ganondorf, the leader of the Gerudos. They hail from the desert far to the west. Though he swears allegiance to my father, I am sure he is not sincere. The dark clouds that covered Hyrule in my dream... They must symbolize that man!|Princess Zelda|OoT}}</ref> and his evil intention to steal the [[Triforce]] of legend from the [[Sacred Realm]].<ref>{{Cite|What Ganondorf is after must be nothing less than the Triforce of the Sacred Realm. He must have come to Hyrule to obtain it! And, he wants to conquer Hyrule... no, the entire world!|Princess Zelda|OoT}}</ref> With the power of the [[Golden Goddess|Goddesses]], his desire to subjugate the world would be realized. In order to do this, Ganondorf would require not only the three Spiritual Stones of Hyrule, but also the mystical {{Term|OoT3D|Ocarina of Time (Item)|link}}. Zelda insists that Link should track down the other two Spiritual Stones, so that they might beat Ganondorf to the Triforce and put an end to his wicked ingenious plot.<ref>{{Cite|You go find the other two Spiritual Stones! Let's get the Triforce before Ganondorf does, and then defeat him!|Princess Zelda|OoT}}</ref> Here, he soon meets [[Impa]], the loyal attendant of the princess and a member of the [[Sheikah]]. 

Link sets out for [[Death Mountain]] and [[Zora's Domain]], where he succeeds in assisting both the [[Goron]]s and the [[Zora]]s (particularly [[Darunia]] and [[Princess Ruto]]) in quelling the calamities that Ganondorf had wrought in his pursuit of the Triforce and is awarded with the other two [[Spiritual Stones]], the [[Goron's Ruby]] and [[Zora's Sapphire]]<ref>{{Cite|You obtained the Goron's Ruby! This is the Spiritual Stone of Fire passed down by the Gorons!|N/A|OoT}}</ref><ref>{{Cite|You obtained Zora's Sapphire! [[File:Escapezelda.jpg|thumb|left|Zelda and Impa flee from Ganondorf|292x292px]]
He returns to Hyrule Castle to inform Zelda of his success, only to witness his ominous nightmare he experienced at the very beginning of his quest become a reality; Ganondorf attacked Hyrule Castle (likely murdering the [[King of Hyrule]] in the process) in an attempt to steal the Ocarina of Time, and Princess Zelda was forced to flee with Impa on horseback in order to keep the Triforce from Ganondorf's hands. As she passes the young hero on the drawbridge of [[Hyrule Castle Town]], she quickly throws the Ocarina into the moat to give him the chance to enter the Sacred Realm and retrieve the Triforce. In hot pursuit, Ganondorf himself charges across the drawbridge on horseback, blasting Link with dark magic when he tries to stand in his way.<ref>{{Cite|You want a piece of me?! Very funny! I like your attitude! Pathetic little fool! Do you realize who you are dealing with?! I am Ganondorf! [[File:OoT Pedestal of Time Artwork.png|thumb|right|Young Link draws the Master Sword|287x287px]]When Link retrieves the Ocarina of Time from the moat, he receives a telepathic message from Zelda, telling him that she won't be around anymore and to play the "[[Song of Time]]" in front of the Temple of Time's altar.<ref>{{Cite|Now, Link. Play this melody in front of the altar in the Temple of Time. Beyond it lies the legendary [[Master Sword]], also known as the Blade of Evil's Bane, resting in the [[Pedestal of Time]]. Link draws the magical blade, unlocking the gateway to the Sacred Realm. Despite the Master Sword accepting Link as its wielder, it suddenly seals the young hero away in the Sacred Realm.<ref>{{Cite|However, you were too young to be the Hero of Time....Therefore, your spirit was sealed here for seven years.|Rauru|OoT}}</ref> Ganondorf, who had suspected that Link might have already acquired the keys to the Sacred Realm, secretly followed him, appears, and mocks him for leading him to the Triforce.<ref>{{Cite|Geh heh heh! ===The Hero of Time===
[[File:OoT Cast.jpg|alt=|left|thumb|289x289px|Artwork depicting several main characters from {{OoT|-}}]]
Seven years later, a grown-up Link is awakened by an old man in the [[Chamber of Sages]]. He introduces himself as [[Rauru]], and reveals himself as one of the ancient [[Sages]] that preside over the Sacred Realm.<ref>{{Cite|I am Rauru, one of the ancient Sages...|Rauru|OoT}}</ref> Rauru explains that only the "Hero of Time" can pull the Master Sword from its Pedestal. However, Link was too young to be this title, so his spirit was sealed in the Sacred Realm for 7 years until he is mature enough to be accounted for.<ref>{{Cite|The Master Sword is a sacred blade that evil ones may never touch... Only one worthy of the title of "Hero of Time" can pull it from the Pedestal of Time... However, you were too young to be the Hero of Time... Therefore, your spirit was sealed here for seven years.|Rauru|OoT}}</ref> Rauru further reveals that Ganondorf obtained the Triforce after gaining access to the Sacred Realm, and used its great power to transform Hyrule into a land of darkness.<ref>{{Cite|Though you opened the Door of Time in the name of peace... Ganondorf, the Gerudo King of Thieves, used it to enter this forbidden Sacred Realm! He obtained the Triforce from the Temple of Light, and with its power, he became the King of Evil...His evil power radiated from the temples of Hyrule, and in seven short years, it transformed Hyrule into a world of monsters.|Rauru|OoT}}</ref> Rauru urges Link to seek out the remaining Sages, and grants the hero his power in the form of the [[Light Medallion]].

Link returns to the Temple of Time, where he is greeted by a mysterious young man named [[Sheik]], one of the few survivors of the ancient [[Sheikah]] tribe. He tells Link of Ganondorf's conquest over the last seven years, and of the [[Sheikah/Sheikah Legends|legend]] of the Sages.<ref>{{Cite|When evil rules all, an awakening voice from the Sacred Realm will call those destined to be Sages, who dwell in the five temples. One in a deep forest... One on a high mountain... One under a vast lake... One within the house of the dead... One inside a goddess of the sand... Together with the Hero of Time, the awakened ones will bind the evil and return the light of peace to the world... This is the legend of the temples passed down by my people, the Sheikah.|Sheik|OoT}}</ref> He urges the hero to awaken the five remaining Sages in the five Temples mentioned in the legend, which are scattered throughout Hyrule.<ref>{{Cite|If you believe the legend, you have no choice. You must look for the five temples and awaken the five Sages...|Sheik|OoT}}</ref> Wielding the Blade of Evil's Bane, Link sets out to break the curse on all of the sacred Temples and awaken the new Sages within, who are each revealed to be his friends he aided during his childhood, and in turn, bestowed the hero more power with their own Medallions like Rauru.
[[File:Triforce (Ocarina of Time).png|thumb|228x228px|The Triforce, the important omnipotent relic created by the Golden Goddesses]]

Sheik tells Link another legend of the Triforce passed down by the Sheikah. The Triforce itself is a grand scale that measures the three virtues ruled by the [[Golden Goddesses]]: Power, Wisdom, and Courage. If the heart of someone who holds the sacred triangle carries all three of these forces in balance, that person will acquire the Triforce intact, the divine authority to govern all.<ref>{{Cite|If the heart of the one who holds the sacred triangle has all three forces in balance, that one will gain the True Force to govern all.|Princess Zelda|OoT}}</ref> If one's heart is not in balance, the relic will separate into three parts, and only one part will remain for the one who touched the Triforce: that part which embodies the force that one most believes in.<ref>{{Cite|But, if that one's heart is not in balance, the Triforce will separate into three parts: Power, Wisdom and Courage. If the one with an unbalanced heart would seek the full Triforce, then that person must strive to acquire the two lost parts, which will rest within two others chosen by destiny who will hold the crest of the goddesses on the backs of their hands.<ref>{{Cite|If that one seeks the True Force, that one must acquire the two lost parts. Those two parts will be held within others chosen by destiny, who will bear the Triforce mark on the backs of their hands.|Princess Zelda|OoT}}</ref> When Ganondorf laid his hands upon the Triforce, the prophecy came to pass; The Triforce indeed split into its three parts, and only the [[Triforce of Power]] remained in Ganondorf's hands.<ref>{{Cite|Seven years ago, Ganondorf, the King of Thieves, used the door you opened in the Temple of Time and entered the Sacred Realm. But when he laid his hands on the Triforce, the legend came true.|Princess Zelda|OoT}}</ref>

Ganondorf had become the Evil King, and the Sacred Realm became a world of evil.|Princess Zelda|OoT}}</ref> but his lust for power was not yet satisfied. Ganondorf began hunting for those chosen by destiny to obtain the other two pieces of the Triforce that had escaped his grasp.<ref>{{Cite|To gain complete mastery of the world, Ganondorf started looking for those chosen by destiny to hold the two other Triforce parts.|Sheik|OoT}}</ref> Sheik reveals that Link himself wields the [[Triforce of Courage]],<ref>{{Cite|The one who holds the Triforce of Courage is... you, Link!|Sheik|OoT}}</ref> and ultimately sheds his disguise to reveal his true identity as Princess Zelda, the seventh Sage as well as their leader who wields the [[Triforce of Wisdom]].<ref>{{Cite|And the other, who holds the Triforce of Wisdom... is the seventh Sage, who is destined to be the leader of them all... However, she did not predict that the villain would follow Link into the Sacred Realm and take the Triforce of Power for his own.<ref>{{Cite|As long as you had the ocarina in your possession, I thought Ganondorf could never enter the Sacred Realm, but... Please forgive me...|Princess Zelda|OoT}}</ref><ref>{{Cite|I passed myself off as a Sheikah and hoped that you would return. I waited for seven years... And... now you are back. The dark age ruled by Ganondorf the Evil King will end!|Princess Zelda|OoT}}</ref> Princess Zelda then tells the Hero of Time that the six Sages must seal Ganondorf inside the [[Dark World|Evil Realm]], in so that she herself must ultimately seal the door to the Sacred Realm itself to keep him imprisoned for ages to come.<ref name="seal">{{Cite|The six Sages will open the sealed door and lure Ganondorf back into the Sacred Realm. I will then seal the door to the Sacred Realm from this world. Thus, Ganondorf the Evil King will vanish from Hyrule.|Princess Zelda|OoT}}</ref> The princess also gives the young hero [[Light Arrow|Light Arrows]] to aid their goal. However, in now at last revealing herself to Link, Zelda also fully exposes herself to Ganondorf, who has been waiting for such a moment. The King of Evil promptly encases her within a large magic [[crystal]] and brings her to his [[Ganon's Castle|castle]], constructed where Hyrule Castle had stood in the past before its destruction. Link rushes to Ganon's Castle to save the princess for a final showdown.<ref>{{Cite|Princess Zelda...you foolish traitor! I commend you for avoiding my pursuit for seven long years. ===The Final Confrontation===
[[File:OoT Ganon's Castle.png|thumb|Ganon's Castle, Ganondorf's stronghold|241x241px|left]]
Upon entering Ganondorf's location, Link breaks the barrier around the castle with the help of the six awakened Sages (who themselves were also captured and must be rescued).<ref>{{Cite|Link...can you hear me? It's Rauru, the Sage. We six will gather our power to create a bridge to the castle where Ganondorf dwells... The castle's keep, which is known as Ganon's Tower, is protected by six evil barriers. Bring down the six barriers and save Princess Zelda!!|Rauru|OoT}}</ref> Storming the keep, he confronts Ganondorf, and a fierce and climactic battle unfolds over the fate of Hyrule and the Triforce. The castle soon collapses, forcing Link and Princess Zelda to evacuate to safety, and the villain even becomes the Demon King [[Ganon]], his actual form, in the aftermath after leaping from the rubble. Eventually, Link and Princess Zelda are victorious. 
[[File:OoT Link Fighting Ganondorf Artwork.jpg|thumb|289x289px|The Hero of Time vs. the King of Evil]]
Without a strong and righteous mind, Ganondorf cannot control the power of the gods, and is ultimately defeated by Link's hand.<ref>{{Cite|Ganondorf...pitiful man... Without a strong, righteous mind, he could not control the power of the gods...|Princess Zelda|OoT}}</ref> With their power now fully restored, the seven Sages cast the evil incarnation of darkness into the void of the [[Dark World|Evil Realm]] that had been the Sacred Realm before being stained by Ganondorf's evil. Zelda then seals the gateway, ultimately imprisoning the Evil King within the Sacred Realm.<ref>{{Cite|Thank you, Link... Thanks to you, Ganondorf has been sealed inside the Evil Realm! Thus, peace will once again reign in this world...for a time.|Princess Zelda|OoT}}</ref> Before he is sealed away, the villain, in utter shock of himself being beaten entirely, angrily warns Link and Zelda that he will return to exterminate their descendants when the seal is broken (foreshadowing ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker|The Wind Waker]]''), as he, unfortunately, still has the Triforce of Power in his hand.<ref>{{Cite|YOU... CURSE YOU... ZELDA! CURSE YOU... SAGES!! CURSE YOU... LINK! Someday... when this seal is broken... That is when I will exterminate your descendants!! As long as the Triforce of Power is still in my hand...|Ganondorf|OoT}}</ref>

While saying goodbye, Princess Zelda instructs Link to place the Master Sword back in its Pedestal and close the Door of Time, closing the road between the two timelines, and she sends him to his original time to regain his lost childhood.<ref>{{Cite|You must lay the Master Sword to rest and close the Door of Time... However, by doing this, the road between times will be closed... Link, give the Ocarina to me... As a Sage, I can return you to your original time with it. When peace returns to Hyrule... It will be time for us to say good-bye... Now, go home, Link. Regain your lost time! Home... where you are supposed to be... the way you are supposed to be...|Princess Zelda|OoT}}</ref> During the ending credits, the future Hyrule, thanks to Ganon's defeat by Link, is now at a longtime peace while the other Sages happily look on at Death Mountain. After Link returns to the past for good, Navi leaves him, as her mission of guiding the young hero to save Hyrule is completed. Link himself still retains all of his knowledge about Hyrule's fate throughout his adventure. With this invaluable information, he immediately visits the young Princess Zelda at Hyrule Castle's courtyard once again in order to prevent such a fate from ever occurring again (foreshadowing ''Majora's Mask'' and, eventually, ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess|Twilight Princess]]''). The game then ends. Link, who traveled through time to save the land, would be forever known in legend as the [[Hero of Time]].<ref>{{Cite|As I see you standing there holding the mythical Master Sword, you really do look like the legendary Hero of Time...|Sheik|OoT}}</ref>
==Gameplay==
===Third Dimension===
[[File:OoT Link fighting a Wolfos.jpg|thumb|261x261px|Screenshot of gameplay, showing Link fighting against a [[Wolfos]] at the [[Forest Temple]] using the innovate Z-Targeting mechanic]]
Although slightly outdated in comparison to the latest 3D ''[[The Legend of Zelda (series)|The Legend of Zelda]]'' installments, the gameplay of {{OoT|-}} was revolutionary for its time that has since become standard for most subsequent titles (and many other games inspired by it).<ref>[http://darkzero.co.uk/game-articles/history-of-zelda-part-05-ocarina-of-time/ History of Zelda: Part 05 – Ocarina of Time]. DarkZero Hour</ref> It has arguably made more of an impact on the franchise than any of its predecessors, even though they each had the same cores of exploration, [[Dungeon|dungeons]], [[Puzzle|puzzles]] and [[item]] usage. Notably, many elements in-game are directly taken from earlier titles while adding new ones. Some of its innovations over the previous 2D games include the [[Targeting]] mechanic which allows [[Link]] to easily target objects/characters and fight individual enemies without relying on free movement, cinematic cutscenes between certain events, [[voice acting]] (though a small degree), the hero reacting to damage more clearly, riding [[Animal|Animals]] (specifically [[Horse|horses]]) and the introduction of the "Action button," which has different uses depending on Link's environment; for instance, standing next to a door prompts the Action button to change to "Open," allowing Link to open the door.

The 3D environment, enhanced sound, and greater graphical capacity of the Nintendo 64 allowed [[Nintendo]] to create a truly immersive environment beyond what had ever been done before, allowing for greater separation between cheerful environments, such as [[Hyrule Castle Town]] and [[Kokiri Forest]], and comparatively dark areas such as the [[Shadow Temple]] and [[Ganon's Castle]]. 

===Time Travel===
[[File:OoT Child and Adult Link together.png|left|thumb|263x263px|Link's unique maturity stages seen in-game, as his traditional age (left) and as a young man (right), where the latter would become a popular image of the character for later media]]
Among the game's particular gameplay mechanics, one of the most noteworthy is the time-traveling system. The game is divided into two periods within a 7-year gap. In the first - the past, the protagonist Link is a [[Young Link|child]], and his mission is to retrieve sacred stones that are the key to open the [[Door of Time]], where the Blade of Evil's Bane, the [[Master Sword]], lies. As a child, he explores a rather peaceful [[Hyrule]], the dangers he faces are not too concerning, and the dungeons are not too complex. In the second period - the future, Link is a [[Hero of Time|young adult]], and has to visit temples to free the [[Sage|Six Sages]] whose goal is to ultimately seal [[Ganon]] from the world of light. This is because the Gerudo King turned Hyrule into a much fiercer land, thus most regions of it suffer from curses that affect the inhabitants in various ways. Other differences between these two periods are the tools, items and treasures available.

Because of this, some parts cannot be accessed by Child Link or Adult Link, the sidequests vary, Child Link cannot use adult weapons, and Adult Link cannot enter small holes. Both periods, from a point of the game onwards, can be alternated anytime; in fact the only way to clear the game is to properly take the actions corresponding to each version of Link. This is similar to the [[Light World]]/[[Dark World]] dichotomy from {{ALttP}}, and thanks to its notability in the game, Adult Link in this title widely became a trademark image of the series' main character for most fans of the franchise, despite the majority of ''Zelda'' games featuring Link himself as a child.<ref>[http://trsrockin.com/link.html Link (The Legend of Zelda) - Step by Step]</ref>

Although not outright confirmed by Nintendo, coincidentally, since the original [[The Legend of Zelda|''The Legend'' ''of Zelda'']]''<nowiki/>'''s plot was originally planned to be set in the past and future during the development of the [[Nintendo Entertainment System|NES]] title according to [[Shigeru Miyamoto]], ''Ocarina of Time'' itself having two different time periods was likely reusing this scrapped concept.

===Music and Transportation===
{{OoT|-}} also introduces the use of music to solve puzzles. This mechanic would later appear in {{MM}}, {{TWW}}, {{TP}} and {{ST}}. As new songs are learned, they can be used to solve puzzles, gain access to new areas and warp to different locations.

The game also introduces [[Epona]], a horse Link can travel with after retrieving her from captivity in [[Lon Lon Ranch]]; she is very useful for travel in [[Hyrule Field]], and there are certain sidequests that require her assistance. Epona can only be used in the adult parts of the game, as she is too young to carry children in the child timeline (despite she can in ''Majora's Mask'').

===Equipment===
Link can also change his equipment through the pause subscreen. Over the course of his adventure, Link may find different [[Sword|swords]], [[Shield|shields]], boots, and [[Clothes|tunics]]. Many areas can only be explored when the hero is correctly equipped.

As a child, Link is initially equipped with the [[Kokiri Tunic]] and the [[Kokiri Boots]]. Early in his quest, he obtains the [[Deku Shield]] and the [[Kokiri Sword]]. Link may acquire the [[Hylian Shield]] later on, but due to it being too big for him, he cannot properly use it. After transforming into an adult, Link keeps his Kokiri Tunic and Boots, but is no longer able to equip the Kokiri Sword and Deku Shield (can now correctly use the Hylian Shield). During his adult quest, he acquires the [[Goron Tunic]] to withstand extreme heat and the [[Zora Tunic]] to breathe underwater. Link also receives the heavy [[Iron Boots]] used to sink to the bottom of bodies of water, and the [[Hover Boots]] to hover in midair briefly. Link also finds the mighty [[Mirror Shield]], which allows him to reflect light and magic. Optionally, Link can also obtain the more powerful [[Giant's Knife]] or [[Biggoron's Sword]].

Other items, which remain stacked for a permanent effect on Link, including the [[Goron Bracelet]] as a child, the [[Gauntlet]]s as an adult (each allowing him to lift stronger objects, from [[Bomb Flower]]s to giant rocks), and [[Zora Scale]]s to dive deeper underwater. Link may also obtain a collection of [[:Category:Bags|bag]] upgrades for [[Bomb|Bombs]], [[Arrow|Arrows]], [[Deku Seed|Bullet Seeds]], [[Deku Nut|Deku Nuts]], and [[Rupee|Rupees]].

==Game Information ==
===Development===
[[File:OoT Spaceworld 1996 trailer.webm|thumb|299x299px|The Space World 1996 trailer, showing early footage of ''Ocarina of Time''|center]]
In 1995, ''Ocarina of Time'' was originally intended and designed for the Japan-only [[Nintendo 64DD]] peripheral for the [[Nintendo 64]] game console. Initially named '''''Zelda 64''''' (possibly '''''The Legend of Zelda 64'''''), it was supposed to be the flagship title of the 64DD, while ''{{Smw|Super Mario 64}}'' of the [[Mario (franchise)|''Mario'' series]] was to be the counterpart for the main system itself. However, as the release of the 64DD became progressively delayed, [[Nintendo]] chose to move {{OoT|-}} to a standard N64 cartridge with 32 MB of storage, only half the size of the 64DD disks, however it is still the largest cartridge ever produced for Nintendo systems up to that time, which saved most of the important content. This shift from 64DD disk to N64 cartridge contributed to the game being delayed significantly, resulting in the final game being released two years after the ''Mario'' title.<ref>[http://www.gamespot.com/gamespot/features/video/hist_zelda/page14.html]</ref> At one point, the game was planned to be a first-person title, but was changed into a third-person perspective to keep [[Link]] visible on screen at all times. 
[[File:OoT beta Hyrule Castle.jpg|thumb|244x244px|Link fighting an [[Iron Knuckle]] enemy (from ''[[Zelda II: The Adventure of Link|The Adventure of Link]]'') inside what appears to be a castle in the beta version of the game]]
In early stages of development, the game was structured similarly to ''Super Mario 64,'' with [[Hyrule Castle]] or [[Ganon's Castle]] as the only setting, and various different rooms in the castle serving as the dungeons, according to series creator [[Shigeru Miyamoto]].<ref>{{Cite Person|quote=Yes. I thought about putting in all kinds of adventures into the different rooms, like making a dark meadow or an ocean—like in Princess Peach's Castle in Super Mario 64...In the worst case, Link wouldn't have been able to go outside the castle!|url=http://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/#/3ds/zelda-ocarina-of-time/4/0|name=Shigeru Miyamoto|title=Iwata Asks: The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time 3D}}</ref> Although this was scrapped for the final release, the [[Forest Temple]]'s design has leftovers of the game's original plot, as it seemingly takes place inside a dark castle and the boss room to battle [[Phantom Ganon]] has multiple portraits of a night sky featuring Ganon's Castle in the background. ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess|Twilight Princess]]'' and to an extent ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask|Majora's Mask]]'' are also known to have taken beta gameplay elements from this game.

When creating [[Hyrule Castle Town|Hyrule Castle Town Market]], Shigeru Miyamoto visited {{Wp|Germany}} for inspiration of its medieval {{Wp|Half-timbering|half-timbered}} architecture in {{Wp|Lower Franconia}}, spending a few weeks in northern {{Wp|Bavaria}}.<ref>{{Cite Magazine|quote=...eine noch realistischere Darstel-lung der hylianischen Welt, ganz nach den Vorstellungen Tanabes und Miyamotos, möglich. Ein Beispiel: Den Marktplatz von Hyrule gestalteten die Programmierer in Anlehnung an unterfränkische Fachwerk- architektur. (Miyamoto verbrachte vor zwei Jahren zur Ideenfindung für das Design u.a. einige Wochen im Norden Bayerns!) |magazine=''Club Nintendo''|publisher=Nintendo of Europe |volume=Special |issue=9 |date=1998 |page=5 |url=https://archive.org/details/zines_club_nintendo_1998_de/page/n4/mode/1up}}</ref> Initially the [[Deku Scrubs]] were added as a character named "Hobbit" inspired by {{Wp|J. R. R. Tolkien}}'s ''{{Wp|The Lord of the Rings}}''.<ref>{{Cite Magazine|quote=-宮本さんのお気に入りのキャラは?  '''[[Miyamoto|宮本いまは:]]'''「デクナッツ」ですね。実  ようしよ は「ホビット」っていうキャラが仕様書  にあって、で、そい が話をしたりモノ |magazine=''64 Dream''|publisher=MyCom |date=January 1999 |page=93 |url=https://archive.org/details/64dream-january-1999-ozidual/64Dream%201999%2001%20%28J%20OCR%29/page/n96/mode/1up}}</ref>

===Graphics===
Being the first 3D ''Zelda'' game of the long-running series, a new engine was used for both this game and eventually {{MM|-}}. Because the engine is based on polygonal graphics, they would require a significant amount of memory in the cartridge, and generating the graphics with simultaneity and consistency would imply the sacrifice of other aspects, such as music or textures. To solve this difficulty, some techniques were used so that the game wouldn't have any problems in this regard: When the camera is facing forward, the game only loads the memory of what is in that direction, be it enemies, characters or simply the space of the territory placed there. This means that there is literally nothing currently loaded on the sides not being witnessed by the camera angle. This technique is still used today in modern games with large overworlds.

===Audio===
Despite being a 3D game, there is no voice acting from the characters, with some slight exceptions: [[Navi]] when she is calling Link, Link himself when he yawns or sneezes, when he is shocked or when he sustains damage. A few characters scream, such as [[Ganondorf]], Sheik and Link when he falls from high altitude, or laugh, such as Princess Zelda, Ganondorf, [[Saria]] and [[Malon]].

Like the earlier 2D ''Zelda'' games, {{OoT|-}} has its music composed by [[Koji Kondo]].<ref>[http://www.musicgeek.org/wp/2010/02/27/koji-kondos-ocarina-of-time-breaks-from-video-game-soundtrack-mold Koji Kondo’s Ocarina of Time breaks from video game soundtrack mold]</ref> Surprisingly, the main theme of ''The Legend of Zelda'' series is mainly absent in this game, as the overworld tune (heard in [[Hyrule Field]], [[Death Mountain Trail]], [[Lake Hylia]] or [[Zora's Fountain]]) for this game is new. The game also uses interactive music: Normally, a background theme correspondent to where Link is can be heard; when an enemy is close to him, however, a particular combat theme can be heard (which does have a small portion of the main ''Zelda'' theme when in the overworld), and won't stop until the enemy is defeated or he moves a certain distance away. In a similar way, the aforementioned overworld theme also changes if Link remains still for some time. Kondo also composed the Ocarina melodies with only five tones of the first three musical notes<ref>[http://cruiseelroy.net/2008/04/ocarina-music-1/ Cruise Elroy - Music in Ocarina of Time, part one]</ref>.

=== Save Mechanics===
The game can be [[Saving|saved]] at any point through the [[Subscreen]]. This will save the game's progress and items obtained up to that point. Upon the loading of a save file, Link's location will revert to one of several possible locations, depending on the progress made and where the game was last saved.<ref>https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/n64/197771-the-legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time/answers/106661-saving-the-game</ref>

*[[Link's House]]: If [[Link]] is a child when the game is saved, and was not in a dungeon, he will start in his house in [[Kokiri Forest]] regardless of his prior location.
*[[Temple of Time]]: If Link is an adult when the game is saved, and was not in a dungeon, he will start in the Temple of Time regardless of his prior location.
*{{Term|OoT|Dungeon}} Entrance: If the game is saved while in a dungeon, loading that save will return Link to the entrance of that dungeon, regardless of his progress within it. This includes Ganon's Castle.

===Setting===
[[File:OoT Link in Hyrule Field.jpg|thumb|259x259px|Link in Hyrule Field, the main area of the game]]
{{OoT|-}} is the first title in the franchise to show Hyrule in three dimensions. The sacred land is a vast region that portrays various different ecosystems, which are populated by Hylians and other races. [[Hyrule Field]] is the central territory, and is connected to the other areas. It also surrounds [[Lon Lon Ranch]], where milk is produced and where numerous animals are raised. Located east from the field is Kokiri Forest, where [[Link]] lives (and where the game begins) along with the [[Kokiri]] race (who inhabit the place and, as long as they do so, do not age); the forest is governed by the [[The Great Deku Tree|Great Deku Tree]] until his death early in the game. Found north of the field is Hyrule Castle Town, where most of the Hylians live, and where Princess Zelda is raised until her turn comes to lead Hyrule. It's also where the [[Temple of Time]] was built to house the legendary [[Master Sword]].

East from Hyrule Castle Town is [[Kakariko Village]], a place formerly inhabited by the Sheikah tribe until Impa made it public for people to live there, and where the [[Graveyard]] houses the remains of the deceased [[Royal Family]] members. Kakariko Village itself is the starting point to reach [[Death Mountain]], the rocky home of the proud Goron race, as well as the fierce [[Dodongo]] population, and where [[Death Mountain Crater|an active volcano]] can be accessed. Northeast from Hyrule Field is Zora's Domain, a crystalline river and fountain inhabited by the Zoras and governed by [[King Zora]] until [[Princess Ruto]] takes the throne to accept her royal duty. [[Lake Hylia]], found south from the Field, is not ruled by any race, but the Zoras did build a {{Term|OoT|Water Temple|link}} in the Lake itself. Finally, [[Gerudo Desert]] is located west from the Field, and consists of a [[Gerudo Valley|Valley]], a [[Gerudo's Fortress|Fortress]], a [[Haunted Wasteland]], and the surroundings of a [[Spirit Temple|Temple]]. It is inhabited by the [[Gerudo]] tribe, and is considered to be a ground isolated from the rest of Hyrule.

Initially, most of these areas are relatively safe from evil, and the few exceptions (Kokiri Forest, for example) have problems too specific to cause any major impact on its inhabitants, mostly having to do with the search of the [[Spiritual Stones]]. Seven years later, however, every single part of Hyrule is affected greatly by Ganondorf's evil influence, so Link as the Hero of Time must visit the sacred Temples so that the Sages can be awakened and the curses can be undone, one by one.

=== Changes Between Versions===
Five different versions of the game were produced for the Nintendo 64. Three versions were made for Japan and North America and two for Europe. The Japanese and North American (NTSC) versions themselves are identical aside from a byte that sets the game's region and determines the language of the game. Several minor changes were made between each version, with a variety of text corrections and glitches fixed.<ref name="OoTVersionDifferences">{{Cite Web|quote= |author= |published= |retrieved=2013-02-02 |url=http://zeldaspeedruns.com/oot/generalknowledge/version-differences |title=Version Differences |site=Zelda Speedruns |type=}}</ref>The original versions are known as the v1.0 and v1.1 NTSC builds.

Corrections and changes were made in version 1.2 (Player's Choice release) and the European (PAL) versions, as well as the recoloring of Ganondorf's blood from red to green, and the alteration of the music heard in the {{Term|OoT3D|Fire Temple|link}} to remove a sample of an Islamic prayer chant.<ref name="GTNinResponse">{{Cite Web|author=GameTrailers |published=September 27, 2016 |retrieved=May 4, 2016|url= |title=Episode 09: The Fire Temple Chants - Pop Fiction |site=YouTube |type=}}</ref> The sample was taken from a commercially available sound library, but the developers did not realize it contained Islamic references. Contrary to popular belief, the chanting was not removed due to public outcry, but instead after Nintendo discovered it violated their own policy to avoid real-world religious material in games.<ref name="OoTVersionDifferences" /><ref name="GTNinResponse" /> All five Nintendo 64 versions of {{OoT|-}} were completed prior to the game's original release date of November 21.<ref name="GTNinResponse" />
[[File:OoT Crest of the Gerudo differences.jpg|left|thumb|185x185px|The revised Crest of the Gerudo seen in later versions of the game]]
A further modified version of 1.2 was produced for the [[Nintendo GameCube]] re-release. The original [[Crest of the Gerudo]], as depicted on [[block]]s, [[switch]]es and the [[Mirror Shield]], was changed from an inverted star and crescent symbol (associated with Islam) to a unique design introduced in {{MM|-}}, which became standard for the series since.<ref name="OoTVersionDifferences" /> Further alterations were made to the game's dialogue, along with minor technical changes. The Virtual Console and Nintendo Switch Online re-releases of the game are identical to the version released for the GameCube, but with the original Nintendo 64 button colors.

===Special Edition===
[[File:OoT Special Edition.png|thumb|243x243px|right|The Special Edition, along with a recreation of its contents.]]
Between December 1998 and 1999, a Special Edition bundle was released exclusively by German mail order company Cyber Games. It included a physical copy of the game, a T-shirt with the {{OoT|-}} logo on the front and a unique artwork of Link on the back, a relief pin in the shape of the {{Term|Series|Master Sword}} and a {{Term|Series|Hylian Shield}} with the word "Zelda" stamped onto it and a gold butterfly clutch, and a flyer promoting merchandise by Cyber Games.<ref name="L3">{{Cite Web|quote= |author= lookslikeLink |published= January 30, 2021 |retrieved= January 31, 2021 |url= https://youtu.be/jcBwE-CWvss |title= Nur 5 Exemplare weltweit?! - Die Geschichte der Zelda Ocarina of Time SPECIAL EDITION! |site=YouTube |type=}}</ref>These are extremely rare, as only a few examples exist today.

===Timeline Placement===
{{Main|Zelda Timeline, The End of Ocarina of Time}}
When it comes to the [[Zelda Timeline]], {{OoT|-}} is likely the most important and decisive installment among the franchise, which is attested by both its placement and the connections with past and future games. According to the book {{HH}}, it is indicated to be the final ''Zelda'' game in the pre-split timeline era, which starts with {{SS}} (which, shortly after its official announcement, was confirmed to take place before {{OoT|-}}).<ref>{{Cite Magazine|quote="I have already talked to Mr. Miyamoto about this so I am comfortable releasing this information--this title [Skyward Sword] takes place before Ocarina of Time."|interviewee= Eiji Aonuma|magazine= Official Nintendo Magazine|publisher=Future Publishing|issue= 62|date= December 2010|page=51}}</ref> The other games preceding {{OoT|-}} are {{TMC}} and {{FS}}. Regardless, {{OoT|-}} has always been a pivotal game in the chronology with the events at the end of the game, where Princess Zelda sends Link back to his youth, splitting the timeline into two. When the official timeline was revealed in {{HH|-}} in 2011, the placement of {{OoT|-}} in the series was revealed to be of even greater value, as the events of the game actually split the series' timeline into ''three'' branches, which the third being possible based on whether Link defeated Ganon or not.

===="Downfall Timeline"====
{{RelatedPage|Imprisoning War}}
[[File:OoT Downfall Timeline Game Over.png|right|frameless|316x316px]]
At the end of {{OoT|-}}, there was one possible outcome in which Ganon actually [[Game Over|defeated Link]], which resulted in the formation of the "Downfall Timeline".<ref name="HH 92">{{Cite Book|quote= |book= HH |publisher= |page= 92}}</ref> It starts with the [[Hero of Time]], Link, failing to defeat Ganondorf, which allowed the Evil King to ultimately obtain the remaining Triforce pieces from Link and Princess Zelda. Despite losing their great beloved hero and the villain is victorious, the [[Sage#Ocarina of Time|Seven Sages]] still proceeded to quickly seal him within the [[Dark World|corrupted Sacred Realm]] like in the game's actual ending.<ref name="HH 92" /><ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= HH |publisher= |page= 87}}</ref> Years later, greedy people entered the Dark World seeking the Triforce, turning into monsters and becoming part of Ganon's army. In the war that ensued, the [[Knights of Hyrule]] protected the [[Sage#A Link to the Past|Seven Wise Men]] of that era from Ganon's minions, while they cast a seal to close off the entrance to the Dark World.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= HH |publisher= |page= 93}}</ref> This marked the conclusion of the [[Imprisoning War]], which laid the foundation for the events of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past|A Link to the Past]]''. After {{ALttP|-}}, {{LA}}, {{OoS}} and {{OoA}}, {{ALBW}}, {{TFH}}, ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Echoes of Wisdom|Echoes of Wisdom]]'', ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]'', and {{TAoL}} take place, in that order.

===="Child Timeline"====
[[File:OoT Ending.png|right|frameless|310x310px]]
If Link succeeds in defeating Ganon, the timeline branches into one of two more timelines. When Princess Zelda sends Link back to his original time, the "Child Timeline" is formed. Although Ganondorf is not granted access to the [[Sacred Realm]], it is very possible that unbeknownst to him, he acquired the [[Triforce of Power]] as a result of Link returning back with the [[Triforce of Courage]] still in his possession (as well as Zelda gaining the [[Triforce of Wisdom]]). Link informs Zelda of the "future" events and leaves Hyrule shortly afterwards to search for [[Navi]], setting the events of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask|Majora's Mask]]'' in motion. Ganondorf is then [[Failed Execution of Ganondorf|executed]] several years later according to the backstory of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess|Twilight Princess]]''.<ref>[http://www.zeldawiki.org/Timeline_Quotes#On_Twilight_Princess Nintendo Dream: Eiji Aonuma Interview]</ref> Over a century later, the events of {{TP|-}} take place and the Link in this game lingers on as the [[Hero's Spirit]] due to his soul-shattering regrets of not being remembered as the Hero of Time at all. Following {{TP|-}}, {{FSA}} takes place featuring [[Vaati]] and another incarnation of Ganondorf.

===="Adult Timeline"====
[[File:TWW Scroll of the Hero of Time 4.png|right|frameless|379x379px]]
In a third branch of the events in {{OoT|-}}, similar to the "Child Timeline", the "Adult Timeline" continues after Link defeats Ganon and the Sages seal him in the Sacred Realm with the Triforce of Power in his possession.<ref name="seal" /> Link is sent back to his childhood by Zelda, ultimately leaving the future Hyrule in this branch without a Hero, as told in the prologue of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker|The Wind Waker]]''. Hyrule was at a longtime peace for many generations and the Hero of Time's legend was fondly remembered, but Ganon eventually overcomes the Sages' seal and attempts to take over Hyrule once more. With no hero to face the evil at all, the kingdom was easily conquered and, as a last resort, the Golden Goddesses [[Great Flood|flood Hyrule]], leading to the events of {{TWW|-}} and consecutively {{PH}}, and later in the timeline, ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Spirit Tracks|Spirit Tracks]]''.

===Reviews ===
The game was universally praised by critics when it was first released, receiving perfect scores from many reviewers, such as Famitsu, Gamespot, IGN, and Edge. Metacritic gives the original version of the game a score of 99/100 and the GameCube re-release 91/100. Features such as the Z-targeting system and context-sensitive Action button were well-received. The GameCube and Wii versions of the game were praised as well.<ref>[http://www.ign.com/articles/2007/02/27/the-legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Wii Review - IGN]</ref>

The game's graphics were called "beautiful" by IGN reviewer Peer Schneider, who commented that "rarely is there such a perfect mixture of graphics, sound and gameplay,"<ref>[http://www.ign.com/articles/1998/11/26/the-legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time-review The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Review - IGN]</ref> giving the game a 10/10. Gamespot editor Jeff Gerstmann agreed, also giving the game a perfect score, saying that "this is the masterpiece that people will still be talking about ten years down the road."<ref>[http://www.gamespot.com/reviews/legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time-the-review/1900-2543677/ The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Review for Nintendo 64 - Gamespot]</ref> Criticism on the game focused on the occasional slow pace associated with some parts, such as the {{Term|OoT|Water Temple|link}}, as well as the use of MIDI for the music quality.

Nonetheless, Nintendo Power placed it ''first'' in their list of best {{TLoZ|-}} games and stated that, despite its age, is still a great game and called it a "masterpiece". 

In 2022, IGN placed ''Ocarina of Time'' as the "third" best ''Legend of Zelda'' game in their "Top 10 Best Zelda Games" list countdown (right behind ''A Link to the Past [#2]'' & ''Breath of the Wild [#1]''); but was then placed as the "fourth" best ''Zelda'' game in their new revamped version of their "Top 10 Best Zelda Games" list in 2023 (right behind ''A Link to the Past [#3], Breath of the Wild [#2]'', & ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom|Tears of the Kingdom]] [#1]'').<ref>IGN. (2022). ''The 10 Best Zelda Games''. ''YouTube''. Retrieved July 24, 2023, from https://youtu.be/Emb8FzEBzZg</ref><ref>IGN. (2023). ''Top 10 Legend of Zelda Games of All Time''. ''YouTube''. ===Fan Reception===
Much like ''Super Mario 64'' for the ''Mario'' franchise, this iconic N64 installment remains standing as a fan-favorite within the long-running series due to its explicit importance in not only the [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The Legend of'' ''Zelda'' franchise]] itself, but the video game industry as a whole, currently holding an average reader score of 9.7 at IGN,<ref>[http://www.ign.com/games/the-legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time/n64-437 The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time - IGN]</ref> as well as a current average user score of 9.6 on GameSpot.<ref>[http://www.gamespot.com/the-legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time/ The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time User Reviews for GameSpot]</ref>

Many years after its release in 1998, ''Ocarina of Time'' continues to be a highly popular title to this day, frequently being featured on compiled lists of all-time best games and in some cases being at the very top. For some players, however, the game's groundbreaking success has overshadowed the potential success of later ''Zelda'' games, therefore becoming a difficult act to follow since,<ref>[http://www.trustedreviews.com/video-games/review/2006/09/12/TrustedReviews-Top-5-Games-Of-All-Time/p8 Trusted Reviews Top 5 Games Of All Time]</ref> and even marking the downfall of the series in some degree, believing the franchise as a whole peaked with this title.<ref>[http://darkzero.co.uk/game-articles/unsheathed-1-dethroning-ocarina/ Dethroning Ocarina]</ref> Some longtime fans believe ''Ocarina of Time'' as a 3D version of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past|A Link to the Past]]'', due to similar elements from the [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System|Super NES]] title such as having two separate overworld features. There is also a debate regarding whether the game is overrated or not, with IGN editor Levi Buchanan analyzing this subject from a historical and technical perspective;<ref>[http://www.ign.com/articles/2009/02/12/is-legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time-overrated Is The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time overrated?]</ref> this debate was revisited when current ''Zelda'' director [[Eiji Aonuma]] said that he would not quit working on the franchise until he created a superior game,<ref>[http://www.engadget.com/2008/11/29/zelda-director-wont-quit-until-he-surpasses-ocarina-of-time/ Zelda Director won't quit until he surpasses Ocarina of Time]</ref> as well as when he [[Controversy in The Legend of Zelda Series|controversially said that the game hasn't aged very well]].<ref>[http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/newsArt.cfm?artid=20397 Aonuma: Ocarina of Time "Not Very Good" Nowadays]</ref> 

WatchMojo placed ''Ocarina of Time'' at the #1 spot in their "Top 10 Legend of Zelda Games of All Time" list countdown, and fellow staff member Johnny Reynolds picked it as his 4th favorite ''Legend of Zelda'' game as part of a "Top 5 Zelda Games (Showdown)" video over on MojoPlays.<ref>Reynolds, Johnny. “[https://www.watchmojo.com/articles/top-10-legend-of-zelda-games-of-all-time Top 10 Legend of Zelda Games of All Time].” ''WatchMojo'', 18 Apr. 2020</ref><ref>Brown, A., & Reynolds, J. === iQue Player===
{{OoT|-}} was ported to the Chinese [[iQue Player]] (a localized version of the Nintendo 64) released in 2003. The dialogue was fully translated to Chinese. This marked the first ''Zelda'' game to become available in China. The game is downloaded to the iQue Player using a points card. The iQue version of {{OoT|-}} is based on version 1.2. Lag is virtually non-existent due to iQue's more powerful hardware. This is most noticeable during the collapse of [[Ganon's Castle]] cutscene, where the castle falls at such a speed that the audio falls out of sync.

===Virtual Console===
{{OoT|-}} has been ported to the Virtual Console on both the [[Wii]] and [[Wii U]].

On the Wii Virtual Console, the Japanese and American 1.2 and European 1.1 Nintendo 64 version was available for download for 1000 Wii Points. The Virtual Console does not support the rumble feature, which renders the [[Stone of Agony]] useless. Although based on the Nintendo 64 version, it still contains the changed moon and star symbols, as it uses the GameCube ROM. This is due to a patch which was put in the Virtual Console emulator which changes them.{{Fact}}

On the Wii U Virtual Console, the game was available for download for approximately $10.

===3DS Remake===
{{Main|The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time 3D}}

==Legacy ==
===Design Influence===
{{OoT|-}} introduced many design elements that would be repeated in later games:
*'''Character models''': {{OoT|-}} was the first game in the series to feature an in-game blond-haired [[Link]] (although the official artwork of previous games had depicted Link with blond hair, none of his character sprites displayed this characteristic due to hardware limitations). It also produced the first detailed model of [[Princess Zelda]]'s character, particularly her dress designs, which have been the same ever since. This game was also the first to portray the human form of [[Ganon]], [[Ganondorf]], which by far became the most common alter-ego of the villain since. Many of the character models of {{OoT|-}} were reused in its sequel, ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask|Majora's Mask]]''.
*'''Races''': Many races also made their debut in ''Ocarina of Time''. The [[Kokiri]], [[Goron|Gorons]], [[Gerudo]], [[Hylian]]s, [[Deku Scrub]]s and [[Sheikah]] all originated or were at least identified in {{OoT|-}}. The [[Zora|Zoras]] technically appeared first in [[The Legend of Zelda|the original game]] as [[River Zora|enemies]] and were given the ability to walk in {{ALttP}}, but their widely accepted character model - the slender, sky-blue, aquatic design - was first made in ''Ocarina of Time''.

=== Other===
*{{OoT|-}}, while not being the first game in the series to feature a [[The Legend of Zelda|musical item]] or a [[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening|list of songs]], as this debuted in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening|Link's Awakening]]'', it is the first to allow the player to play the songs note by note rather than simply selecting the item or the song being used.<ref name="PH Guide 5">{{Cite Guide|quote= |game= PH|publisher= Prima|page=5}}</ref>
* As mentioned before, it introduced the [[Targeting]] system, eliminating difficulty for camera control, and also helping to focus visually on very distant spots, enemies or characters.<ref name="PH Guide 5" />
*It introduced the ability to wear [[Mask|masks]].<ref name="PH Guide 5" />
*The game also introduced many [[Side Quest|sidequests]] allowing Link to collect hundreds of optional items.<ref name="PH Guide 5" />
*It was the first ''Zelda'' game to include quarter units of health; previous games only featured half units of health.
* This game introduced an optional sword more powerful than the Master Sword in damage (although the legendary blade can technically be upgraded in ''A Link to the Past'').
*Outside of the ''Zelda'' franchise, the game caused an unprecedented impact on the video game industry, to the point that many other games and series were influenced by the gameplay style from the game, especially the 3D [[wikipedia:Action-adventure_game|action-adventure]] genre.<ref name="PH Guide 5" />
*Many properties of this game were borrowed by the [[Super Smash Bros. (series)|''Super Smash Bros.'' series]], especially the original and ''Melee'', including several characters, stages, soundtracks, and numerous trophies.
*Alongside setting the standard for later incarnations like Princess Zelda and Ganon, the Link from this game, the [[Hero of Time]], is later mentioned in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker|The Wind Waker]]'' and would also appear in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess|Twilight Princess]]'' as the [[Hero's Spirit]] where he teaches the hero in that title the [[Hidden Skill|Hidden Skills]] (though this was met with controversy). In addition, aside from ''Super Smash Bros.'', he also makes a playable guest appearance in the Namco fighting game ''[[SoulCalibur II]]'' exclusively for the [[Nintendo GameCube]] release. Most of his unlockable weapons are from this title and its [[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask|sequel]], but a few are from installments predating them, specifically ''The Legend of Zelda'' and ''A Link to the Past''.
* A [[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (Himekawa)|manga adaptation of the game]] was created by [[Akira Himekawa]] in 2000. Although loosely based on the ''Ocarina of Time''<nowiki/>'s story, some of the original elements are featured.

==Trivia==
*{{OoT|-}} runs on a heavily modified version of ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_64|Super Mario 64]]'''s engine, as both games were co-developed.<ref>{{Cite Person|quote= We were using the Super Mario 64 engine for Zelda, but we had to make so many modifications to it that it's a different engine now.|name= Shigeru Miyamoto|url= http://web.archive.org/web/20071005172351/http://www.miyamotoshrine.com/theman/interviews/111998.shtml|title= Miyamoto Interviews}}</ref> This is essentially a repeat of their original 2D equivalents, where ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]'' used a highly upgraded engine of ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_Bros.|Super Mario Bros.]]'' and also created by the same team. According to [[Shigeru Miyamoto]], however, because the engine itself was so altered during development, he considered their final products entirely different.
*The game's main theme on the title screen is an extended version of the melody played by the [[Flute|Recorder]] item from the original ''The Legend of Zelda'', performed by the titular [[Ocarina of Time (Item)|Ocarina of Time]].
*The original Japanese release date of the game, November 21st, is coincidentally the same as ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past|A Link to the Past]]'' as well as the [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System|Super Famicom]], themselves seven and eight years prior, respectively.
*There is a hidden erase save data code, which can be used to reset the entire game in case a save file is corrupted. It is performed by pressing a complex input of D-Up, D-Down, D-Left, D-Right, Start, B, C-Down, L, C-Right, C-Left, A, C-Up, R, and Z on the third controller.
*Due to time constraints, the Spanish localization was not included in the European release of the game. However, a 150 page text guide was bundled with the game in Spain, which included the entire Spanish localization alongside the English text.<ref>{{Cite Web|quote= |author= Censored Gaming|published= March 31, 2019|retrieved= April 11, 2019|url= https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R_saTwX9mKc|title= Why Ocarina Of Time Sucked For Spanish Players|site= YouTube|type= Video}}</ref>[[File:OoT beta Deku Tree.png|thumb|Adult Link by the [[The Great Deku Tree|Great Deku Tree]] in a May 1997 beta build]]
*Surprisingly, the prototype builds of the game did not feature Child Link but only the hero as an adult until late in development, and were to begin his adventure in that maturity stage.
*''Ocarina of Time'', although a clear favorite among [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The Legend of Zelda'' series]] fanbase or the video game community in general due to its high impact, is also arguably the [[Controversy in The Legend of Zelda series|most controversial title of the franchise]], as it has more questionable content or debates than every other installment so far.
*This is the final installment of the series where its original composer [[Koji Kondo]] created the music by himself.
*An early logo used in some promotional materials and [[ESRB]]'s section for the game used the subtitle "The Ocarina of Time".<ref>{{Cite Web|quote= The Legend of Zelda: The Ocarina of Time|author= |published= |retrieved= March 26, 2020|url= https://www.esrb.org/ratings/3438/The+Legend+of+Zelda%3A+The+Ocarina+of+Time/|title= The Legend of Zelda: The Ocarina of Time - ESRB|site= esrb.com|type= }}</ref>
*Due to the graphical limitations of the [[Nintendo 64]], the strap belt that Link normally wears on his shoulder to carry his [[scabbard]] and [[Shield|Shields]] is missing. Likewise, his shadow counterpart, [[Dark Link]], also appears to have his model being a simplified version of the hero's. This is however, fixed in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time 3D|Ocarina of Time 3D]]''.
*Although there are twelve [[Dungeon]]s to explore, two of them are not designated as dungeons in the game's data, which means the official total is only ten. [[Ganon's Castle]] and the [[Gerudo Training Ground]] both have the dungeon entrance icon on the maps, but because the dungeons themselves do not use a [[Dungeon Map|Map]] and [[Compass]] or allow the player to use a [[Warping|warp song]], they are not true dungeons.[[File:OoT Link defeats Ganon gif.gif|thumb|320x320px|Link's final blow inflicted on Ganon during the final battle is widely seen as one of the most brutal scenes of the entire franchise (v1.2 version shown)]]
*[[Ganon]]'s ultimate defeat by [[Link]] in this game is often considered one of, if not ''the'' most climactic finishes of a [[Final Boss]] battle in ''The Legend of Zelda'' series as well as video game history. However, it is also arguably the most violent; Although Ganon himself survived the seemingly fatal ordeal, the original v1.0/v1.1 release depicts the [[Hero of Time]] savagely slashing the beast's face multiple times before thrusting the [[Master Sword]] directly into his mouth with red blood spewing ([[Ganondorf]] also pukes blood when defeated) - something which appears over the top for the series' standards (or most [[Nintendo]] games, in general). In later v1.2 releases and subsequent ports of ''Ocarina of Time'', it was censored by Nintendo for the blood to become green, and the [[Nintendo 3DS]] remake has the Blade of Evil's Bane hitting Ganon on the forehead instead like some later titles' finishing blows, due to controversy and to avoid the game being re-rated to "T" (Teen), as it was in fact "E" (Everyone) by the [[ESRB]]. ''Ocarina of Time 3D'', however, has the "E10+" (Everyone 10+) rating. The manga adaptation of the game also shows Link striking the Dark Beast's forehead as well. No other installment in the franchise since this Nintendo 64 title makes use of realistic colored blood or the main protagonist himself impaling lead antagonists on the mouth area.
** Blood is also seen when defeating [[Spike|Spikes]] and [[Shell Blade|Shell Blades]] underwater, though like Ganon, their own is also censored to green in later releases. However, the latter is still red in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask|Majora's Mask]]''.
** Stained red blood can also be seen in the [[Bottom of the Well]] and [[Shadow Temple]] even in v1.2 versions and the remake.
** ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker|The Wind Waker]]'', ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess|Twilight Princess]]'', and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Skyward Sword|Skyward Sword]]'' would later feature dramatic finishing moves with the Master Sword as well, though in those cases they were given to Ganondorf and [[Demise]] instead and the attacks by Link rather resemble the [[Down Thrust]] via the [[Parry Attack]]. [[Ending Blow]], and [[Fatal Blow]], respectively. Unlike with Ganon, they do not survive.
** On a related note, the final battle itself was originally going to be completely different, as Ganon was intended to become gigantic and Link to to scale his archnemesis and attack various parts of his body, but the development team found major difficulties due to hardware limitations of the N64, forcing them to rework it entirely for the final release. The original concept, however, would eventually be successfully reused for the [[Mario (franchise)|''Mario'' series]], specifically the boss [[mariowiki:Megaleg|Megaleg]] in ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_Galaxy|Super Mario Galaxy]]''.
** The objective of Link hitting Ganon by the tail to defeat him is a parallel to [[Bowser]] in ''Super Mario 64'' where [[Mario]] must grab his own tail to throw him toward bombs in order to take down the Koopa King.
*In the post-credits cutscene of Link returning to meet Princess Zelda in the [[Hyrule Castle Courtyard]] for the second time, the [[Triforce of Courage]] can be seen resonating on his left hand. This same scene is what would happen of the Hero of Time explaining Ganondorf's conspiracy, leading up to the events of the Child Timeline.[[File:OOT Link sent to the past by Zelda.png|thumb|291x291px|Link being permanently sent to the past by Princess Zelda at the very end of the game marks the beginning of a series of dark events for the Hero of Time as well as Hyrule itself]]
*In spite of the game itself being widely known as a classic, some fans of ''The Legend of Zelda'' series strongly believed ''Ocarina of Time''<nowiki/>'s story is not a happy ending for Link, as the Hero of Time's adventure is filled with sad events, and his return to his original time (and splitting the timeline) by [[Princess Zelda]] and then [[Navi]] leaving him without saying goodbye marks the beginning of yet more traumatic journeys to come afterwards, as seen in ''Majora's Mask'' and ''Twilight Princess'', making this incarnation of the main protagonist a tragic hero. Even himself saving Hyrule [[Great Flood|isn't permanent]], as seen in ''The Wind Waker''.
*In the beta version of the game, the Sword (B) and Action (A) buttons were in reverse, echoing the gameplay of ''The Legend of Zelda'' and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening|Link's Awakening]]''.
*An [[Unused Content|unused]] screen transition exists in the game where the symbol of the [[Triforce]] is shown rotating when Link enters different locations. It is unknown why this was cut in the final release, but it can be accessed by hacking. This also applies for ''Majora's Mask'', as ''Ocarina of Time''<nowiki/>'s engine was reused.
*Several gameplay actions of Link seen in later games such as ''Majora's Mask''<nowiki/>, ''Twilight Princess'', and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild|Breath of the Wild]]'' were originally intended for this title but were scrapped during development. Examples include running freely while targeting an enemy, delivering a horizontal slash while running, and using the [[Bow]] while crouching.
*''[[starfox:Star_Fox_Adventures|Star Fox Adventures]]'' of the [[starfox:Star_Fox_(series)|''Star Fox'' series]], which was created by Rareware,<nowiki/> was heavily inspired by this game and has similar gameplay.
**Ironically, an unused [[Arwing]] enemy from ''[[starfox:Star_Fox_64|Star Fox 64]]'' can be found in the ga<nowiki/>me's code which itself was used by the developers to test the flight patterns of the boss [[Volvagia]].
*According to Shigeru Miyamoto, Ganondorf was originally going to change in<nowiki/>to Ganon slowly as the game's story progresses, becoming more grotesque. However, this was scrapped, as in the final release, he instead transforms into his true form via the Triforce of Power during the final battle.
*Out of all the boxes that were produced for ''The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina o''<nowiki/>''f Time Special Edition'', 75 units are proven to never have been filled. Estimations suggest that a maximum of 25 units were ever sold, making it the rarest limited-time available edition for any ''Zelda'' game to date.<ref name="L3" />[[File:LinkRuto2.jpg|thumb|307x307px|Link nearly kissed by Princess Ruto after rescuing her from [[Inside Jabu-Jabu's Belly]], one of the most explicit scenes of a love interest for the main protagonist in the main series]]
*Despite Nintendo strictly intends to keep Link a gender-neutral figure and<nowiki/> his friendships rather platonic due to its focus on innovative gameplay experiences, this game is well-known among the ''Zelda'' fanbase for having him in a greater degree of potential love interests with various female characters than in all other installments of the franchise, specifically Princess Zelda, [[Saria]], [[Princess Ruto]], [[Malon]], and possibly [[Nabooru]] and Navi, which led many to believe that Link in general is a ladies' man or in a love triangle, especially since the Hero of Time is canonically engaged with Ruto, who is a [[Zora]] (though this was met with controversy). Shockingly, according to the developers, ''Ocarina of Time'' was originally planned to have elements of a [[wikipedia:Dating_sim|dating sim]] game (which are highly popular in Japan), explaining the reason why those key characters played such a role throughout the plot, but was apparently scrapped as they realized that most of Link's dating choices would become completely unavailable at some point during the story, which is quite evident by Zelda, Saria, Ruto, and Nabooru becoming Sages later in the game and himself being sent into the Child Timeline at the end.
*After the staff credits in the game's ending, when Link returns the Master<nowiki/> Sword to the [[Temple of Time]], Navi flies away and the Nintendo logo appears, bells from a clock tower can be heard. This may be a reference to the Hero of Time's next adventure, ''Majora's Mask'', which may have begun development around the game's release, and the sequel itself indeed has bells heard from the [[Clock Tower]] as days pass.
*In comparison, Link's adult model is roughly double the height of his child counterpart.
*If Link were to learn the [[Scarecrow's Song]], at "The End" screen after the credits, it can be heard after waiting a few minutes, played by the Ocarina of Time and then in Malon's singing voice.
*The [[Sword Beam]] technique can be seen in beta screenshots of the game but was scrapped in the final release for unknown reasons.
*Only three characters from Link's childhood - Princess Zelda, Princess Ruto and Malon, grow up into young adults when he becomes the Hero of Time. All of which are female.
*Considering the Downfall Timeline is marked by the Hero of Time being defeated by Ganon, this would technically make receiving a [[Game Over]] during the final battle the game's Bad Ending.
*There was a commercial featuring Link as an adult where he is in [[Kakariko Village]] and finds a chest full of a soda drink called Mirinda, this commercial supposedly aired in India during the late 1990s.
*The [[Chain Chomp]], an enemy from the ''Mario'' series, was originally going to appear in this game for the [[Gerudo's Fortress]], but was cut late in development.
* Like ''Super Mario 64'', ''Ocarina of Time'' had brought enough impact to the series that many 3D game developers from other franchises say that no title's gameplay since then hasn't borrowed a mechanic from either game, as they "blazed trails".
*The scene before the fight with Ganondorf was apparently modeled after a similar scene in the ''[https://sailormoon.fandom.com/wiki/Pretty_Soldier_Sailor_Moon_(anime_series) Sailor Moon]'' episode "''The Death of Uranus and Neptune: The Talisman Appears''".
*Due to the game's sheer popularity, in spite of its current age, alongside [[Speedrun|speedrunning]], ''Ocarina of Time'' has received the most fan-made ROM hacks than any other ''The Legend of Zelda'' series title today, with some intending to recreate the Nintendo 64 installment with new graphics or gameplay. However, like others such as ones based on ''A Link to the Past'' or ''Majora's Mask'', such projects have often been hit with [[wikipedia:Cease_and_desist|cease and desist]] letters or even takedowns by Nintendo if caught with copyright infringement.
