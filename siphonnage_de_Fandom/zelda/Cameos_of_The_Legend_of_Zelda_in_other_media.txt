===''[[animalcrossing:Animal_Crossing_(series)|Animal Crossing]]'' series===

* On the back wall of Katrina's tent, there is a picture of the [[Triforce]].

===''[https://donkeykong.fandom.com/wiki/Donkey_Kong_(series) Donkey Kong]'' franchise===
[[File:Cranky'sVideoGameHeroes.png|frame|right|Cranky's Video Game Heroes in ''Donkey Kong Country 2: Diddy's Kong Quest'', on the [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System|Super NES]]]]
====''{{Smw|Donkey Kong Country 2: Diddy Kong's Quest}}''====
* If the game is beaten with 18 Video Game Hero Coins (in the form of DK Coins) or less, third place in Cranky's Video Game Heroes will be held by [[Link]], behind {{Smw|Yoshi}} and [[mariowiki:Mario|Mario]], while [https://sonic.fandom.com/wiki/Sonic_the_Hedgehog Sonic the Hedgehog]'s [https://sonic.fandom.com/wiki/Sonic%27s_shoes shoes] and [https://earthwormjim.fandom.com/wiki/Earthworm_Jim Earthworm Jim']s [https://earthwormjim.fandom.com/wiki/Plasma_Blaster Plasma Blaster] are at the bottom near a trash can and sign saying "NO HOPERS" (a reference to Nintendo's infamous rivalry with Sega at the time). Link's appearance in-game is based on the design used for official artwork in {{aLttP}} and {{LA}}, albeit with darker hair, possibly due to palette limitations of the [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System|Super NES]]. He appears unequipped, and looks around the room before crossing his arms. In the [[Game Boy Advance]] remake, Link and Yoshi swap positions. As ''Donkey Kong Country 2'' uses pre-rendered sprites, this technically marks the first time Link had been rendered in 3D, predating {{OoT}} by three years. This also applies for Mario and Yoshi themselves.

====''{{Smw|Donkey Kong Land 2}}''====
This [[Game Boy]] game also includes the same Video Game Heroes lineup as the Super NES equivalent.

====''{{Smw|Donkey Kong Country 3: Dixie Kong's Double Trouble}}''====
[[File:Bazaar mentions Link.png|thumb|219x219px|Bazaar mentioning Link in ''Donkey Kong Country 3: Dixie Kong's Double Trouble'']]
If the main characters ask about the castle in Bazaar's Shop (available after buying the shell), and refuse to pay for the story, Bazaar will recount how a cheeky boy the previous week also asked about the castle and had paid 500 [[Rupee|Rupees]] for it, a homage to [[Link]]. This reference was removed in the Game Boy Advance remake.

===''[[mariowiki:Mario_(franchise)|Mario]]'' franchise===
====''{{Smw|Super Mario Bros. 2}}''====
*The sound effect of {{Smw|Birdo}} spitting an egg and [[mariowiki:Tryclyde|Tryclyde]] shooting fireballs is taken from the one that plays when [https://noproblo.dayjo.org/zeldasounds/LOZ/LOZ_MagicalRod.wav firing a magic projectile] with the [[Magical Rod]] (and when [[Wizzrobe]]s fire their projectiles) in {{TLoZ}}. In addition, the sound effect of enemies being defeated by thrown [[mariowiki:Vegetable|Vegetables]] is also taken from the original ''Zelda'' game when [https://noproblo.dayjo.org/zeldasounds/LOZ/LOZ_Enemy_Hit.wav foes take damage] by Link. The Super NES remaster (''All-Stars'') and Game Boy Advance remake, however, use completely different sounds based on its own series, specifically ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_World|Super Mario World]]''.

==== ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_Bros._3|Super Mario Bros. 3]]'' ====

====''{{Smw|Mario Golf (Game Boy Color)|Mario Golf}}''====
* Random names of characters from other [[Nintendo]] games as well as {{TLoZ|-}} are put on the scoreboards in ''Mario Golf'' for both the [[Nintendo 64]] and [[Game Boy Color]] versions. Although they do not physically appear, several characters from {{OoT}} are referenced, including [[Darunia]], [[Deku Scrub]], [[Goron]] Kid (likely [[Link (Goron)|Link the Goron]]), [[Impa]], [[Malon]], [[Nabooru]], [[Navi]], [[Saria]], [[Sheik]], [[Talon]], [[Princess Zelda]], and [[Link]] himself. These names may also appear on the scorecards of other courses as well. This therefore implies the ''Zelda'' characters may be playing golf themselves with the ''Mario'' cast, but elsewhere out of the player's view (though Navi's case is questionable given she is a [[Fairy]]). The Links Club for the latter version, while a reference to the golfing term, may also be a pun on Link from {{TLoZ|-}} as well.

====''{{Smw|Super Mario Galaxy 2}}''====

====''{{Smw|Super Mario 3D Land}}''====
* {{Smw|World 5-2 (Super Mario 3D Land)|World 5-2}} is heavily based on [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|{{TLoZ|-}} series]] and was created to honor the franchise's [[The Legend of Zelda 25th Anniversary|25th anniversary]]. The course is played predominantly from a top-down perspective like 2D games and involves Mario moving from room to room in a similar manner to a traditional ''Zelda'' dungeon. A puzzle to obtain a Star Coin involves lighting torches using [[mariowiki:Fire_Mario|Fire Mario]], which causes the ''Zelda'' "puzzle solved" jingle to play. The course also includes traps that mimic those that appear in ''Zelda'' dungeons, such as [[Guruguru Bar]]s and [[Rolling Spike Trap]]s.

==== ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_3D_World|Super Mario 3D World]]'' ====
[[File:Super Mario 3D World Link Easter Egg.png|thumb|276x276px|Link's cameo in ''Super Mario 3D World'']]

* The [[mariowiki:Rainbow_Run_(level)|Rainbow Run]] level features an 8-bit Link based on the original {{TLoZ|-}}. When all of the Color Panels are lit to obtain the second Green Star, the "get item" jingle from {{OoT|-}} plays.

====''{{Smw|Luigi's Mansion: Dark Moon}}''====
* In Mission C-3: Roundhouse Brawl in the Old Clockworks, the Ancient Poltergeist (Elder Boffin in the PAL release) is briefly seen arranging six bricks into the shape of the [[Triforce]]. As he does this, the background music briefly plays the "item found" jingle from the ''Zelda'' games.

====''{{Smw|Mario & Luigi: Dream Team}}''====
* One of the Luiginoid moves is called {{Smw|Luiginoid Generator#Hookshot|Hookshot}}, which is a reference to the [[Hookshot|item of the same name]]. This Luiginoid move works in a similar manner to the aforementioned item, causing multiple Luigis to stretch out in a chain to latch onto targets and pull Mario and the remaining Luiginoids to it.

====''{{Smw|Paper Mario: The Origami King}}''====
The fourth chapter in the game has Mario sailing across an ocean called {{Term|TWWHD|Great Sea|link|display=The Great Sea}} on a boat with similar mechanics to the {{Term|TWWHD|King of Red Lions|link}} from {{TWW}}. In order to unlock a dungeon called the Sea Tower, which looks similar to the {{Term|TWWHD|Tower of the Gods|link}} from the outside, Mario must travel to Diamond Island and complete the Trials of Power, Courage and Wisdom to collect the three sacred orbs, which is a reference to the {{Plural|TWWHD|Golden Goddess|link}} and the {{Plural|TWWHD|Goddess Pearl|link}}. When arriving on Diamond Island, {{Smw|Captain T. Ode}} remarks on the scenery by saying "It seems so familiar. I would swear I've heard a legend about this place. I won't try to force a name upon it."

A reference to {{BotW}} can be found earlier in the game, where a Toad in Autumn Mountain complains he doesn't have enough stamina to climb a wall, before commenting that at least it isn't raining.

In the Japanese version, the Whispering Woods area is known as {{Romanize|デクの山|Deku no yama|Deku Mountain|2}}. One of its inhabitants, an elderly tree stump named {{Smw|Ol' Grandsappy}}, is called {{Romanize|デクじい|Deku jī|Grandfather Deku|2}}. His face has similarities to the {{Term|OoT3D|The Great Deku Tree|link}} from {{OoT|-}}.

===''[[metroidwiki:Metroid_(series)|Metroid]]'' franchise===
[[File:Metroid Prime 3 Crest Sticker.jpg|thumb|right|251x251px|The [[Royal Crest]] bumper sticker from ''Metroid Prime 3'']]
====''{{Mew|Metroid Prime 2: Echoes}}''====
The game's concept of light/dark duality, as well as the collection of Temple Keys, is comparable to the gameplay mechanic from {{aLttP}}. In fact, the developer of the game, {{Mew|Retro Studios}}, was assisted by one of the developers of the Super NES ''Zelda'' game to properly incorporate the aforementioned concept into the new game.<ref>[http://www.gamasutra.com/php-bin/news_index.php?story=16389 MIGS 2007: Retro Studios On The Journey Of Metroid Prime] - Gamasutra</ref>

During the final phase of the last Dark Samus battle, Samus has to use her charge beam to absorb Dark Samus's Phazon Bullets and then shoot them back at her to damage her, conceptually resembling [[Dead Man's Volley]].

====''{{Mew|Metroid Prime 3: Corruption}}''====
Players can unlock a special feature known as Bumper Stickers, which decorates {{Mew|Samus Aran|Samus}}'s {{Mew|Samus Aran's Gunship|Gunship}} with stickers based on certain Nintendo games that have data stored in the players' [[Wii]] consoles. One of the stickers is the [[Wing Crest]] from {{TP|-}}.
