'''{{TLoZ|-}}''' (ゼルダの伝説, ''Zeruda no Densetsu''), often shortened to simply '''''Zelda''''', is a long-running multi-billiondollar [[wikipedia:Media_franchise|media franchise]] developed and published by [[Nintendo]], created by [[Shigeru Miyamoto]] and [[Takashi Tezuka]], and [[Eiji Aonuma]] being the current director/producer. It is also originally composed by [[Koji Kondo]]. With a setting based on the [[wikipedia:Middle_Ages|Middle Ages]] and [[wikipedia:Historical_fantasy|fantasy]], ''Zelda'' usually takes place in the fictional [[Hyrule|Kingdom of Hyrule]],<ref>[https://www.nintendo.co.uk/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-Ocarina-of-Time-3D/Vol-5-Mr-Shigeru-Miyamoto/5-A-Sword-Sorcery-Tale-Admired-Worldwide/5-A-Sword-Sorcery-Tale-Admired-Worldwide-224778.html Iwata Asks: A Sword & Sorcery Tale Admired Worldwide] "And I was really happy that we here in Japan could make a medieval tale of sword and sorcery liked by the people of the world." [[Shigeru Miyamoto]]</ref> often led by the eponymous [[Princess Zelda]], who, depending on the game, is a damsel-in-distress or the deuteragonist. The main protagonist and mascot of the series is the [[Hylian]] swordsman adventurer in green named [[Link]], a child or young man who is destined, in most primary installments, to save Hyrule from the clutches of the evil Demon King [[Ganon]] (or his human alter-ego [[Ganondorf]]), the main antagonist, and protecting the [[Triforce]]. Some of the games feature additional protagonists such as [[Navi]] the [[Fairy]], [[Ezlo]] the talking cap, or [[Midna]] the Twili, who serve as [[Link's Partners|sidekicks]] throughout the course of the game, or different antagonists, such as [[Vaati]] the Wind Mage, the ancient [[Majora's Mask (Item)|Majora's Mask]], the General of Darkness [[General Onox]], or the Usurper King [[Zant]]. Though ''The Legend of Zelda'' is an [[wikipedia:Action-adventure_game|action-adventure]] video game series, similar to ''[https://assassinscreed.fandom.com/wiki/Assassin%27s_Creed_(series) Assassin's Creed]'', ''[https://tombraider.fandom.com/wiki/Tomb_Raider_Games Tomb Raider]'', ''[https://gta.fandom.com/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto Grand Theft Auto]'' or others, an [[The Legend of Zelda (television series)|animated series]], [[Manga|manga adaptations]], [[comics]], and a few spin-offs also make up aspects of the franchise. In addition, it is often referenced in popular culture such as Namco's ''[[SoulCalibur II]]'' ([[Nintendo GameCube]] version only), and an upcoming [[The Legend of Zelda (film)|live-action film]] based on the main games is planned to be released in April 2027.

[[File:Oracle Series Link.png|thumb|257x257px|[[Link]], the mascot and protagonist of ''The Legend of Zelda'']]One of Nintendo's flagship series alongside ''[[mariowiki:Mario_(franchise)|Mario]]'' and ''[[pokemon:Pokémon_(franchise)|Pokémon]]'', the franchise as a whole made its debut on February 21, 1986 with the very first game being the titular [[Nintendo Entertainment System]] title, {{TLoZ}} (though Japan has it originally released on the [[Family Computer Disk System|Famicom Computer Disk System]]), and has each main installment released on every major Nintendo console since. The vast majority of which were met with critical acclaim and some are even considered among of the [[wikipedia:List_of_video_games_considered_the_best|greatest of all-time]]. Despite not being the first for the action-adventure genre, ''The Legend of Zelda'', specifically the original game and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]'' (on the [[Nintendo 64]]), is widely known for setting its standards and inspired many different games on other media, cementing itself as one of the most important franchises in the video game industry.

''The Legend of Zelda'' was mainly inspired by Shigeru Miyamoto's "explorations" as a young boy in the hillsides, forests, and caves surrounding his childhood home in [[wikipedia:Sonobe,_Kyoto|Sonobe, Japan]], where he ventured into forests with secluded lakes, caves, and rural villages. According to Miyamoto, one of his most memorable experiences was the discovery of a cave entrance in the middle of the woods. After some hesitation, he apprehensively entered the cave, and explored its depths with the aid of a lantern. Miyamoto has referred to the creation of the ''Zelda'' games as an attempt to bring to life a "miniature garden" for players to play with in each game of the series. He also confirmed most of the inspiration for Link's many quests are based on the ''[https://indianajones.fandom.com/wiki/Indiana_Jones_(franchise) Indiana Jones]'' film series and wanted to bring that sense of adventure to a video game. Takashi Tezuka used J. R. R. Tolkien's ''[https://lotr.fandom.com/wiki/The_Lord_of_the_Rings The Lords of the Rings]'' novel trilogy as the primary inspiration for the franchise's iconic [[wikipedia:Sword_and_sorcery|sword and sorcery]] theme.
[[File:TLoZ Gameplay.png|thumb|256x256px|Gameplay screenshot of the franchise's origin title, ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]''|left]]
A particularly interesting note about ''The Legend of Zelda'', being one of Nintendo's earliest main franchises, is the original NES game was developed concurrently with ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_Bros.|Super Mario Bros.]]'' from the [[Mario (franchise)|''Mario'' series]], released one year prior on the same console, and intended to be its polar opposite in gameplay, as it focuses on [[wikipedia:Nonlinear_gameplay|non-linearity]]''.'' From the start, in spite of their contrasting settings (though both are based on a kingdom), ''Zelda'' has had various parallels to ''Mario'', which were both created by the same development team. For example, various ''Super'' ''Mario'' enemies have frequently appeared in {{TLoZ|-}} games, although rarely vice versa, and recurring ''Mario'' elements like [[Chain Chomp]]s and [[Fire Bar]]s were even originally created for ''Zelda'' before they were given their prime association to the fellow Nintendo franchise. In addition, every major media based on either iconic series has been released almost simultaneously with each other since their origins. While it has never been stated outright by Nintendo, due to the many [[Cameos in The Legend of Zelda|Easter]] [[Cameos of The Legend of Zelda in other media|Eggs]] in either series over the decades, more so than every other classic series by the company such as [[metroidwiki:Metroid_(series)|''Metroid'']], [https://kirby.fandom.com/wiki/Kirby_(series) ''Kirby''], or ''[[starfox:Star_Fox_(series)|Star Fox]]'', the frequent connections between ''The Legend of Zelda'' and ''Super'' ''Mario'' suggest them to be partner franchises and even possibly sharing the same universe, nearly among the likes of ''[https://yoshi.fandom.com/wiki/Yoshi_(series) Yoshi]'', ''[https://wario.fandom.com/wiki/Wario_(series) Wario]'', and ''[https://donkeykong.fandom.com/wiki/Donkey_Kong_(series) Donkey Kong]''. Their main characters [[Mario]] and Link have crossed paths in one way or another at times, most prominently in merchandise, the [[Super Smash Bros. (series)|''Super Smash Bros.'' series]] and ''[[mariowiki:Mario_Kart_8|Mario Kart 8]]''/''[[mariowiki:Mario_Kart_8_Deluxe|Deluxe]]'' of the ''[[mariowiki:Mario_Kart_(series)|Mario Kart]]'' series.

Overall, {{TLoZ|-}} is [[wikipedia:List_of_best-selling_video_game_franchises|one of the most successful video game franchises]], and has sold over 170 million copies worldwide as of September 2025. There are currently 21 titles released for the core series, but 33 in total. It remains as Nintendo's third best-selling franchise, behind ''Mario'' and ''Pokémon''.

As of 2026, the franchise is currently celebrating its 40th anniversary.

== Concept ==

=== Story ===
''See Also: [[The Legend of Zelda timeline|Zelda Timeline]] and [[Medieval elements in The Legend of Zelda series|Medieval elements of The Legend of Zelda series]]''
[[File:OoT3D Hyrule Field Artwork 2.png|left|thumb|279x279px|Hyrule, the main setting of the franchise]]
Most games in ''The Legend of Zelda'' series take place in the [[Hyrule|Kingdom of Hyrule]], a beautiful world of peace and royalty. Although the story in each installment varies, the overarching theme is the main antagonist [[Ganon]], the incarnation of [[Demise]]'s hatred, persistently insists to steal the [[Triforce]], the mystical golden artifact created by the [[Golden Goddess|Golden Goddesses]] consisting of three sacred triangles that serve as the balance of the [[universe]] ([[Triforce of Power|Power]], [[Triforce of Wisdom|Wisdom]], and [[Triforce of Courage|Courage]]) resting within the [[Sacred Realm]] that possess ultimate power and has the ability to grant any wish, in order to conquer the world, and [[Link]], the main character and a young but exceptional swordsman, must stop and defeat the wicked warlord by traveling through various areas of Hyrule, fight and slay evil creatures, and overcome dangerous obstacles and hardships. The kingdom's princess and the mortal descendant of the goddess [[The Goddess Hylia|Hylia]], [[Princess Zelda|Zelda]], often guides or even assists Link in completing his objectives, though some games have her as the damsel-in-distress (except for ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Echoes of Wisdom|Echoes of Wisdom]]'' where she is the primary playable character). In the few games where the plot does not take place in Hyrule such as ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening|Link's Awakening]]'' or ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask|Majora's Mask]]'', Link meets different characters and adapts to foreign lands, but continues his usual battles against other antagonists, as like Ganon, their own goals often involve world domination or simply causing havoc. In the end of the majority of games, Hyrule is freed from Ganon's evil and Link and Princess Zelda part ways while the hero usually returns the [[Master Sword]] - an important weapon of the series, back to its [[pedestal]].

=== Gameplay ===
''See Also: [[Gameplay Elements of The Legend of Zelda Series|Gameplay elements of The Legend of Zelda series]]''[[File:BotW Hyrule Ridge.png|thumb|304x304px|Gameplay screenshot of a modern installment of the series, ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild]]'']]As an action-adventure series, ''The Legend of Zelda''<nowiki/>'s gameplay is famously known for its mix of real-time open-world exploration, fantasy, puzzles, use of many items via an [[inventory]], and often contains many side-quests. Although much of it emphasizes a typical [[wikipedia:Hack_and_slash|hack and slash]] game due to its medieval-like setting, several [[wikipedia:Role-playing_video_game|role-playing]] elements are evident, most prominently in ''[[Zelda II: The Adventure of Link|The Adventure of Link]]''. Generally, several meters on the game's HUD are visible to the player including the [[Life Gauge]], [[Magic Meter]], or selected [[Item|items]] (some use ammunition). The former, for example, appearing as [[hearts]], measures Link's current amount of health and decreases whenever he takes damage from [[Enemy|enemies]] or hazards and if it's completely depleted, the [[Game Over|game is over]]. This important meter can be increased by obtaining [[Heart Container|Heart Containers]] after defeating [[Boss|bosses]] or finding [[Piece of Heart|Pieces of Hearts]] in hidden areas. Many core series games consist of three key areas: The [[overworld]] connects all other areas and allows multidirectional movement, giving players some degree of freedom (though in 2D games, the player's top-down view is limited to one screen which scrolls when Link reaches the edge); areas of interaction with other characters ([[Cave|caves]], [[Town|towns]], etc.) in which Link can purchase equipment from [[Shop|shops]], gain special items or advice, or complete side quests and [[Dungeon|dungeons]] (or temples, towers, etc.); The dungeons themselves - dangerous labyrinths normally found underground, comprise a wide range of enemies, bosses, and items, serving as the games' levels. Every dungeon usually has at least one major item that can be essential for solving its [[Puzzle|puzzles]] and plays a crucial role in defeating its boss and/or progressing through the game. ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link Between Worlds|A Link Between Worlds]]'' introduced a more open-ended progression system by allowing Link to rent items to reach and complete the last seven dungeons. Following this, every main dungeon in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild|Breath of the Wild]]'' and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom|Tears of the Kingdom]]'' can be accessed in any order before reaching the [[Final Boss]] in their open world setting. Neither game for the [[Nintendo Switch]] has items hidden inside dungeons for progression, but the latter involves the help of specific companions to solve tasks in their respective Temples.

Although the majority of ''Zelda'' games can technically be completed with minimum exploration and side-quests, Link is frequently rewarded with useful items or increased abilities for solving certain puzzles or exploring hidden areas. Some items are consistently appear throughout the series (Master Sword, [[Hylian Shield]], [[Bow]], [[Bomb]], [[Boomerang]], etc.), while others are unique to a single game such as the [[Ocarina of Time (Item)|Ocarina of Time]], [[Wind Waker (Item)|Wind Waker]] and so on. Items can be bought, rented, or found as a puzzle-solving reward, depending on the game. In ''Breath of the Wild'' and ''Tears of the Kingdom'', [[Sword|Swords]] and other weapons originally found in dungeons or purchased from shops are now found in the game world and stolen from defeated enemies. These are generally used by swapping out to one or more selected button prompts through a menu, while the sword is usually fixed.

Similarly to other long-running Nintendo media, some games in ''The Legend of Zelda'' series have introduced gameplay features that were innovative at their time of release and would become a pioneer for later titles and other media. For example, the original ''The Legend of Zelda'' was the first console game with a [[Saving|save function]] that enabled players to stop playing and then resume later, while ''Ocarina of Time'' introduced a [[Targeting|targeting system]] that let the player lock the camera onto enemies or non-player characters which simplified 3D combat.

=== Early years (1986-1997) ===

===''The Legend of Zelda''===
[[File:TLoZ NES NA Box.png|left|227x227px|US boxart]][[File:TLoZ FDS Box.png|right|197x197px|Japanese boxart]]
{{TLoZ}} (known as '''''The Hyrule Fantasy: The Legend of Zelda''''' in Japan), the title that marks the grand debut of the long-running and highly successful franchise, was first released in Japan in 1986 for the [[Family Computer Disk System|Famicom Disk System]] as its launch title, and internationally in 1987 for the [[Nintendo Entertainment System]]. The North American release is well-known for having unique gold cartridges rather than gray, and its package has a small portion of the box cut to reveal the cartridge. Though basic by today's standards and in comparison to most later installments of the franchise, the original ''The Legend of Zelda'' game was revolutionary for its time. Its innovations include the ability to use multiple different items, a vast world full of secrets to explore, and the cartridge's ability to save progress via battery-backed memory (which was a novelty at the time, as most games then well into the 1990s only used passwords or cannot remember progress at all). The game also features a more challenging "[[Second Quest]]" (a feature very rarely seen in subsequent games), accessible upon completing the game or if players set their name as "ZELDA", where dungeons and the placement of items are completely different and enemies are stronger, though the overworld remains mostly identical. Besides the game's technical innovations, the gameplay (finding items and using them to solve puzzles, battling monsters in real-time, and exploring a vast environment) was a successful formula and became widely copied onto later games and other series inspired by it. ''The Legend of Zelda'' was wildly popular in Japan and North America during its release, selling 6.5 million copies worldwide, and many to this day consider it one of the most important video games ever made among the likes of ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_Bros.|Super Mario Bros.]]''. The original ''Zelda'' is notable for not featuring the full [[Triforce]] in the game, as only the [[Triforce of Power|Power]] and [[Triforce of Wisdom|Wisdom]] pieces existed and [[Triforce of Courage|Courage]] was not yet introduced (despite later artwork showing it as part of the complete Triforce).

During the late years of the Famicom, in 1994, the game was re-released in standard cartridge format (which omits the FDS' unique sounds). A modified 16-bit version known as {{BSTLoZ}} was released for the [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System|Super Famicom]]'s satellite-based expansion, [[BS-X Broadcasting System|Satellaview]], in 1995. ''BS Zelda no Densetsu'' was re-released for the Satellaview in 1997 with re-arranged dungeons and a re-arranged overworld (more or less like the "Second Quest" in the original game). This re-release was dubbed ''BS Zelda ~MAP 2~''.
===''The Adventure of Link''===
[[File:TAoL NA NES Box Artwork.png|left|209x209px|US boxart]][[File:TAoL JP FDS Box Artwork.png|right|188x188px|Japanese boxart]]The second game, the direct sequel to the first, known as ''[[Zelda II: The Adventure of Link]]'' (making it the first and so far only ''Zelda'' game not to directly have "Zelda" in the title) was released in 1987 in Japan (again on the FDS) and 1988 internationally, and was a departure from the concept of the previous NES game as well as all later titles. It exchanged the top-down perspective for side-scrolling and introduced RPG elements (e.g., experience points) not found in any other ''Zelda'' installments, and the gameplay itself was comparable to the ''[https://castlevania.fandom.com/wiki/Castlevania_(series) Castlevania]'' series released on the console at the time. It is also the only ''Zelda'' title until {{FSA|-}} in which Link does not collect [[Rupee|Rupees]]. Because of these fundamental changes exclusive to this game, as well as having a notoriously high difficulty, many consider ''The Adventure of Link'' the "black sheep" of the series. In spite of this, the game was still successful, as it sold 4.38 million copies. Like its predecessor, this game was notable for its gold-colored cartridges and cut-out box in the North American release. This is an irony as both games were later re-released in the final years of the Nintendo Entertainment System with normal gray cartridges that happen to be more difficult to find than their initial gold counterparts. ''The Adventure of Link'' is the first game in the series where the main antagonist is not [[Ganon]], as instead the [[Final Boss]] of this installment is [[Link]]'s own evil shadow, [[Dark Link]] (called [[Link's Shadow]] in-game), and Link himself being at an older age than usual. The Triforce of Courage is also introduced in this title, debuting the full Triforce essence.
==={{ALttP|-}}===
[[File:Zelda SNES.jpg|left|248x248px|US boxart]][[File:ALttP JP Box.jpg|right|258x258px|Japanese boxart]]Four years later, a third title, {{ALttP}} (known as '''''Triforce of the Gods''''' in Japan) returned to the top-down view (under a 3/4 perspective) from ''The Legend of Zelda'' and added an important concept of an alternate dimension to explore, a land known as the [[Dark World]], a concept itself later reused for {{OoT|-}} and {{TP|-}}. The game was originally released for the [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System]] in 1991 in Japan and 1992 for overseas. It is the first installment of the series to have a proper backstory for Link's adventure and the motivation of Ganon, and also introducing the concept of a seemingly main antagonist ([[Agahnim]]) before revealing the actual villain. ''A Link to the Past'' is widely known for introducing the [[Master Sword]], a highly important weapon for the rest of the series, and the game itself is a cult classic among fans for its importance in the franchise and significant improvement over the earlier NES games - often called the best 2D era ''Zelda'' game, selling 4.61 million copies worldwide.

It was later re-released for the [[Game Boy Advance]] on 2002 in North America, on a cartridge with {{FS}}, the first multiplayer ''Zelda.'' In addition, both this game (unchanged except for being converted into a downloadable format) and an exclusive "loosely based" sequel (which used the same game engine) called {{AST}} were released on the [[Satellaview]] in Japan in 1997, similar to ''BS The Legend of Zelda'' for the NES title. In 2013, over two decades after the original release of the game, a sequel to {{ALttP|-}}, {{ALBW|-}}, was released for the [[Nintendo 3DS]].
==={{LA|-}}===
[[File:Link's Awakening US boxart.jpg|left|191x191px|US boxart]][[File:LA JP Box.jpg|right|226x226px|Japanese boxart]]The next game, {{LA}} (known as '''''The Dreaming Island''''' in Japan) was the first ''Zelda'' title ever released on a handheld console, for the [[Game Boy]] in 1993, and is also the first game to take place outside of [[Hyrule]], in the land of [[Koholint Island]]. The gameplay of the game itself is similar to that of ''A Link to the Past'', and even shares the same developers, as it was originally intended to be that game's sequel. Although not as influential compared to its predecessor or ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]'', it introduced playable [[Song|songs]], [[fishing]], and a trading sequence to the series. ''Link's Awakening'' was praised among fans and sold 3.83 million copies worldwide. ''Link's Awakening'' is known to have many [[Cameos in The Legend of Zelda|crossover appearances]] from various other Nintendo franchises, specifically ''Super Mario'' and even ''[https://kirby.fandom.com/wiki/Kirby_(series) Kirby]'' (which was relatively new at the time). It was re-released for the [[Game Boy Color]] in 1998 as {{LA|DX}}, which is identical with color graphics, with some additional features, including an extra color-based dungeon and a photo shop that allowed interaction with the [[Game Boy Printer]]. The game would have a [[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening (Nintendo Switch)|3D remake]] in 2019 for the [[Nintendo Switch|Switch]].

=== [[:Category:3D Games|The 3D Era]] (1998-2002) ===

==={{OoT|-}}===
[[File:OoT NA Box.jpg|left|223x223px|US boxart]][[File:OoT JP Box.jpg|right|234x234px|Japanese boxart]]After another hiatus, the series made the transition to 3D with the next installment of {{OoT}}, which was released for the [[Nintendo 64]] in 1998. This game, initially known as ''Zelda 64'', retained the core gameplay of the previous 2D games, and quickly became one of the most universally successful games of all time in both commercial and critical terms. Selling 7.6 million copies and essentially a repeat of the original ''The Legend of Zelda''<nowiki/>'s revolution for three-dimensions, it is widely considered by many to this day to be ''the'' greatest video game ever created, and scored perfect scores in several video game publications, including the first 40/40 score in Famitsu (a prestigious Japanese gaming magazine). In 2006 it was also ranked by [[Nintendo Power]] as the best game to ever appear on a Nintendo console. The title was originally slated for the ill-fated, Japanese-only [[Nintendo 64DD]], but was ported to a 32MB cartridge when the hardware was delayed. It features many then-new innovations, including the use of lock-on targeting, a new gameplay mechanic that focuses the camera on a nearby target and alters the player's actions to be relative to that target, which would become standard for all later 3D-based installments. Such mechanics allow precision-based swordfighting in a 3D space and were a revolutionary development at the time. The [[Light Arrow|Light Arrows]], which replaced the [[Silver Arrow|Silver Arrows]] in earlier games, were introduced in this game, and Ganon's human form, [[Ganondorf]], makes his first appearance as well. It is also the first and so far only time Link and [[Princess Zelda]] are shown in both young and adult age stages, setting the template for some later games and other media based on the latter for the characters, and introducing the main protagonist's most iconic incarnation, the [[Hero of Time]]. Like its NES predecessors, {{OoT|-}} was notable for being released on a limited Collector's Edition gold cartridge (available mainly to those who pre-ordered the game) though most were in the traditional gray cartridge. Although ''Ocarina of Time'' was considered a masterpiece and has explicit importance in not only in the ''Zelda'' series but the video game industry as a whole (alongside ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_64|Super Mario 64]]''), due to its age today, however, current series director [[Eiji Aonuma]] [[Controversy in The Legend of Zelda Series|controversially claimed the game didn't age well]] and would never quit working on the franchise until he created a superior game. Some fans also believed the N64 game to be overrated or overshadowed the potential success of later titles with similar gameplay until ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild|Breath of the Wild]]'', implying the franchise peaked with this particular game.

{{OoT|-}} was re-released on the [[GameCube]] in 2002 when it was offered as a pre-order incentive for {{TWW|-}} in the US and Japan. Europe continues to receive it free in every copy of {{TWW|-}}, except for the discounted [[Player's Choice]] version. It included a previously unreleased 64DD expansion known as ''Ura Zelda'' in Japan and {{MQ}} in North America. However the original ''Ura Zelda'' planned for the 64DD at the time was to enhance {{OoT|-}} in most aspects, instead of being a dungeon remix, which ''Master Quest'' is and much like that of the [[Second Quest]] in ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]''. ''Ocarina of Time'' was included as part of {{CE}} for the GameCube in 2003.

The game was remade in 2011 as {{OoT3D}} on the Nintendo [[3DS]], which added improved graphics and framerate, 3D effects, a [[Boss Challenge]] mode and included the ''Master Quest'' (with mirrored graphics). It is also re-released on the [[Nintendo Switch Online]] in HD resolution.
==={{MM|-}}===
[[File:MM NA Box Art.png|left|210x210px|US boxart]][[File:MM JP wMemCard Box.jpg|right|225x225px|Japanese boxart]]The follow-up title of ''Ocarina of Time'', {{MM}}, its direct sequel, which was released in November 2000, used the same 3D game engine as the previous Nintendo 64 game (dropping the fixed 3D elements), but uses the Expansion Pak for slightly improved graphics (itself required to run the game), adding a unique novel [[Time|time-based concept]]. Although critically acclaimed like its predecessor, the in-game 72 hour (54 minutes in real time) time limit feature gained somewhat mixed reactions from the series' fanbase. It was originally called ''Zelda Gaiden'', a Japanese title loosely translating to "''Zelda, Another Story"''. Gameplay, though virtually identical to the previous game, had changed significantly; in addition to a form of time limit, Link could use [[Mask|masks]] to transform into different creatures with unique skills. While {{MM|-}} retained the graphical style of the landmark {{OoT|-}}, it was also a departure, particularly in atmosphere; ''Majora's Mask'' is one of, if not ''the'' darkest installment of the entire series, dealing with death and tragedy in a manner not previously (or later) seen in the franchise, and has a sense of impending doom as a large sinister [[Moon]] slowly descends upon the land of [[Termina]], a parallel universe of Hyrule. Alongside being a ''Zelda'' title not taking place in Hyrule like ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening|Link's Awakening]]'', this game is also known for its main antagonist not being [[Ganon]], as [[Majora's Mask (Item)|Majora's Mask]] takes his place, similar to ''[[Zelda II: The Adventure of Link|The Adventure of Link]]''. All copies of {{MM|-}} are gold cartridges, instead of a limited Collector's Edition lenticular cartridge label that was offered as the pre-order incentive. Copies of the game that weren't actual Collector's Editions featured the traditional sticker cartridge label rather than a holographic one. As a result of the game being released late in the N64's lifespan and fans were skeptical about the title during its release, ''Majora's Mask'' sold 3.36 million copies worldwide, less than half of ''Ocarina of Time''<nowiki/>'s sales.

A remake of the game was released in 2015 as {{MM3D}} on the Nintendo [[3DS]], which added, among many other changes, improved graphics and controls, 3D effects, and a [[Fishing|fishing minigame]], similar to ''Ocarina of Time 3D''.
===''Oracle of Ages/Oracle of Seasons''===
[[File:Oracleofages.jpg|left|147x147px|US boxart]][[File:OoA JP Box.jpg|right|149x149px|Japanese boxart]][[File:Oracleofseasons.jpg|left|145x145px|US boxart]][[File:OoS JP Box.jpg|right|152x152px|Japanese boxart]]The next two games, {{OoA}} and {{OoS}}, were released in 2001 simultaneously for the [[Game Boy Color]], similar to the ''[[pokemon:Pokémon_(franchise)|Pokémon]]'' series' releases, and interact using passwords or a Game Link Cable. After one game has been completed, the player is given a password that allows the other game to be played as a sequel. They were developed by [[Capcom]] in conjunction with Nintendo, with supervision from [[Shigeru Miyamoto]], the franchise's creator, making them the first ''Zelda'' games to be co-developed by a different gaming company assisting Nintendo. After the team experimented with porting the original ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]'' to the Game Boy Color, they decided to make an original trilogy to be called the "Triforce Series". When the password system linking the three games proved too troublesome, the concept was reduced to two titles at Miyamoto's suggestion. These two titles became {{OoA|-}}, which is more puzzle-based, and {{OoS|-}}, which is more action-oriented. The overall graphics and gameplay are very similar to ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening|Link's Awakening]]'' but have completely original stories. The canceled third game was originally going to be called ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Mystical Seed of Courage]]''. Although they were released late in the GBC's lifespan, ''Ages'' and ''Season'' each sold 3.96 million copies. They are also the first ''Zelda'' titles to share the same boxart for all regions, though this would not return until ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Four Swords Adventures|Four Sword Adventures]]'', then ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tri Force Heroes|Tri Force Heroes]]''.

''Oracle of Ages''/''Seasons'' were later re-released for the Virtual Console and Nintendo Switch Online in 2013 and 2023, respectively.

=== Reboot and {{FS|-}} sub-series (2002-2005) ===

==={{FS|-}}===
[[File:Four Swords Box.png|left|185x185px|US boxart]]
[[File:ALttPFS JP Box.jpg|right|245x245px|Japanese boxart]]
In December 2002, {{ALttP&FS}} was released for the [[Game Boy Advance]], comprising a remake port of {{ALttP|-}} for the Super NES, and an original multiplayer-only game titled {{FS}}—the first multiplayer ''Zelda'' game. 

The Link and Princess Zelda featured in {{FS|-}} are childhood friends. This is also the first game in which [[Vaati]] and the [[Four Sword]] appear. As part of the [[The Legend of Zelda 25th Anniversary|25th Anniversary of ''The Legend of Zelda'' series]] (2011), {{FSAE}} was briefly made available as a free download for both the [[Nintendo DSi]] and [[Nintendo 3DS]]. This game was a special edition of {{FS|-}} now featuring a single player mode. {{ALttP&FS|-}} sold 2.82 million copies worldwide.
==={{TWW|-}}===
[[File:Windwakerbox.jpg|left|283x283px|US boxart]][[File:TWW JP Box.jpg|right|268x268px|Japanese boxart]]When Nintendo revealed the [[Nintendo GameCube]] on August 24, 2000, the day before Nintendo's SpaceWorld 2000 exposition, [[SpaceWorld 2000 GameCube Tech Demo|one software demonstration]] showed a realistically-styled real-time duel between Link and Ganondorf in graphics similar to the Nintendo 64 titles. Fans and the media speculated that the battle might be from a new ''Zelda'' game under development. At Spaceworld 2001, Nintendo showed an unusual [[wikipedia:Cel_shading|cel-shaded]] ''Zelda'' title, later released in December 2002 in Japan and 2003 internationally as ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker|The Wind Waker]]'' (known as '''''The Baton Wind''''' in Japan), which Shigeru Miyamoto felt would "extend ''Zelda''<nowiki/>'s reach to all ages". The media reported that ''Zelda'' was shifting to a younger audience, much to Miyamoto's surprise. Nothing further was shown until a playable demonstration was ready, which was well-received. The gameplay focused on controlling wind with a magical baton called the [[Wind Waker (Item)|Wind Waker]] and sailing a small boat around a massive, island-filled ocean. Due to its cartoonish graphics, the setting being heavily focused on sailing on an [[Great Sea|ocean]] rather than exploring [[Hyrule]], and is also the first ''Zelda'' game to explicitly feature a different [[Link]] and [[Princess Zelda]] story-wise as opposed to previous titles (which would set the template for the [[Zelda Timeline]], ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Hyrule Historia|Hyrule Historia]]'' and all future games) - therefore rebooting the franchise, {{TWW|-}} was met with [[Controversy in The Legend of Zelda series|significant outrage]] among fans in all regions, and even almost tarnished the series' reputation entirely. Despite the initial controversy and sold poorly in Japan, the game was still quite successful in North America and Europe (selling 4.3 million copies) and eventually became beloved, which would lead to more titles with cel-shading as well as additional incarnations of the main characters. This game marks the first time Zelda is able to join Link in his final battle against Ganondorf, who makes a return (but doesn't change to his beast form). The version of the Links throughout the series that used cel-shaded graphics from {{TWW|-}} onwards would famously be called "[[Toon Link]]" by fans, due to such a name used for the [[Super Smash Bros. (series)|''Super Smash Bros.'' series]] since ''Brawl'' to distinguish from his traditional counterpart.

The game was re-released for [[Wii U]] in 2013 as ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker HD|The Wind Waker HD]]'', which added enhanced graphics, faster sailing, [[Hero Mode]], and online integration with the now-defunct [[Miiverse]].
==={{FSA|-}}===
[[File:FourSwordsA Box.jpg|left|228x228px|US boxart]][[File:FSA JP Box.jpg|right|222x222px|Japanese boxart]]Next in the series came {{FSA}} for the GameCube, which was released in the first half of 2004 in Japan and United States, and in January 2005 in Europe. Based on the [[Game Boy Advance]] {{FS}}, {{FSA|-}} was another deviation from previous ''Zelda'' gameplay, focusing on multiplayer gameplay and "level-based" action (like many ''Super Mario Bros.'' titles). The game contains 24 individual stages and a map screen; there is no connecting overworld. For the multiplayer features of the game, each player is required to use a Game Boy Advance system linked to the Nintendo GameCube via a GBA-GCN cable. Although it focuses on multiplayer, the game does feature a single player campaign, in which using a Game Boy Advance is optional.

{{FSA|-}} is actually two games in one: Hyrulean Adventure (with a storyline and action somewhat similar to a traditional ''Zelda'' adventure) and Shadow Battle (a free-for-all melee "battle mode" which pits multiple Links against each other as the players struggle for dominance in Hyrulean arenas). The Japanese and Korean versions include a third segment, known as [[Navi Trackers]] (originally designed as the stand-alone game [[Tetra]]'s Trackers), which is not included in any other incarnation of the title. Navi Trackers contains an important first for ''Zelda'', as the game has spoken dialogue for most of the characters. The game itself sold only around 937,000 copies worldwide, making this the least successful title in the franchise and the first and so far, only, to sell less than one million.
==={{TMC|-}}===
[[File:ZeldaMinishCap BoxArt.jpg|left|171x171px|US boxart]][[File:TMC JP Box.jpg|right|229x229px|Japanese boxart]]In November 2004 in Japan and Europe, and in January 2005 in America, Nintendo released a new game for the [[Game Boy Advance]], {{TMC}} (known as '''''The Wonder Cap''''' in Japan). The central concept of {{TMC|-}} is Link's ability to shrink in size with the aid of a mystical sentient hat named [[Ezlo]]. While tiny, Link can see previously-explored parts of a [[dungeon]] from a new perspective, and enter new areas through otherwise impassable openings. Link is able to switch from big to small at special portals throughout the land, once again giving Link two "worlds" to play in. Like the ''Oracle'' games, this installment was also developed by Capcom under Nintendo's supervision. ''The Minish Cap'' sold over 1 million copies, making it one of the least successful titles. This game is noticeable for having Link in a very similar appearance to ''The Wind Waker/Four Swords'', but has the voice of the young [[Hero of Time]] from ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]''/''[[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask|Majora's Mask]]'' in spite not being such incarnation at all from those installments, like ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past & Four Swords|A Link to the Past & Four Swords]]'', leading to some confusion among fans.

=== The Next Generation (2006-2011) ===

==={{TP|-}}===
[[File:TP Wii.jpg|left|211x211px|US box art (Wii version)|alt=US box art]][[File:TP BoxArt JP.jpg|right|217x217px|Japanese boxart (Wii version)|alt=Japanese boxart]]In November 2006, {{TP}} arrived as the first ''Zelda'' game on the [[Wii]], although it was originally going to be a [[Nintendo GameCube|GameCube]]-only title. During the following month, it was ported on the GameCube as well (also the final game released on the console), making it the first ''Zelda'' game to be released on more than one home console, which would repeat again for ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild|Breath of the Wild]]''. The new game once again strives for a realistic look, improved even beyond the aforementioned SpaceWorld demo. Similar to ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask|Majora's Mask]]'', it makes a return of the darker aspects of the series. Just like ''The Wind Waker'', {{TP|-}} is set many years after the events of ''Ocarina of Time''/{{MM|-}}, but with a different story and another descendant of the legendary Hero of Time (who himself appears as the [[Hero's Spirit]] in-game). This game chronicles the struggle of a more mature Link to rid Hyrule of the "[[Twilight Realm]]", a mysterious force plaguing the land. When Link enters this realm, he transforms into a [[Wolf Link|wolf]] and the gameplay shifts radically. {{TP|-}} also focuses more heavily on [[Epona|horseback transportation]] and mounted battle scenarios (including [[boss]] battles) than previous installments. Because of the Wii's controls, this version of the game is played on mirrored graphics while the rarer Gamecube release has it normal. The game was very successful, and the Wii release sold 5.82 million copies while the GCN sold just 1.32 million, making a total of 8.85 million copies worldwide, surpassing ''Ocarina of Time''<nowiki/>'s standalone sales of 7.6 million.
==={{PH|-}}===
[[File:Phantomhour.jpg|left|192x192px|US boxart]][[File:PH JP Box.jpg|right|191x191px|Japanese boxart]]A new ''Zelda'' game for the [[Nintendo DS]] was once rumored to be a new {{FS|-}} game, but Nintendo later retracted those statements. Instead, at the 2006 Game Developers Conference, a trailer for {{PH}} for the DS was shown. The trailer revealed traditional top-down Zelda gameplay optimized for the DS’s features, a cel-shaded graphical style directly recalling {{TWW|-}}. At E³ 2006, Nintendo confirmed its status as a direct sequel to {{TWW|-}}. Released on June 23, 2007, {{PH|-}} features unique new controls making use of the Nintendo DS hardware capabilities, and incorporates a 1-on-1 multiplayer [[Battle Mode|battle mode]], supporting play both locally and online through the [[Nintendo Wi-Fi Connection]]. It was successful with reviews agreeing to be a worthy sequel of its predecessor and sold 4.13 million copies worldwide.

==={{ST|-}}===
[[File:Spirit Tracks Cover.jpg|left|203x203px|US boxart]][[File:ST JP Box.jpg|right|197x197px|Japanese boxart]]
On March 25, 2009, a trailer for the next [[Nintendo DS]] ''Zelda'' title: {{ST}}, was revealed at GDC 2009. It is set in the new-found Hyrule, taking place a century after the events of {{PH|-}}. Another new Link appears wearing a conductor's outfit and hat. Early on in the game, Link obtain the [[Hero's Clothes]], similar to in ''The Wind Waker''. {{ST|-}} features the same cel-shaded graphics style that was seen in ''The Wind Waker'', ''The Minish Cap,'' and {{PH|-}}. Also, the game features many new [[Song|songs]], with a few select melodies returning from {{PH|-}}. The game was released towards the end of December worldwide, and features the unique train mechanic, which replaces the steamboat from ''Phantom Hourglass.'' Although it was successful, ''Spirit Tracks'' sold much less than its predecessor, 2.61 million copies.
==={{SS|-}}===
[[File:SS NA Box Art.png|left|235x235px|US boxart]][[File:SkywardSwordJapaneseBoxArt.png|right|239x239px|Japanese boxart]]
On June 2, 2009, IGN talked to [[Shigeru Miyamoto]] about the upcoming ''Zelda'' game for Wii. He made comments about the changes that the new motion plus would bring to the game, and said "Think of ''Zelda'' while you're trying some of the archery and sword play in ''{{Wp|Wii Sports Resort}}''." At E3 2010, the game, ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Skyward Sword|Skyward Sword]]'' was finally unveiled. According to the details given, Link lives in a land known as [[Skyloft]] which floats high above Hyrule. He learns that Hyrule itself is in danger and travels to the ground to save the kingdom. Much of the gameplay focuses heavily on the use of the Wii Motion Plus, allowing the players to have a much greater level of control over Link. At E3 2010, much of the game was unraveled by Miyamoto and [[Eiji Aonuma|Aonuma]], including a new flying mechanism in the game. As for characters, a villain under the name [[Ghirahim]] causes some controversy in [[Skyloft]], which leads to the kidnapping of Link's friend, [[Princess Zelda#Skyward Sword|Princess Zelda]]. The game was released on late 2011. It was a successful title, selling 3.52 million copies worldwide, though ''Skyward Sword'' received some criticism for its flying mechanism and the story being too linear. According to ''Hyrule Historia'', this particular game is the origins of the franchise's story.

A [[The Legend of Zelda: Skyward Sword HD|high-definition remaster of the game]] was released for the [[Nintendo Switch|Switch]] in July 16, 2021.

=== Rethinking the Conventions of Zelda (2013-present) ===

==={{ALBW|-}}===
[[File:A Link Between Worlds cover.jpg|left|187x187px|US boxart]][[File:A Link Between Worlds JP.jpg|right|191x191px|Japanese boxart]]
The next game, {{ALBW}}, is the first ''Zelda'' title released for the Nintendo [[Nintendo 3DS|3DS]]. It takes place in the same world as {{ALttP|-}} and is a sequel to the significantly older [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System|Super NES]] game released in 1991. It takes place hundreds of years later, when forces from the parallel universe of [[Lorule]], which even has an upside-down [[Triforce]], invade Hyrule and turn its [[Sage|sages]] into paintings. The game is remarkable for its open-ended nature. Link can rent or buy key items from [[Ravio]] instead of finding them in dungeons, allowing him to complete them in almost any order. Link can also turn into a painting to slip by obstacles or cross wide chasms. It features StreetPass support, allowing other players to enter the game as [[Shadow Link]]s that Link can defeat for a large prize. The game was released on November 22, 2013 in North America and December 26 in Japan, marking the first time a ''Zelda'' installment was initially released in America rather than Japan. ''A Link Between Worlds'' sold 2.51 million copies worldwide.

==={{TFH|-}}===
[[File:TFH NA Box Art.png|left|198x198px|US boxart]][[File:TFH JP Box Art.png|right|190x190px|Japanese boxart]]
The next game on the 3DS is ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tri Force Heroes|Tri Force Heroes]],'' released in 2015. Despite reusing the cel-shaded graphics from ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker|The Wind Waker]]'' once again, it stars the same Link as its predecessor, {{ALBW|-}}, who has fled Hyrule and disguised himself in order to avoid recognition. He stumbles across the land of [[Hytopia]], whose [[Princess Styla|princess]] was cursed to wear ugly clothing by [[Lady Maud|a villainous witch]]. Link teams up with [[Links|two similar heroes]] to journey through the [[Drablands]] and stop Lady Maud to free Princess Styla. The game uses an internet connection to allow three players to each control a Link, working together and communicating with icons, similar to the ''Four Sword''s games. They can also [[Totem|stack]] on top of each other, a special ability used throughout the game. The game is also fashion-based, with players being able to craft outfits with [[Madame Couture]] to gain special abilities. This game is the worst-selling game in the main series, with 1.36 million copies sold as of 2020.

==={{BotW|-}}===
[[File:BotW NA Switch Box Art.png|left|217x217px|US boxart (Switch version)|alt=US boxart]][[File:BotW JP Switch Box Art.png|right|233x233px|Japanese boxart (Switch version)|alt=Japanese boxart]]
Two years later, a new game, {{BotW}}, is released, with completely new gameplay features. Yet another new Link, awakens from a deep, 100-year long sleep and is tasked with destroying [[Calamity Ganon]] before the world is destroyed. This game returns to the original ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]'' concept of a realistic open-world adventure, where Link is free to explore the overworld in any direction at any given moment. Major dungeons found in previous games have been replaced with four smaller [[Divine Beast]]s and numerous small [[Ancient Shrine]] scattered throughout the land. Several new elements were introduced as well, such as weapon durability, weather effects, taming and registering wild horses, and abilities like [[Shield Surfing]] and {{Term|BotW|Perfect Dodging|link|display=Perfect Dodge}} and [[Perfect Guard|Guard]]. Similar to ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess|Twilight Princess]]'', the game was released simultaneously worldwide for the [[Wii U]] and [[Nintendo Switch]] on March 3, 2017, as the final Nintendo-published game released for the former and a launch title for the latter. Due to its marketing and new features, game was wildly successful, as just 13 months after its release, it already sold 9.56 million copies and as of 2023 reached a whopping 33.31 million, making it by far the best-selling game in the franchise. It is also the first ''Zelda'' game to utilize full voice acting as opposed to scrolling text. Modern fans debate this title as the best ''Zelda'' game over ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]''.

The game would have a release for the [[Nintendo Switch 2]] console on June 5, 2025, known as ''Breath of the Wild - Nintendo Switch 2 Edition'', featuring improved resolution, framerate, HDR support, and additional functionality with the Nintendo Switch App.

==={{TotK|-}}===
[[File:The Legend of Zelda TOTK (NA cover art).png|left|207x207px|US boxart]][[File:TotK Japanese cover.jpg|right|222x222px|Japanese boxart]]
The following game, now in its 20th title, {{TotK}}, is also released on the Switch. As the direct sequel of ''Breath of the Wild'' and completely reusing its engine, Link and Princess Zelda journey into the depths of [[Hyrule Castle (Breath of the Wild)|Hyrule Castle]] and are attacked by the mummified body of [[Ganondorf]]. The [[Master Sword]] is [[Decayed Master Sword|nearly destroyed]], and the heroes are separated once more. Link sets out to restore the Master Sword and save Hyrule once more from the threat of Ganondorf, who finally returns since ''Twilight Princess''. The game operates on the same mechanics as its prequel, along with introducing the [[Zonai Devices]], which allow players to create all manner of devices to aid gameplay. The game was released on May 12, 2023. It has received acclaim like its predecessor and as of March 2024, it successfully sold over 20.61 million copies, although it also gained criticism for its mixed story writing and being too closely similar to the prequel by completing reusing its engine. The game itself makes a return to the darker aspects seen in ''Majora's Mask'' and ''Twilight Princess''.

Like ''Breath of the Wild'', Tears of the Kingdom would have a release for the Nintendo Switch 2 on the same date, known as ''Tears of the Kingdom - Nintendo Switch 2 Edition''.

==={{EoW|-}} ===
[[File:Echoes of Wisdom Box Art.webp|left|frameless|206x206px]]The latest game of the series so far'', [[The Legend of Zelda: Echoes of Wisdom|Echoes of Wisdom]]'', a top-down style game for the Switch, released on September 26, 2024. It appears to utilize the same game engine and art style featured in the [[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening (Nintendo Switch)|Switch re-release of ''Link's Awakening'']]. This particular game marks the first mainline title in the entire franchise since its debut in 1986 to feature [[Zelda|Princess Zelda]] as the main playable character rather than Link. Rather than Ganon, the main antagonist of the game is [[Null]].

[[File:Tingle's Balloon Fight.jpg‎|left|100px]]''[[Tingle's Balloon Fight DS]]'' is a game that was only made available to Club Nintendo members in Japan. It was released in 2007, supposedly as a sequel to ''Tingle's Rosy Rupeeland''. It's very similar to the original Balloon Fight game for the NES, but it has several differences as well. It stars Tingle as the main character, and adds music from ''Tingle's Rosy Rupeeland''. It also has 20 pieces of artwork which can be "unlocked" by completing various tasks. The game has three different modes of play. The first is similar to the original ''Balloon Fight'', in which the player must advance through all 99 levels. The second is a [[multiplayer]] version of the first game type. The third mode is Balloon Trip, in which Tingle must advance from level to level while crossing to sea. He must dodge lightning bolts while collecting "gifts".

==={{RTBToL|-}}===
[[File:Tingle's Love Balloon Trip JP boxart.jpg‎|left|100px]]{{RTBToL}} was released in Japan in August 2009, and was not released overseas. Similar to ''Tingle's Rosy Rupeeland'', the game starts with an ordinary, 35-year-old man. While he watches a sales program on television, the man learns of a book that is deemed to make its readers popular amongst the ladies. He orders the book, but he gets sucked into the world of a picture book when he opens it and transforms into Tingle. He learns that the only way to get out of the book is to dance with the princess of this world, which is the main goal of the game. The game is a parody of the {{Wp|The Wonderful Wizard of Oz|The Wizard of Oz}}, where Tingle meets three friends, [[Kakashi]], the scarecrow, [[Buriki]], the tin-woman, and [[Lion (Tingle's Balloon Trip of Love)|Lion]], the lion. Together, they follow the yellow-brick road, and advance from page to page. Unlike the previous installation of this series, this is a point-and-click game. This game was also developed by Vanpool using Nintendo's permission.

== Trivia ==
[[File:ALBW Triforce Artwork.png|thumb|240x240px|The Triforce, itself an essential relic in the series, serves as ''The Legend of Zelda''<nowiki/>'s trademark symbol]]
* The [[Triforce]] and [[Master Sword]] are the main and secondary symbols of the franchise, respectively. Both of them can be seen together in the title screen of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past|A Link to the Past]]'' as well as its Japanese box art.
* Being one of [[Nintendo]]'s flagship series, ''The Legend of Zelda'' is one of the highest-grossing video game franchises of all-time, with a revenue of over $3.8 billion as of January 2025.
** However, it pales in comparison to that of ''[[mariowiki:Mario_(franchise)|Mario]]'' and ''[[pokemon:Pokémon_(franchise)|Pokémon]]'', which have over $55 and $150 billion, respectively.
* Due to the iconic logo of the series since ''A Link to the Past'' has the word "Zelda" being in a much larger font, some older games and media have the franchise being named "'''''The Legend of ZELDA'''''".
** On a similar note, as the "Z" on [[Princess Zelda]]'s name in the logo is bigger than the other already enlarged letters, some newcomer fans have mistaken the pronunciation as "Z-Elda".
** The franchise's name - ''The Legend of '''Zelda''''', is also a misnomer, as some fans mistakenly believe Princess Zelda as the main protagonist of the series rather than [[Link]] or Link's name being "Zelda". Ironically, ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Echoes of Wisdom|Echoes of Wisdom]]'', released in 2024, is the first mainline game where the princess herself serves as the main playable character.
** Others in the fandom have also believed the franchise should be named "The Legend of Link" for this reason. Only ''[[Zelda II: The Adventure of Link]]'' is the title in the main series a similar event has occurred since.

* ''The Legend of Zelda'' franchise shares the distinction with the ''Metroid,'' ''Kid Icarus'', and Konami's ''[https://castlevania.fandom.com/wiki/Castlevania_(series) Castlevania]'' series in that each made their debut in 1986 on the [[Family Computer Disk System|Famicom Disk System]], nearly simultaneously - as the latter three were introduced in August, December, and September, respectively.
** Interestingly, the debut dates of ''Zelda'' and fellow Nintendo flagship series ''Pokémon'' were also almost exactly 10 years apart. Both appeared in February, on the 21st and 27th, respectively, and the latter in 1996.
** Another Nintendo series, ''[[starfox:Star_Fox_(series)|Star Fox]]'', coincidentally shares the same date as ''The Legend of Zelda'''s, seven years later in 1993.
* The series' respective breakthrough 2D and 3D installments, ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]'' and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]'', interestingly used a heavily modified version of the game engines used to create ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_Bros.|Super Mario Bros.]]'' and ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_64|Super Mario 64]]'' from the ''Mario'' series, due to those titles being developed alongside them.
* Unlike other early Nintendo game franchises, ''The Legend of Zelda'' doesn't have any titles released for the arcades.
* The famous 1928 orchestral song "''Boléro''" by Maurice Ravel was the main inspiration for the franchise's [[The Legend of Zelda (Theme)|theme song]] according to [[Koji Kondo]], its original composer. However, shortly before finishing ''The Legend of Zelda'', the development team learned that the music was still under copyright at the time (though it is now public domain). Since they could not delay the release of the [[Family Computer Disk System]] and the game itself, Kondo himself wrote the actual title theme in a single night. 
** The 1969 hit song "[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aoPabJ6dnUg ''April'']" from the rock band [[wikipedia:Deep_Purple|Deep Purple]] is widely believed by many to be also an influence for the main theme, as well as the [[dungeon]] theme of the original ''The Legend of Zelda'' and [[Hyrule Castle]] of some later games, though this has not been officially confirmed by Kondo.
** Another song, "''[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r5PH4RjofGE Morning Glory]''" by [[wikipedia:Tatsuro_Yamashita|Tatsuro Yamashita]], released in 1982, is an influence of the [[Fairy Fountain]] theme heard in some games since ''A Link to the Past'', often at the [[File-Selection Screen|File Selection]] screen. Coincidentally, the [[mariowiki:Water_Land|Water Land]] theme from ''[[mariowiki:Super_Mario_Bros._3|Super Mario Bros. 3]]'' in the ''Mario'' franchise is also a similar music.
* [[Eiji Aonuma]] is the current director and producer of the series. In the earliest games prior to ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask|Majora's Mask]]'', however, the two roles were managed by [[Takashi Tezuka]] and [[Shigeru Miyamoto]], respectively.

[[File:LinkPraying.png|thumb|266x266px|The rare artwork of Link praying to a crucifix from ''A Link to the Past'']]
* ''The Legend of Zelda'' series was likely originally going to use [[wikipedia:Christianity|Christianity]] as its in-universe religion, as the [[The Legend of Zelda|original game]] and its [[Zelda II: The Adventure of Link|sequel]] depicts [[Shield|Shields]] with the [[wikipedia:Christian_cross|Christian cross]], the [[Book of Magic]] is actually the [[wikipedia:Bible|Bible]] as it was called in the Japanese release for the former, and an artwork of ''A Link to the Past'' even shows [[Link]] praying at a [[wikipedia:Crucifix|crucifix]] in the [[Sanctuary]] (itself named a church in the Japanese version). Even the Triforce is possibly a reference to the [[wikipedia:Trinity|Holy Trinity]] due to their similarities (despite the golden symbol was inspired by the [[wikipedia:Hōjō_clan|Hōjō clan]], which is historically identical and its fragments were planned to be electronic chips in ''The Legend of Zelda''). However, since ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]'', due to Nintendo's strict policy of avoiding real-world religious material, it was changed to have its own fictional culture based on various historical events.
* Due to the overall gameplay of Link using [[Item|items]] and swordfighting to progress in his many adventures, there has been heavy debate in the gaming industry whether or not ''The Legend of Zelda'' should be classified as an [[wikipedia:Action_role-playing_game|action role-playing]] series rather than action-adventure. According to its creator Shigeru Miyamoto, he disagrees, as he said that he was "not interested in systems where everything in the game is decided by stats and numbers" but wanted "to preserve as much of that "live" feeling as possible", and felt that action games are better suited to convey to players.[[File:TLoZ Japanese Logo 3.png|thumb|236x236px|The original Japanese logo of ''The Legend of Zelda'', featuring "The Hyrule Fantasy" on its title]]
* The name of the franchise was originally going to be '''''The Hyrule Fantasy''''', as depicted in the initial Japanese version of ''The Legend of Zelda''<nowiki/>'s title. It was changed to its surname since its international release, as adding a third subtitle would make the core series games' wording too long, and also Nintendo wanting to avoid comparisons with Square Enix's ''[https://finalfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Final_Fantasy_series Final Fantasy]'' series, which made its debut around the same time in Japan.
* Two early installments of the series, ''A Link to the Past'' and ''Ocarina of Time'', coincidentally share the same Japanese release date as each o<nowiki/>ther as well as the [[Super Nintendo Entertainment System|Super Famicom]]: November 21st (but not year).
* The longtime franchise beginning on its [[The Legend of Zelda 25th Anniversary|25th anniversary]] (2011) has an [[The Legend of Zelda timeline|official timeline]] to show the placement of every game's chronological order, with ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Hyrule Historia|Hyrule Historia]]'' providing additional information. However, although generally accepted, it is met with [[Controversy in The Legend of Zelda Series|controversy]] among some fans, especially for the Downfall Timeline section, as they find it confusing or seeing it as complete nonsense, as well as older games before ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker|The Wind Waker]]'' have no clear evidence of its main characters (Link, Princess Zelda, and [[Ganon]]) having different incarnations or connected events with later titles in their plot, and games between the [[Nintendo Entertainment System]] and [[Nintendo 64]] eras according to developers and official strategy guides at the time were supposed to be a single Link/Zelda having adventures at different points of their lives, prior to the series' many [[History of the Zelda Timeline|retcons]] since the early 2000s.
* Despite the franchise's critical acclaim, a common criticism among players is that the core series lack post-ending gameplay to allow Link to explore a completely peaceful [[Hyrule]] or other lands after defeating the main antagonist, unlike some other series by Nintendo such as ''Mario'' or ''Pokémon''.
* According to Shigeru Miyamoto, contrary to popular belief, ''Super Mario Bros.'' was '''not''' the first planned game being co-developed alongside ''The Legend of Zelda''. However, as the latter was in fact initially created for the Famicom Disk System rather than the traditional NES cartridge unlike the former, he and his team decided to finish up the ''Mario'' game, first, explaining the reason why the original ''Zelda'' title was released one year (or two internationally) after its platformer counterpart.
* ''The Legend of Zelda'' partially serves as the inspiration of the ''[[animalcrossing:Animal_Crossing_(series)|Animal Crossing]]'' franchise, as Miyamoto said this series encouraged interaction among people to share their ideas with each other to find the various hidden secrets, and the latter is based on communication.[[File:OoT blood censorship.jpg|thumb|239x239px|The long-running ''The Legend of Zelda'' franchise normally doesn't have blood in spite of its medieval theme, but ''Ocarina of Time'' is infamously known to break this rule by Nintendo]]
* Although the highly successful ''The Legend of Zelda'' series is known to be one of the more violent or darker franchises directly produced by Nintendo, among the likes of the ''Metroid'' series and possibly ''Star Fox'', due to Link slashing down many monsters with his [[Sword]] during gameplay and some cutscenes implying or even showing death, none featured any clear depictions of blood. However, ''Ocarina of Time'' is the sole exception of this, as the original release of the game indeed shows genuine blood upon the defeat of Ganon in both of his human and beast forms during the [[Final Boss]] battle via the [[Hero of Time]] stabbing him in the mouth for the latter. As graphic violence is usually against Nintendo's policy and to avoid the risk of obtaining a Teen ("T") rating by the [[ESRB]], as it was in fact Everyone ("E"), it was changed to green in subsequent revisions and ports, and its [[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time 3D|3DS remake]] has the Master Sword hitting Ganon's forehead instead. Despite the censorship, ''Ocarina of Time 3D'' has the Everyone 10+ ("E10+") rating and stained red blood is seen in the [[Bottom of the Well]] and [[Shadow Temple]] in all versions of the game. Unsurprisingly, the initial release of ''Ocarina of Time'' having red blood was met with controversy among others within the N64 title.
* Although unconfirmed by Nintendo, the original ''The Legend of Zelda'' is considered by some fans as a "spiritual successor" to the then-revolutionary 1980 [[wikipedia:Atari_2600|Atari VCS]] game ''[https://atari.fandom.com/wiki/Adventure_(2600) Adventure]'' released by Atari, Inc., as many similarities between them in the story and basic gameplay are evident. 
* Namco's challenging 1984 action role-playing maze arcade game ''[https://towerofdruaga.fandom.com/wiki/The_Tower_of_Druaga_(video_game) The Tower of Druaga]'' is seen as an influence for ''The Legend of Zelda'' series.
* ''[[starfox:Star_Fox_Adventures|Star Fox Adventures]]'' from the ''Star Fox'' series has similar gameplay mechanics to ''The Legend of Zelda'' franchise, specifically the Nintendo 64 installments'.
