'''{{LCT|-}}''' is a spin-off of the {{TLoZ|Series}} for the Nintendo [[Wii]] Console. The game ships with the [[Wii Zapper]], and was created to demonstrate the use of the Wii Zapper.<ref>{{Cite Person|quote= ...Because something like "Link’s Crossbow Classroom"  sounds a little funny, don’t you think? Since it is a kind of introduction to FPS games, we were originally thinking of calling it "Introduction to Wii Zapper", but then we decided that people might get that confused with "Introduction to Wii", and we didn’t want that to happen. Also, if we had given it a name like "The Legend of Zelda: Phantom Crossbow", it would have seemed like a grand-scaled sequel in the Zelda Series, and we didn’t want it to be misinterpreted as such. That’s why, in the end, we went with "training" for the title. |name= Shigeru Miyamoto|url= https://web.archive.org/web/20080512131410/http://us.wii.com/iwata_asks/crossbow/vol1_page1.jsp|title= Wii.com - Iwata Asks: Link's Crossbow Training Page 1}}</ref> {{LCT|-}} takes place in the {{Term|TPHD|Hyrule|link}} from {{TP}} at an unknown time, where {{Term|LCT|Link|link}} must pass a series of tests to perfect his marksmanship with the {{Term|LCT|Crossbow|link}}.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= E |publisher= |page= 309}}</ref> {{LCT|-}} also supports a two to four player turn-based [[Multiplayer]] mode, where the Wii Zapper is passed among the players who play individually and compete for the highest score.

==Gameplay==
===Controls===
With the [[Wii Remote]] and the [[Nunchuk]] inside the Wii Zapper casing, the Zapper should be aimed as it would with the Wii Remote. The trigger must be pulled to fire the {{Term|LCT|Crossbow}} (this effectively presses the Wii Remote's B button). Holding down the trigger will cause the {{Term|LCT|Crossbow}} to charge a shot which will fire a {{Term|LCT|Bomb Arrow|link}} (unless {{Term|LCT|Link|link}} has the automatic {{Term|LCT|Crossbow}} power-up, in which case it will rapid-fire arrows). Pressing the A button will pause the game, allowing the player to return to the title screen,<ref>{{Cite|Return to title screen|Pause Menu|LCT}}</ref> return to the {{Term|LCT|Stage Select|link}}<ref>{{Cite|Return to Stage Select|Pause Menu|LCT}}</ref> (Or return to level select depending on if the player is on Score Attack),<ref>{{Cite|Back to Level Select|Pause Menu|LCT}}</ref> to continue in the stage<ref>{{Cite|Continue|Pause Menu|LCT}}</ref> or to adjust the alignment of the motion controls.<ref>{{Cite|Adjust alignment|Pause Menu|LCT}}</ref> In the adjust alignment menu, the player can adjust the motion controls based on their preferences. The player can change the height of the cursor, and how fast the cursor moves when the player moves the Wii Zapper.<ref>{{Cite|Point the Wii Zapper at the center, and make adjustments using the Control Stick.|Adjust Alightment Menu|LCT}}</ref>  The Z button will zoom in, much like {{TP|-}}'s {{Term|TPHD|Hawkeye|link}}. The control stick on the Nunchuk will change function depending on the game mode: In ranger-type {{Plural|LCT|Stage}}, it will move {{Term|LCT|Link}}. In target shooting-type {{Plural|LCT|Stage}} and defender-type {{Plural|LCT|Stage}}, the stick serves no purpose.

===Scoring===
The scores for various targets will change with the {{Term|LCT|Stage}}. Hitting a target will add 1 to a multiplier, so the next successful shot will earn you that target's points times the multiplier. However, a miss will cause the multiplier to reset to 1, and the multiplier will only rise when shooting a {{Term|LCT|Stage}}'s designated target (i.e. the {{Plural|LCT|Moldorm|link}} in {{Term|LCT|Gerudo Moldorm: Defender|link|display=Stage 5-2}}); shooting other certain objects will earn you points, but will not use or increase your multiplier.

===Medals===
At the end of each {{Term|LCT|Level|link}}, {{Term|LCT|Link|link}} will be awarded with a medal. These serve little purpose other than to be a "rank" of sorts. These are awarded based on the overall score for that {{Term|LCT|Stage|link}} (all three {{Plural|LCT|Stage}}' scores added together). An overall score of at least 20,000 points will get {{Term|LCT|Link}} a bronze medal. 40,000 points will earn a silver medal and 60,000 a gold medal. Finally, scoring 80,000 points or higher will reward {{Term|LCT|Link}} with a platinum medal, the highest achievable medal within the game. If {{Term|LCT|Link}} scores below 20,000 points, he will not awarded with any medals. Earning any level of medal in a {{Term|LCT|Stage}} will unlock the next {{Term|LCT|Stage}}, except for {{Term|LCT|Level 1|link}} and {{Term|LCT|Level 2|link}}, as the following {{Plural|LCT|Level}} are already unlocked upon starting the game.

===Game Modes===
====Target Shooting====
[[File:LCT Ordon Target Practice Area 2.jpg|left|thumb|200px|An example of the Target Shooting mode in the {{Term|LCT|Ordon Target Practice|link}} {{Term|LCT|Stage}}]]
In target shooting, the goal is to shoot down targets.<ref>{{Cite manual|quote= Shoot the targets as they appear on-screen. |game= LCT |page= 9}}</ref> Most of the targets are red, and worth 30 points in the bull's-eye. However, there is a rarer gold variation that is worth 150 points in the bull's-eye. During earlier {{Plural|LCT|Stage}}, the targets are stationary, but over time the difficulty increases and the targets begin to move in various ways and speeds.

Most target shooting {{Plural|LCT|Stage}} are split into three 20-second segments, each taking place in a different area of the {{Term|LCT|Stage}}. Once a 20-segment is over, a whistle will blow and the timer will stop while the camera moves to the new area, at which point the timer will start again. Targets and other items can still be shot during this paused-timer session.

All target shooting {{Plural|LCT|Stage}} feature a {{Term|LCT|Scarecrow|link}} in the first 20 seconds. If the {{Term|LCT|Scarecrow}}'s chest is shot 8 times, and then the head is shot, the head will explode for 1000 points. This will cause another {{Term|LCT|Scarecrow}} to appear in the next 20-second segment. If the same tactic is used against this {{Term|LCT|Scarecrow}}, then the player will be taken to an alternate area for the final 20 seconds, wherein there are many more gold targets.

There are 11 Target Shooting {{Plural|LCT|Stage}} in {{LCT|-}}, including {{Term|LCT|Ordon Target Practice|link}}, {{Term|LCT|Goron Target Practice|link}}, {{Term|LCT|Skull Shooting|link}}, {{Term|LCT|Kakariko Target Practice|link}},  {{Term|LCT|Fruit Balloons|link}}, {{Term|LCT|Zora River Target Practice|link}}, {{Term|LCT|Oocca Target Practice|link}}, {{Term|LCT|Ordon Target Practice 2|link}}, {{Term|LCT|Underground Target Practice|link}}, {{Term|LCT|Horseback Target Practice|link}} and {{Term|LCT|Ranch Target Practice|link}}.

====Defender====
[[File:LCT Gerudo Stalfos： Defender.jpg|left|thumb|200px|{{Term|LCT|Link}} defending himself against the attacking {{Plural|LCT|Stalchild|link}} and {{Plural|LCT|Stalfos|link}} in the {{Term|LCT|Gerudo Stalfos: Defender|link}} {{Term|LCT|Stage}}]]
In this game mode the player remains in a fixed position, but may move the camera freely in 360 degrees.<ref>{{Cite manual|quote= Turn to face in a different direction by aiming the Wii Zapper off screen in any direction. |game= LCT |page= 9}}</ref> {{Plural|LCT|Enemy}} will appear all around {{Term|LCT|Link}}, slowly moving closer to him to attack him.<ref>{{Cite manual|quote= Take out hordes of enemies as they attack. |game= LCT |page= 9}}</ref> If {{Term|LCT|Link}} is hit by any {{Plural|LCT|Enemy}} before he can shoot them, he will lose 100 points. Most of these {{Plural|LCT|Stage}} have a green {{Term|LCT|Enemy}} which can be shot to gain 100 automatic crossbow shots, which will be continuously fired for as long as the trigger is held down.

There are 10 defender {{Plural|LCT|Stage}} within {{LCT|-}}, one for every level. These {{Plural|LCT|Stage}} include {{Term|LCT|Gerudo Stalfos: Defender|link}}, {{Term|LCT|Zora River: Defender|link}}, {{Term|LCT|The Great Bridge: Defender|link}}, {{Term|LCT|Hyrule Castle: Defender|link}}, {{Term|LCT|Gerudo Moldorm: Defender|link}}, {{Term|LCT|Bridge of Eldin: Defender|link}}, {{Term|LCT|City in the Sky: Defender|link}}, {{Term|LCT|Snowpeak Ruins: Defender|link}}, {{Term|LCT|Sacred Grove: Defender|link}} and {{Term|LCT|Fossil Stallord Battle|link}}.
{{Clear}}

====Ranger====
[[File:LCT Arbiter's Grounds: Ranger.jpg|left|thumb|200px|{{Term|LCT|Link}} attacking a group of {{Plural|LCT|Bulblin|link}} in the {{Term|LCT|Arbiter's Grounds: Ranger|link}} {{Term|LCT|Stage}}]]
In ranger mode {{Plural|LCT|Stage}}, players have free control and may move across the environment freely by using the nunchuk attachment's analog stick.<ref>{{Cite manual|quote=  Move using the Control Stick, and change your facing by aiming the Wii Zapper off-screen in any direction. |game= LCT |page= 9}}</ref> The goal is to find a number of a certain {{Term|LCT|Enemy}} (from {{Plural|LCT|Bulblin|link}} to {{Plural|LCT|Skulltula|link}}) by moving around the given {{Term|LCT|Stage}}.<ref>{{Cite manual|quote= Seek out your enemies in a number of different settings.  |game= LCT |page= 9}}</ref> Many other objects can be shot in the process, and is in fact necessary sometimes as seen in {{Term|LCT|The Shootout|link|display=Stage 5-3}}, wherein  {{Term|LCT|Link}} must shoot several windows to break them in order to be able to shoot the enemies.<ref>{{Cite manual|quote= Enemies and targets aren’t the only things you can shoot. See what else you can shoot for points |game= LCT |page= 9}}</ref>

There are six ranger {{Plural|LCT|Stage}} in {{LCT|-}}, including {{Term|LCT|Arbiter's Grounds: Ranger|link}}, {{Term|LCT|Skulltula Forest: Ranger|link}}, {{Term|LCT|The Shootout|link}},{{Note|Although the name of this {{Term|LCT|Stage}} doesn't include ": Ranger", this {{Term|LCT|Stage}} still functions as a Ranger {{Term|LCT|Stage}}.}} {{Term|LCT|Snowpeak Ruins: Ranger|link}}, {{Term|LCT|Temple of Time: Ranger|link}} and {{Term|LCT|Darknut Battle|link}}.{{Note|The {{Term|LCT|Darknut Battle}} {{Term|LCT|Stage}} isn't a a full ranger {{Term|LCT|Stage}} with multiple targets and objectives, but it functions as a ranger {{Term|LCT|Stage}} would, with {{Term|LCT|Link}} being allowed to move around the arena.}}

===Boss Fights===
While they are not technically defined as {{Plural|Series|Boss|link}}, there are two fights in later {{Plural|LCT|Stage}} based on battles with {{Plural|TPHD|Boss}} and {{Plural|TPHD|Sub-Boss}} from {{TP|-}}. In {{Term|LCT|Darknut Battle|link|display=Stage 8-3}}, {{Term|LCT|Link}} fights a {{Term|LCT|Darknut|link}} in a battle similar to the one in the {{Term|LCT|Temple of Time|link}}, while in {{Term|LCT|Stage}} {{Term|LCT|Stallord Battle|link|display=Finale-3}} he fights a newly-resurrected {{Term|LCT|Stallord|link}} in the {{Term|LCT|Gerudo Mesa|link}}. The {{Term|LCT|Darknut}} fight plays like a Ranger level in that {{Term|LCT|Link}} can freely move to dodge the {{Term|LCT|Darknut}}'s attacks, but the {{Term|LCT|Fossil Stallord}} fight plays like a Target Shooting round, in that {{Term|LCT|Link}} is immobile and must shoot oncoming enemies and targets to defeat {{Term|LCT|Fossil Stallord}}.


===Multiplayer Mode===
The [[Multiplayer]] mode supports from 2 to 4 players. The players start by choosing the {{Term|LCT|Stage}} they will play.{{Note|They players cannot play a full 3-level {{Term|LCT|Stage}}, but merely one {{Term|LCT|Stage}}, i.e. Stage 1-3, Stage 6-2, etc.}} Each player completes the {{Term|LCT|Stage}} one at a time, passing the Wii Zapper on to the next person as necessary. Once the final player has completed the {{Term|LCT|Stage}}, a star will be awarded to the player who attained the highest score. This can be continued for as long as desired.

Multiplayer scores are not recorded in the game itself, so once multiplayer mode is left, all multiplayer scores will be erased.

===Secrets and Bonuses===
In the midst of all the shooting of targets and enemies, there are objects in most, if not all, {{Plural|LCT|Stage}} that can be shot at for extra points. These include pots and jars, doors, windows, and [[barrel]]s, amongst many other background objects. Most give minor amounts of points, though Orange [[Rupee]]s can give significant amounts if shot as soon as they appear, and strategically shooting scarecrows can cause the last part of Target Shooting {{Plural|LCT|Stage}} to have a significant increase in targets, specifically gold targets. Certain {{Plural|LCT|Stage}} have hidden {{Plural|LCT|Fairy}}, which will grant 1000 points when revealed. Each {{Term|LCT|Stage}} contains a segment of the [[Triforce]], which grants bonus points and can be found through various means, such as defeating every enemy in the {{Term|LCT|Stage}} or hitting all of the targets.

==Game Information==
===Development===
{{LCT|-}} came about as a result of [[Shigeru Miyamoto]]'s love for first-person shooter games.<ref>{{Cite Person|quote= I’ve always been into first person shooter style games...|name= Shigeru Miyamoto|url= https://web.archive.org/web/20080512131410/http://us.wii.com/iwata_asks/crossbow/vol1_page1.jsp|title= Wii.com - Iwata Asks: Link's Crossbow Training Page 1}} Archived from [http://us.wii.com/iwata_asks/crossbow/vol1_page1.jsp the original] on May 12, 2008</ref> Miyamoto and the other staff who had previously worked on {{TP|-}} had wanted to do a sort of "side-story", similar to what was done with {{OoT}} and {{MM}},<ref name="page2ref">[https://web.archive.org/web/20080512131415/http://us.wii.com/iwata_asks/crossbow/vol1_page2.jsp Wii.com - Iwata Asks: Link's Crossbow Training Page 2], Archived from [http://us.wii.com/iwata_asks/crossbow/vol1_page2.jsp the original] on May 12, 2008.</ref> trying to keep the same setting from {{TP|-}} in use for those who enjoyed the game. Eventually, it was decided that trying to come up with a new "epic" story would cause game development to take too much time, and taking inspiration from Miyamoto's aforementioned love for first-person shooters, the idea was eventually adapted to utilize the then-upcoming Wii Zapper for the game in some way.

The development for Link's Crossbow Training began with the idea of creating a side-story game that connected to''Twilight Princess ''and had it's own story''. ''However, partway into development, Miyamoto began to insist that the game actually be a third-person or first-person  shooter game the new Wii utensil, the Wii Zapper. This was a great shock to the development team who had already developed the game's story. Miyamoto also set several guidelines for the game. It could not include an "epic story", there could not be any long movies, and it had to be made up of "stages" that took no longer then a couple of minutes to finish.

[[File:Wii Zapper Prototypes.jpg|frame|left|Prototype Wii Zappers]]
According to Miyamoto, many of the staff working on the game were upset by this news as it essentially destroyed everything they had come up with for the game up to that point,<ref>{{Cite Person|quote= They were kind of shocked. It was like killing all the ideas they were working with until then.|name= Shigeru Miyamoto|url= https://web.archive.org/web/20080512131415/http://us.wii.com/iwata_asks/crossbow/vol1_page2.jsp|title= Wii.com - Iwata Asks: Link's Crossbow Training Page 2}} Archived from [http://us.wii.com/iwata_asks/crossbow/vol1_page2.jsp the original] on May 12, 2008.</ref> However, once a working prototype was created, the prototype was then given to [[Nintendo]] of America to test out, using prototype and even makeshift Wii Zappers. Nintendo of America then gathered a group of "die-hard ''Zelda'' fans" and had them test out the prototype, and it was a success. Further work on the new game then commenced, Nintendo of Japan continued to receive feedback from Nintendo of America so that they could further tweak the final product.

Miyamoto had already mentioned during the beginning of the creation process that there were certain "don'ts", such as no long cutscenes, and levels must be made as short, rapid-fire levels as opposed to long, drawn-out levels. In fact, Miyamoto had even said not to make any bosses, so that they could just focus on making the game fun; however, due to pressure from the team, he allowed them to add one boss, claiming that making one boss excellent is better than having three to focus on, which is what the team originally wanted.<ref name="page2ref" />

The development team struggled with making a shooter game fit in with the ''Zelda'' universe as Link wielding a gun did not feel right. Nevertheless, Miyamoto insisted that it be set in the ''Zelda'' universe, and suggested a "terminator-style" plot in which Link time-warps into the modern world where he finds and begins using an [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AK-47 AK-47] to defend Tokyo City and his own Kingdom from monsters. This idea was shot down immediately, dismissed by everyone else on the team as atrocious. In the end, it was decided to give Link a crossbow, and to flex its capabilities so that it acted more akin to a modern day firearm.<ref>{{Cite Person|quote=  Then we argued that it would’ve been kind of strange for us to give Link a gun, so I proposed a sort of Terminator style story about a time warp from the future, but…|name= Shigeru Miyamoto|url= https://web.archive.org/web/20080512131420/http://us.wii.com/iwata_asks/crossbow/vol1_page3.jsp|title= Wii.com - Iwata Asks: Link's Crossbow Training Page 3}} Archived from [http://us.wii.com/iwata_asks/crossbow/vol1_page3.jsp the original] on May 12, 2008.</ref>
 
The game's original Japanese title was ''Introduction to Wii Zapper,'' but this was changed, both due to being far too simplistic and to avoid confusion with games like ''[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wii_Play Wii Play] (which is called ''"Your First Step to Wii" in Japan). However, The team did not want to call it "The Legend of Zelda: [Subtitle]", to avoid people mistaking the game for a large-scale main series Zelda title. In the end, "Link's Crossbow Training" was decided as a middle-ground.<ref>Iwata, Satoru (2008-05-08). "[http://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/#/wii/crossbow/0/0 Iwata Asks: Link's Crossbow Training". iwataasks.nintendo.com]. Retrieved 2019-04-22.</ref>

===Reception===
The game was received rather well, currently holding an average review score of 70% on GameRankings.com.<ref>[http://www.gamerankings.com/htmlpages2/943270.asp Link's Crossbow Training Reviews]</ref> In fact, reviewers were far more disappointed with the Wii Zapper itself. Regarding the game itself, however, many critics were disappointed with the length of the game, but forgave it given the price. IGN reviewer Matt Casamassina says that "I would have preferred more stages and more options, but for $19.99, I'm a little more forgiving."<ref>[http://wii.ign.com/articles/836/836867p2.html IGN: Link's Crossbow Training Review]</ref> Another widely-criticized portion of the game was the multiplayer aspect, which EuroGamer says that "sadly there's absolutely nothing to it."<ref>[http://www.eurogamer.net/article.php?article_id=88233 Link's Crossbow Training + Wii Zapper Review // Wii // Eurogamer]</ref> Overall, though, most critics were pleased with the game. GameSpot mentions that "As long as you don't expect more than a short but sweet shooting game, you'll be pleased with what {{LCT|-}} has to offer."<ref>[http://www.gamespot.com/wii/action/linkscrossbowtraining/review.html Link's Crossbow Training for Wii Review - Wii Link's Crossbow Training Review]</ref>
