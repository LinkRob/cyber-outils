==Characteristics==
==={{OoT|-}}===
{{Navi
|quote1= {{Color|OoT White|Spike}}<br/>Aim for it when it retracts its spikes!
}}

In their first appearance in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]'', Spikes are metallic balls with dangerous spikes that float around posing potential threats to [[Link]]. They are found within the {{Term|OoT3D|Water Temple|link}}. They appear to be controlled by [[Ganondorf]]'s magic, as the spiked balls themselves home in the hero. In several rooms, these enemies are triggered to release by a [[switch]] causing several of them alongside [[Shell Blade|Shell Blades]] to flow out of a cage at a slow speed, obstructing an entire passage or endangering a room. These Spikes can be subdued with a flick of the [[Hookshot]] where they retract into a more dense, rock-like object; another hit from the Hookshot is enough to destroy them. When on land, they can be killed with the [[Sword]]. Spikes also retract into their rock form when they hit the [[Hero of Time]] or after a short period of time when chasing him.

==={{MM|-}}===
In [[The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask|''Majora's Mask'']], Spikes are not directly enemies, as they instead serve as stationary explosive devices that activate on contact with either Link or another Spike. They can be found suspended from chains in aquatic areas as mines or from ceilings as a swinging aerial hazard, mainly in the [[Pirates' Fortress]], [[Great Bay Temple]], the [[Ancient Castle of Ikana]] and [[Stone Tower Temple]].

==={{TFH|-}}===
{{Plural|TFH|Spike}} are bronze-colored traps that float in water and explode upon making contact in {{TFH}}. They sit stationary, even in the path of a moving water current, forming obstacles that the {{Plural|TFH|Links|link}} must try to avoid while swimming. Any nearby {{Plural|TFH|Spike}} caught in the explosion will also explode, causing a chain reaction. While the {{Term|TFH|Link}} should be cautious of this effect,  they can also use it to their advantage by remotely damaging enemies or activating {{Plural|TFH|Switch|link}}, as exemplified in the first stage of {{Term|TFH|Abyss of Agony|link}}.

{{Plural|TFH|Spike}} can be safely detonated from a distance by shooting {{Plural|TFH|Arrow|link}} at them with the {{Term|TFH|Bow|link}}, or by executing a carefully-placed {{Term|TFH|Spin Attack|link}}. Wearing the {{Term|TFH|Zora Costume|link}} and pressing the A button to Boost while swimming can also destroy a {{Term|TFH|Spike}} without harming {{Term|TFH|Link}}.

In Floor B7 of the {{Term|TFH|Den of Trials|link}}, a single {{Term|TFH|Keeleon|link}} spews silver-colored {{Plural|TFH|Spike}} instead of {{Plural|TFH|Bomb|link}}. As they do not disappear until they are destroyed, these {{Plural|TFH|Spike}} can accumulate over a large area, making the Floor dangerous and difficult to navigate.

== Trivia ==
[[File:OoT Spike blood.png|thumb|Blood of a destroyed Spike in the original release of ''Ocarina of Time'']]

* In ''Ocarina of Time'', when a Spike is defeated underwater, bubbles of red blood can be seen in the initial v1.0/v1.1 NTSC release of the [[Nintendo 64]] title. This is shared with the [[Shell Blade]]. In the PAL version and subsequent releases, it was changed to green by [[Nintendo]] due to [[Controversy in The Legend of Zelda series|controversy]]. Strangely, in ''Majora's Mask'', for the latter enemy, the original red color of the blood is retained.
** The Spike itself having blood is rather odd, as these enemies are not in any way organic.
