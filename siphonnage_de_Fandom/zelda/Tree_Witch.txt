The '''Tree Witch''' is a character featured in {{TCT}}. She is an old woman who lives near a canyon and who proves herself to be of enormous help in {{Term|TCT|Princess Zelda|link}}'s quest to save {{Term|TCT|Link|link}}. 

Zelda can only meet the {{Term|TCT|Tree Witch}} if she decides to climb the rocky path as she travels through the {{Term|TCT|Forest (The Crystal Trap)|link}}. The old woman invites the princess inside her hut and calls Zelda by her name, which surprises the young hero and asks the witch how she knew her name.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote='Well, don't lie there like a thief!' the figure shouts in a high, crackly voice. 'You! Come down and have some lunch.' [...] 'It's the boiled moss scum,' she cackles. 'You'll get used to it, Zelda.' 'How do you know my name?' Zelda says, her hand on the hilt of her dagger.|book= TCT|publisher= |page=60}}</ref> The {{Term|TCT|Tree Witch}} explains that her people have the ability to see most things "in these woods" and explains to the princess that {{Term|TCT|Greenbeard|link}} cautioned Zelda of the {{Term|TCT|Tree Witch}} only because she took some of his prickle-berries last autumn.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote='Oh, I know most things in these woods,' says the old crone with a toothless smile. 'I'm a tree witch, ain't I?' 'Tree witch?' Zelda cries. 'But Greenbeard said--' 'That old wood sprite been bad-mouthing me again?' cackles the witch. [...] 'He's been mad at me since I stole his prickle-berries last autumn. Don't worry. Have some stew.'|book= TCT|publisher= |page=60}}</ref> Afterward, the old witch offers Zelda some stew, making the princess hesitate at her offer and thus asking her how to get out of the canyon. The witch points down the canyon and tells Zelda to follow the creek bed. Before Zelda departs, the old woman gives her a {{Term|TCT|Large Brass Key|link}} and offers her to stay and eat.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote='I--I have to go,' she says quickly. 'Can you tell me the way out of here?' The witch points down the canyon. 'Follow the creek bed and you'll get where you have to go. Oh, you'd better take this.' She fumbles in her town and dirty rags for a moment and pulls out a large brass key. She offers it to Zelda. 'Sure you don't want some stew?' she asks.|book= TCT|publisher= |page=61}}</ref>

If Zelda decides to eat with the tree witch, the old woman will reward her courage with a {{Term|TCT|Witch's Ring|display=ring|link}}, saying that no one had dared to try her stew.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote='I told you!' the witch laughs. 'Course, not many folks have the courage to try some tree witch stew, but them that do deserve a reward. So here's yours.' [...] 'A ring!' Zelda says. It's a thing band of gold, with an oldy shaped pink crystal set in it. Zelda slides it on her finger. Suddenly she doesn't feel tired at all.|book= TCT|publisher= |page=111}}</ref> Before Zelda departs, she thanks the {{Term|TCT|Tree Witch}} for her help and then quickly walks down the path the witch had shown her.

Both items given to Zelda by the {{Term|TCT|Tree Witch}} prove to be useful in obtaining the {{Term|TCT|Magnifying Glass|link}}, which is one of the items necessary to free {{Term|TCT|Link|link}} from the crystal.
