'''''The Legend of Zelda''''' is an animated series loosely based on {{TLoZ}} and {{TAoL}}. Produced by DIC Entertainment and distributed by Viacom Enterprises, the show aired from September 8 to December 1, 1989 with 13 episodes in total.

==General Information==
The ''Zelda'' cartoons were screened every week as part of ''[https://www.mariowiki.com/The_Super_Mario_Bros._Super_Show! The Super Mario Bros. Super Show!].'' While the [[Mario (franchise)|''Mario'']] cartoons were aired from Monday to Thursday a week, the ''Zelda'' animated series was shown only on Fridays.<ref>{{Cite Web|quote=The Zelda series screened once a week as part of The Super Mario Bros. Super Show. Four days a week, they would screen Mario cartoons, but once a week it was a Zelda cartoon.|url=http://www.thesacredrealm.net/main.php?page=zeldacartoon.html|title=Zelda: The Animated Series|site=TheSacredRealm.net}}</ref> Most episodes revolve around [[Ganon]] or his minions trying to obtain the [[Triforce of Wisdom]] and conquer [[Hyrule]] while he himself has the [[Triforce of Power]]. However, [[Link]], accompanied by [[Princess Zelda]] and sometimes [[Spryte]], always ruins Ganon's plans by either zapping him into the [[Evil Jar]] or eliminating the minions that were doing the work for the Prince of Darkness. The episodes each run for approximately 15 minutes.<ref>{{Cite Web|quote=Most of the episodes follow a common plotline: Ganon wants the Triforce and devises numerous schemes to obtain it. He holds the Triforce of Power, and the Triforce of Wisdom is guarded by Link, who lives in the castle. Whoever holds both Triforces will rule the land of Hyrule. Each episode lasted approximately 15 minutes.|url=http://www.thesacredrealm.net/main.php?page=zeldacartoon.html|title=Zelda: The Animated Series|site=TheSacredRealm.net}}</ref>

The animated series was considered unsuccessful and was canceled after 13 episodes along with ''The Super Mario Bros. Super Show!''. Later on, compressed versions of the episodes were shown along with another Nintendo-based cartoon which was also produced by DIC Entertainment: {{CN}}.<ref>{{Cite Web|quote=The cartoon was not regarded as a success, and after one 13-episode series it was dropped. Later on, cut down versions of the episodes were repeated with another Nintendo-based cartoon, Captain N the Gamemaster.|url=http://www.thesacredrealm.net/main.php?page=zeldacartoon.html|title=Zelda: The Animated Series|site=TheSacredRealm.net}}</ref>

==Plot Elements==
[[File:Items (Cold Spells).png|thumb|right|The cartoon's explanation for how Link is able to store many items in his pockets|226x226px]]
Unlike most early video games of the [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|core series]], where Princess Zelda is typically captured by the story's villain and in need of rescue, the animated series portrays the princess in more of a protagonist role, often fighting alongside Link with weapons such as the [[Zelda's Bow|Bow]] or the [[Boomerang]], similar to later titles since ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker|The Wind Waker]]''. 

One of the show's running jokes is Link's repeated attempts at trying to get Zelda to kiss him for saving her life, a parallel to [https://www.mariowiki.com/Mario Mario] does for [https://www.mariowiki.com/Princess_Peach Princess Peach] after rescuing her from [https://www.mariowiki.com/Bowser Bowser] in the [https://www.mariowiki.com/Super_Mario_(franchise) ''Super Mario'' franchise]. Most of the time, the princess rejects the young hero; however, even when she agrees to indulge him,<ref>{{Cite Episode|quote=I'm glad too. And thanks for rescuing me. You still want a kiss?|name=Princess Zelda|show=The Legend of Zelda|episode=4}}</ref><ref>{{Cite Episode|quote=Mmm...shut up and kiss me!|name=Princess Zelda|show=The Legend of Zelda|episode=6}}</ref><ref>{{Cite Episode|quote=We did it! Kiss me!|name=Princess Zelda|show=The Legend of Zelda|episode=9}}</ref> it never occurs: they are interrupted by monsters, [[Spryte]] or any number of unfortunate circumstances such as something making Zelda so mad she no longer wants to kiss Link. This often resulted in Link using his infamous catchphrase: "[[Iconic Quotes from The Legend of Zelda|Well, excuuuuuse me, princess!]]", which would later be regarded as a "beloved inside joke of many gamers."<ref name=ign>{{Cite Web|quote=On the other hand, The Legend of Zelda animated series does have its camp merits. Link's awkward plea, "Well excuuuuuse me, Princess," which later made its way into the infamous CD-i Zelda games, is a beloved inside joke of many gamers. The sound effects incorporated into the show also add some quaintness to the show.|url=http://dvd.ign.com/articles/655/655002p1.html|title=The Legend of Zelda: The Complete Animated Series|site=IGN.com}}</ref>

Being loosely based on the first two ''Zelda'' games on [[Nintendo Entertainment System|NES]], the animated series borrows many elements from those titles. The most noticeable ones are the sound effects when Link shoots [[Sword Beam]]s (here called "Zaps") from his [[Crissword|sword]] or when he retrieves an item from his pouch, which are taken directly from the games.<ref>{{Cite Web|quote=The music is awesome, much of it inspired by the Overworld and Underworld music from the first ''Zelda'' game. Sound effects taken directly from this game are also common, such as when Link defeats enemies or uses items.|url=http://www.thesacredrealm.net/main.php?page=zeldacartoon.html|title=Zelda: The Animated Series|site=TheSacredRealm.net}}</ref> The songs are also inspired by the Overworld and [[Underworld]] music, but they are orchestrated for the show. Although the show seems to mostly reference the original {{TLoZ}}, such as featuring only the Power and Wisdom fragments of the [[Triforce]], the series also takes some aspects from {{TAoL}}, such as the [[Lowder]] Ganon rides or the [[Moby]] seen in "[[Cold Spells]]".

While the games never truly explain how Link can carry so many items in his [[inventory]], the cartoons interpret this by magically shrinking the items when they are placed in pouches, allowing the young hero to return the item back to its normal size whenever he retrieves it from his pocket.

Unlike the main series, Link almost never uses his sword to slash down enemies, preferring to use [[Sword Beam]]s instead. This was likely done to tone down the violence in the cartoon series (as well as [[Nintendo]] has a strict policy against violent content at the time). Likewise, the [[Evil Jar]] is an original contribution to the Zelda mythos. It serves both to explain why enemies respawn after Link kills them, and to soften the violence. Only magical monsters are sent to the Evil Jar when they die, and Link's sword beams cannot kill humans.

==VHS and DVD Re-releases==
[[File:LoZ DVD cover.jpg|thumb|right|The Shout! Factory DVD release]]
In the early 1990s, the series was released on VHS with two episodes per tape, but only four volumes were released in total. Although the animated series had been released in DVD before by {{Wp|Allumination FilmWorks}} in the early 2000s, it did not include all of the episodes. On October 18, 2005, however, {{Wp|Shout! Factory}}, {{Wp|Sony Pictures Home Entertainment}} and NCircle Entertainment released the complete ''Zelda'' animated series with all 13 episodes. It was made of three DVDs which contained bonus content such as interactive games. One of them is the ''Trivia Game'' that consists of several multiple-choice questions about the show and the ''Zelda'' game series.<ref>{{Cite Web|quote=The Legend of Zelda Trivia Game is a fun diversion. There are a series of multiple-choice questions (with one answer obviously wrong) about the show and the game series. Although none of the questions are hard, fanatics of the series probably won't mind proving to themselves that they know their stuff.|url=http://dvd.ign.com/articles/655/655002p2.html|title=The Legend of Zelda: The Complete Animated Series|site=IGN.com}}</ref> The other, called the ''Match Game'', gives the viewer about five seconds to "look at twelve pictures and then [are] asked detailed questions about them."<ref>{{Cite Web|quote=The Match Game on the other hand is way too difficult. You're given about five seconds to look at twelve pictures and then asked detailed questions about them.|url=http://dvd.ign.com/articles/655/655002p2.html|title=The Legend of Zelda: The Complete Animated Series|site=IGN.com}}</ref> The bonus features also had content that demonstrated "easy-to-draw representations of the ''Legend of Zelda'' characters."<ref>{{Cite Web|quote=Finally, there is some DVD-ROM content that features easy-to-draw representations of the Legend of Zelda characters.|url=http://dvd.ign.com/articles/655/655002p2.html|title=The Legend of Zelda: The Complete Animated Series|site=IGN.com}}</ref>

==Reception==
IGN.com comments that "younger viewers will probably feel bored and a bit put-off by the cheesy storylines and bad dialogue" of the series.<ref>{{Cite Web|quote=This set is probably only valuable to video game fans and people who grew up with the show. Younger viewers will probably feel bored and a bit put-off by the cheesy storylines and bad dialogue.|url=http://dvd.ign.com/articles/655/655002p2.html|title=The Legend of Zelda: The Complete Animated Series|site=IGN.com}}</ref> The website also critiques the show for the repetitiveness of the plot,<ref>{{Cite Web|quote=The show's plot isn't its strong point, usually following a very repetitive structure of action sandwiched between comical set-up scenes.http://dvd.ign.com/articles/655/655002p2.html|title=The Legend of Zelda: The Complete Animated Series|site=IGN.com}}</ref> but comments on how the sound effects add "quaintness" to the cartoon.<ref name=ign/> As for the DVD release, complaints were mostly about the overall quality, further saying that the colors were "a bit too bright" in some parts and how a character's hair changes from "frame to frame."<ref>{{Cite Web|quote=During my time with DVD I have seen some fantastic transfers and clean up of material from years ago, unfortunately this is not an example of that. The video here is barely even VHS quality and is riddled with flaws. There is a ton of grain in many scenes as well as some cross coloration and even a washed out looking image. Outdoor and indoor environments are covered with a severe amount of speck and dirt in the picture. Many times there are also soft edges and everything has a slightly blurred appearance.|url=http://www.dvdtalk.com/reviews/18015/legend-of-zelda-complete-animated-series/|title=Legend of Zelda: Complete Animated Series|site=DVDTalk.com}}</ref><ref>{{Cite Web|quote=The first thing you'll notice about this set is its extreme artifact problem. Specks and spots pop up constantly and everywhere. Especially notable in the out-door scenes in which the darker artifacts contrast with the lighter backgrounds, the artifacts really age this show. Colors are also a bit of a problem, looking a bit too bright.|url=http://dvd.ign.com/articles/655/655002p2.html|title=The Legend of Zelda: The Complete Animated Series|site=IGN.com}}</ref> IGN also cites how the DVD release felt rushed due to the episodes always starting with the ''Super Mario Bros. Super Show!'' animated segment.<ref>{{Cite Web|quote=One annoying aspect of this DVD set is that, regardless of whether or not you choose to watch the Legend of Zelda episodes with or without the Mario Brothers live-action segments, the show still always starts with the Super Mario Brothers Super Show animated beginning.|url=http://dvd.ign.com/articles/655/655002p1.html|title=The Legend of Zelda: The Complete Animated Series|site=IGN.com}}</ref>

==Trivia==
*The appearance of [[Spryte]] as [[Link]]'s [[Fairy]] companion predates [[Navi]]'s appearance by almost ten years.
*The running time of all thirteen episodes combined is approximately 255 minutes.
*In the episode "[[Doppelganger]]," Link whistles the iconic "Ground Theme" from the [[Mario (franchise)|''Mario'' series]] - its main theme - while practicing with his [[Sword]] at the beginning of the episode.
*This series is the first media of [[The Legend of Zelda (series)|''The'' ''Legend of Zelda'' franchise]] where Link is shown speaking as opposed to his role in the video games as a "silent protagonist".
*According to the back of a VHS distributed by Tempo Video, Link and [[Princess Zelda]] are both age 15 in the show.<ref>"This animated fantasy-adventure (with a good deal of humour) stars Link - a fifteen-year-old adventurer, and Zelda - a princess of the same age." (''The Legend of Zelda'' (Tempo Video))</ref>
*[[Ganon]]'s appearance in this series is more humanoid than in the [[Nintendo Entertainment System|NES]] games, resembling the [[Magician]] or [[Agahnim]].
*The [[Triforce of Courage]] does not appear nor was mentioned in the TV series. The reason for that is most likely because the [[Triforce]] only had [[Triforce of Power|two]] [[Triforce of Wisdom|pieces]] in the [[The Legend of Zelda|original game]].
*A [https://twitter.com/hour_mario/status/1139184754396356608 set of promotional production art slides] found from the estate sale of a former DiC employee in 2019 suggest that a continuation of the cartoon titled "''The Adventure of Link''" was planned, but ultimately scrapped.
*Link and Princess Zelda appeared in several cameos in the 1990 cartoon series "''Captain N and the Video Game Masters''". In the episode "Having A Ball", it is revealed that the two managed to defeat Ganon and put him into permanent suspended animation. However, their victory does not stop other evil creatures trying to steal the Triforces of Power and Wisdom, similar to ''[[Zelda II: The Adventure of Link|The Adventure of Link]]''. Link and Zelda also had cameos in the episodes [[Quest for the Potion of Power]], [[Once Upon a Time Machine]], The Trojan Dragon, and When Mother Brain Rules.
*The title card of ''The Legend of Zelda'' TV series showing Link holding his magical sword in the air is similar to [https://he-man.fandom.com/wiki/He-Man He-Man] wielding the [https://he-man.fandom.com/wiki/Power_Sword Power Sword] from the intro of the 1983 ''[https://he-man.fandom.com/wiki/He-Man_and_the_Masters_of_the_Universe He-Man and the Masters of the Universe]'' series, which itself is among the most popular American animated shows at the time.
