{{Term/Store|Dera Zol||Series, FS}} is the boss of the {{Term|FS|Talus Cave|link}} in {{FS}}.<ref>{{Cite Book|quote= |book= E |publisher= |page= 174}}</ref>

==Characteristics==
{{Term|FS|Dera Zol}} is a large slug-like creature that attacks by jumping up and down, primarily to crush the {{Plural|FS|Links|link}} should they move underneath its body, but also to shake off icicles that will injure them should one land on them. To defeat the beast, the {{Plural|FS|Links}} must repeatedly charge into it using the {{Term|FS|Pegasus Boots|link}} to bump it into the ice wall, freezing it.<ref>{{Cite|Dash into the ice wall!|N/A|FS}}</ref> At this point the heroes can slash at it with their {{Plural|FS|Sword}} to cause damage. On higher difficulties, {{Term|FS|Dera Zol}} will begin creating illusions of itself after taking damage. The true body can be identified by its shadow.

After hacking away at the frozen {{Term|FS|Zol|link}} enough, the ice will shatter to instead reveal a smaller creature similar to an {{Term|FS|Octorok|link}}. This form then blows up four balloon-like spheres to surround it. Striking any of these spheres will electrify the heroes, except the orb that matches his own color. However, each {{Term|FS|Links}} can only see the color of one orb that does not correspond to his tunic, meaning the {{Plural|FS|Links}} must work together to deduce which sphere they can strike.<ref>{{Cite|Whose color do you see? Share it with the others!|N/A|FS}}</ref> After dealing this form enough damage, {{Term|FS|Dera Zol}} will explode.

==Trivia==
*{{Term|FS|Dera Zol}}, as the name and slime-like appearance suggests, seems to be related to a {{Term|FS|Zol|link}}. Despite this, its second form bears a closer resemblance to an {{Term|FS|Octorok|link}}.
*"{{exp|デラ|Dera}}" appears to be short for "{{exp|どえらい|Doerai}}", a Japanese word meaning "immense" or "awesome".
*The way in which Dera Zol is found frozen solid before the boss battle officially begins is similar to [[Goht]] from {{MM}} and the [[Big Octorok]] from {{TMC}}.
