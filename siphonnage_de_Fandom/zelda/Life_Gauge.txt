==Uses==
{{Incomplete|type=listing|reason=Missing totals for Life Gauge Hearts for some games.}}
[[File:TLoZ Life Gauge.jpg|thumb|263x263px|The Life Gauge HUD as seen from ''[[The Legend of Zelda]]'' (Japanese [[Family Computer Disk System|FDS]] version)]]
Represented in the form of hearts for every game of the longtime series with exception of ''[[Zelda II: The Adventure of Link|The Adventure of Link]]'', where it is instead represented by square segments, the Life Gauge measures [[Link]]'s current amount of health remaining. It's usually seen at the top-left or top-right of the screen from the game's HUD. Whenever Link himself takes damage, either by an [[enemy]] attack, a hazard, or by falling into bottomless pits or from a high altitude, the young hero will lose a certain amount of [[Heart Container|Heart Containers]] or a portion of one Heart equal to the damage taken. In the earliest games, the Life Gauge's Hearts are in fractions of two. Starting from {{OoT}}, they are in fractions of four. Anytime the Life Gauge falls below a certain point - usually less than two Hearts, a warning beep continuously sounds as an indication of low health, and Link will become fatigued due to his injuries if he stands still in the 3D games until he recovers. If all of the Heart Containers are completely depleted, Link dies and the [[Game Over|game is over]], therefore failing the mission on the player. The hero's Life Gauge can be replenished by picking up [[Heart|recovery hearts]]. The Gauge can also be replenished by drinking recovery [[Potion]]s, using a [[Fairy]], visiting a [[Great Fairy]], or by resting while [[sitting]] or sleeping. In many games, Link can also instantly be revived and some or all of his Hearts restored provided he has a [[Bottle]]d [[Fairy]] or in the case of ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening|Link's Awakening]]'', [[Secret Medicine]] with the effect to prevent a Game Over. Normally, when Link resumes his adventure after he is defeated if the player chooses, only three Hearts of the meter are recovered.

In most multiplayer games, such as {{FS}} and {{FSA}}, the {{Plural|Series|Links|link}} each have their own Life Gauge, and Game Over occurs when all players are defeated. The Life Gauge is shared in single-player campaigns, as well as in {{TFH}}, wherein the {{Plural|Series|Links}} all share the damage taken.

In other games where Link is not the playable protagonist, specifically ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Echoes of Wisdom|Echoes of Wisdom]]'', the Japan-exclusive [[Satellaview]] games, or ''[[Hyrule Warriors]]'', the Life Gauge is used for [[Princess Zelda]], the [[Player|BS-X Player]], and the [[Characters in Hyrule Warriors|Warriors]], respectively, in which it serves the same purpose.

In the vast majority of games, Link's Life Gauge starts with three Hearts. The Life Gauge can usually be increased by obtaining [[Heart Container]]s after defeating a [[boss]] or gaining four [[Piece of Heart|Pieces of Heart]], which add an additional Heart to the Life Gauge for every Heart Container acquired. In some cases, Link can also temporarily increase his maximum health through the use of items or equipment. The maximum total of Hearts the Life Gauge varies by game-to-game; Normally, the highest amount of Hearts Link or other protagonists can gain is 16 or 20, but some games have less or more. ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild|Breath of the Wild]]'', ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom|Tears of the Kingdom]]'', and especially ''Hyrule Warriors'', however, are known to have an extreme amount of Heart Containers available, reaching as many as 60. On the other hand, [[The Legend of Zelda Game Watch|''The Legend of Zelda'' Game Watch]] game has no more than 5.

Often, it is also possible for Link to gain a {{Term|Series|Defensive Power|link}} upgrade against enemy attacks, thereby taking less damage than normal. This may either take the form of wearing certain equipment, using items, or by receiving permanent upgrades. On the other hand, in certain games with more difficult gameplay options such as ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time Master Quest|Master Quest]]'' or [[Hero Mode]], Link will take more damage than normal.
[[File:HWDE Link Weak Point Smash.png|left|thumb|233x233px|Gameplay of [[Hyrule Warriors: Definitive Edition|''Hyrule Warriors: Definitive Edition'']], where foes have their own health visible to the player other than the character they're controlling such as Link]]
Although normally associated with Link or other playable protagonists, enemies, including bosses, can have their own Life Gauge shown to the player in a few games, most prominently ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker|The Wind Waker]]'' through the [[Hero's Charm]], ''Hyrule Warriors'', ''Breath of the Wild'', and ''Tears of the Kingdom''. In these cases, however, rather than Hearts, their health is represented as a horizontal bar, similar to as seen in fighting games such as Namco's ''[[SoulCalibur II]]''.

Another important meter in gameplay, the [[Magic Meter]], is often seen beside or below the Life Gauge on the HUD from ''The Adventure of Link'' to ''The Wind Waker''. Ironically, the Life Gauge in ''SoulCalibur II'' closely resembles such a meter, as its bar is shown as green.

There are few cases where Link's Life Gauge can be instantly depleted regardless of any special defenses. These include talking to the [[Shopkeeper]] after [[Thievery|stealing]] an item from a [[shop]] in ''Link's Awakening'', drowning (except for ''The Wind Waker'') or burning from hot places where certain protection is required, or falling from an extreme height in ''Breath of the Wild'' and ''Tears of the Kingdom''.

==={{TLoZ|-}}===
Link's Life Gauge is known as Life or Life Hearts in {{TLoZ}}.<ref name="TLoZ"/><ref name="Life Hearts"/> Link's Life can be extended to a total of 16 Hearts. When Link is full on Life Hearts, he can throw his {{Term|TLoZ|Sword|link}}. In the [[Second Quest]], [[Old Man|Old Men]] encountered in certain [[Dungeon]]s will request that Link either pay them an amount of [[Rupee]]s or give up one of his Life Hearts in order to pass. If Link chooses to sacrifice a Heart, it can never be recovered; there is a [[Glitches in The Legend of Zelda|glitch]] where touching the heart when the hero have 3 hearts will display his health to 0 while still retaining 3 hearts.

Link can enhance his defense to enemy attacks by obtaining the [[Blue Ring]] and the [[Red Ring]], which reduce damage taken by one quarter and one half respectively.

==={{TAoL|-}}===
In {{TAoL}}, Life Points are represented as a meter marked in square segments as opposed to Hearts.<ref name="Life Points"/> Link's Life Points start with 4 segments and can be extended to a total of 8 segments.

When Link is at full health, he can shoot Sword Beams from his Sword.

Whenever Link loses all of his Life Points, he loses an [[Link Doll|Extra Life]] and restarts at the entrance of the area he was last in. However, if Link loses all of his Lives and perishes, he will be forced from restart from the [[North Castle]]. The exception to this is if Link loses all his Lives while in the [[Great Palace]], where he will restart from the Palace entrance instead.

Link can enhance his defense to enemy attacks by increasing his Life whenever he [[Experience|Levels up]]; this is separate from extending the meter, which can only be extended by collecting Heart Containers. Link's Life can be leveled up a total of eight times.

==={{ALttP|-}}===
The Life Gauge,<ref>{{Cite manual|quote=Life Gauge: This decreases when you are wounded. If it reaches zero, the game ends.|game=ALttP&FS|page=16}}</ref> originally referred to as Heart Containers or Life,<ref name="Heart Containers"/> can be extended to a total of 20 Hearts in {{ALttP}}.

When Link is at full health, he can shoot Sword Beams with the [[Master Sword]], [[Tempered Sword]], and [[Golden Sword]].

Link can enhance his defense to enemy attacks by obtaining the [[Blue Mail|Blue]] and [[Red Mail]].

==={{LA|-}}===
The Heart Meter, originally referred to as Heart Containers,<ref>{{Cite manual|quote=Heart Containers This displays your life force in Heart Containers. Each time you get hurt, your Heart Containers will empty. If all your Heart Containers empty, your quest ends.|game=LADX|page=12}}</ref> can be extended to a total of 14 Hearts in {{LA}}.

When Link is at full health, he can shoot Sword Beams with the [[Level 2 Sword]].

Link can temporarily enhance his defense to enemy attacks by picking up a [[Guardian Acorn]], which reduces damage taken by half. In {{LA|DX}}, Link can permanently enhance his defense by selecting the [[Blue Clothes]] after clearing the [[Color Dungeon]], which holds the same effect as a Guardian Acorn.

==={{OoT|-}}===
The Life Gauge,<ref name="Life Gauge"/> originally referred to as Life Energy,<ref>{{Cite manual|quote=Life Energy|game=OoT|page=12}}</ref> can be extended to a total of 20 Hearts in {{OoT}}. This is the first ''Zelda'' game where Link can take a quarter of a Heart of damage.

In {{OoT3D}}, playing [[Second Quest#Ocarina of Time 3D|Master Quest]] mode will make Link take twice as much damage.

Link can enhance his defense to enemy attacks by receiving an upgrade from the [[Great Fairy#Ocarina of Time|Great Fairy of Courage]] found in the [[Great Fairy Fountain|Fountain]] near [[Ganon's Castle]]. The upgrade creates a white outline around the Hearts in Link's Life Gauge, signifying the enhancement.

[[Nayru's Love]] can be used to temporarily negate all damage taken.

==={{MM|-}}===
The Life Energy meter can be extended to a total of 20 Hearts in {{MM}}.<ref name="Life Energy"/>

Link can enhance his defense to enemy attacks by receiving an upgrade from the [[Great Fairy#Majora's Mask|Great Fairy of Courage]] in the [[Great Bay Coast]]. The Great Fairy will enhance his defense after Link brings back her scattered [[Stray Fairy|Stray Fairies]] from the [[Great Bay Temple]]. Identically to {{OoT|-}}, this upgrade creates a white outline around Link's Hearts to signify the enhancement.

==={{OoS|-}} and {{OoA|-}}===
In a non-[[Linked Game]] of {{OoS}} and {{OoA}}, the Life Gauge,<ref>{{Cite manual|quote=The hearts represent Link's life gauge. When you take damage it will decrease and if it runs out your game will be over.|game=OoS|version= Virtual Console|page=8}}</ref> originally referred to as the Life Energy Meter,<ref>{{Cite manual|quote=Your life energy is represented by Hearts. The Life Energy Meter decreases as you take damage. When your life energy is all gone, your game will be over.|game=OoS|page=20}}</ref> starts with 3 Hearts. In a Linked Game, it starts with 4 Hearts. The Life Gauge can be extended to a total of 14 Hearts in a non-Linked Game, a total of 15 Hearts in a Linked Game, and a maximum total of 16 Hearts in a Linked Game while exchanging [[Secret]]s.

When Link is at full health, he can shoot Sword Beams with the [[Noble Sword]] or Master Sword. While wearing the [[Light Ring L-1]] or [[Light Ring L-2]], Link can shoot Sword Beams up to two and three Hearts missing, respectively.

Link can enhance his defense to enemy attacks by wearing certain [[Magic Rings]]. Some Rings will reduce damage taken overall, while others will reduce or negate damage taken only from specific attacks or hazards. The [[Protection Ring]] ensures that all damage taken is always equal to one Heart. Conversely, the [[Cursed Ring]] increases damage to attacks.

==={{FS|-}}===
In {{FS}}, the {{Plural|FS|Links|link}} each have their own individual Life Gauge, and start with 6 Hearts each. In a single-player game in {{FSAE}}, the two {{Plural|FS|Links}} each share a Life Gauge. The Life Gauge can be temporarily extended by collecting Heart Containers, however, this extension lasts only for the duration of the Stage.

When all four {{Plural|FS|Links}} are at full health, the Stage enters [[Rupee Fever]], where the value of Rupees are doubled for as long as the {{Plural|FS|Links}} maintain their health. This occurrence does not happen in a single-player game. If the Master Sword is obtained, the {{Plural|FS|Links}} will shoot Sword Beams when he is at full health.

Whenever a {{Term|FS|Links}} loses all of his Hearts in his Life Gauge, a Rupee penalty is paid in order to revive the fallen {{Term|FS|Links}}. The cost will increase each time a {{Term|FS|Links}} is revived. If the {{Plural|FS|Links}} do not have enough Rupees to revive the fallen {{Term|FS|Links}}, the Stage ends.

A {{Term|FS|Links}} can temporarily enhance his defense to enemy attacks by picking up [[Armor Seed]]s, which reduce damage taken by half. This effect lasts only for the duration of the Stage. 

==={{TWW|-}}===
The Life Gauge can be extended to a total of 20 Hearts in {{TWW}}.

In {{TWWHD}}, enabling [[Hero Mode]] will make Link take double damage.

The [[Magic Armor]] can be used to temporarily negate all damage taken, at the cost of draining Link's [[Magic Meter]], or his Rupees whenever he is struck in {{TWWHD|-}}.

==={{FSA|-}}===
In a single-player game of {{FSA}}, the {{Plural|FSA|Links}} share a single Heart Meter and start with 4 Hearts.<ref name="Heart Meter"/> In a multiplayer game, the {{Plural|FSA|Links}} each have their own individual Heart Meter. The Heart Meter can be temporarily extended by collecting Heart Containers, however, this extension lasts only for the duration of the Stage.

Whenever a {{Term|FSA|Links}} loses all of his Hearts in his Heart Meter, a [[Force Fairy]] is used to revive him. If the {{Plural|FSA|Links}} do not have any Force Fairies to revive a fallen {{Term|FSA|Links}}, the Stage ends.

A {{Term|FSA|Links}} can temporarily enhance his defense to enemy attacks by finding a [[Blue Bracelet]], which reduces damage taken by half. This effect lasts only for the duration of the Stage.

==={{TMC|-}}=== 
The Life Gauge,<ref>{{Cite manual|quote= The filled hearts in your life gauge will decrease when you take damage from an enemy. When all of your hearts are empty, it is game over. |game= TMC |version= Virtual Console |page= 10}}</ref> originally referred to as the Heart Meter,<ref>{{Cite manual|quote= Heart Meter Decreases whenever you take damage. Once it runs out, the game is over. |game=TMC |page= 10}}</ref> can be extended to a total of 20 Hearts in {{TMC}}.

Link can temporarily enhance his defense to enemy attacks by using the [[Charm (The Minish Cap)|Blue]] or [[Charm (The Minish Cap)|Green Charm]].

==={{TP|-}}===
The Life Gauge can be extended to a total of 20 Hearts in {{TP}}.

In {{TPHD}}, enabling Hero Mode will make Link take double damage.

In {{TPHD|-}}, tapping certain [[amiibo]] will have an effect on Link's Life Gauge. Upon completing a section of the [[Cave of Shadows]], the [[Wolf Link]] amiibo may be tapped to save a checkpoint of the amount of Hearts Link has preserved. The saved amount can then be used to restore Link's Life Gauge to that amount by scanning the amiibo while in the Cave of Shadows, and is also used to determine the number of Hearts Wolf Link will have when scanned into {{BotW}}. Scanning [[Ganondorf]]'s amiibo will make Link take twice as much damage, and will turn his Life Gauge blue to signify the change. Scanning the amiibo while in Hero Mode quadruples the damage taken.

The [[Magic Armor]] can be worn to negate all damage taken, at the cost of draining Link's Rupees. Conversely, wearing the [[Zora Armor]] increases damage to fire and ice-based attacks.

==={{PH|-}}===
The Life Gauge can be extended to a total of 16 Hearts in {{PH}}.

In addition to Link's Life Gauge, the [[SS Linebeck]] also has its own Life Gauge to measure its endurance and stability. Its Life Gauge is represented in Green Hearts. These Hearts will deplete whenever the ship is attacked or crashes into an obstacle. When the Life Gauge is fully depleted, the ship is destroyed, resulting in a Game Over. The ship's Life Gauge can be replenished by finding Green Hearts in the overworld, either by defeating enemies or destroying objects, or can be fully replenished by disembarking at an island. Initially, the default ship starts with four Hearts, and can be increased by assembling matching [[Ship Parts]].

In the [[Goron Temple]], Link shares his Life Gauge with [[Gongoron]].

Link can enhance his defense to enemy attacks if he is accompanied by [[Neri]] and has given her [[Wisdom Gem]]s.

==={{ST|-}}===
The Life Gauge can be extended to a total of 16 Hearts in {{ST}}.

Identically to the SS Linebeck in {{PH|-}}, the [[Spirit Train]] also has its own Life Gauge represented in Green Hearts. Its Hearts will deplete whenever the train is attacked or crashes. The Train becomes destroyed if its Life Gauge is fully depleted. It can be replenished by finding Green Hearts in the overworld or fully recovered by disembarking at a station. The default train starts with four Hearts, and can be increased by assembling matching [[Train Cars]].

Link can shoot Sword Beams while at full health if he has obtained the [[Swordsman's Scroll #1]] and the [[Lokomo Sword]].

==={{SS|-}}===
The Life Gauge starts with 6 Hearts in {{SS}}. The Life Gauge can be extended to 18 Hearts by collecting Heart Containers and all [[Pieces of Heart]]s. It can also be extended by an additional Heart by wearing [[Life Medal]]s, which altogether can extend the Life Gauge to a maximum 20 Hearts.

Playing in Hero Mode will make Link take double damage.

Link can temporarily enhance his defense to enemy attacks by drinking a [[Guardian Potion]]. Drinking a [[Guardian Potion +]] will temporarily negate all damage taken for three minutes.

When Link takes damage, hearts are displayed around Link indicating the amount of health lost.

==={{ALBW|-}}===
The Life Gauge can be extended to a total of 20 Hearts in {{ALBW}}.

Playing in Hero Mode will make Link take double damage.

When Link is at full health, he can shoot Sword Beams with the Master Sword and its upgraded forms.

Drinking a [[Yellow Potion]] will temporarily negate all damage taken.

==={{TFH|-}}===
In {{TFH}}, the {{Plural|TFH|Links}} (as well as the {{Plural|TFH|Doppel|link}} in a single-player game) all share a single {{Term|TFH|Life Gauge}} and start with 9 {{Plural|TFH|Heart}}. The length of the {{Term|TFH|Life Gauge}} does not permanently change, but it can be extended whenever the {{Plural|TFH|Links}} wear certain {{Plural|TFH|Outfit|link}}, including the {{Term|TFH|Jack of Hearts|link}}, the {{Term|TFH|Queen of Hearts|link}}, or the {{Term|TFH|Lady's Ensemble|link}}. After {{Term|TFH|Link}} acquires 30 {{Plural|TFH|Hero Point|link}}, wearing the {{Term|TFH|Bear Minimum|link}} extends the {{Term|TFH|Life Gauge}} by two {{Plural|TFH|Heart}}.

If any of the {{Plural|TFH|Links}} wear the {{Term|TFH|Bear Minimum}} with fewer than 30 {{Plural|TFH|Hero Point}}, one {{Term|TFH|Heart}} will be subtracted from the {{Term|TFH|Life Gauge}}. Certain [[Levels in Tri Force Heroes|Levels]] have a {{Term|TFH|Drablands Challenge|link}} called "Fewer Heart Containers!", which subtracts five {{Plural|TFH|Heart}} from the {{Term|TFH|Life Gauge}}.

When {{Term|TFH|Link}} is at full health, he can shoot {{Plural|TFH|Sword Beam|link}} if he is wearing the {{Term|TFH|Sword Suit|link}}, the {{Term|TFH|Sword Master Suit|link}}, or the {{Term|TFH|Fierce Deity Armor|link}}. The {{Term|TFH|Fierce Deity Armor}} allows him shoot {{Plural|TFH|Sword Beam}} even without full health by performing a {{Term|TFH|Spin Attack|link}}.

Whenever the {{Term|TFH|Life Gauge}} is fully depleted, the {{Term|TFH|Links}} who last took damage will faint. A {{Term|TFH|Fairy|link}} is used to revive him, and the {{Plural|TFH|Links}} will restart the current {{Term|TFH|Stage}} of the {{Term|TFH|Level}} they are in. If the {{Plural|TFH|Links}} do not have any more {{Plural|TFH|Fairy}} to revive themselves, they receive a [[wikipedia:Asystole|Game Over]]. When the {{Term|TFH|Life Gauge}} is down to three {{Plural|TFH|Heart}} or less, warning messages such as "Danger!" and "You're low on hearts!" will occasionally scroll across the screen.

==={{BotW|-}}===
In {{BotW}}, the Life Gauge can be temporarily extended by consuming certain [[Food]]s that grant Link yellow temporary Hearts. These Hearts are lost when Link takes damage and cannot be recovered. The {{Term|BotW|Life Gauge}} starts with three Hearts and can be extended by one by trading four {{Plural|BotW|Spirit Orb|link}}; the {{Term|BotW|Life Gauge}} can hold a maximum of 30 hearts (equivalent to a maximum of 120 hit points).

[[Wolf Link]], when scanned into {{BotW|-}} with the Wolf Link [[amiibo]], has his own Life Gauge. The amount of Hearts in his Life Gauge can be increased by scanning the amiibo in {{TPHD}} after completing a section of the Cave of Shadows, which saves a checkpoint of Link's number of preserved Hearts that determine Wolf Link's health in {{BotW|-}}. When all of Wolf Link's Hearts are depleted, he will vanish and cannot be scanned into the game until the following real-world day.

Damage numbers are shown numerically in {{BotW|-}}. Each point of damage deals a quarter of a Heart to Link. For example, an attack with a power of 4 will damage Link by one full Heart. However, Link can increase his defense by wearing [[Armor]]. Each piece of Armor adds to Link's defense rating, which is subtracted from the strength value of physical attacks. Nevertheless, Link will take at least a quarter of a Heart of damage from any attack, and elemental damage such as fire and electricity cannot be reduced without special resistances.

Some enemies in {{BotW|-}} are quite powerful, and may do more damage in one hit than Link has health. As long as Link's Life Gauge is full, he is protected from any attack that would instantly kill him, and will instead be left alive with a quarter Heart remaining. However, this has limitations: if the damage is more than five Hearts above Link's maximum health, he will still fall. Many powerful attacks also knock Link down, potentially causing him to roll downhill and lose his last quarter Heart. This protection does not exist in [[Master Mode]].

Damages are displayed briefly as white hearts as Link loses health.

==={{TotK|-}}===
The Life Gauge system from previous games is mostly retained. The introduction showed Link having the previous 30 heart maximum until Ganondorf's resurrection saps Link's hearts until he has 3 left. It should be noted that in the introduction, each row of hearts is 15 hearts, once Link have 21 or more hearts, the Life Gauge now have rows of 20.

Link's Life Gauge starts with three hearts and he can obtain a maximum of forty hearts. This allows Link to have a maximum of 160 hit points. The Life Gauge can be extended by trading four Lights of Blessing to a goddess statue. Armor and damage behave largely the same as in {{BotW}}.

The Life Gauge can also have its maximum temporarily reduced by damage caused by [[Gloom]] (as displayed as the last N hearts being cracked greyed out hearts), found primarily in the Depths. In this case, receiving any form of Gloom damage will reduce the Life Gauge's maximum by a certain number of hearts. Sections of hearts cannot be reduced, only full hearts (meaning max hearts reduction cannot be reduced by quarters). Damage from Gloom can be reversed by eating certain foods (such as [[Gloom|Sundelions]]), touching a Lightroot, or emerging from the [[The Depths|Depths]]. It should be noted that damage with gloom will only reduce the max hearts, it will not reduce the current amount of health Link has unless if the gloom reaches at or below Link's current number of hearts filled. Therefore it is better to use healing items that merely restore gloom-damaged hearts if only the type of damage possible from enemies and hazards is gloom.

The final battle with [[The Demon King|Demon King Ganondorf]] and [[Demon Dragon]] have unique Gloom attacks (dark-red orbs, sparks or stones on the ground) that will permanently remove a heart container (an animation will play with the bottom and rightmost heart shattering and disappearing completely), so normal remedies for Gloom will not work. Fortunately, you cannot save your game from that point. Additionally, upon defeating Demon King Ganondorf, all hearts permanently erased are restored during the Demon Dragon battle.

==={{HW|-}}===

In {{HW}}, each playable Warrior possess their own '''Health''' gauge.<ref>{{Cite manual|quote=Health|game=HW|page=4}}</ref> At Level 1, Warriors begin with 10 Hearts, however, Warriors unlocked at higher Levels may start with more than 10 Hearts. Their Health can be extended by collecting Heart Containers and Pieces of Heart hidden in Scenarios, but can also gradually be increased as the character [[Experience|Levels up]]. Warriors who do not have any Heart Containers or Pieces of Heart to find can only extend their Health through gaining Levels.

The Health gauge for each warrior can be extended to a maximum of 60 Hearts. The maximum can only be reached after [[Hyrule Warriors#Updates|updating]] to Version 1.6.0, which increased the maximum Level to 255 in both {{HW|-}} and {{HW|L}}. However, without purchasing any [[Hyrule Warriors#DLC Packs|DLC Packs]], only [[Medli]] will be able to reach the maximum in {{HW|-}}. In {{HW|L|-}}, no Warriors can extend their Health gauge to the maximum without purchasing a DLC Pack. Any Character obtained as part of a DLC Pack will be able to extend their Health gauge to 60 Hearts without purchasing any additional DLC Packs.

Allied and opposing characters alike also carry their own visible Health gauge, displayed as a colored gauge above their heads that matches the color of their [[Forces|Force]]. Their Health totals can also be found in the Battlefield Info menu. Allied characters whose Health are low can be recovered by touching the Rescue Area circle that forms around them.

If Link uses the Master Sword at full Health, all of his Combo Attacks will shoot Sword Beams.

Warriors will take increased damage when using Weapons with the Special [[Skill]]s Regen and Sturdy Feet.

During "All attacks are devastating!" Battles or Battles following the "Don't Get Hit!" ''Master Quest'' or Great Sea [[Rule]] in [[Adventure Mode]], the player Warrior will take devastating damage if hit. This will reduce the Warrior's Health to one Quarter-Heart unless they already had one Quarter-Heart, in which case the Health gauge will fully deplete. In {{HW|L|-}}, [[Companion Fairy|Companion Fairies]] with the Glass Cannon [[Rental Skill]] can also cause all damage a Warrior takes to become devastating.

The player Warrior's Health gauge cannot fully deplete unless they take damage when they have a Quarter-Heart of Health. If the player Warrior takes enough damage that their Health gauge should fully deplete, they will instead be reduced to a Quarter-Heart. Whenever the player Warrior's Health is fully depleted, they collapse. In {{HW|-}}, the Battle is lost but in {{HW|L|-}}, if the Warrior is battling with a Companion Fairy with the Hard Worker Rental Skill, the Skill will automatically be used and revive the Warrior with 28 and a quarter Hearts. If the Warrior's Health gauge fully depletes again or the Warrior didn't battle with a Companion Fairy with the Hard Worker Rental Skill, the Battle is lost. Depending on a Battle's circumstances, allied characters who lose all of their Health may either retreat from the battlefield without dire consequence, or may also cause the Battle to result in a defeat.

== Trivia ==

* Like health bars, hearts used as a measurement of the player character or NPC's hit points are common in action-adventure or platformer games.
* In the [[Nintendo 64]] games, when [[Link]] gains the increased [[Defense|defensive]] powers by the [[Great Fairy of Courage]], the Life Gauge has a while outline, making it strongly resemble how it appears in ''[[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past|A Link to the Past]]'', ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Four Swords|Four Swords]]'', ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Minish Cap|The Minish Cap]]'', and ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Phantom Hourglass|Phantom Hourglass]]''.
* In a few 3D games, it is possible to pause the game when Link's health is completely depleted, most often when he is still falling from the air.
