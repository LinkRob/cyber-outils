'''''[[smashwiki:Super_Smash_Bros._(series)|Super Smash Bros.]]''''' (大乱闘スマッシュブラザーズ, ''Dairantō Smash Brothers'') is a series of crossover fighting games released by [[Nintendo]] and directed by {{ssb|Masahiro Sakurai}}. The series debuted on the [[Nintendo 64]] in 1999 with the titular ''[[Super Smash Bros.]]'' title, and has appeared in every Nintendo home console since, with {{SSB4|3DS}} being the first to appear in a handheld console. The {{SSB|-}} series features iconic Nintendo characters, brawling across stages based on a majority of their franchises - classic or modern, with {{SSBB}} introducing third party characters and stages as well. The series also features items and collectables based on many Nintendo and third party franchises, such as [[Trophy (Super Smash Bros.)|Trophies]] and [[Sticker]]s. The latest installment so far is ''[[Super Smash Bros. Ultimate]]'' for the [[Nintendo Switch]], released in 2018.

As one of Nintendo's flagship spin-off series, several ''[[The Legend of Zelda (series)|The Legend of Zelda]]'' characters, items, and stages have appeared across all the entries of the series.

==Games==
==={{SSB|-}}===
{{Main|Super Smash Bros.}}
{{SSB|-}} was released for the Nintendo 64 in 1999. It introduced [[Link]] as a playable character, [[Hyrule Castle#Super Smash Bros.|Hyrule Castle]] as a stage, and [[Heart Container]]s as recovery items. Although rather basic in comparison to later installments, the concept of the original game was a novelty at the time. Upon the release of ''Super Smash Bros. Melee'' and future games, the first title was initially forgotten by fans, but in recent years it now regained a cult-following due to competition events. It has sold 5.55 million copies.

==={{SSBM|-}}===
{{Main|Super Smash Bros. Melee}}
{{SSBM|-}} was released for the [[Nintendo GameCube]] in 2001. It introduced [[Young Link]], [[Princess Zelda]]/[[Sheik]], and [[Ganondorf]] as playable characters alongside Link. The game also features the [[Great Bay#Super Smash Bros. Melee|Great Bay]] and a [[Hyrule: Temple|Temple]] as stages. {{SSBM|-}} also introduced Trophies, with 30 of them based on ''The Legend of Zelda'' series, and the [[Bunny Hood]] as an item. This installment is widely seen as a fan-favorite among veterans, due to the significant improvements over the first game and much of the general gameplay and graphics are considered to age well. It has sold 7.41 million copies, making it the best-selling title of the console.

==={{SSBB|-}}===
{{Main|Super Smash Bros. Brawl}}
{{SSBB|-}} was released for the [[Wii|Nintendo Wii]] in 2008. It introduces [[Toon Link]] as a playable character, replacing Young Link. The game also introduced [[Final Smash]]es, powerful special attacks that can be performed after destroying the {{ssb|Smash Ball}}, and Stickers, with 71 of them based on ''The Legend of Zelda'' series. {{SSBB|-}} includes the [[Tetra's Ship#Super Smash Bros. Brawl|Pirate Ship]] and the [[Bridge of Eldin#Super Smash Bros. Brawl|Bridge of Eldin]] as stages, with the Temple returning from {{SSBM|-}}, and 47 Trophies based on ''Zelda''. The game also introduces [[Deku Nut]]s as items and [[Tingle]] as an [[Assist Trophy]]. It has sold 13.32 million copies.

==={{SSB4|-}}===
{{Main|Super Smash Bros. for Nintendo 3DS / Wii U}}
{{SSB4|3DS|-}} and {{SSB4|Wii U|-}} were released in 2014 for the [[Nintendo 3DS]] and [[Wii U]], respectively. They introduced Sheik as a separate character, no longer being Zelda's transformation. The games include [[Skyloft]], [[Gerudo Valley]], and the [[Spirit Train]] as stages, with the Hyrule Castle, Temple, and Bridge of Eldin stages returning. The games introduced [[Cucco]]s, the [[Gust Bellows]], the [[Beetle (Item)|Beetle]], a [[Fairy]] in a [[Bottle]], and [[Bombchu]] as items, with [[Skull Kid]], [[Midna]], and [[Ghirahim]] as Assist Trophies. They also feature many enemies from ''The Legend of Zelda'' series in the Nintendo 3DS exclusive mode, {{ssb|Smash Run}}. The Wii U version features 49 Trophies based on ''The Legend of Zelda'' series, while the Nintendo 3DS version features 58. The 3DS version sold 9.65 million copies while the Wii U counterpart is only 5.38 million, and therefore 15.03 million in total.

==={{SSBU|-}}===
{{Main|Super Smash Bros. Ultimate}}
{{SSBU|-}} was released on December 7, 2018 for the [[Nintendo Switch]]. Link, Zelda, Ganondorf, Sheik and Toon Link return as playable fighters, with Young Link returning after 17 years from {{SSBM|-}}. The Hyrule Castle, Great Bay, Temple, Bridge of Eldin, Pirate Ship, Gerudo Valley, Spirit Train, and Skyloft stages all return in this installment. Every item from {{SSB|-}} history makes a return. Midna and Ghirahim return as Assist Trophies. This game is by far the best-selling title of the series, as 36.93 million copies are sold worldwide.

== Trivia ==

* In terms of popularity of [[Nintendo]]'s spin-off games, the ''Super Smash Bros.'' series is on-par with the ''[[mariowiki:Mario_Kart_(series)|Mario Kart]]'' series. As of September 2025, over 77 million copies are sold worldwide.
* Alongside ''[[The Legend of Zelda (series)|The Legend of Zelda]]'', ''Super Smash Bros.'' is also a spin-off series of the ''[[Mario (franchise)|Mario]]'' franchise.
* Masahiro Sakurai, alongside the series itself, is also the creator of [https://kirby.fandom.com/wiki/Kirby Kirby] from the [https://kirby.fandom.com/wiki/Kirby_(series) ''Kirby'' franchise]. Kirby himself is depicted as one of its mascots alongside [[Mario]], [[Link]], and [[pokemon:Pikachu|Pikachu]], and is also a friendly rival to the former.[[File:SSB Link intro.png|thumb|221x221px|Link as seen in ''Super Smash Bros.'', which is the iconic Hero of Time incarnation later used for ''Melee'' and Young Link]]
* In terms of the incarnations of the main ''The Legend of Zelda'' characters, Link, [[Princess Zelda]] and [[Ganondorf]] are usually based on the ones seen from the contemporary released installments of the ''Zelda'' franchise. For example, the Link in ''Super Smash Bros.'' and ''Melee'' is the [[Hero of Time]] from ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time|Ocarina of Time]]'' (this also applies for [[Young Link]]), ''Brawl'' and ''Super Smash Bros. for 3DS/Wii U'' features the Hero of Twilight from ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess|Twilight Princess]]'', and ''Ultimate'' features the Hero of the Wild from ''[[The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild|Breath of the Wild]]''. Toon Link is based on the Hero of Winds from ''[[The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker|The Wind Waker]]''.
* Surprisingly, the [[Final Smash]] concept was originally intended to appear in ''Super Smash Bros.'', but the development team was unable to do so due to limited processing power until the release of ''Brawl''.
* In the original ''Super Mario Bros.'', the [[Heart Container]] item was capable of recovering all damage, similar to when obtaining one in the 3D ''The Legend of Zelda'' games. However, since ''Super Smash Bros. Melee'', it only recovers 100% damage.
* Among ''The Legend of Zelda'' stages, the [[Temple (Stage)|Temple]], which is based on ''[[Zelda II: The Adventure of Link|The Adventure of Link]]'' and debuted in ''Super Smash Bros. Melee'', is a popular favorite among players, due to its large size and the lower part of it is known to allow opponents with high percentage damage to hide themselves, since the design of that area makes it difficult for a fighter to be KO'd. It is referred to as the fight club area by fans. It is also normally banned in professional tournaments because of this.
* The series is known to have varied ratings by the [[ESRB]]. The original game was rated Everyone ("E"), while ''Melee'' and ''Brawl'' both have a considerably higher Teen ("T") level. The modern installments have the Everyone 10+ ("E10+") rating, as Nintendo currently avoids making the games too violent.
* Despite being a fighting game series, ''Super Smash Bros.'' is considered distinct from conventional titles of the genre, due to its unique ruleset of battling on platforms with a set of lives rather than a 2D-style one-on-one battle seen in other franchises such as [[Capcom]]'s ''[https://streetfighter.fandom.com/wiki/Street_Fighter_series Street Fighter]''.
** Link would make his first and so far only true fighting game appearance in Namco's ''[[SoulCalibur II]]'' of the ''[https://soulcalibur.fandom.com/wiki/Soul_series Soul]'' series exclusively for the [[Nintendo GameCube]] version.
* ''Super Smash Bros. Brawl'' marks the first time Mario and [https://sonic.fandom.com/wiki/Sonic_the_Hedgehog Sonic the Hedgehog] are officially able to fight each other in combat, which is met with great praise and shock by fans in North America due to the previous business rivalry of Nintendo and Sega in the 1990s.
** However, it's not the first game to have an official crossover between the ''Super'' ''Mario'' and ''[https://sonic.fandom.com/wiki/Sonic_the_Hedgehog_series Sonic the Hedgehog]'' franchises, as ''[[mariowiki:Mario_&_Sonic_at_the_Olympic_Games_(Wii)|Mario & Sonic at the Olympic Games]]'' on the [[Wii|Nintendo Wii]] was released one year prior in 2007.
* Although the ''Super Smash Bros.'' series is not canon to any of the Nintendo or third-party franchises it featured, due to its high popularity, some fans have believed the playable character's fighting abilities match their canonical strength. For example, in ''Brawl'', in the [[Adventure Mode: The Subspace Emissary|Subspace Emissary]] mode, King Dedede can be seen defeating [[Bowser]] in its story, when the former is more of a comical villain while the latter in comparison is more ruthless if not durable in their actual games' lore.
