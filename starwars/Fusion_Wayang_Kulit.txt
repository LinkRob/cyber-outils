'''Fusion Wayang Kulit''' is a [[Wikipedia:Malaysia|Malaysian]] group dedicated to [[Wikipedia:Wayang kulit|wayang kulit]] [[Wikipedia:Shadow play|shadow puppetry]], aiming to continue the tradition via performances that adapt contemporary popular culture, including ''[[Star Wars]]''.<ref name="Cilisos">{{WebCite|url=https://cilisos.my/wayang-kulit-is-a-dying-art-but-these-msians-are-reviving-it-with-star-wars-puppets|text=Wayang kulit is a ‘dying art’, but these M’sians are reviving it with… Star Wars puppets?|author=Nicole Ng|date=2020-06-11|work=[https://cilisos.my Cilisos]|archivedate=20241008055649}}</ref> It is led by character designer Tintoy Chuo, art director Teh Take Huat, and master puppeteer Muhammad Dain bin Othman.<ref name="WSJ">{{WebCite|url=https://www.wsj.com/articles/star-wars-as-a-shadow-play-1404383321|text='Star Wars' as a Shadow Play|author=John Krich|date=2014-07-03|work=[[Wikipedia:The Wall Street Journal|''The Wall Street Journal'']]|archivedate=20170210180841}}<!-- text excerpt accessible without paywall via https://theatreroomasia.com/2014/07/14/shadows-of-the-empire --></ref>

Chuo and Huat were initially invited to contribute to an art exhibition in [[Wikipedia:Kuala Lumpur|Kuala Lumpur]] in [[2012]], and they sought to highlight an aspect of Malaysian culture. They researched wayang kulit, a traditional form of puppetry in [[Wikipedia:Southeast Asia|Southeast Asia]]<ref name="TED 2013">{{YouTube|video=B3sQV0SC8BE|text=Peperangan Bintang Wayang Kulit: Tintoy Chuo & Take Huat at TEDxKLwomen 2013|channel=@goetheinstitut|name=TEDx Talks|date=2014-03-24}}</ref>&mdash;believed to have originated among the [[Wikipedia:Hinduism|Hindus]] of [[Wikipedia:India|India]] over a thousand years ago and considered one of the first forms of animation<ref name="Goethe">{{YouTube|video=Q97CPlYlGec|text=Shadow of Change - Neue Hoffnung für eine traditionelle malaysische Kunst / KSWE21|channel=@TEDx|name=Goethe-Institut|date=2021-06-16}}</ref>&mdash;and focused on its Malaysian form, distinguished by a single moveable arm in each puppet. They found that wayang kulit was increasingly neglected by young Malaysians and thus wanted to combine the tradition with the characters of the original [[1977]] [[Star Wars: Episode IV A New Hope|''Star Wars'']] film by [[George Lucas]],<ref name="TED 2013" /> who created ''Star Wars'' as a modern mythology borrowed from older myths and religions.<ref name="Archives">''[[The Star Wars Archives: Episodes I–III, 1999–2005]]''</ref> Chuo searched for wayang kulit masters in rural Malaysia, and Muhammad Dain Othman, a master puppeteer in [[Wikipedia:Kelantan|Kelantan]], connected with Chuo on [[Wikipedia:Facebook|Facebook]] and they met to discuss the project. "Pak" (uncle) Dain agreed with the goal of "A New Hope" to make wayang kulit popular again in Malaysia, maintaining the authenticity of the ''Star Wars'' project by continuing to involve the key elements of the master puppeteer, light, the white screen, and the puppets together with traditional Malay music.<ref name="WSJ" />

Fusion Wayang Kulit further invested in new technology such as a synchronized video projector and sound effects as overlay, and their ''Peperangan Bintang'' ([[Wikipedia:Malay language|Malay]] for ''Star Wars'') performance was well-received<ref name="WSJ" /> at their first public performance on [[October 18]], [[2013]], narrated in Malay.<ref name="Preview">{{YouTube|video=ssCxFxeDgjw|text=Preview of 'Peperangan Bintang' (Star Wars inspired wayang kulit)|channel=@FusionWayangKulit|name=Fusion Wayang Kulit|date=2018-09-01}}</ref> The puppets were designed by Chuo and approved by Pak Dain, and crafters in Kelantan made the puppets from buffalo hide, nails, and bamboo, though they used [[Wikipedia:Polypropylene|polypropylene plastic]] for ''Peperangan Bintang''. Although he only watched ''Star Wars'' after meeting Chuo, Pak Dain considered adapting ''Star Wars'' to be acceptable per tradition since the stories augment traditional mythology<ref name="WSJ" /> with the same overarching theme of good versus evil,<ref name="CNN">{{WebCite|url=https://edition.cnn.com/videos/great-big-story/2020/09/16/great-big-story-malaysia-darth-vader-gbs.great-big-story|text=In Malaysia, Darth Vader is leading a shadow puppetry revival|date=2020-09-16|work=[[Wikipedia:CNN|CNN]]|archivedate=20200920061952}}</ref> and he had thought that the art, which had once drawn crowds of up to a thousand people<ref name="AJ">{{YouTube|video=OAMCxGlqa5c|text=Star Wars used for revival of Malaysia's old art form|channel=@aljazeeraenglish|name=Al Jazeera English|date=2015-12-13}}</ref> but was left with only several living master puppeteers would become extinct in Malaysia within ten years' time before embarking on the project.<ref name="WSJ" /> Pak Dain's main goal was to train a student to inherit his skills as a master puppeteer and had been training two pupils for two years as of [[2021]].<ref name="Goethe" />

Fusion Wayang Kulit received permission from [[Lucasfilm]] to perform ''Star Wars''&ndash;inspired wayang kulit, touring across Malaysia despite relying on their own money without charging audiences<ref name="AJ" /> and showing to people worldwide, including in [[Wikipedia:Thailand|Thailand]], [[Wikipedia:Germany|Germany]], and [[Wikipedia:Austria|Austria]]. They also maintain a gallery in the GMBB mall in Kuala Lumpur.<ref name="Cilisos" /> The group attended [[2025]]'s [[Celebration Japan (2025)|''Star Wars'' Celebration Japan]], where they performed a short adaptation of the transition between ''[[Rogue One: A Star Wars Story]]'' and ''A New Hope'' and they were interviewed about their work by [[Kristin Baver]] on [[April 20]].<ref name="Celebration">{{SWYouTube|video=p20GM68wyC4|text=Star Wars Celebration LIVE! 2025 - DAY 3|date=2025-04-20}}</ref>
