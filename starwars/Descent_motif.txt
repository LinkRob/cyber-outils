A '''musical motif''' composed by [[John Williams]] and performed by the [[London Symphony Orchestra]] first appeared in the [[2002]] [[prequel trilogy]] film, [[Star Wars: Episode II Attack of the Clones|''Star Wars'': Episode II ''Attack of the Clones'']]. A combination of [[Wikipedia:Dies irae|dies irae]] and "[[Across the Stars]]," it is played in the tracks "[[Star Wars Episode II: Attack of the Clones (soundtrack)|The Dinner Scene]]" and "Anakin Changes," as [[Anakin Skywalker]] descends to the [[Dark side of the Force|dark side]]. It reappears in [[Star Wars: Episode III Revenge of the Sith|''Star Wars'': Episode III ''Revenge of the Sith'']], again symbolising Skywalker's fall in the tracks "[[Anakin's Betrayal]]," "Lament," and "The Immolation Scene." The lattermost is played when Skywalker, after losing his [[Mission to Mustafar|duel]] against his former mentor, [[Obi-Wan Kenobi]], is burned by [[Magma|lava]] on the [[planet]] [[Mustafar]]. For the [[Star Wars Episode III: Revenge of the Sith (soundtrack)|score]] of ''Revenge of the Sith'', both the London Symphony Orchestra and [[London Voices]] performed the musical motif at [[Abbey Road Studios]].<ref name="Making">''[[The Making of Star Wars Revenge of the Sith]]''</ref>

A fragment of the motif is played during the [[Battle of Crait]] sequence of the [[sequel trilogy]] film [[Star Wars: Episode VIII The Last Jedi|''Star Wars'': Episode VIII ''The Last Jedi'']], released in [[2017]]. It is played towards the end of the track "[[Star Wars: The Last Jedi (soundtrack)|The Battle of Crait]]."
