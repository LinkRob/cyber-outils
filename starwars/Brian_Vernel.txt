'''Brian Vernel''' is a [[Wikipedia:Scottish people|Scottish]] actor who portrayed [[Bala-Tik]] in the [[2015]] film [[Star Wars: Episode VII The Force Awakens|''Star Wars'': Episode VII ''The Force Awakens'']].<ref name="STV">{{WebCite|url=https://news.stv.tv/scotland/1336232-seven-interesting-connections-to-scotland-the-star-wars-films-have|text="Seven interesting connections to Scotland, the Star Wars films have"|date=2015-12-17|archivedate=20151225045139}}</ref>

Vernel made his first on-screen appearance in [[Wikipedia:BBC One|BBC One]] crime drama television series [[Wikipedia:The Field of Blood (TV series)|''The Field of Blood'']] (2011).<ref name="STV" /> In 2012, Vernel starred in the Davey Anderson play ''The Static'', which played at the [[Wikipedia:Traverse Theatre|Traverse Theatre]] in [[Wikipedia:Edinburgh|Edinburgh]]. He played the lead role of Sparky, an intelligent but volatile teenager with psychic abilities. Rohanne Udall of ''Exeunt Magazine'' wrote: "Vernel is confident and accomplished, bursting with energy and vigour from his first moments and transfixing the audience with the challenging gaze of a demanding youth."<ref>{{WebCite|url=http://exeuntmagazine.com/reviews/the-static|text=The Static|date=2012-09-17|Last=Uhall|archiveurl=https://archive.is/VIKGF}}</ref> The performance earned Vernel a nomination for Best Actor at The Stage Awards of 2012.<ref>{{WebCite|url=https://www.edfringe.com/media/news/winners-revealed-for-the-stage-awards-for-acting-excellence-at-the-edinburgh-festival-fringe-2012|text=Winners revealed for The Stage Awards for Acting Excellence at the Edinburgh Festival Fringe 2012|author=McAuliffe, Nichola|archiveurl=https://archive.is/YLXwr}}</ref> In 2013, Vernel appeared in the [[Wikipedia:Donna Franceschild|Donna Franceschild]] play ''Takin' Over the Asylum'', a stage adaptation of the writer's [[Wikipedia:Takin' Over the Asylum|television miniseries of the same name]], which played at the [[Wikipedia:Citizens Theatre|Citizens Theatre]] in [[Wikipedia:Glasgow|Glasgow]]. [[Wikipedia:The Daily Telegraph|''The Daily Telegraph'']]{{'s}} Mark Brown called his performance "an impressive, high octane outing".<ref>{{WebCite|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/culture/theatre/theatre-reviews/9880366/Takin-Over-the-Asylum-Citizens-Theatre-Glasgow-review.html|text=Takin' Over the Asylum, Citizens Theatre, Glasgow, review|author=Brown, Mark|date=2013-02-19|archiveurl=https://archive.is/YQJIK}}</ref> He won the Citizens Theatre Award for Best Actor for the performance.<ref>{{WebCite|url=http://curtisbrown.co.uk/client/brian-vernel|text=Brian Vernel|archiveurl=https://archive.is/qzn9u}}</ref>

Vernel starred in the [[Wikipedia:BBC|BBC]] [[Wikipedia:Miniseries|miniseries]] [[Wikipedia:The Casual Vacancy (miniseries)|''The Casual Vacancy'']] (2015), based on the [[Wikipedia:The Casual Vacancy|novel of the same name]] by [[Wikipedia:J. K. Rowling|J. K. Rowling]].<ref name="STV" /> He also co-stars in the BBC [[Wikipedia:Historical period drama|historical drama]] series [[Wikipedia:The Last Kingdom (TV series)|''The Last Kingdom'']], an eight-part adaptation of [[Wikipedia:Bernard Cornwell|Bernard Cornwell's]] historical novels series [[Wikipedia:The Saxon Stories|''The Saxon Stories'']].<ref name="STV" /> In 2015, Vernel portrayed Paul in the [[Wikipedia:Barrie Keeffe|Barrie Keeffe]] play ''Barbarians'' at the [[Wikipedia:Young Vic|Young Vic]] in the [[Wikipedia:London Borough of Lambeth|London Borough of Lambeth]]. Andrew Dickson, a reviewer with [[Wikipedia:The Guardian|''The Guardian'']], wrote: "The show comes close to being stolen by Brian Vernel as Paul, for whom the only real constants are anger and betrayal; with his shaved skull and bruise-dark eyes, he resembles a caged snake, waiting to rear and strike."<ref>{{WebCite|url=http://www.theguardian.com/stage/2015/dec/04/barbarians-review-young-vic-london-barrie-keeffe-liz-stevenson|text=Barbarians review – like a furious, punk Godot|author=Young Vic, London|date=2015-12-04|archiveurl=https://archive.is/Sh2Ae}}</ref>
