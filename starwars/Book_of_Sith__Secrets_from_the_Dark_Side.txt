'''''Book of Sith: Secrets from the Dark Side''''' is a reference book on the [[Sith/Legends|Sith Order]] and the [[Dark side of the Force/Legends|dark side of the Force]] by [[Daniel Wallace]]. It was released on [[February 10]], [[2012]] bundled with a collector's case and additional items. It was released on [[April 16]], [[2013]] without the case or additional items.<ref name="Amazon Box" /><ref name="Amazon Book" />

The book was released again on [[August 8]], [[2014]] as part of ''[[Star Wars: The Jedi Path and Book of Sith Deluxe Box Set]]'' with [[The Jedi Path: A Manual for Students of the Force (real-life book)|''The Jedi Path'']] that included two art prints depicting figures from [[Duel between The Dreamer and The Monster-Slayer|Sith]] and [[Trikking The Blind|Jedi]] history, and again in [[September 27]], [[2016]] as ''[[Star Wars: Secrets of the Galaxy Deluxe Boxed Set]]'' with the [[Star Wars: Imperial Handbook: A Commander's Guide|''Imperial Handbook'']] and [[The Bounty Hunter Code: From the Files of Boba Fett|''Bounty Hunter Code'']].

==Publisher's summary==
===Boxed set===
''Remove the disposable exterior box to reveal the black-and-red pyramid-shaped Sith case. It appears innocuous at first.''

''The [[Sith/Legends|Sith]] have existed in the galaxy for centuries, lurking, waiting for their chance to seize control. As various [[Sith Lord/Legends|Sith Lords]] emerged and rose to power, they recorded their thoughts, exploits, and plots for Sith control of the galaxy. When they fell, their knowledge vanished with them forever. Or so it seemed. . . .''

''Over the years, these writings were passed among numerous Sith and [[Jedi/Legends|Jedi]], who added their comments to the pages. In his quest for domination, [[Palpatine/Legends|Darth Sidious]] tracked down what remained of five pivotal Sith texts written by his most powerful predecessors. Then, drawing on the knowledge within the compiled pages, he wrote a sixth text&mdash;his own manifesto. Together, these documents, along with several collected objects associated with them, shed light on the philosophy, achievements, and failures of the [[Sith Order]]. For years, this collection remained hidden, existing as legend only. But now all who are tempted may unlock the [[Book of Sith]] and delve into the [[Dark side of the Force/Legends|dark side]]. . . .''

''From the creators of [[The Jedi Path: A Manual for Students of the Force (real-life book)|The Jedi Path]], in collaboration with [[Lucasfilm]], Book of Sith reveals the mystery and madness of the dark side&mdash;while introducing new characters, history, as well as sinister insights into what it means to be a [[Sith Master/Legends|Sith Master]]''.

''Recovered dark side texts'':
*''A chronicle by [[Sorzus Syn]] of her encounters with [[Sith (species)/Legends|Sith Purebloods]], explorations in [[Sith alchemy/Legends|alchemy]], and the foundations of the Sith Order''.
*''A [[Journal of Darth Malgus|war journal]] kept by [[Darth Malgus]] during the [[Great Galactic War]], describing methods of war and the campaigns of the [[Imperial Army (Sith Empire)|Sith army]]''.
*''A [[The Rule of Two|source book]] by [[Darth Bane/Legends|Darth Bane]] that details the idea and the philosophy behind the [[Rule of Two/Legends|Rule of Two]] as well as the training required for a Sith.''
*''An [[Wild Power|instructional manual]] by [[Mother (title)/Legends|Mother]] [[Talzin/Legends|Talzin]] exploring the Nightsisters's use of the dark side and their role as dark side mercenaries in [[The galaxy/Legends|the galaxy]]''.
*''A [[The Science of Creating Life|scientific journal]] by [[Darth Plagueis/Legends|Darth Plagueis]] with notes about his experimentation with and manipulation of [[The Force/Legends|the Force]].''
*''[[Palpatine/Legends|Darth Sidious's]] [[Absolute Power|manifesto]]'' about the philosophy and political manipulation that marked his rise to power and established his [[Galactic Empire/Legends|Empire]]''.

''Those who added their comments: [[Palpatine/Legends|Darth Sidious]], [[Anakin Skywalker/Legends|Darth Vader]], [[Asajj Ventress/Legends|Asajj Ventress]], [[Yoda/Legends|Yoda]], [[Mace Windu/Legends|Mace Windu]], [[Quinlan Vos/Legends|Quinlan Vos]], and [[Luke Skywalker/Legends|Luke Skywalker]]''

''Removable features: A scrap of a Sith [[Burial/Legends|burial]] shroud, a battle map from the [[Great Galactic War]], a [[Synthetic lightsaber crystal/Legends|Sith lightsaber crystal]], a [[Talisman of Finding/Legends|Nightsister talisman]], a diagram detailing Palpatine's political strategies during the [[Clone Wars/Legends|Clone Wars]], and a propaganda poster for the [[Galactic Empire/Legends|Galactic Empire]], and a note from Luke Skywalker''<ref name="Amazon Box" />

===Regular edition===
''In his quest for total domination, Darth Sidious compiled six legendary dark side texts detailing Sith history and philosophy by Sorzus Syn, Darth Malgus, Darth Bane, Mother Talzin, Darth Plagueis, and himself. Together these documents form the Book of Sith. Over the centuries, the texts were passed among Force users who left handwritten notes and annotations in the margins, including Darth Vader, Yoda, Mace Windu, and Luke Skywalker, among others. Collected by acclaimed Star Wars writer [[Daniel Wallace]] and embellished by numerous esteemed Star Wars illustrators, this volume introduces new characters and history, and delves deeper into understanding the philosophies and methods behind the dark side of the Force.''<ref name="Amazon Book" />

==Continuity==
The caption under the picture on Page 13 claims that [[Xendor]] and the [[Legions of Lettow]] fought the Jedi on "[[Corbos]]." Based on the text on the same page this was a typo, and instead, the caption should have read "[[Columus/Legends|Columus]]."<ref name="endnotes1">{{Blogspot|subdomain=geekosity|url=2012/03/endnotes-for-star-wars-book-of-sith.html|name=Daniel Wallace's Geekosity|text=Endnotes for Star Wars: Book of Sith (part 1)}}</ref> Corbos was correctly referred to in the caption and text of the previous page.

On Page 24, the [[Sith Abattar]] is misspelled as "Sith abbatar", which the author has confirmed was a typo.<ref name="endnotes1" />

Both typos were later amended in all future publications of the book.
