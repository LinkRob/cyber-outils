'''Dancing-girl costumes''' were a style of performance [[Clothing|costume]] for [[Gender|female]] dancers that were also worn by some beings who had been [[Slavery|enslaved]]. They consisted of an ornamented [[bronzium]] [[Breast|chest]] harness on top with varying garments on bottom, such as a [[lashaa silk]] skirt with [[jerba]] [[leather]] [[boot]]s, and they revealed much of the wearer's skin. During the [[Clone Wars]], the costumes were worn by many [[Twi'lek]] women in locations where they were enslaved or performed for other beings, including [[Nal Hutta]] and [[Zygerria]].

When the [[Hutt]] [[Jabba Desilijic Tiure]] captured [[Princess]] [[Leia Skywalker Organa Solo|Leia Organa]] of the [[Alliance to Restore the Republic|Rebel Alliance]] at [[Boba Fett's Palace|his palace]], he forced her to wear a dancing-girl costume and a chain. She strangled him to [[death]] with that chain and then destroyed his [[Khetanna|sail barge]] as she escaped. A [[holocube]] recording of Organa killing the Hutt won her the respect of many [[Nikto]]s, who dubbed her "the Huttslayer." Later in [[Senator]] Organa's life, [[Rinnrivin Di]] offered her the footage when she investigated his [[Rinnrivin Di's cartel|cartel]]. Di failed to learn from Jabba's fate and he, too, was [[Mission to Sibensko|killed]] by Organa.

==Description==
Dancing-girl costumes were a style of [[Clothing|costume]] worn for performances by [[Gender|female]] dancers.<ref name="Bloodline" /> They were also worn by some beings who had been [[Slavery|enslaved]] by<ref name="Slaves of the Republic" /> the [[Zygerrian Slave Empire]] on the [[planet]]<ref name="Zygerria">{{Databank|zygerria|Zygerria}}</ref> [[Zygerria]],<ref name="Slaves of the Republic" /> by [[crime lord]]s of the [[Hutt]] [[species]]<ref name="USW NE" /> on the planet [[Tatooine]],<ref name="ROTJ" /> and by the [[Hutt Clan]] and the [[Grand Hutt Council]] that ruled it<ref name="Hunt for Ziro" /> on the Hutt-controlled planet<ref name="Nal Hutta">{{Databank|nal-hutta|Nal Hutta}}</ref> [[Nal Hutta]].<ref name="Hunt for Ziro" /> For such beings, the costume could serve as everyday attire<ref name="Friends and Enemies" /> or palace garb.<ref name="USW NE" />

The costumes were distinguished by their top half,<ref name="TCW S3">[[Star Wars: The Clone Wars The Complete Season Three|''Star Wars: The Clone Wars'' The Complete Season Three]]</ref> a molded [[bronzium]] harness<ref name="TCVD NE" /> worn on the [[Breast|chest]] that had [[metal]] filigree on the cups and was fastened with cords crisscrossed behind the neck and back.<ref name="ROTJ" /> The top was accompanied by variable pieces on the lower half, such as a [[Swimsuit|bikini]] brief with filigree complementing the harness<ref name="TPM">{{Film|I}}</ref> or a [[lashaa silk]] skirt with separate panels suspended from a metal [[belt]].<ref name="TCVD NE" />

==History==
===Pre&ndash;civil war===
{{Quote|Sounds like that rough crowd out there is an easy one tonight.|A Twi'lek chorus girl wearing the costume, to Sy Snootles|<ref name="Hunt for Ziro" />}}
When the Hutt [[Jabba Desilijic Tiure]] presided over the [[Boonta Eve Classic]] [[Podracing|podrace]] on Tatooine<ref name="TPM" /> in [[32 BBY]],<ref name="Galactic Atlas">''[[Star Wars: Galactic Atlas: Updated Edition]]''</ref> he brought one of his favorite enslaved [[Musician|singers]], the half-[[Theelin]] [[Diva Shaliqua]],<ref name="Geektionary">''[[Star Wars: Geektionary: The Galaxy from A - Z]]''</ref> to his viewing platform in the [[Mos Espa Grand Arena]]. She wore a dancing-girl costume consisting of a matching harness and briefs layered over baggy trousers, chunky metal bangles on both wrists, [[leather]] armbands on both [[Limb|arms]], and a collar.<ref name="TPM" />

[[File:ChorusGirl-HuntForZiro.png|thumb|left|220px|A chorus girl at Gardulla the Hutt's Palace]]
During the [[Clone Wars]],<ref name="Lethal Trackdown">{{TCW|Lethal Trackdown}}</ref> in [[21 BBY]],<ref name="Date: 222">{{TCWcanondates|222}}</ref> an advertisement for the [[Coruscant Underworld|underground]] nightclub [[Show Girls]]<ref name="Lethal Trackdown concept">{{SW|url=series/clone-wars/lethal-trackdown-concept-art-gallery|text="Lethal Trackdown" Concept Art Gallery}} {{C|slide 9}}</ref> on the planet<ref name="USW NE" /> [[Coruscant]] depicted a female [[Twi'lek]] in a dancing-girl costume.<ref name="Lethal Trackdown" /> When Jabba's [[Desilijic|uncle]] [[Ziro Desilijic Tiure]] was imprisoned at [[Gardulla Besadii the Elder]]'s [[Gardulla the Hutt's Palace|palace]] on Nal Hutta, three Twi'lek chorus girls wore dancing costumes to perform for the Grand Hutt Council alongside [[Pa'lowick]] singer [[Sy Snootles]]. Their costumes paired ornamented harnesses with skimpy briefs, thigh-high stockings, collars on their necks,<ref name="Hunt for Ziro" /> and leather strapping wrapped around their legs and feet to create shoes. They also wore headdresses styled to resemble the heads of specific Hutts,<ref name="TCW S3" /> crowned with vibrantly [[color]]ed feathers. The [[Unidentified chorus girl|chorus girl]] styled after Jabba spoke with Snootles after their performance.<ref name="Hunt for Ziro" />

On Zygerria<ref name="Slaves of the Republic">{{TCW|Slaves of the Republic}}</ref> in [[20 BBY]],<ref name="Date: 412">{{TCWcanondates|412}}</ref> an enslaved Twi'lek clad in a dancing-girl costume was displayed in a [[Zygerrian slave market|market]] on the planet by a [[Zygerrian]] [[enslaver]] to a fellow Zygerrian and a [[Neimoidian]].<ref name="Slaves of the Republic" /> Later that [[Standard year|year]],<ref name="Date: 416">{{TCWcanondates|416}}</ref> several Twi'leks wore the outfit in the [[city]] of [[Bilbousa]] on Nal Hutta, including a pair who escorted a [[Alcohol|drunken]] Sy Snootles out of a [[Bilbousa saloon|saloon]] and others within the [[Cantina|saloon]] who danced on the bartop and conversed with customers.<ref name="Friends and Enemies" />

===The Huttslayer===
{{Quote|Come on. We gotta get out of here.|Leia Organa, after killing Jabba the Hutt, to R2-D2|<ref name="ROTJ" />}}
In [[4 ABY]],<ref name="Galactic Atlas" /> the [[Force-sensitive]] human [[Princess]] [[Leia Skywalker Organa Solo|Leia Organa]] of the [[Alliance to Restore the Republic|Rebel Alliance]] infiltrated [[Boba Fett's Palace|Jabba's Palace]] on Tatooine disguised as a [[bounty hunter]],<ref name="ROTJ" /> the [[Ubese]] male [[Boushh]],<ref name="Boushh CT">{{SWCT|set=Base Series 1|cardname=Boushh - Bounty Hunter}}</ref> who had seemingly captured the [[Wookiee]] rebel [[Chewbacca]]. Organa and Chewbacca planned to [[Rescue of Han Solo|rescue]] the rebel [[Han Solo]] from imprisonment in [[carbonite]], and although she freed Solo from his captivity, the Hutt had not been fooled and captured Organa as well.<ref name="ROTJ" />

[[File:Jabbachoke.jpg|thumb|right|300px|Using the chains he put on her, Leia Organa strangles and kills Jabba Desilijic Tiure.]]
Jabba ordered two servants&mdash;the musicians [[Jess]] and [[Damaris Viell]]&mdash;to help dress Organa<ref name="FACPOV Dune Sea">{{StoryCite|story=Dune Sea Songs of Salt and Moonlight|book=From a Certain Point of View: Return of the Jedi}}</ref> in the garb of a palace slave,<ref name="USW NE" /> which included a maroon lashaa silk skirt suspended from a belt, [[jerba]] leather [[boot]]s, and a bronzium harness.<ref name="TCVD NE" /> Her [[hair]] was [[braid]]ed in a bun high on her head and a single long plait down her back. She also wore hair ornaments, small hoop [[Piercing|earrings]], a cuff in a spiral design on her upper left arm, a [[bracelet]] on her right wrist,<ref name="ROTJ" /> and a heavy collar around her neck,<ref name="Bloodline" /> all in a metal that matched the harness. The Hutt used a thick chain threaded through the loops on the collar to keep her at his [[Jabba the Hutt's dais|throne]].<ref name="ROTJ" />

Organa's [[twin]] brother, the [[Jedi Knight]] [[Luke Skywalker]], later entered the palace as well.<ref name="ROTJ" /> Although the costume was meant to demean her, she refused to outwardly display her true feelings and maintained her dignity.<ref name="Skywalker">''[[Skywalker: A Family at War]]''</ref> [[Force sense|Sensing]] her concern for Solo and her anger at her mistreatment by the Hutt,<ref name="Encyclopedia">{{EncyclopediaCite|The Battle of Hoth and the Second Death Star}}</ref> Skywalker had to control his own emotions and avoid the temptation of using the [[dark side of the Force]] to [[Death|murder]] Jabba.<ref name="Beware!">''[[Return of the Jedi: Beware the Power of the Dark Side!]]''</ref> When Jabba dropped Skywalker into the [[rancor]] pit below the throne, Organa could only watch as he fought and killed Jabba's [[Pateesa|pet rancor]] while the Hutt held her back with the chain. Jabba subsequently sentenced Skywalker, Solo, and Chewbacca to be [[Death penalty|executed]] by the [[sarlacc]] beast within the [[Great Pit of Carkoon]], and Organa alongside him to observe from his [[sail barge]],<ref name="ROTJ" /> the ''[[Khetanna]]''.<ref name="USW NE" /> As the group and their friend [[Landonis Balthazar Calrissian|Lando Calrissian]] fought back against the scheduled execution, Organa took advantage of the chaos to knock out the controls to Jabba's throne and throw the chains around the Hutt's neck.<ref name="ROTJ" /> Her pure hatred for the Hutt fueled her<ref name="Bloodline" /> as she unknowingly drew upon the dark side of [[the Force]] for the physical strength<ref name="Skywalker" /> to strangle Jabba and kill him.<ref name="ROTJ" />

After killing Jabba, the rebel [[astromech droid]] [[R2-D2]] assisted Organa with breaking the chain, and she joined the battle on the barge until Skywalker directed her to point a deck [[cannon]] at the deck itself. The pair held onto each other and used a [[rope]] to swing to safety on a [[Bantha-II cargo skiff|skiff]] as the cannon fired and exploded Jabba's barge behind them.<ref name="ROTJ" />

===The legend of Leia Organa===
{{Quote|Huttslayer. This is what we call you among ourselves, and it is a far greater title than either ''senator'' or ''princess'' could ever be. The Niktos know you for the warrior you are, Huttslayer, and you will always have friends among us.|Rinnrivin Di, regarding his holocube footage|<ref name="Bloodline" />}}
By [[28 ABY]],<ref name="BloodTime">''[[Star Wars: Timelines]]'' dates the events of [[Bloodline (novel)|''Bloodline'']] to [[28 ABY]].</ref> [[Senator]] Leia Organa of the [[New Republic]] sometimes had clear memories of sensory details, particularly the weight of the slave collar and the stench of her surroundings, when something reminded her of the events at Jabba's palace. One such memory struck her while fellow senators discussed whether or not to officially intervene with the [[Rinnrivin Di's cartel|cartel]] of the [[Kajain'sa'Nikto]] crime lord [[Rinnrivin Di]], rumored to be growing as powerful as the Hutts had once been. Organa volunteered to launch an [[Mission to Bastatha|investigation]] of Di at his [[Rinnrivin's headquarters|base]] on the planet [[Bastatha]].<ref name="Bloodline" />

[[File:Rinnrivin_Di.jpg|thumb|left|250px|Rinnrivin Di, one of the many Niktos who admired Leia Organa, had obtained footage of the "Huttslayer" event.]]
When Organa met with Di, he welcomed her and presented her with a [[holocube]] containing a recording of Jabba's death. He told her that many [[Nikto]]s revered her as the "Huttslayer," a warrior who rid the galaxy of Jabba the Hutt. He also indicated that very few copies of the footage were in circulation; the Hutts had suppressed it, not wanting proof of their own vulnerability to be passed around. Di told Organa that she would always have allies among the Niktos before trying to leverage her reputation for this lawless act to forge an alliance with her, proposing that she turn a blind eye to his cartel's [[Spicerunner|spicerunning]] and other underworld dealings. Organa later showed the holocube to her colleague, Ransolm Casterfo, while explaining her run-in with Di. Instead of disgust, as she anticipated, Casterfo expressed admiration.<ref name="Bloodline" />

On her next meeting with Di, Organa returned the holocube to him. He played the recording repeatedly and confessed that he envied that Organa had the memory while he only had the recording. Unbeknownst to Di, however, Organa had slipped a microscopic [[sensor beacon]] into the cube, predicting that he would keep the prize close. She used the tracker to narrow down his location on the planet [[Sibensko]] and discovered an [[Water|underwater]] [[Sibensko complex|complex]] used by his cartel. By then, Di had discovered the tracker and confronted her, once more replaying the recording. Despite his vocal admiration, however, Di had failed to learn from the Hutt's fate and underestimated Organa to his peril: she escaped, killing him and the warriors backing him up.<ref name="Bloodline" />

===Legacy===
{{Quote|It was not enough for Jabba to incarcerate Leia. He wanted to humiliate her. Leia was forced to don the attire of a servant with a heavy shackle fixed around her neck, tethering her to the Hutt’s grubby, slime-slicked hand. The outfit alone was demeaning. Outwardly, she took it in stride and held her head high, but for years to come, Leia would recall the acrid stench of grease and smoke that clung to Jabba’s dingy lair, and the weight of her chains. They epitomized all the reasons Leia fought against the Empire, a tangible reminder of the alternative if the rebellion lost.|Kitrin Braves, ''Skywalker: A Family at War''|<ref name="SFAW">''[[Skywalker: A Family at War]]''</ref>}}
The dancing-girl costume Organa had worn on Tatooine was mentioned in the twenty-fourth chapter of<ref name="SFAW" /> [[Skywalker: A Family at War (in-universe)|''Skywalker: A Family at War'']],<ref name="SFAW SW.com">{{SW|url=news/star-wars-reads-recommendations|text=Our ''Star Wars'' Reads Picks For Every Type of Reader in Your Life}}</ref> an exhaustive biography of the [[Skywalker family]] written by the [[journalist]] [[Kitrin Braves]]<ref name="RAF">''[[Star Wars: The Rise and Fall of the Galactic Empire]]''</ref> and published at some point after the [[35 ABY]] release<ref name="SFAW Date">{{SkywalkerFamilyAtWarDate}}</ref> of the [[historian]] [[Beaumont Kin]]'s [[book]], ''[[The Rise and Fall of the Galactic Empire]]''.<ref name="RAF" /> The work discussed Jabba's incarceration of Organa, forcing her to don an outfit that would maximize humiliation. The author noted that while Organa outwardly took it in stride, she would remember the weight of the outfit's chains for years afterward, using the experience to fuel her resolve to fight the Empire.<ref name="SFAW" />

==Behind the scenes==
===Conception===
Dancing-girl costumes first appeared in the third [[original trilogy]] film, [[Star Wars: Episode VI Return of the Jedi|''Star Wars'': Episode VI ''Return of the Jedi'']],<ref name="ROTJ" /> which premiered on [[May 25]], [[1983]].<ref name="Year by Year">''[[Star Wars Year By Year: A Visual History, New Edition]]''</ref> Versions were worn by actress [[Carrie Fisher]] and stunt performer [[Tracey Eddon]] as Princess Leia Organa. The [[Wikipedia:Bikini|bikini]]-style costume consisted of a [[Wikipedia:Brassiere|brassiere]], a skirt made with two panels hanging from a belt, [[Wikipedia:Boot|boots]], matching jewelry, and hair accessories.<ref name="ROTJ" />

[[File:LeiaDancingCostume.png|thumb|right|250px|1981 concept art, illustrated by Nilo Rodis-Jamero in collaboration with Aggie Guerard Rodgers]]
From [[July 13]] to [[July 17]], [[1981]], ''Return of the Jedi'' screenplay co-writer [[Lawrence Kasdan]] and co-producer [[Howard G. Kazanjian]] met with Marquand and Lucas for a story conference in which they plotted out how Leia would end up wearing that costume. As they discussed the characters infiltrating Jabba's palace, Marquand suggested that she could enter in disguise and get discovered, then turned into a dancing girl; he also felt it would also be nice to add a chain. Kasdan asked how the group felt about Leia being the one to kill Jabba, and Marquand and Lucas both suggested that she use the chain to strangle the Hutt. The second draft of the script on [[September 21]] included a description that Leia was "dressed in the skimpy costume of a dancing girl; a chain runs from a manacle/necklace on her neck to her new master, Jabba the Hutt."<ref name="Making of ROTJ" /> In the "Slave Leia costume" featurette for the [[2011]] [[Star Wars home video releases|Blu-ray]] set ''[[Star Wars: The Complete Saga]]'', sculptor and jeweler [[Richard Miller]] recalled Lucas telling him the purpose of the costume's skimpiness was to show that Leia had grown up.<ref name="The Complete Saga">''[[Star Wars: The Complete Saga]]''</ref> Interviewed in the [[July 21]], 1983 issue of [https://www.rollingstone.com ''Rolling Stone''] magazine, Fisher commented that the filmmakers had decided one of the ways to make Leia Organa more feminine was removing her clothing.<ref name="Rolling Stone 1983">{{BookCite|author=Caldwell, Carol|container=Carrie Fisher: A Few Words on Princess Leia, Fame and Feminism|title=Rolling Stone|contributor=Wenner, Jann (managing editor and publisher)|number=no. 400/401|date=1983-07-21|issn=0035-791X|quote=From the first film, she was just a soldier, front line and center. The only way they knew to make the character strong was to make her angry. In ''Return of the Jedi'', she gets to be more feminine, more supportive, more affectionate. But let's not forget that these movies are basically boys' fantasies. So the other way they made her more female in this one was to have her take off her clothes.|link=https://web.archive.org/web/20210513003513/https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/movie-news/carrie-fisher-a-few-words-on-princess-leia-fame-and-feminism-190633}}</ref>

In the [[1997]] book ''[[Star Wars: The Annotated Screenplays]]'', written by [[Laurent Bouzereau]], Rodis-Jamero said that Lucas always talked about a slave-girl outfit, but the artist struggled and kept coming up with clunky concepts reminiscent of [[Wikipedia:Ben-Hur: A Tale of the Christ (1925 film)|''Ben-Hur'']].<ref name="Annotated">''[[Star Wars: The Annotated Screenplays]]''</ref> He met with Richard Miller, who was a friend of [[Industrial Light & Magic]] modeler [[Lorne Peterson]]<ref name="Making of ROTJ" /> and had created bronze sculptures in a style similar to Rodis-Jamero's concepts. Miller created three-dimensional representations and would later sculpt the faux metal components of the costume<ref name="SW Costumes" /> as the film's credited jeweler.<ref name="ROTJ" /> Because Rodis-Jamero designed costumes from a conceptual point of view and did not have practical knowledge, he and co-producer [[Jim Bloom]] realized an experienced costume designer would be needed to execute any designs.<ref name="SW Costumes" />

[[File:MarileeHeyer-LeiaConcept.jpg|thumb|left|200px|Marilee Heyer's 1981 illustration for Paul LeBlanc's hair design]]
[[Aggie Guerard Rodgers]], who had previously worked with Lucas on his films [[Wikipedia:American Graffiti|''American Graffiti'']] and [[Wikipedia:More American Graffiti|''More American Graffiti'']], was hired<ref name="SW Costumes" /> in fall 1981.<ref name="Making of ROTJ" /> Rodis-Jamero and Rodgers collaborated to produce sketches of their concepts, with the former creating the actual illustrations.<ref name="SW Costumes" /> Lucas specifically requested that they create a bikini, according to Rodgers.<ref name="Yahoo ROTJ at 35">{{WebCite|url=https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/return-jedi-35-secret-history-leias-golden-bikini-131634526.html|text="'Return of the Jedi' at 35: The secret history of Leia's golden bikini"|author=Alter, Ethan|date=2018-05-25|work=''Yahoo Entertainment''|archivedate=20210506004523}}</ref> She envisioned having twenty-five<ref name="SW Costumes" /> or forty-five yards of silk flowing through the air, but it was not feasible.<ref name="Making of ROTJ" /> The paintings of [[Wikipedia:Frank Frazetta|Frank Frazetta]], one of Lucas's favorite artists, were an inspiration for her.<ref name="SW Costumes" /> Although she has said that he had requested a Frazetta-inspired costume,<ref name="Yahoo ROTJ at 35" /> she was also quoted in [[Jonathan W. Rinzler's]] [[2013]] book ''[[The Making of Return of the Jedi]]'' as saying Lucas did ''not'' tell her "Frank Frazetta" when designing the costume.<ref name="Making of ROTJ" /> Leia's hairstyle was designed by [[Paul LeBlanc]]<ref name="SYFY: Paul LeBlanc">{{WebCite|url=https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire/obituary-paul-leblanc-star-wars|text="Paul LeBlanc, hairstylist for Princess Leia on Return of the Jedi, dies at 73"|author=Weiss, Josh|date=2019-10-07|work=[https://www.syfy.com/syfy-wire ''SYFY Wire'']|archivedate=20220813094619}}</ref> as one of the hairdressers for the film,<ref name="ROTJ" /> along with [[Patricia McDermott]], while artist [[Marilee Heyer]] illustrated LeBlanc's designs.<ref name="The Art of ROTJ 1994">''[[The Art of Star Wars Episode VI: Return of the Jedi]]'' {{C|[[1994]] reissue}}</ref>

====Costume fitting and construction====
{{Quote|It wasn't my choice. When he showed me the outfit, I didn't&mdash;I thought he was kidding, and it made me very nervous. And, you know, they wouldn't let me&mdash;I had to sit very straight 'cause I couldn't have lines in the side of&mdash;on my sides, you know, like a little crease. No creases were allowed. So I had to sit very, very rigid straight.|Carrie Fisher, asked about the costume's appearance|<ref name="NPR">{{WebCite|url=https://www.npr.org/transcripts/503580112|text="Carrie Fisher Opens Up About 'Star Wars,' The Gold Bikini And Her On-Set Affair"|author=[[Wikipedia:Terry Gross|Gross, Terry]]|date=2016-11-28|work=[https://www.npr.org/programs/fresh-air ''Fresh Air'']|archivedate=20210126164528}}</ref>}}
[[File:RichardMiller-Leia.png|thumb|right|200px|Jeweler Richard Miller describes the structural components of the costume.]]
Lucas had no objections to the costume concept but was doubtful that Fisher could be convinced to wear it. ''[[Star Wars Costumes: The Original Trilogy]]'', a [[2014]] reference book written by [[Brandon Alinger]], states that she did not hesitate;<ref name="SW Costumes" /> Fisher's own accounts differ. About two months before filming began,<ref name="Celebration Europe">{{SWYouTube|QpgNRJscUCE|Carrie Fisher: The Princess Diaries &#124; Star Wars Celebration Europe 2016|date=2016-07-21}}</ref> Lucas invited Fisher to [[Wikipedia:San Francisco|San Francisco]] to show her a picture of the costume.<ref name="ROTJ Commentary">Audio commentary on [[Star Wars: Episode VI Return of the Jedi|''Star Wars'': Episode VI ''Return of the Jedi'']] [[Star Wars Trilogy (DVD)|2004 DVD]]</ref> She later described herself as "aghast,"<ref name="The Princess Diarist">{{BookCite|author=[[Carrie Fisher|Fisher, Carrie]]|title=[[Wikipedia:The Princess Diarist|The Princess Diarist]]|publisher=Blue Rider Press|date=2016-11-22|isbn=0-399-17359-5}}</ref> thinking at first that he was kidding,<ref name="NPR" /><ref name="Celebration Anaheim">{{SWYouTube|wuW7vNrgT_g|Carrie Fisher Interview with StarWars.com &#124; Star Wars Celebration Anaheim|sw_url=video/carrie-fisher-interview-star-wars-celebration-anaheim|date=2015-04-29}} "I looked at that costume when [George Lucas] showed it to me and I thought: A. he's joking, B. how do I&mdash;how am I gonna look in that, how am I gonna&mdash;I have to make myself look better to be able to wear that, and then I got preoccupied with that." [&hellip;] "The worst thing about it, though, is they have these sites where they take people that were&mdash;what is that called, when you're a&mdash;''sex!'' No, centerfold." [&hellip;] "And so they did all these people that had iconic bathing suits and I'm there with, like, Raquel Welch&mdash;my peer&mdash;and Brigitte Bardot&mdash;also someone I identity with strongly. ''That'' crowd." [&hellip;] "And that, I didn't ''like'' that, because you have to live up to something there, and I forget what it is, but it wasn't something I was really focused on in ''that'' way."</ref> and she was very nervous about it.<ref name="NPR" /> For ''A New Hope'', she had been required to lose weight, while for ''The Empire Strikes Back'', she was told to gain.<ref name="Rolling Stone 1980" /> Believing Lucas had shown her the picture to successfully frighten her into exercising,<ref name="ROTJ Commentary" /> she grew preoccupied with how she would look in the costume.<ref name="Celebration Anaheim" /> Fisher experienced [[Wikipedia:Body dysmorphic disorder|body dysmorphia]] throughout her life.<ref name="The Daily Beast" /><ref name="WSJ">{{WebCite|url=https://www.wsj.com/articles/carrie-fisher-on-her-return-to-star-wars-1449088590|text="Carrie Fisher on Her Return to 'Star Wars'"|author=Calia, Michael|date=2015-12-02|work=[https://www.wsj.com ''The Wall Street Journal'']|archivedate=20151203042728}}</ref>

The so-called "metal bikini" was not made of metal.<ref name="SW Costumes" /><ref name="The Complete Saga" /><ref name="Power of Costume text">Exhibit text for Princess Leia: Jabba's Slave Bikini with Slave Collar and Chain. ''[[Rebel, Jedi, Princess, Queen: Star Wars and the Power of Costume]]''. 2016-11-13&ndash;2017-04-09, Denver Art Museum, Denver.</ref> Instead of making a full-body or torso cast of Fisher, which Miller described as an extremely uncomfortable process that required getting naked and having plaster thrown on one's body, he utilized a [[Wikipedia:Lost-wax casting|wax casting technique]] used in [[Wikipedia:Bronze sculpture|bronze sculpting]]. The costume components were first sculpted in soft wax around bendable armature wire at his home studio, placed in an ice chest, and transported to Lucas's Park Way house. Fisher's friend and fellow actress [[Wikipedia:Penny Marshall|Penny Marshall]] accompanied her there for the fitting with Rodis-Jamero, who explained the process to Fisher. By wearing the cold wax against her bare skin, her body heat warmed and softened the wax so she could shape it for a custom fit before he placed the structures back in the ice chest. Miller then used the wax to create molds and cast the final structural components in orange-colored, dense but flexible urethane rubber for Fisher to wear and a softer, more flexible latex rubber for Tracey Eddon's stunt version. The paint for the faux metal elements was touched up during filming with Treasure Gold polish.<ref name="SW Costumes" />

[[File:Leia-mannequin-closeup.png|thumb|left|250px|In the decades since filming, the flaking gold paint on the faux metal elements has exposed the urethane rubber.]]
The finished costume was made of silk, painted resin, leather, and rubber.<ref name="Power of Costume text" /> At a cost of $8,000, Miller produced a total of three sets of costume castings to have "one to use, one to lose, and one to break." When the castings were sent to ILM's [[Wikipedia:San Rafael, California|San Rafael]] wardrobe shop, Rodgers's team finalized the costume with fabric elements, and added leather linings<ref name="SW Costumes" /> to the backs of the structural pieces to improve its wearability for Fisher.<ref name="The Complete Saga" /> One of Miller's students at the College of Marin made the earrings, and boot maker John Shrader made the suede boots to which Miller added sculptural filigree.<ref name="SW Costumes" />

Fisher arrived in England shortly after New Year's Day in [[1982]] for makeup and costume fittings prior to the beginning of filming at [[Elstree Studios]].<ref name="Making of ROTJ" /> Due to Fisher's weight loss since the initial fitting, the costume did not have the intended snug fit. Miller made a replacement to send to England,<ref name="The Complete Saga" /> but he was never fully satisfied with its appearance in the film. Miller had designed the bra's support cords to be crossed in front, but the on-set dressers instead crossed them in the back, telling Miller that it had looked too confining.<ref name="SW Costumes" />

===Production===
====Filming====
{{Quote|The thing that killed me about this setup was, okay, you put me in this bathing suit&mdash;but then I have to stop talking from here on? Strip me&mdash;and I'm silent! I am defiant with everyone else&mdash;Tarkin, Darth Vader&mdash;but this slug really shuts me up. Any defiance I had in the other movies&mdash;all gone.|Carrie Fisher, regarding Leia Organa's characterization in the costume|<ref name="Making of ROTJ" />}}
[[File:CarrieFisherSmirk.jpg|thumb|right|300px|Carrie Fisher posing in her dancing-girl costume on the set of ''Return of the Jedi''.]]
"It was a problem keeping it in place," Fisher said in the [[Bantha Tracks 26|twenty-sixth issue]] of [[Bantha Tracks (department)|''Bantha Tracks'']], published in fall [[1984]]. "It drove the wardrobe person nuts."<ref name="Bantha Tracks 26">{{BanthaCite|26|An Hour with Howard Kazanjian, Warren Franklin, Richard Marquand, Sidney Ganis and Carrie Fisher}}</ref> Although not made of genuine metal,<ref name="SW Costumes" /> the costume still had little flexibility on Fisher and did not move with her body. Surrounded by a predominantly male cast and crew, she was initially embarrassed before eventually finding that "dignity was out of the question." At first, a prop man would check after shots to see if the top had slipped, and she then began checking herself between takes and would be asked about her breasts before they resumed filming.<ref name="People">{{BookCite|author=Jerome, Jim|container=Return of the Fisher|title=People|number=vol. 19 no. 22|publisher=Time Inc.|date=1983-06-06|issn=0093-7673|link=https://web.archive.org/web/20200516083856/https://people.com/archive/cover-story-return-of-the-fisher-vol-19-no-22}}</ref> She later made light of it to ''[[Star Wars Insider]]'' magazine: "It was like steel, not steel, but hard plastic, and if you stood behind me you could see straight to Florida. You'll have to ask [[Boba Fett]] about that."<ref name="Insider 68">{{InsiderCite|68|Carrie on... Star Wars, Celebrity & the Art of Conversation|Carrie on... ''Star Wars'', Celebrity & the Art of Conversation}}</ref>

Uncredited wardrobe assistant [[Janet Wakely|Janet Lucas-Wakely]], who dressed Fisher throughout filming, said the actress was happy in her princess dress and fighting gear but hated the dancing costume and was uncomfortable in it.<ref name="SW Costumes" /> The throne room shoots at Elstree Studios were freezing cold,<ref name="Starlog 71" /> but the more confined studio set for Jabba's sail barge grew so hot from the lighting that Fisher may have been the only one relatively comfortable with the temperature while the fully covered performers and crew struggled with the heat.<ref name="Making of ROTJ" />

Fisher enjoyed joking around with Jabba's puppeteers during downtime;<ref name="People" /> according to Marquand, Fisher loved Jabba during filming and would tell the crew operating the puppet's hand to pull the chain more taut so she would know how Leia felt.<ref name="Making of ROTJ" /> She later remarked that Leia's character changed when put in the costume, going from unafraid of [[Anakin Skywalker|Darth Vader]] and [[Grand Moff]] [[Wilhuff Tarkin|Tarkin]] to barely speaking while chained by the Hutt, despite Fisher attempting to [[Wikipedia:Ad libitum|ad lib]] lines. In her interview from the [[September]] 1983 issue of [[W:c:magazinesfromthepast:Starburst|''Starburst'']] magazine, she commented, "At that point I was amazed that Leia would just sit there, in those skimpy clothes, saying practically nothing. The only way they could justify that, I told them, was if Jabba pulled my chains real tight so I couldn't speak. I couldn't see my character ''not'' talking."<ref name="Starburst 61">{{BookCite|author=Team, Ed|container=Starburst Interview Section: Carrie Fisher|title=[[W:c:magazinesfromthepast:Starburst|''Starburst'']]|number=no. 61|publisher=Starburst Publishing Ltd|partialdate=[[September]] [[1983]]|issn=0955-114X|link=https://archive.org/details/Starburst_Magazine_061_1983-09_Marvel-UK}}</ref> Frustrated that Leia had changed from defiant to silent in the costume, Fisher said that she decided to become a writer while shooting the palace scenes.<ref name="Making of ROTJ" />

In addition to her weight loss, Fisher was required to sit up straight in front of Jabba so that she would not have lines or wrinkles around her waist from bending or moving.<ref name="ROTJ Commentary" /> The discomfort of maintaining the rigid posture while wearing the costume contributed to her satisfaction in the scene where Leia strangled and killed Jabba with the chain.<ref name="NPR" /> She was asked if she wanted a stunt double to do the scene but said she really wanted to kill Jabba herself.<ref name="Making of ROTJ" />

[[File:CarrieFisher_and_TraceyEddon.jpg|thumb|left|275px|Carrie Fisher and stunt double Tracey Eddon, who were not supposed to be tanned, sunbathed in the Yuma Desert.]]
The desert exterior scenes were filmed outside of [[Yuma]], [[Wikipedia:Arizona|Arizona]] in [[April]] 1982.<ref name="Making of ROTJ" /> In the downtime between filming, Fisher and Eddon would sunbathe in their costumes. Fisher told ''Starlog'' that this angered the crew as they were not supposed to be tanned;<ref name="Starlog 71" /> she would later say they were popular with the crew during those times.<ref name="Making of ROTJ" /> Sunbathing gave her and Eddon a chance to have fun while feeling ridiculous in their costumes,<ref name="Starlog 71" /> and she would fondly recall decades later that they were "like the [[Wikipedia:Doublemint|Doublemint]] Intergalactic Twins."<ref name="Celebration VI">''[[Celebration VI]]''</ref>

Fisher wore the costume with a woven cloak over it and black goggles for a sandstorm scene that was filmed for ''Return of the Jedi'' but ultimately deleted.<ref name="SW Costumes" /> Sometime during the production, Fisher also wore the costume while portraying herself in footage shot for the unfinished mockumentary ''[[Return of the Ewok]]''. The short film would have starred [[Warwick Davis]] as his [[Ewok]] character [[Wicket Wystri Warrick]]. Davis shared a clip of Fisher's scene at the [[2016]] [[Celebration Europe (2016)|Celebration Europe]] convention. In it, Wicket asks the costumed actors [[Harrison Ford]], [[Mark Hamill]], and Fisher where the Ewoks were supposed to report for filming ''Revenge of the Jedi''.<ref name="Celebration Europe" />

====Marketing====
[[File:RollingStone400-401.jpg|thumb|right|250px|''Rolling Stone'' cover photograph by Aaron Rapoport]]
While in England in [[February]] 1982, Fisher did a promotional photoshoot wearing her various ''Return of the Jedi'' costumes, including the dancing-girl costume. Those and other photographs of Fisher in the bikini were subsequently utilized in marketing for the film. Multiple artists also created poster concept art. Two proposals by [[John Alvin]] rendered the costume with a bikini bottom rather than a skirt. Ultimately, Japanese-American [[Kazuhiko Sano]]'s artwork became the [[:File:ReturnOfTheJediPoster1983.jpg|"Style B" poster]], with Leia centered in the bottom half.<ref name="Making of ROTJ" />

In 1983, a production still of Leia in the costume was featured on the cover of [https://people.com ''People''] magazine's [[June 6]] issue, which included an interview with Fisher.<ref name="People" /> She did a beach photo shoot with [[Wikipedia:Aaron Rapoport|Aaron Rapoport]] for ''Rolling Stone'', and the photographs were featured on the cover and in an interview with Fisher for the [[July]] 1983 issue. The cover depicts her as Leia Organa sitting on a beach towel alongside an Ewok, Darth Vader holding a [[Wikipedia:Boombox|boombox]], and a [[Gamorrean]] guard holding a [[Wikipedia:Beach ball|beach ball]].<ref name="Rolling Stone 1983" /> Fisher later shared during convention panels, such as her "Date with a Princess" at [[Celebration VI]] in [[2012]], that she really liked doing the photo shoot.<ref name="Celebration VI" />

===Reception and legacy===
====By Carrie Fisher====
{{Quote|Listen! I am not a sex symbol, so that's an opinion of someone. I don't share that.''"<br />"''I don't think that's the right&mdash;''"<br />"''Word for it? Fisher sometimes expressed pride looking back at how she had appeared in the costume,<ref name="The Daily Beast" /><ref name="Insider 68" /> but during the time that she wore it and for years later, she felt otherwise. In 2011, she was a guest on [[Wikipedia:The Oprah Winfrey Show|''The Oprah Winfrey Show'']] and discussed becoming a spokesperson for the [[Wikipedia:Jenny Craig, Inc.|Jenny Craig]] [[Wikipedia:Weight loss|weight loss]] program, saying, "I didn't even realize at the time that I was sort of the ''Star Wars'' pin-up, the geek pin-up. I thought I was fat then, so now finally it all caught up with me."<ref name="Oprah">"Living Legend Debbie Reynolds and Her Daughter, Icon Carrie Fisher." [[Wikipedia:The Oprah Winfrey Show|''The Oprah Winfrey Show'']], hosted by [[Wikipedia:Oprah Winfrey|Oprah Winfrey]], aired [[February 11]], [[2011]].</ref> Later that year, she wrote in her memoir [[Wikipedia:Shockaholic|''Shockaholic'']] that reports of her death would include "a picture of a stern-looking girl wearing some kind of metal bikini lounging on a giant drooling squid."<ref name="Shockaholic">{{BookCite|author=[[Carrie Fisher|Fisher, Carrie]]|title=Shockaholic|publisher=[https://www.simonandschuster.com Simon & Schuster]|date=2011-11-01|isbn=978-0743264822}}</ref>

[[File:Carrie_and_Gary_Fisher_wax.jpg|thumb|left|300px|Carrie and her beloved dog Gary Fisher posed with her wax figure at Madame Tussauds in May 2016.]]
At [[Celebration Anaheim (2015)|Celebration Anaheim]] in [[2015]], Fisher told host [[James Arnold Taylor]] that she disliked being regarded as a [[Wikipedia:Centerfold|centerfold]] in an iconic bathing suit and that she had not thought of herself that way while wearing it.<ref name="Celebration Anaheim" /> Also in that year, she advised her [[sequel trilogy]] co-star [[Daisy Ridley]], who portrayed [[Rey Skywalker|Rey]], to fight if asked to wear similar costumes.<ref name="Interview Magazine" />

Fisher wrote about her faux metal "Jabba Killer" bikini in her final memoir, [[Wikipedia:The Princess Diarist|''The Princess Diarist'']],<ref name="The Princess Diarist" /> which was published on [[November 22]], 2016,<ref name="Penguin: Princess Diarist">{{PenguinRandomHouse|url=books/317741/the-princess-diarist-by-carrie-fisher|text="The Princess Diarist by Carrie Fisher"}}</ref> several weeks before her death on [[December 27]].<ref name="Death">{{SW|url=news/carrie-fisher-our-princess-passes-away|text=Carrie Fisher, Our Princess, Passes Away}}</ref> She commented on [[Wikipedia:Cosplay|cosplaying]] at [[Wikipedia:Science fiction convention|conventions]] by women and men who looked fantastic in the costume and wrote that killing Jabba the Hutt was her favorite moment of her personal film history. One of her anecdotes was about her wax figure at [[Wikipedia:Madame Tussauds|Madame Tussauds]], which she visited in [[May]] of that year with her emotional support dog Gary Fisher. In her closing remarks, she wrote that if she had never been Princess Leia, she would "[n]ever have been asked if I thought I'd been objectified by silently wearing a gold bikini, while seated on a giant laughing cruel slug, while everyone chatted gaily around me."<ref name="The Princess Diarist" />

====In ''Star Wars''====
[[File:Diva_Shaliqua_Episode_I_Snapshot.jpg|thumb|right|200px|Behind the scenes photograph of an extra portraying Diva Shaliqua]]
A dancing-girl costume appeared in [[Star Wars: Episode I The Phantom Menace|''Star Wars'': Episode I ''The Phantom Menace'']], the first [[prequel trilogy]] film<ref name="TPM" /> which opened on [[May 19]], [[1999]].<ref name="Year by Year" /> It was worn by an uncredited extra<ref name="TPM" /> whose character was later identified as Diva Shaliqua.<ref name="Episode I TVD">''[[Star Wars: Episode I: The Visual Dictionary]]''</ref> The ''Jedi Temple Archives'' special feature for the DVD set [[Star Wars: The Clone Wars The Complete Season Three|''Star Wars: The Clone Wars'' The Complete Season Three]] includes concept art for the chorus girls seen in the ''[[Star Wars: The Clone Wars]]'' third season episode "[[Hunt for Ziro]],"<ref name="TCW S3" /> which first aired on [[November 12]], [[2010]].<ref name="Hunt for Ziro EG">{{SW|url=series/clone-wars/hunt-for-ziro-episode-guide|text="Hunt for Ziro" Episode Guide &#124; The Clone Wars}}</ref> The art notes that their attire is "similar to slave Leia bikini."<ref name="TCW S3" /> Other versions of the costume appeared throughout the [[Star Wars Legends|''Star Wars'' Legends]] continuity, including in [[Star Wars comic books|comic books]]<ref name="Jango Fett">[[Star Wars: Jango Fett (one-shot)|''Star Wars: Jango Fett'']]</ref> and multiple [[Star Wars video games|video game]]s.<ref name="SWG: Bikini">{{SWGcite|exp=AED|type=item|Metal Bikini" and "Extremely Revealing Skirt}}</ref><ref name="KOTOR II">''[[Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic II: The Sith Lords]]''</ref><ref name="SWTOR">{{TORcite|type=item|Slave Girl Hat," "Slave Girl Top," "Slave Girl Bottom," "Slave Girl Slippers," and "Slave Girl Bracelets|[[Gandra Palis|Gandra]] in [[Slippery Slopes Cantina]]}}</ref><ref name="LEGO Star Wars II">''[[LEGO Star Wars II: The Original Trilogy]]''</ref> Leia's version also appears throughout the current [[canon]] continuity established on [[April 25]], 2014<ref name="EU Turns a New Page">{{SW|url=news/the-legendary-star-wars-expanded-universe-turns-a-new-page|text=The Legendary Star Wars Expanded Universe Turns a New Page}}</ref> and in non-canonical material released since the continuity split; for instance, she wore it in the mobile game ''[[Star Wars: Galactic Defense]]'',<ref name="Galactic Defense">''[[Star Wars: Galactic Defense]]''</ref> released on [[October 30]] of that year,<ref name="Galactic Defense Date">{{WebCite|url=https://dena.com/intl/press/2091|text="Disney, Lucasfilm and DeNA Release ''Star Wars: Galactic Defense'' for Mobile Devices"|date=2014-10-30|work=[[DeNA]] Co., Ltd.|archivedate=20221010002740}}</ref> as an unlockable "melee champion" that players could deploy into a battlefield.<ref name="Galactic Defense" /> Artist [[Diego Zúñiga]] created concept art for the design<ref name="ArtStation Zúñiga">{{ArtStation|username=novanim|url=artwork/RBGLW|name=[[Diego Zúñiga]]|text="Star Wars: Galactic Defense" Characters|nolive=1}}</ref> while [[David Marín]] illustrated the character's in-game key art.<ref name="ArtStation Marín">{{ArtStation|username=louten|url=artwork/NDVlJ|name=[[David Marín]]|text=Star Wars: Galactic Defense}}</ref>

The film-used costume was exhibited as part of [[Star Wars: The Magic of Myth|''Star Wars'': The Magic of Myth]] at the [[Wikipedia:National Air and Space Museum|National Air and Space Museum]] from 1997 to 1999 before going on tour with the [[Wikipedia:Smithsonian Institution|Smithsonian Institution Traveling Exhibition Service]].<ref name="Magic of Myth">{{WebCite|url=https://airandspace.si.edu/exhibitions/star-wars/online/sw-unit12.htm|text="Star Wars @ NASM, Unit 12, The Hero's Return"|author=[[Wikipedia:National Air and Space Museum|National Air and Space Museum]]|date=1997-11-22|work=[https://airandspace.si.edu/exhibitions/star-wars/online ''Star Wars:The Magic of Myth, National Air and Space Museum'']|archivedate=20210513004331}}</ref> It was the put on display in ''[[Star Wars Identities: The Exhibition]]''.<ref name="Insider 150">{{InsiderCite|150|Launchpad|Launch Pad}}</ref> The Smithsonian Institution exhibited it on tour again from 2015 to [[2018]] as part of ''[[Rebel, Jedi, Princess, Queen: Star Wars and the Power of Costume]]''.<ref name="Power of Costume Itinerary">{{WebCite|url=http://www.powerofcostume.si.edu/intinerary.html|text="Itinerary"|author=[https://www.sites.si.edu Smithsonian Institution Traveling Exhibition Service]|work=[https://www.powerofcostume.si.edu/index.html ''Rebel, Jedi, Princess, Queen: Star Wars and the Power of Costume'']|archivedate=20201111181401|partialdate=[[2017]]}}</ref>

In 2015, Richard Miller put a set-used costume, design materials, and his concept sculpts up as an auction lot in a film memorabilia auction.<ref name="Auction">{{WebCite|url=https://www.profilesinhistory.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/Hollywood74web.pdf|text="Lot 1558. Carrie Fisher 'Slave Leia' costume collection and display from ''Star Wars: Episode VI &mdash; Return of the Jedi''."|author=[[Wikipedia:Profiles in History|Profiles in History]]|format=PDF|work=''Hollywood Auction 74''|pages=[https://profilesinhistory.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/Hollywood74web.pdf#page=518 p.518-9]|archivedate=20190612113128|partialdate=[[September]] [[2015]]}}</ref> The costume was sent back to Miller and not used in filming because it did not fit Fisher's changed measurements. The winning bidder was [[Gus Lopez]], a ''Star Wars'' collector and author of articles on collecting who operates [http://theswca.com ''The'' Star Wars ''Collectors Archive''] website. He wrote an article about his acquisition for [[StarWars.com]]<ref name="The Mystery of the Princess Leia Costume">{{SW|url=news/the-mystery-of-the-princess-leia-costume|text=The Mystery of the Princess Leia Costume|nolive=1}}</ref> and posted photographs of the various items on his website.<ref name="The SWCA">{{WebCite|url=http://theswca.com/index.php?action=disp_item&item_id=81867|text="Slave Leia Costume"|work=[http://theswca.com ''The'' Star Wars ''Collectors Archive'']|archivedate=20221217190542}}</ref> In [[July]] [[2024]], an auction hosted by Heritage Auctions sold a seven piece dancing-girl costume worn by Fisher for $175,000.<ref name="BBC Auction">{{WebCite|url=https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cn08q0qg2lzo|text=Princess Leia's Star Wars bikini fetches $175,000|author=Gallagher, Charlotte|date=2024-07-24|work=[[Wikipedia:BBC|BBC]]|archiveurl=https://archive.is/6HlFl}}</ref>

====Controversy about merchandise====
{{Quote|The father who flipped out about it, 'What am I going to tell my kid about why she's in that outfit?' Tell them that a giant slug captured me and forced me to wear that stupid outfit, and then I killed him because I didn't like it. And then I took it off. Backstage.|Carrie Fisher, in ''Wall Street Journal''|<ref name="WSJ" />}}
The news station FOX 29 Philadelphia ran a story on July 13, 2015 titled "Star Wars Action Figure Has Parents Furious" about a father who was shocked that stores were selling [[Hasbro]]'s<ref name="Fox29">{{WebCite|url=https://www.fox29.com/news/star-wars-action-figure-has-parents-furious|text="Star Wars Action Figure Has Parents Furious"|author=FOX 29 Philadelphia|date=2015-07-13|work=[https://www.fox29.com fox29.com]|archivedate=20220920190553}}</ref> "Princess Leia (Slave Outfit)" action figure, which had been released in 2013 as part of ''[[Star Wars: The Black Series]]''.<ref name="Black Series">{{HasbroCite|set=Star Wars: The Black Series|text=''Star Wars: The Black Series'' (2013)|packtype=6" Figure|pack=#05: Princess Leia (Slave Outfit)|altlink=https://www.rebelscum.com/hasbro-star-wars/the-black-series/6-inch-figures/princess-leia-slave-outfit-05/default.asp}}</ref> The story went viral<ref name="Los Angeles Times 2015">{{WebCite|url=https://www.latimes.com/entertainment/herocomplex/la-ca-hc-the-women-of-star-wars-the-force-awakens-20151206-htmlstory.html|text="The women of 'Star Wars' speak out about their new Empire"|author=Woerner, Meredith|date=2015-12-04|work=[https://www.latimes.com ''Los Angeles Times'']|quote=How about telling his daughter that the character is wearing that outfit not because she's chosen to wear it. She's been forced to wear it. She's a prisoner of a giant testicle who has a lot of saliva going on and she does not want to wear that thing and it's ultimately that chain, which you're now indicating is some sort of accessory to S&M, that is used to kill the giant saliva testicle&hellip;. That's asinine.|archivedate=20151205021746}}</ref> and led to rumors that [[The Walt Disney Company]] would be phasing out all imagery and merchandise featuring "Slave Leia," including comic book artist [[Wikipedia:J. Scott Campbell|J. Scott Campbell]] alleging that Disney was actively in the process of doing so.<ref name="FANgirl Blog">{{WebCite|url=http://fangirlblog.com/2015/11/slave-leia-deserves-equal-treatment-respecting-star-wars-canon|text="Slave Leia Deserves Equal Treatment: Respecting Star Wars Canon"|author=[[Tricia Barr|Barr, Tricia]]|date=2015-11-10|work=[http://fangirlblog.com ''FANgirl Blog'']|archivedate=20201130075942}}</ref> When asked to comment, Fisher disagreed with the idea of eliminating it. In the newspaper [https://www.wsj.com ''The Wall Street Journal''], she addressed a specific complaint regarding how parents could explain to their children why Leia wore it: the bikini and chain were not her choice, but she used the latter to kill Jabba and then removed the former.<ref name="WSJ" /> She made similar comments to [https://www.thedailybeast.com ''The Daily Beast'']<ref name="The Daily Beast" /> and the ''Los Angeles Times''.<ref name="Los Angeles Times 2015" />

An interview with Lucasfilm president [[Kathleen Kennedy]] in the February 2016 issue of [https://www.vanityfair.com ''Vanity Fair''] magazine addressed the merchandise rumors, saying that Disney was not banning the bikini's image from future ''Star Wars'' paraphernalia. The article noted that Kennedy had a statuette outside her office of the ''Return of the Jedi'' cast, including Leia in the costume. Kennedy doubted Lucas would put Leia in a bikini if the film were made in more recent times. [[Wikipedia:Mellody Hobson|Mellody Hobson]], Lucas's wife, disagreed with Kennedy; she said, "George is not apologetic about that bikini," and added, "He thinks that was a very important scene. He would probably do the same thing today. He is not apologetic at all."<ref name="Vanity Fair">{{WebCite|url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2016/02/kathleen-kennedy-hollywood-producer|text="Meet the Most Powerful Woman in Hollywood"|author=Ellison, Sarah|date=2016-02-08|work=[https://www.vanityfair.com ''Vanity Fair'']|archivedate=20160209074900}}</ref>

[[File:Jabba_choking_Luke.png|thumb|left|225px|In "Gambit on Geonosis," LEGO versions of Leia Organa and C-3PO try to stop Jabba the Hutt from choking on Luke Skywalker.]]
Contrary to the rumors that began in 2015, Disney continued depicting Leia wearing the costume. Later that year, it appeared on [[July 28]]<ref name="Little Golden Book: Date">{{PenguinRandomHouse|url=books/253638/star-wars-return-of-the-jedi-star-wars-by-geof-smith-illustrated-by-ron-cohee/9780736435482|text=Star Wars: Return of the Jedi (Star Wars)}}</ref> in the [[Return of the Jedi (Little Golden Book)|''Return of the Jedi'' Little Golden Book]] by author [[Geof Smith]] and illustrator [[Ron Cohee]],<ref name="Little Golden Book">[[Return of the Jedi (Little Golden Book)|''Return of the Jedi'' Little Golden Book]]</ref> as well as in "[[Gambit on Geonosis]]," the finale of the non-canonical [[Disney XD]] television series ''[[LEGO Star Wars: Droid Tales]]''<ref name="Droid Tales">{{DroidTales|Gambit on Geonosis}}</ref> that first aired on [[November 2]].<ref name="Droid Tales: Date">{{DisneyXDYouTube|f5fII_1N-uQ|Gambit on Geonosis Trailer}}</ref> ''[[Star Wars Galaxy of Adventures]]'', an animated microseries adapting stories from the ''Star Wars'' films, depicted Leia wearing the attire in episodes that debuted on [[StarWarsKids.com]] in 2019: "[[Luke vs. Jabba - Sail Barge Escape]]" on [[May 10]]<ref name="Luke vs. Jabba">{{GOA|Luke vs. Jabba - Sail Barge Escape}}</ref> and "[[Luke vs. the Rancor - Wrath of the Rancor]]" on [[May 31]].<ref name="Luke vs. The Rancor">{{GOA|Luke vs. the Rancor - Wrath of the Rancor}}</ref> The [[2022]] video game ''[[LEGO Star Wars: The Skywalker Saga]]'' also includes Leia wearing the costume.<ref name="The Skywalker Saga">''[[LEGO Star Wars: The Skywalker Saga]]''</ref>

====Canonizing the Huttslayer====
{{Quote|Not only did I like recasting that outfit as a memory of Leia being really strong and kick-ass, but think about it&mdash;for a human being to kill a Hutt with her bare hands? That's unbelievable. Anybody who would be able to pull that off would be remembered for it. That would be legend.|Claudia Gray, on reframing "Slave Leia" as "Huttslayer"|<ref name="EW Bloodline">{{WebCite|url=https://ew.com/article/2016/05/03/star-wars-bloodline-princess-leia|text=''Star Wars: Bloodline'': A galactic princess haunted by her past|archivedate=20200606022736|author=Breznican, Anthony|date=2016-05-03}}</ref>}}
[[File:SW_Bloodline_cover.jpg|thumb|right|220px|''Bloodline'' by Claudia Gray canonized the fanon nickname "Huttslayer" and identified the outfit as a "dancing-girl costume."]]
During the peak of the merchandising controversy, a [[Wikipedia:Twitter|Twitter]] user with the pseudonym Angie P.<ref name="Twitter: Angie">{{Twitter|gospelflare|status/624285607724978176|Angie P.|quote=Can we petition to replace the term "Slave Leia" with "Leia the Huttslayer"? (cc:@FullOfSith)|date=2015-07-23|archiveurl=https://archive.is/bbDoN}}</ref> called for Leia to be known as the "Huttslayer" instead of "Slave Leia."<ref name="Wired 2016">{{WebCite|url=https://www.wired.com/2016/05/star-wars-leia-book|text="Leia Gets Her Badassery Back in the New Star Wars Book"|author=Kehe, Jason|date=2016-05-20|work=[https://www.wired.com ''Wired'']|archivedate=20160521121842}}</ref> The 2016 novel [[Bloodline (novel)|''Bloodline'']], written by [[Claudia Gray]], canonized the term "Huttslayer."<ref name="Bloodline" /> Gray credited Angie P. for bringing the [[fanon]] term to her attention and said she noticed it around the time she was writing that section of the book. Rather than referring to it in ''Bloodline'' as a slave outfit, she identified it as a dancing-girl costume and treated Leia's actions in the Huttslayer outfit as legendary [[in-universe]].<ref name="EW Bloodline" />

The 2018 [[Dorling Kindersley|DK Publishing]] reference book ''[[Star Wars: The Complete Visual Dictionary, New Edition]]'', written by [[Pablo Hidalgo]] and [[David West Reynolds (real person)|David Reynolds]] and published on [[September 18]],<ref name="TCVD NE: Date">{{PenguinRandomHouse|url=books/586309/star-wars-the-complete-visual-dictionary-new-edition-by-pablo-hidalgo-and-david-reynolds|text=Star Wars The Complete Visual Dictionary New Edition}}</ref> includes the costume on a page that largely matches<ref name="TCVD NE" /> the original version from the [[1998]] reference book ''[[Star Wars: The Visual Dictionary]]''. However, the updated version changes the label "Slave girl harness" and the heading "Jabba's Slave"<ref name="SWTVD">''[[Star Wars: The Visual Dictionary]]''</ref> to "Bronzium harness" and "Huttslayer," respectively.<ref name="TCVD NE" />

===Details disputed by Carrie Fisher===
On Twitter in 2015, Fisher responded to a request by the user @hamstachick to fact-check the article<ref name="Twitter: Fisher">{{Twitter|carrieffisher|status/608670727634427905|[[Carrie Fisher]]|archivedate=20210523230252}}</ref> "15 Interesting Facts About the Slave Leia Costume [Movies]," which was published in 2014 by ''The Geek Twins'' blog.<ref name="The Geek Twins 15">{{WebCite|url=http://www.thegeektwins.com/2014/02/15-interesting-facts-about-slave-leia.html|text="15 Interesting Facts About the Slave Leia Costume <nowiki>[Movies]</nowiki>"|author=Mitchell, Nigel G|date=2014-02-11|work=[http://www.thegeektwins.com ''The Geek Twins'']|archivedate=20210121181707}}</ref> In a follow-up to Fisher's tweet, ''The Geek Twins'' published a new article that listed their sources for the prior article, including<ref name="The Geek Twins Carrie Fisher">{{WebCite|url=http://www.thegeektwins.com/2015/06/carrie-fisher-fact-checked-our-slave.html|text="Carrie Fisher Fact-Checked Our 'Slave Leia' Article: The Run-down"|author=Mitchell, Nigel|date=2015-06-11|work=[http://www.thegeektwins.com ''The Geek Twins'']|archivedate=20210128160924}}</ref> the [[2006]] article "Star Wars Secrets: Leia's Teeny Bikini" on the website [https://www.ign.com ''IGN'']<ref name="IGN">{{WebCite|url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2006/08/24/star-wars-secrets-leias-teeny-bikini|text="Star Wars Secrets: Leia's Teeny Bikini"|author=Gilchrist, Todd|date=2006-08-24|work=[https://www.ign.com ''IGN'']|archivedate=20210514150705}}</ref> and a "Slave Leia costume" article on the ''Star Wars'' fan wiki {{Srlink|Wookieepedia}}.<ref name="Wookieepedia">{{WookCite|url=Special:PermanentLink/4765709|text=Slave Leia costume}}, as it appeared on February 5, 2014 prior to the first article on [http://www.thegeektwins.com ''The Geek Twins'']</ref> From the details cited to Wookieepedia, Fisher said it was not true that a moldmaker was replaced when he was too excited about prospectively making a mold of her torso. Another she indicated was not true was an item stating two things: her being unhappy with the costume and calling it "what supermodels will eventually wear in the seventh ring of hell."<ref name="The Geek Twins Carrie Fisher" /> As the quoted phrase came from a 1999 [[Wikipedia:Newsweek|''Newsweek'']] magazine article written by Fisher,<ref name="Newsweek">{{BookCite|author=[[Carrie Fisher|Fisher, Carrie]]|container=Postcards From The Edge Of The Galaxy|title=Newsweek|number=vol. 133, no. 20|publisher=[[Wikipedia:Graham Holdings|The Washington Post Company]]|date=1999-05-17|issn=0028-9604|link=https://web.archive.org/web/20141224071250/http://www.newsweek.com/postcards-edge-galaxy-167072}}</ref> it was unclear to ''The Geek Twins'' what aspect of the two-part claim Fisher considered untrue.<ref name="The Geek Twins Carrie Fisher" />
