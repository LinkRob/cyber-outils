'''Aurebesh''' was a [[writing]] system comprising 34 letters used to transcribe [[Galactic Basic Standard]], the most spoken [[language]] in [[the galaxy]].<ref name="VE">''[[Star Wars: The Visual Encyclopedia]]''</ref> In the [[Outer Rim Territories]], Aurebesh was sometimes used alongside [[Outer Rim Basic]], another alphabet.<ref name="Empire Concept">{{SW|url=series/star-wars-rebels/empire-day-concept-art-gallery|text="Empire Day" Concept Art Gallery}} {{C|Slide 2}}</ref>

==Examples of Aurebesh throughout the galaxy==
===In general===
Aurebesh was often used inside [[macrobinoculars]], in the [[Republic Era]],<ref name="Ambush">{{SW|url=ambush-trivia-gallery|text="Ambush" Trivia Gallery|quote=The aurebesh <small>''[sic]''</small> legend at the bottom of Thire's macrobinocular viewplate reads "infrared mode". The top reads "regular mode."}} {{C|Slide 2}}</ref> the [[Imperial Era]]<ref name="Rogue One">''[[Rogue One: A Star Wars Story]]''</ref> and the [[New Republic Era]].<ref name="Chapter 9: The Marshal">{{TheMandalorian|Chapter 9: The Marshal}}</ref>

The [[Galactic Credit Standard|credit]] symbol (
[[File:AurebeshSans-Serif_credit.png|class=darkmodeinvert]]) resembled an Aurebesh letter [[resh]] with two vertical strokes at the top.<ref name="Commander">''[[Star Wars: Commander]]''</ref>

===Republic Era===
Aurebesh text could be seen on the screens of various types of technology, including screens in the cockpits of [[N-1 starfighter]]s,<ref name="Episode I">{{Film|I}}</ref> and the [[Confederacy of Independent Systems|Separatist]] facility known as [[Skytop Station]].<ref name="Duel">{{TCW|Duel of the Droids}}</ref>

During the [[Clone Wars]], some [[clone trooper]]s customized their [[Clone trooper armor|armor]] with Aurebesh phrases. The back of clone trooper [[Ponds]]' [[helmet]] had the phrase "SOME [[Gender|GUYS]] HAVE ALL THE LUCK" written in Aurebesh.<ref name="Ponds">{{Databank|clone-commander-ponds|Clone Commander Ponds|quote=A veteran of such battlefields as Geonosis, Ryloth, and Malastare, Ponds had an Aurebesh phrase stenciled on the back of his helmet that read, "some guys have all the luck."}}</ref> Others got Aurebesh tattoos. [[Cloning|Clone]] [[medic]] [[Kix]] had the phrase "A GOOD [[Droid|DROID]] IS A [[Death|DEAD]] ONE" [[tattoo]]ed on the side of his head,<ref name="Deserter">{{TCW|The Deserter}}</ref> although the tattoo was eventually covered up when he grew his [[hair]] out late in the war,<ref name="The Bad Batch">{{TCW|The Bad Batch (episode)}}</ref> while trooper [[Sketch]] had his initial, [[Senth]], tattooed on his forehead.<ref name="The Hidden Enemy">{{TCW|The Hidden Enemy}}</ref> In the same vein, some [[Low Altitude Assault Transport]]s were personalized with Aurebesh, such as the ''[[Spaceward Ho!]]''<ref name="Landing at Point Rain">{{TCW|Landing at Point Rain}}</ref> and ''[[Lucky Lekku]]'', both of which bore their names,<ref name="10 Things in TCW 7" /> and the ''[[Crumb Bomber]]'', which bore the phrase "LAUGH THIS OFF".<ref name="10 Things in TCW 7">{{SWArchive|url=kids/read/cwspotter20080822.html|text=Checklist: 10 Things to Spot in ''The Clone Wars''|date=2010-08-10|quote=This blue one, labeled the "Lucky Lekku", is seen on one of the gunships that brings the clones to Teth. (A "lekku" is a tentacle hanging from a Twi'lek's head). This one, of a Kowakian monkey lizard, is seen in the hangar bay of the ship Obi-Wan Kenobi lands on after returning from Tatooine. It says "Laugh This Off!"}} {{C|Note that the guide erroneously says "Laugh this off!" with an exclamation point}}</ref> The [[LAAT/le patrol gunship|patrol transports]] used on Coruscant were emblazoned with the word "[[Law enforcement agency|POLICE]]".<ref name="SW-To Catch a Jedi Trivia Gallery">{{SW|url=series/clone-wars/to-catch-a-jedi-trivia-gallery|text="To Catch a Jedi" Trivia Gallery}} {{C|Slide 5}}</ref>

Establishments throughout the galaxy used Aurebesh signs to identify themselves, such as the [[The Hole]] and [[Purple Werm]].<ref name="Heist Trivia">{{SW|url=series/clone-wars/holocron-heist-trivia-gallery|text="Holocron Heist" Trivia Gallery|quote=Among the signage seen on the rather seedy buildings of the Coruscant underworld is an establishment called "The Hole," and "Purple Wer".}} {{C|Slide 4}} {{C|Note that the Trivia Gallery erroneously says "PURPLE WER"; that is because there is no light in the last symbol on the neon sign; making "WERM" look like "WER"}}</ref> The [[Droid Spa]] on [[Coruscant]] used one such sign to show the services it provided. The sign read "OIL CHANGES, RUST REMOVAL", "MEMORY FLUSHING, POLISHING MOTIVATOR REPAIR, DE-FLUTTERING, [[Restraining bolt|RESTRAINING BOLT]] APPLICATION AND REMOVAL, GENERAL MAINTENANCE OVERHAULS, JUNK REMOVAL".<ref name="Evil Plans Trivia">{{SW|url=series/clone-wars/evil-plans-trivia-gallery|text="Evil Plans" Trivia Gallery|quote=The signage outside the droid spa promises "oil changes, rust removal, memory flushing, polishing, motivator repair, de-fluttering, restraining bolt application and removal, general maintenance overhauls, junk removal" while the signs inside promise "recharge, repolish, repair" and "refit."}} {{C|Slide 5}}</ref> Several [[Painting|posters]] with Aurebesh text hung in the [[Trueping's]] bar during the Clone Wars, including one for "DJ [[Rangthang|RANGTHANG]]," and one for for [[Sy Snootles]] (also seen in the bar room) that read "THE SY SNOOTLES SHOW" • "IN PERSON" • "SY SNOOTLES" • "SOMEWHERE ON CORUSCANT" • "[[Friday|FRI]] – [[Saturday|SAT]] 12 – 4" • "SOLD OUT".<ref name="Deception Trivia">{{SW|url=series/clone-wars/deception-trivia-gallery|text="Deception" Trivia Gallery|quote=Among the posters seen in the bar's back room where Hardeen sleeps is one for DJ Rangthang, one for Sy Snootles, a Felucian pin-up, and a travel poster for Pantora. In the corridor leading to the room is a poster for a "Miss Theelin" beauty pageant.}} {{C|Slide 4}}</ref>

Technical readouts used by the [[Galactic Republic]] during the Clone Wars were also written in Aurebesh.<ref name="Downfall Trivia">{{SW|url=series/clone-wars/downfall-of-a-droid-trivia-gallery|text="Downfall of a Droid" Trivia Gallery|quote=The Atte Rul3z text seen in "Rookies" appears once again (though, technically, this one came first... and fits the context quite well), this time on Ahsoka's tactical screen aboard the Resolute. Wanted posters in the [[Jedi Archives]] on Coruscant were written in Aurebesh text.<ref name="Lightsaber Lost">{{TCW|Lightsaber Lost}}</ref> Similarly, bounties in Aurebesh were posted on the walls of [[Chalmun's Spaceport Cantina]], including information such as "[[Nim Mindbend|NIM MINDBEND]]" – "WANTED": "DEAD" for "KIDNAPPING, THEFT AND MURDER" – "REWARD: 120,000"; [[Omar Toggs|OMAR TOGGS]] – "WANTED": "DEAD" or "ALIVE" for "RACKETEERING AND EXTORTION" – "REWARD: 75,000"; "[[Zug Tassik|ZUG TASSIK]]" – "WANTED": "DEAD" or "ALIVE" for "MASS MURDER" – "REWARD: 300,000"; "SAVAGE OPRESS" – "WANTED" for "FLEEING THE SCENE OF A CRIME" – "REWARD: 550,000".<ref name="Revenge Trivia">{{SW|url=series/clone-wars/revenge-trivia-gallery|text="Revenge" Trivia Gallery|quote=Among the bounties listed are: Nim Mindbend, a Siniteen, wanted dead for 120,000 credits for kidnapping, theft and murder; Omar Toggs, a Rodian, wanted dead or alive for 75,000 credits for racketeering and extortion; and Zug Tassik, a Trandoshan, wanted dead or alive for 800,000 for mass murder. Though the hunters say Savage is worth a million credits, his on-screen bounty only lists 550,000. His warrant lists "fleeing the scene of a crime."}} {{C|Slide 2}}</ref>

The [[electro-proton bomb]] that was attached to [[Goji]] and [[Rod]]'s [[BTL-B Y-wing starfighter/bomber|bomber]] and dropped during the [[Battle of Malastare]] had a warning printed on the nose cone that read "CAUTION ION DETONATOR". The body of the bomb had "CODE SWITCH DESIGNATOR" • "POSITION 1" • "POSITION 2" • "POSITION 3" • "POSITION 4" printed on it, and on one of the tail fins, the words "THIS IS FOR YOU [[Dooku|DOOKU]]" were handwritten.<ref name="Zillo Trivia">{{SW|url=series/clone-wars/the-zillo-beast-trivia-gallery|text="The Zillo Beast" Trivia Gallery|quote=The electro-proton bomb (called the ion bomb during production) is covered with Aurebesh lettering: official red print say such things as "Caution Ion Detonator" and "Code Switch Designator" while a hand-scrawled notation on the fin says "This is for you Dooku.}} {{C|Slide 3}}</ref>

[[Loubo]], the owner of [[Plop Dribble's]], had a tattoo on his right arm of a double-[[heart]] that read "[[Parent|MOM]]" in Aurebesh.<ref name="Brothers Trivia">{{SW|url=series/clone-wars/brothers-trivia-gallery|text="Brothers" Trivia Gallery|quote=Loubo has a tattoo on his right arm of a double-heart that says "MOM".}} {{C|Slide 7}}</ref>

At [[Hondo's complex|Hondo Ohnaka's camp]] on [[Florrum]], a huge piece of superstructure read "HONDO'S SALVAGE" – "PRICES SLASHED" stenciled on it in Aurebesh.<ref name="SW-Revival Trivia Gallery">{{SW|url=series/clone-wars/revival-trivia-gallery|text="Revival" Trivia Gallery|quote=In the background of Hondo's camp is a huge piece of superstructure with the Aurebesh words, "HONDO'S SALVAGE - PRICES SLASHED," stenciled on it.}} {{C|Slide 6}}</ref> Meanwhile, around the time of the [[Bombing of the Jedi Temple hangar]], protesters carried Aurebesh signs that read "THE JEDI ARE CORRUPT" and "STOP CLONING VIOLENCE!".<ref name="Sabotage Trivia">{{SW|url=series/clone-wars/sabotage-episode-guide|text="Sabotage" Episode Guide &#124; The Clone Wars|quote=In addition to an image of a crossed-out clone trooper, the Temple protestor signs say "The Jedi are corrupt," and "Stop cloning violence."}} {{C|Slide 8}}</ref>

==Behind the scenes==
{{Quote|The Aurebesh is a lot like Boba Fett&mdash;it is a facet of the Star Wars phenomenon that had its origin as a cinematic aside, but which has come to be widely embraced, far out of proportion to its humble origins.|Stephen Crane|<ref name="Aurebesh Soup">{{WebCite|url=http://www.echostation.com/features/aurebesh.htm|text=Aurebesh Soup: ''Recipe for a Star Wars Font Phenomenon''|author=[[Stephen Crane]]|date=2000-10-21|work=Echo Station|language=English|quote=The evolution of the Star Wars alphabet told by the guy who designed the font for West End Games.|archivedate=20160520153232}}</ref>}}
[[File:Crane_Aurebesh_sheet.jpg|thumb|left|250px|Stephen Crane's original sample sheet of the first incarnation of the Aurebesh]]
An Aurebesh-like script first appeared in the 1983 movie [[Star Wars: Episode VI Return of the Jedi|''Star Wars'': Episode VI ''Return of the Jedi'']], the last installment in the [[original trilogy]] of ''[[Star Wars]]''. It could be seen on the monitor readouts on the [[DS-2 Death Star II Mobile Battle Station|second Death Star]] at the beginning of the movie, when [[Anakin Skywalker|Darth Vader's]] [[ST 321|shuttle]] is scanned while approaching the [[battle station]]. Erik Schroeder's decoding of the technical readouts further suggest that this readout is illegible, consisting of lines of character repeats.<ref name="erikstormtrooper">{{WebCite|url=http://www.erikstormtrooper.com/ed_galbasic2.htm|text=Editoral: What Does That Screen Say? The Discovery|work=Erikstormtrooper's Imperial Gallery|archivedate=20211217130717}}</ref>

[[File:Aurebesh-GMSR.png|thumb|right|250px|Aurebesh letters and punctuation, from the Legends game accessory ''Star Wars Gamemaster Screen, Revised'']]
In [[1993]], while writing the ''[[Star Wars Miniatures Battles Companion]]'' for [[West End Games]]' [[Star Wars Miniatures Battles|''Star Wars Miniatures Battles'']], [[Stephen Crane]] decided to develop an alphabet for gamers to use. Upon receiving [[Lucasfilm]]'s approval, Crane came up with the "Aurebesh", a 34-letter alphabet, by redrawing many symbols from ''Return of the Jedi'' and assigning them each to a letter or pair of letters from the Latin Alphabet. He later added punctuation marks in ''[[Imperial Entanglements (Star Wars Miniatures Battles)|Imperial Entanglements]]'', a [[1996]] supplement for the ''Miniatures Battles'' game. The Imperial Credit symbol (not designed by Crane) was created for the ''[[Monopoly Star Wars (board game)|Monopoly Star Wars]]'' board game also released in 1996. The name "Aurebesh" is derived from the first two letters of the Aurebesh alphabet, [[Aurek]] and [[Besh]].<ref name="Aurebesh Soup" /> This is similar to the real-world word "alphabet", which comes from the first two letters of the [[Wikipedia:Greek alphabet|Greek alphabet]]—''[[Wikipedia:alpha|alpha]]'' and ''[[Wikipedia:beta|beta]]''.<ref>{{WebCite|url=https://www.britannica.com/topic/alphabet-writing|text=alphabet|archivedate=20220121035602|quote=The word ''alphabet'', from the first two letters of the Greek alphabet—''alpha'' and beta'' (...)|work=''[[Wikipedia:Encyclopædia Britannica|Encyclopædia Britannica]]''}}</ref>

Stephen Crane's alphabet was subsequently adopted in many ''Star Wars'' works, and even made its way into the movies. In [[1999]], a [[Unidentified Aurebesh script (N-1 Starfighter)|variant style of Aurebesh]] appeared in [[Star Wars: Episode I The Phantom Menace|''Star Wars'': Episode I ''The Phantom Menace'']], on a readout screen of Anakin Skywalker's Naboo fighter. As Skywalker heads toward the battle, the screen reads: "Anakin turn the ship around and go back home right away."<ref name="Episode I" /> The words on the [[tractor beam]] control on the [[DS-1 Orbital Battle Station|Death Star]], originally written in the Latin Alphabet, were changed to Aurebesh for the [[2004]] DVD release of [[Star Wars: Episode IV A New Hope|''Star Wars'': Episode IV ''A New Hope'']] and all subsequent releases of the film.<ref name="ANH">{{Film|IV}}</ref>

[[File:Galactic-Basic-e1443451408574.png|thumb|250px|The 34 letters of Aurebesh as they appear in ''Star Wars Rebels: Battle Plans from Darth Vader'']]
In [[April]], [[2014]], much of the media in which Aurebesh previously appeared were designated [[Star Wars Legends|''Star Wars'' Legends]] (formerly known as the Expanded Universe) and no longer considered [[canon]].<ref name="EUNewPage">{{SW|url=news/the-legendary-star-wars-expanded-universe-turns-a-new-page|text=The Legendary Star Wars Expanded Universe Turns a New Page}}</ref> This included all sources that defined the correspondence between the Aurebesh and the Latin Alphabet. However, subsequent canon material has upheld the original West End Games mapping of Aurebesh. These included a "''Ghost'' Crew Identification Card" available on the [[Disney XD]] website<ref name="Identification Card">{{Disney|subdomain=disneyxd|url=star-wars-rebels/rebel-resistance-kit|text=Rebel Resistance Kit|nolive=1}}<br />{{WebCite|url=http://a.dolimg.com/en-US/dxd/media/starwarsrebels/SWR_Ghost_Crew_ID_card.pdf|text=''Ghost'' Crew Identification Card|format=PDF|work=[[Disney XD]]|language=English|quote=Translate Aurebesh (official Lothal language) using the guide on the back side of the card|archivedate=20230319161617}}</ref> and the [[2015]] children's book ''[[Star Wars Rebels: Battle Plans from Darth Vader]]''.<ref name="Plans">''[[Star Wars Rebels: Battle Plans from Darth Vader]]''</ref>

[[Wikipedia:Coca-Cola|Coca-Cola]] products are sold in special in-universe Aurebesh designs at the [[Star Wars: Galaxy's Edge|''Star Wars'': Galaxy's Edge]] attraction.<ref>{{WebCite|url=https://www.slashfilm.com/aurebesh-coke-at-star-wars-galaxys-edge|text=First Look: Aurebesh Coke Coming To 'Star Wars: Galaxy's Edge', Served In Reclaimed Thermal Detonator Packaging|author=Sciretta, Peter|date=2019-04-13|work=''[[Wikipedia:/Film|Slashfilm]]''|archivedate=20210515021342}}</ref>

===Foreign language inserts===
Some Aurebesh texts contain excerpts from other real-world languages:

In the ''[[Star Wars: The Clone Wars]]'' episode "[[Liberty on Ryloth]]," an all-Aurebesh [[Galactic Republic|Republic]] monitor includes the text "UND UBERHAUPT,"<ref name="Liberty">{{TCW|Liberty on Ryloth}}</ref> [[Wikipedia:German language|German]] for "and anyway".

In the ''Star Wars: The Clone Wars'' episode "[[The Academy]]," when [[Soniee]] and her fellow [[Imperial Academy of Mandalore|students]] break into the [[Government warehouse]], her [[datapad]] says "ENTRARE" in Aurebesh,<ref name="Academy">{{TCW|The Academy}}</ref> which is [[Wikipedia:Italian language|Italian]] for "enter".<ref name="SW-The Academy Trivia Gallery">{{SW|url=series/clone-wars/the-academy-trivia-gallery|text="The Academy" Trivia Gallery|quote=Once Soniee slices through the lock on the warehouse, her computer screen says "Entrare," which is Italian for "enter."}} {{C|Slide 6}}</ref>

In the noncanon ''[[Star Wars: Visions]]'' episode "[[T0-B1 (short film)|T0-B1]]," the big monitor in [[Professor]] [[Mitaka]]'s laboratory is filled with Aurebesh text. Most of it is written in [[Wikipedia:Spanish language|Spanish]]; for example, there is "PERMANENCIA DESARROLLO DATOS," Spanish for "continuation development data"; and "ATMOSFERA MINERALES,"<ref name="T0-B1" /> meaning "[[atmosphere]] [[mineral]]s". ===Punctuation===
====Aurebesh vs. English====
[[File:Tech_visor_Aurebesh.png|thumb|left|250px|Aurebesh parentheses and slashes on Tech's visor in the ''Star Wars: The Bad Batch'' episode "The Crossing," along with regular English colons and an English hyphen]]
Aurebesh punctuation, originally created for the Legends game accessory ''Imperial Entanglements'',<ref name="Aurebesh Soup" /> is also used in ''Star Wars'' canon. For example, an apostrophe appears on the marquee outside [[Ziro's Palace]] in the animated film ''[[Star Wars: The Clone Wars (film)|Star Wars: The Clone Wars]]'',<ref name="TCW film" /> and an Aurebesh exclamation point can be seen in the inscription on the [[Low Altitude Assault Transport/infantry|Republic gunship]] ''[[Spaceward Ho!]]'' in the ''[[Star Wars: The Clone Wars]]'' episodes "[[Storm Over Ryloth]]"<ref name="Storm">{{TCW|Storm Over Ryloth}}</ref> and "[[Landing at Point Rain]],"<ref name="Rain">{{TCW|Landing at Point Rain}}</ref> and the animated film;<ref name="TCW film">[[Star Wars: The Clone Wars (film)|''Star Wars: The Clone Wars'' film]]</ref> and in a [[Star Tours]] advertisement for [[Glee Anselm]] in "[[Lightsaber Lost]],"<ref name="Lightsaber Lost">{{TCW|Lightsaber Lost}}</ref> a sign for the [[Abafar Junior Dejarik Club]] in "[[Missing in Action]],"<ref name="Missing in Action">{{TCW|Missing in Action}}</ref> and on the poster ''[[The Sport's Greatest Rivalry]]'' in "[[The Rise of Clovis]]."<ref name="The Rise of Clovis">{{TCW|The Rise of Clovis}}</ref> In the ''[[Star Wars: The Bad Batch]]'' episode "[[The Crossing]]," a scan by [[Data visor|Tech's visor]] results in a reading containing Aurebesh parentheses and slashes.<ref name="Crossing">{{TBB|The Crossing}}</ref>

However, regular English punctuation, which does not match the one created by Stephen Crane, can also be seen in several episodes of ''Star Wars: The Clone Wars'' (such as full stops on the display on [[Cad Bane]]'s security chip [[bomb]] in "[[Holocron Heist]],"<ref name="Holocron Heist">{{TCW|Holocron Heist}}</ref> the [[Holoprojector/Legends|hologram projector]] in [[Great Vaults of Cato Neimoidia|Lott Dod's library]] in "[[Senate Spy]],"<ref name="Senate Spy">{{TCW|Senate Spy}}</ref> [[Admiral]] [[Wullf Yularen|Yularen's]] intelligence report in "[[Cat and Mouse]],"<ref name="Cat and Mouse">{{TCW|Cat and Mouse}}</ref> [[Ahsoka Tano]]'s board in "[[The Academy]],"<ref name="The Academy">{{TCW|The Academy}}</ref> and the case files for [[Sifo-Dyas]] and his aide in "[[The Lost One]]"<ref name="The Lost One">{{TCW|The Lost One}}</ref>) and ''[[Star Wars Rebels]]'' (such as full stops in an advertisement at [[Nixus Hub 218]] in "[[Brothers of the Broken Horn]],"<ref name="Brothers of the Broken Horn">{{Rebels|Brothers of the Broken Horn}}</ref> the [[DTS-series dismantler droid|dismantler droid's]] scopes in ''[[Star Wars Rebels: Steps Into Shadow]]'',<ref name="Star Wars Rebels: Steps Into Shadow">''[[Star Wars Rebels: Steps Into Shadow]]''</ref> a set of blueprints aboard [[Tiber Saxon]]'s [[Star Destroyer]] in ''[[Star Wars Rebels: Heroes of Mandalore]]'',<ref name="Star Wars Rebels: Heroes of Mandalore">''[[Star Wars Rebels: Heroes of Mandalore]]''</ref> and a billboard in a cargo hold in "[[In the Name of the Rebellion]]";<ref name="In the Name of the Rebellion">{{Rebels|In the Name of the Rebellion}}</ref> while an English colon appears in the blueprint for the [[TIE/D Defender Elite]] in "[[Crawler Commandeers]]"<ref name="Crawler New Hope|''Star Wars'': Episode IV ''A New Hope'']] and all subsequent releases of the film.<ref name="ANH" /> 

Three stylized English exclamation points appear on the console of [[Freya Fenris]]' [[racing starfighter]], the ''[[Red Ace]]'', in the ''[[Star Wars Resistance]]'' episode "[[The Voxx Vortex 5000]]."<ref name="Voxx">{{Resistance|The Voxx Vortex 5000}}</ref> In addition, when [[Kanan Jarrus]] breaks into [[Fenn Rau]]'s [[Fang-class Protectorate fighter|''Fang'' fighter]] in the ''Star Wars Rebels'' episode "[[The Protector of Concord Dawn]]," the monitor shows an Aurebesh exclamation point ''on top of'' an English one,<ref name="Protector">{{Rebels|The Protector of Concord Dawn}}</ref> and the aforementioned scan in ''Star Wars: The Bad Batch'' contains regular English colons and an English hyphen, even though the parentheses and slashes are all Aurebesh.<ref name="Crossing" />

====Ampersand====
The ampersand ("&") does not appear in any ''Star Wars'' mappings of Aurebesh; however, a fan-made ampersand, originally created for a fan-made font called Aurabesh,<ref name="Ampersand">{{WebCite|url=https://aurekfonts.github.io/?font=Aurabesh|text=Aurabesh (free)|archiveurl=https://archive.ph/pcu89|archivedate=2023-01-20}}<br /><br />{{Twitter|AurekFonts|status/1286777573985275904|AurekFonts|date=July 24, 2020|quote=In 1996 Mike E Web created a fan font for Aurebesh, called "Aurabesh." <nowiki>https://aurekfonts.github.io/?font=Aurabesh</nowiki> Because this was created before the above punctuation was made, Web went ahead and added his own custom punctuation, including the first Aurebesh ampersand!|archiveurl=https://archive.ph/pcu89}}</ref> appears in the ''Star Wars: The Bad Batch'' episode "[[Faster]]," where one of the advertisements at the [[Safa Toma Speedway]] says "[[T&J Repair|T&J REPAIR]]."<ref name="Faster">{{TBB|Faster}}</ref> The ad also appears in [[Suria space station]] in the episode "[[Bad Territory]]."<ref name="Bad Territory">{{TBB|Bad Territory}}</ref> The same custom character was also used in the [[Star Wars video games|video game]] [[Star Wars: Hunters|''Star Wars: Hunters'']] via one of the advertisements in the [[Coruscant Underworld (battlefield)|Coruscant Underworld]] map.<ref name="Star Wars: Hunters">''[[Star Wars: Hunters]]''</ref> The Aurabesh font was originally developed by Mike E. Webb in 1996 and can be downloaded online.<ref name="Ampersand" /> In another instance, ''Star Wars: Hunters'' also used the letter Enth ({{AurB|ae}}) to represent an ampersand.<ref name="Star Wars: Hunters" />

[[File:TorraDozaStats-TheRecruitPart2.jpg|thumb|right|220px|Torra Doza's statistics in "The Recruit" feature several instances of the percent symbol]]In Part 2 of the ''Resistance'' episode "[[The Recruit]]," [[Torra Doza]]'s stats for the [[Race between Kazuda Xiono and Torra Doza|race between her and Kazuda Xiano]] read "[[Time|TIME]] IN TOP 5: 100% • CHALLENGE RATE: 87% • FINISHES: 100% •  WINS: 100% • WINS BY DISQUALIFICATION: 100% • TOP SPEED: 92%".<ref name="The Recruit">{{Resistance|The Recruit}}</ref> In ''The Bad Batch'' episode "Bad Territory," ''mirror-inverted'' text reading "<small>''[obscured]''</small>0% OFF" appears on the wall behind [[Fennec Shand]] in [[Tanda's Cantina]].<ref name="Bad Territory" /> In the ''[[Star Wars: Tales of the Underworld]]'' episode "[[The Good Life]]," there is a sign reading "50% DISCOUNT" on the [[planet]] [[Duro]]; while another sign says "20% OFF."<ref name="The Good Life">{{TOTU|The Good Life}}</ref> The percent sign (%) does not appear in any ''Star Wars'' mappings of Aurebesh either, but appears in the fan-made Aurabesh font.<ref name="Ampersand" />

===Alternate numerical system===
{{Quote|I can't recall which came first, Stock Ships or Gamemaster Screen Revised, but I worked on both and both have that chart.|Pablo Hidalgo on [[Wikipedia:Twitter|Twitter]]|<ref name="Hidalgo New Aurabesh">{{Twitter|pabl0hidalgo|status/1535405496089186307|[[Pablo Hidalgo]]|date=2022-06-10|quote=I can't recall which came first, Stock Ships or Gamemaster Screen Revised, but I worked on both and both have that chart.|archivedate=20220610233632}}</ref>}}
[[File:New_Aurabesh_numbers.png|thumb|left|300px|Alternate Aurebesh numerals]]
Stephen Crane never created numbers for the Aurebesh alphabet, because the Death Star monitors in ''Return of the Jedi'' show regular Arabic numerals.<ref name="Aurebesh Soup" /> However, an [[Alternative Aurebesh numerals|unrelated numerical system]] made up of dots and lines first appeared in the [[1995]] West End Games sourcebook ''[[Platt's Starport Guide]]'', which features a graphic called "Current [[Transponder code/Legends|transponder code]] profile", with the numerals along both axes of a graph. Unlike later appearances, numerals for 0 through 16 are represented.<ref name="Platt">''[[Platt's Starport Guide]]''</ref> The graph was reused for the West End Games publication ''[[Star Wars Gamemaster Screen, Revised]]''<ref name="Screen Revised">''[[Star Wars Gamemaster Screen, Revised]]''</ref> and ''[[Stock Ships]]''.<ref name="Stock Ships">''[[Stock Ships]]''</ref><ref name="Hidalgo New Aurabesh" /> These numerals were digitized in a fan-made font known as New Aurabesh, which was created by Peter Schuster in [[1998]].<ref name="New Aurabesh">{{WebCite|url=https://ko-fi.com/post/New-Aurabesh-A-Casual-Interview-with-Peter-Schu-Q5Q41QAX6|text="New Aurabesh": A Casual Interview with Peter Schuster|date=2020-05-19|work=Ko-fi|archivedate=20220509195609}}</ref> and can be downloaded online.<ref name="New Aurabesh font">{{WebCite|url=https://aurekfonts.github.io/?font=NewAurabesh|text=New Aurabesh (free)|work=AurekFonts|archiveurl=https://archive.ph/z1KU2|archivedate=May 9, 2022}}</ref> While the original West End Games sources represented numerals 0 through 16,<ref name="Platt" /> later appearances of the numerals follow Schuster's designation, being limited to numerals 0 through 9.

[[File:Kessel_gate.png|thumb|right|200px|"D7 X371K" written in Aurebesh over a gate on Kessel in the movie ''Solo: A Star Wars Story'', using alternate Aurebesh numerals originally created for ''Platt's Starship Guide'']]
Although most canon appearances of Aurebesh use Arabic numbers, the alternative West End Games numerals have also appeared in ''Star Wars'' media: ''[[7189]]'', a wall mural in the [[Capital City]] of [[Lothal]], uses these numerals and first appears in the ''[[Star Wars Rebels]]'' short "[[Entanglement]]".<ref name="Entanglement">{{Rebels|Entanglement}}</ref> The [[2015]] reference book ''[[Star Wars: The Force Awakens: The Visual Dictionary]]'' contains pictures of the [[rank insignia of the First Order]], with alternate Aurebesh numbers appearing in the insignias for [[sergeant]] ("[[Ro]] 8") and [[squad leader]] ("[[Hal (Imperial officer)|Hal]] 4").<ref name="VE" /> The "Hal 4" insignia can be seen on [[Lank Paze]] in the [[2017]] movie {{Film|VIII}}, while the "Ro 8" insignia can be seen on [[Armitage Hux's First Order monitor]] in the same film. In another scene in the film, the databank of the ''[[Libertine]]'', which [[DJ]] gains access to after he steals the [[Yacht|Star Yacht]] in [[Canto Bight]], uses these numerals to list prices in the [[Korfé Bennux-Ai|owner's]] catalog of [[starfighter]]s for sale.<ref name="TLJ">{{Film|VIII}}</ref>

The 2017 reference book ''[[Star Wars: The Visual Encyclopedia]]'' presents an Aurebesh font which includes two examples of Aurebesh numbers; 3 and 7, both of which use West End Games' alternate font.<ref name="Visual Dictionary">''[[Star Wars: The Visual Encyclopedia]]''</ref> During the [[raid on Kessel]] in the [[2018]] film ''[[Solo: A Star Wars Story]]'', [[Qi'ra]] says, "They're in subsector four, coming up on gate X-3-7-1-K." The film then switches to a shot of [[Han Solo]] and [[Chewbacca]] coming out a door with a sign at the top, saying "D7 X371K", using the alternate numerals.<ref name="Solo">''[[Solo: A Star Wars Story]]''</ref> In ''The Mandalorian'', the numbers numerals can be seen on the console aboard the ''[[Razor Crest]]'', and first appear in "[[Chapter 2: The Child]]". As first seen in ''[[Star Wars: The Book of Boba Fett]]'' episode "[[Chapter 5: Return of the Mandalorian]]," the numbers 51 and 11 appear on a [[Color|red]] monitor on the console of [[Din Djarin's N-1 starfighter]], and on a small blue monitor on the opposite side of the console, which lists every number from 1 to 9, although the latter is upside-down.<ref name="BF5">{{BOBF|Chapter 5: Return of the Mandalorian}}</ref> In ''The Mandalorian'' episode "[[Chapter 19: The Convert]]," the monitor on the control panel of a [[Six-O-Two Mitigator]] used by the [[New Republic]] [[New Republic Amnesty Program|Amnesty Program]], lists every alternative numeral from 1 to 9, representing different voltage levels.<ref name="Man19">{{TheMandalorian|Chapter 19: The Convert}}</ref>

[[File:Plazir-15_camera.png|thumb|left|300px|Security camera footage from [[Plazir-15]] in ''The Mandalorian'' episode "[[Chapter 22: Guns for Hire]]," using alternative Aurebesh numerals. The caption says "CAMERA 7709-739".]]
The numerals can also be seen on monitors in [[Daiyu spaceport]] in the ''[[Star Wars: Obi-Wan Kenobi]]'' episode "[[Part II]]"<ref name="Kenobi2">{{Kenobi|Part II}}</ref> and on the blueprints of [[Fortress Inquisitorius]] in "[[Part IV]]."<ref name="Kenobi4">{{Kenobi|Part IV}}</ref> The numbers also appear on in ''Star Wars: The Bad Batch''. For example, the monitor in [[Riyo Chuchi]]'s [[repulsorpod]] in the [[Imperial Senate]] in the ''[[Star Wars: The Bad Batch]]'' episode "[[Truth and Consequences]]," although regular Aurebesh digits also appear on the screen.<ref name="Truth">{{TBB|Truth and Consequences}}</ref> In the episode "[[The Crossing]]," [[Clone Force 99]] member [[Tech]] wears a [[Data visor|special visor]] that can be used to scan his surroundings. When used in [[Cid's mine]], the scan results in "[[Ipsium|IPSIUM]] DETECTED", followed by a list of alternate numerals.<ref name="Crossing">{{TBB|The Crossing}}</ref>

===Dishabesh and Domabesh===
{{Quote|It's called domabesh, and was made for Rogue One.|Pablo Hidalgo|<ref name="Pablo Tweet">{{Twitter|pabl0hidalgo|status/1399068407740321792|[[Pablo Hidalgo]]|date=2021-05-30|quote=It's called domabesh, and was made for Rogue One.|archivedate=20210530185253}}</ref>}}
[[File:Domabesh_font.png|thumb|right|300px|A representation of the Domabesh font.]]
For the [[2016]] film ''[[Rogue One: A Star Wars Story]]'', two alternate Aurebesh fonts were created by the art department and were given the production names [[Unidentified Aurebesh script (battle helmet)|Dishabesh]]<ref name="Dillon">{{Twitter|glyn_dillon|status/1110995215219855363|[[Glyn Dillon]]|date=2019-03-27|quote=The fonts were made at Gareth's request for R.O. he didn't want a universe with just one text (and mostly one font). They were designed by the art dept graphics team. I was able to use them in any designs that needed text. Dishabesh just seemed to suit Enfys more than the others.|archiveurl=https://archive.ph/z5GaLno|nolive=1}}<br />{{Twitter|LelalMekha|status/1111001692626210818|Alban Leloup|date=2019-03-27|quote=Dishabesh, eh?
