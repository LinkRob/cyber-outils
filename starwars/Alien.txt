"'''Alien'''" was a general-purpose term used in [[the galaxy]] by [[human]]s and even some [[droid]]s to refer to anyone of a [[Sentience|sentient]] [[species]] that was not a human.<ref name="whatwookiee" /> Sometimes, the term '''nonhuman''' was used interchangeably.<ref name="edge">''[[Servants of the Empire: Edge of the Galaxy]]''</ref> To the [[Chiss]], the term was used to describe non-Chiss species, as well as by the [[Kilji]] to refer to non-Kilji.<ref name="Chaos Rising">''[[Thrawn Ascendancy: Chaos Rising]]''</ref> There existed within both the [[Galactic Republic]] and [[Galactic Empire]] a set of [[Unknown Alien protocols]] that governed official encounters with unknown alien species.<ref name="thrawn">[[Thrawn (novel)|''Thrawn'']]</ref> Species that resembled humans, like [[Kiffar]], were referred to as "[[Near-human]]s."<ref name="Aftermath">[[Aftermath (novel)|''Aftermath'']]</ref>

==Xenophobia==
{{Main|Xenophobia}}
{{Quote|&hellip; I have no issues with aliens myself&mdash;some of my best friends are nonhumans&mdash;but some grav-ball fans object to alien physiognomies, seeing them as giving nonhumans an edge.|Janus Fhurek|<ref name="edge" />}}
As the [[Galactic Empire]] looked down on aliens, it was rare to find nonhumans serving as [[Imperial officer]]s. Nonhuman species like [[Wookiee]]s were regarded as animals and exploited as [[Slavery|slave]] labor.<ref name="Rebels Visual Dictionary">''[[Star Wars Rebels: The Visual Guide]]''</ref> The Empire favored humans in its government and [[Imperial Military|military]].<ref name="Epic Battles">''[[Star Wars Rebels: Visual Guide: Epic Battles]]''</ref> In addition, the Empire discouraged the learning of alien [[language]]s and cultures such as [[Ithorese]], and sought to impose high "Imperial standards" for its citizens.<ref name="Aftermath" />

This pro-human bias was an intentional part of Imperial policy. Imperial [[propaganda]] actively supported state-sponsored ethnocentric [[xenophobia]] and fear-mongering. To the Empire, aliens were by and large unwelcome within its [[New Order|order]], with aliens being seen as "different" and "strange." As such, the Empire treated nonhuman segments of its populations as serfs, slaves, or obstacles needing to be tamed, removed, or ignored. The Empire supported the idea that aliens were untrustworthy&mdash;unlike humans&mdash;and behind their veneer of innocence lurked a vicious monster. The Imperial propaganda machine was so effective in its message that even [[near-human]] species exhibited an inherent distrust towards nonhuman populations.<ref name="Aftermath LD">''[[Aftermath: Life Debt]]''</ref>

Nevertheless, some nonhumans were known to occupy lofty roles within the Empire, such as the [[Chagrian]] [[Mas Amedda]], who served as the Empire's [[Grand Vizier]],<ref name="Tarkin">[[Tarkin (novel)|''Tarkin'']]</ref> the [[Chiss]] [[Grand Admiral]] [[Mitth'raw'nuruodo|Thrawn]],<ref name="Star Wars Rebels: Steps Into Shadow">''[[Star Wars Rebels: Steps Into Shadow]]''</ref> the [[Pau'an]] [[The Grand Inquisitor|Grand Inquisitor]],<ref name="Rebels Visual Dictionary" /> and four other [[Inquisitorius|Inquisitors]] known as the [[Seventh Sister]], the [[Fifth Brother]],<ref name="ATTA">{{Rebels|Always Two There Are}}</ref> [[Ninth Sister]]<ref name="JFO">''[[Star Wars Jedi: Fallen Order]]''</ref> and the [[Eighth Brother]].<ref name="TotA">{{Rebels|Twilight of the Apprentice}}</ref>

Some fans of [[grav-ball]] during the [[Imperial Era|Age of the Empire]] objected to the presence of alien players on grav-ball teams, feeling that their alien physiognomies gave them an unfair edge over the human players. The [[Junior Academy of Applied Sciences]]'s [[athletic director]] [[Janus Fhurek]] attempted to convince his grav-ball center-striker [[Zare Leonis]] to remove the [[AppSci SaberCats]]' two alien players: the [[Rodian]] [[Frid Kelio]] and the [[Aqualish]] [[Hench Sina]]. When Leonis refused, Fhurek unilaterally transferred the alien players to another school.<ref name="edge" /> Reflecting on his experiences with Fhurek, Leonis regarded the athletic director as xenophobic.<ref name="Imperial Justice">''[[Servants of the Empire: Imperial Justice]]''</ref>

The [[Kilji]] [[Religion|religious]] [[species]] were xenophobic: they viewed beings who did not follow their beliefs&mdash;the [[Kilji way of order and enlightenment]]&mdash;as inferior, and ignored them and treated them badly until they were enlightened to their philosophy. The Kilji ruler, [[Generalirius]] [[Nakirre]], allied himself with the [[Grysk]] "[[Jixtus]]," an alien, who covered all of his body so no one saw a Grysk body or face until he sat at the ancient thrones of the ancient kings of the Chiss planet [[Csilla]].<ref name="Lesser Evil">''[[Thrawn Ascendancy: Lesser Evil]]''</ref>

==Behind the scenes==
{{Quote|Their motives were driven based on psychological motifs that had been around for thousands of years in mythology ''[&hellip;]'' I mean it's also from Episode IV, which is the first time you treated aliens as humans, as if there was nothing special about them, they just look funny. They were unique but they weren't monsters. They weren't crazy aliens. They were just characters. And I don't think anybody had seen that before and I think they liked it.|George Lucas, on what made his characters timeless and still resonating with audiences|<ref name="GL-TESB">{{SW|url=news/empire-at-40-george-lucas-interview|text=''Empire'' at 40 &#124; George Lucas on Making "Something That Had Never Been Done Before," Again}}</ref>}}
Aliens first appeared in the [[1977]] [[original trilogy]] film, [[Star Wars: Episode IV A New Hope|''Star Wars'': Episode IV ''A New Hope'']].<ref name="ANH">{{Film|IV}}</ref> Director [[George Lucas]] stated that the ''[[Star Wars]]'' mythology, beginning with ''A New Hope'', was the first time aliens are shown not as monsters, but simply common characters.<ref name="GL-TESB" />
