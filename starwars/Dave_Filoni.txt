'''Dave Filoni''' is a film and animation producer, the Chief Creative Officer of [[Lucasfilm]], and, from [[2026]], President of Lucasfilm alongside Co-President [[Lynwen Brennan]]. Filoni was chosen by ''[[Star Wars]]'' creator, [[George Lucas]], as the Supervising Director of the animated series ''[[Star Wars: The Clone Wars]]'' in [[2005]] and has since been involved in additional animation and live-action television series for ''Star Wars''. These include the roles of creator, writer, and executive producer for ''[[Star Wars Rebels]]'', ''[[Star Wars Resistance]]'', ''[[Star Wars: The Mandalorian]]'', and ''[[Star Wars: Ahsoka]]''.

==Biography==
===''The Clone Wars''===
{{Quote|When ''Revenge of the Sith'' was over, I found myself in the incredible position of carrying the torch forward.|Dave Filoni|<ref name="Will be forever">{{SW|url=news/star-wars-will-be-forever|text=''Star Wars'' Will Be Forever}}</ref>}}
Dave Filoni was born  in [[Wikipedia:Mt. Lebanon, Pennsylvania|Mt. Lebanon]], [[Wikipedia:Pennsylvania|Pennsylvania]]<ref name="FiloniBirth" />. He grew up with ''[[Star Wars]]'' and read the [[Star Wars Legends|Legends]] novels and books, including ''[[Heir to the Empire]]'' by [[Timothy Zahn]]. He went to the same high school as future [[Fennec Shand]] actress [[Ming-Na Wen]].<ref name="Filoni joins SWCA">{{SWYouTube|Vs1UIKxJFJI|Dave Filoni joins us on stage at SWCA 22 &#124; Star Wars Celebration LIVE!}}</ref> Filoni then worked as part of [[Wikipedia:Disney Television Animation|Walt Disney Television Animation]] including on series like ''[[Wikipedia:Teamo Supremo|Teamo Supremo]]'', ''[[Wikipedia:Kim Possible|Kim Possible]]'', ''[[Wikipedia:Lilo & Stitch: The Series|Lilo & Stitch: The Series]]'', ''[[Wikipedia:Dave the Barbarian|Dave the Barbarian]]'', and ''[[Wikipedia:Fillmore!|Fillmore!]]''<ref name="Will be forever" /> and later as an episodic director on [[Wikipedia:Nickelodeon Animation Studio|Nickelodon]]'s animated series ''[[Wikipedia:Avatar: The Last Airbender|Avatar: The Last Airbender]]''.<ref name="Art of TCW">''[[The Art of Star Wars: The Clone Wars]]''</ref>

[[File:DaveFiloni.jpg|thumb|left|200px|Filoni around 2005 during the creation of ''Star Wars: The Clone Wars''.]]
In [[2005]], Filoni, joined [[Lucasfilm]] as an apprentice to [[George Lucas]] and they developed the ''[[Star Wars: The Clone Wars]]'' animated series together,<ref name="Rebellion televised">{{WebCite|url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2022/05/star-wars-cover-the-rebellion-will-be-televised|text=''Star Wars:'' The Rebellion Will Be Televised|author=Breznican, Anthony|date=2022-05-17|work=[[Wikipedia:Vanity Fair (magazine)|''Vanity Fair'']]|archivedate=20220517110251}}</ref> with Filoni working as supervising director. Filoni had initially gotten a call in early 2005 for the role by Executive producer [[Catherine Winder]] and had at first wanted to hang up because he thought it was a prank, before being first interviewed by her, then [[Gail Currey]] and later George Lucas.<ref name="Art of TCW" /> Many of the tools Filoni and<ref name="Filoni joins SWCA" /> Lucas developed were used to feed [[Star Wars: Underworld|future live-action projects]] that Lucas wanted to do. Lucas taught Filoni the language of cinema, editing, and ways to tell a story.<ref name="Filoni joins SWCA" />

During the show's early development, the character who became the [[Jedi]] [[Ahsoka Tano]] was very involved in the [[black market]] and worked with Jedi without getting involved in the [[Clone Wars]]. She would try to prevent criminals from taking advantage of the war. Filoni and Lucas decided that including a [[Padawan]] was important, and Lucas wanted her to be [[Anakin Skywalker]]'s Padawan.<ref name="Ahsoka Oral history">{{WebCite|url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2020/04/ahsoka-tano-clone-wars-finale-star-wars|text=Ahsoka Tano—A Star Wars Oral History|author=Breznican, Anthony|date=2020-04-24|work=[[Wikipedia:Vanity Fair (magazine)|''Vanity Fair'']]|archivedate=20200424215049}}</ref> As of 2005, the character was named Ashla,<ref name="Ahsoka Oral history" /> a reference to an early name for the [[light side of the Force]] when Lucas was developing [[Star Wars: Episode IV A New Hope|''Star Wars'': Episode IV ''A New Hope'']].<ref name="Lasat Trivia">{{SW|url=series/star-wars-rebels/legends-of-the-lasat-trivia-gallery|text=Legends of the Lasat Trivia Gallery}} {{C|Slide 2}}</ref> Filoni played with the idea of her being the same character as the [[Ashla (Jedi)|Togruta youngling]] in [[Star Wars: Episode II Attack of the Clones|''Star Wars'': Episode II ''Attack of the Clones'']], but he realized the ages would not work. Lucas gave her the name Ahsoka, and she was one of the very first characters that Filoni drew when he was working at Lucasfilm. Ahsoka Tano's voice actor, [[Ashley Eckstein]], often asked Filoni how her character's story would end, and he gave several different answers over the years. Some ideas held that Tano would die in the series. However, Filoni opposed the idea that Tano's story existed only as a smaller part of Skywalker's own:<ref name="Ahsoka Oral history" /> while Tano is indeed an important part of Skywalker's life story and his fall to darkness,<ref name="Family">''[[Skywalker: A Family at War]]''</ref> with Filoni himself agreeing,<ref name="TCW4">[[Star Wars: The Clone Wars The Complete Season Four|''Star Wars: The Clone Wars'' The Complete Season Four]] "Obi-Wan Undercover"</ref> he believed that, if Tano existed solely as another factor that pushed Skywalker to the dark side, she would have been included in the films.<ref name="Ahsoka Oral history" />

When the [[Mandalorian]]s were being developed for ''The Clone Wars'', Filoni and Lucas looked at [[Mandalore]] in the Expanded Universe and decided to keep the broad strokes of their history.<ref name="Creating Mandalore">[[Star Wars: The Clone Wars The Complete Season Two|''Star Wars: The Clone Wars'': The Complete Season Two]] Featurette: "Creating Mandalore"</ref> Lucas pointed out that the Mandalorians are currently pacifists, but he and Filoni agreed that cultures are capable of change.<ref name="Rebellion televised" />

Filoni made an appearance at [[Celebration IV]] on Sunday, [[May 28]], [[2007]] with Catherine Winder in the Celebration Theater (Room 408AB), to discuss the beginnings of the new television series and reveal how ''The Clone Wars'' was being created. During [[Wikipedia:San Diego Comic-Con|San Diego Comic Con]] [[2014]], Filoni admitted that he knew about contradicting the Expanded Universe by establishing The Clone Wars as [[canon]]. In his defense, he said that they still included the Expanded Universe elements when they could.<ref name="SDCC2014">{{SW|url=news/sdcc-2014-star-wars-a-new-dawn-panel-liveblog|text=SDCC 2014: Star Wars: A New Dawn Panel - Liveblog}}</ref> When Filoni and the crew were writing the story about Tano leaving the [[Jedi Order]], they made it about her being pulled in different directions and becoming frustrated. They wanted to show young people that they can take a predetermined path or use the information to make their own choice.<ref name="Ahsoka Oral history" /> Filoni first teased the story of Tano leaving the Order in the "Obi-Wan Undercover" commentary track for [[Star Wars: The Clone Wars The Complete Season Four|''Star Wars: The Clone Wars'' The Complete Season Four]]: he teased that a story in [[The Clone Wars: Season Five|''The Clone Wars'': Season Five]] would help explain why Skywalker felt betrayed by the Jedi Order.<ref name="TCW4" />

===''Rebels'' and ''Bad Batch''===
The ''[[Star Wars Rebels]]'' animated series was created by Filoni, [[Simon Kinberg]], and [[Carrie Beck]], with Filoni serving as one of the executive producers,<ref name="Premiere">{{SW|url=news/star-wars-rebels-spark-of-rebellion-premieres-friday-october-3-on-disney-channel|text=''Star Wars Rebels: Spark of Rebellion'' Premieres Friday, October 3 on Disney Channel}}</ref> writers,<ref name="Twilight">{{Rebels|Twilight of the Apprentice}}</ref> and directors.<ref name="Insider 152: Launch Pad">{{InsiderCite|152|Launchpad|Launch Pad}}</ref> He voiced [[C1-10P|Chopper]] in the series, a role which was not revealed until the series [[Family Reunion – and Farewell|finale]].<ref name="Family Reunion and Farewell">{{Rebels|Family Reunion – and Farewell}}</ref> Filoni wanted [[Sabine Wren]] to be more colorful than other ''Star Wars'' characters, which was shown by her constantly [[hair dye|dying]] her hair. He also wanted her to be a character that kids could get attached to.<ref name="Sabine Perfect">{{WebCite|url=https://thedirect.com/article/dave-filoni-disney-ahsoka-sabine-actress|text=Dave Filoni Explains Why New Sabine Actress Is Perfect for Disney+'s Ahsoka|author=Nebens, Richard|date=2022-08-28|work=[https://thedirect.com The Direct]|archivedate=20221028074717}}</ref> He [[tuckerization|named]] the character [[Gooti Terez]] after [[Andi Gutierrez]],<ref name="Filoni joins SWCA" /> the digital communications manager for [[StarWars.com]], host of ''[[Rebels Recon]]'', and co-host of ''[[The Star Wars Show]]''.<ref name="SW Andi Gutierrez">{{SW|url=news/contributor/agutierrez|text=Andi Gutierrez}}</ref> Filoni set up a further story involving Wren and Ahsoka Tano for the end of the series.<ref name="Sabine Perfect" />

Filoni wanted to see if Tano worked outside of animation, leading to the creation of the [[Ahsoka (novel)|''Ahsoka'']] novel. He told Lucasfilm's publishing division that they should show people unaware of ''The Clone Wars'' and ''Rebels'' how popular the character is.<ref name="Ahsoka Oral history" /> Filoni served as an executive producer of the animated series ''[[Star Wars: The Bad Batch]]'',<ref name="The Bad Batch">{{SW|url=news/star-wars-the-bad-batch-series-announce|text=Star Wars: The Bad Batch, An All-New Animated Series, to Debut on Disney+ in 2021}}</ref> where he would orchestrate creative and narrative decisions but leave the execution of his vision to figures like fellow Executive Producer [[Athena Yvette Portillo]].<ref name="Behind the Batch">{{InsiderCite|issue1=224|issue2=225|Behind the Batch}}</ref> He asked Ming-Na Wen if she would want to reprise her role as Fennic Shand in ''The Bad Batch'' and she said she would love it. Filoni thought it would be neat if Shand encountered [[bounty hunter]] [[Cad Bane]].<ref name="Filoni joins SWCA" />

===Live action===
Around the debut of [[Star Wars Rebels Season Four]], Filoni had hinted that he'd been exploring other forms of storytelling and learning from live-action directors.<ref name="Gizmodo">{{WebCite|url=https://io9.gizmodo.com/star-wars-rebels-dave-filoni-on-its-final-season-and-h-1819877875|text=''Star Wars: Rebels'' Dave Filoni on Its Final Season and His Future Role in a Galaxy Far, Far Away|author=Lussier, Germain|date=2017-10-27|work=[[Wikipedia:io9|''io9'']]|archivedate=20180921024020}}</ref><ref>{{YouTube|video=o9w202ewiiE|text=Harloff Interviews Filoni! They Talk Future of Lucasfilm Ahead of Star Wars Rebels Finale|channel=channel/UCWvMmm_sSdgALpo1Ci4WvtQ|name=Collider Videos|archivedate=20190413001127}}</ref> (He visited the sets and studied the productions of [[Star Wars: Episode VII The Force Awakens|''The Force Awakens'']] and [[Star Wars: Episode VIII The Last Jedi|''The Last Jedi'']]).<ref name="Pittsburgh" /> After expressing interest in working in live-action, Lucasfilm CEO [[Kathleen Kennedy]] had Filoni visit the film sets of the [[Sequel trilogy|sequel]] and [[Star Wars Anthology Series|anthology]] films to study the work of their directors. He received particular help and encouragement from [[Rian Johnson]].<ref name="Art of Mandalorian">[[The Art of Star Wars: The Mandalorian (Season One)|''The Art of Star Wars: The Mandalorian'']]</ref>

[[File:Mandalorian_pram_sketch.png|thumb|left|200px|Dave Filoni drew the Child reaching out of a floating bassinet toward his burly protector.]]
Kennedy arranged a meeting between Filoni and [[Jon Favreau]] in [[Wikipedia:Los Angeles|Los Angeles]] in which they exchanged ideas and drawings for a Mandalorian show. Filoni combined his knowledge of Mandalorian history with Favreau's concept of a lone gunslinger. Favreau shared his idea for [[Din Grogu|the Child]], which caused a debate between the two because Filoni wanted to protect the mystery Lucas built around [[Jedi Master]] [[Yoda]]. One of the original drawings was drawn by Filoni and showed the Child's hand reaching out of a floating [[Hover pram|bassinet]] toward his burly protector.<ref name="Rebellion televised" /> When Filoni started exploring the possibility of directing a live-action project, he asked Kennedy about how you know an actor is right, and she replied "You just know."<ref name="Tells all">{{WebCite|url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2020/11/rosario-dawson-dave-filoni-mandalorian-ahsoka-tano-grogu|text=Star Wars The Mandalorian: Rosario Dawson Tells All About Ahsoka Tano|author=Breznican, Anthony|date=2020-11-30|work=[[Wikipedia:Vanity Fair (magazine)|''Vanity Fair'']]|archivedate=20201207031817}}</ref> Kennedy then paired Filoni up with Favreau on ''[[Star Wars: The Mandalorian]]'',<ref name="Art of Mandalorian" /> for which he serves as executive producer and has directed several episodes<ref name="Production begins">{{SW|url=news/the-mandalorian-revealed|text=''The Mandalorian'' First Image, Directors Revealed}}</ref><ref name="IGN Mando">{{WebCite|url=https://www.ign.com/articles/2019/09/28/the-mandalorian-ship-razor-crest-photo-first-look-dave-filoni-star-wars-clone-wars-rebels|text=Get a Look Inside The Mandalorian's Ship: The Razor Crest - IGN First|author=Prudom, Laura|date=2019-09-29|work=[[Wikipedia:IGN|IGN]]|archivedate=20200422213941}}</ref>&mdash;his first venture outside the realm of animation.<ref>{{StarWarsShow|kStsZpG3Sgg|First Look at The Mandalorian &#124; The Star Wars Show}}</ref>

In [[February]], [[2017]], a ''[[Star Wars]]'' fan asked [[Rosario Dawson]] on [[Wikipedia:Twitter|Twitter]] if she would ever play Ahsoka Tano, and she replied that she would want to play her. According to Dawson, someone at Lucasfilm forwarded the reply to Filoni<ref name="Best of Jedi">{{SW|url=news/rosario-dawson-ahsoka-tano-interview|text=The Best of the Jedi: Rosario Dawson on Bringing Ahsoka Tano to Live-Action and ''The Mandalorian''}}</ref> and he thought she would make a good Ahsoka Tano.<ref name="Tells all" /> Filoni and ''Star Wars'' then followed Dawson on Twitter, but nothing happened for a long time.<ref name="Best of Jedi" /> Filoni kept loosely aware of Dawson's [[Wikipedia:Marvel Cinematic Universe|Marvel]] career and he watched interviews of her expressing excitement for the role. When he started working with Favreau, he would bring up Tano and say that Dawson was at the top of his list for the role.<ref name="Tells all" /> Eventually, Filoni and Dawson had a discussion about the show in which he revisited Dawson's casting and compared the actress' age with Tano's age, and he decided that the casting was feasible.<ref name="Best of Jedi" />

Filoni and Favreau did not want to include Tano in [[The Mandalorian Season One|Season One]]<ref name="Best of Jedi" /> because Filoni did not want to mess up Tano's portrayal,<ref name="Ahsoka transformation">{{SW|url=news/star-wars-the-mandalorian-guide-to-season-two-exclusive-excerpt|text=Explore Ahsoka Tano's Transformation in ''Star Wars: The Mandalorian: Guide to Season Two'' &ndash; Exclusive Excerpt}}</ref> but it remained at the back of his mind.<ref name="Best of Jedi" /> During early production of Season One, Favreau told Filoni that the Child's name would be Grogu, which made Filoni think of ways the audience could learn his name. He thought that Tano could learn Grogu's name by communicating through memories and experiences, and she could tell the [[Din Djarin|Mandalorian]] and the audience. Filoni and Favreau knew they wanted Tano in the [[The Mandalorian Season Two|second season's]] story, so they started visualizing Dawson in the role, and art of the actress playing Tano was created. Near the release of the first season, Dawson got a [[Wikipedia:FaceTime|FaceTime]] call from Filoni and Favreau, and they showed her visuals and concept from their plans for Season Two, including art of Dawson as Tano. They asked Dawson if she wanted to play Tano and she agreed.<ref name="Tells all" /> Filoni's ultimate goal was to give Tano her own television series.<ref name="Rebellion televised" />

Tano appeared in the episode [[Chapter 13: The Jedi]], which was written and directed by Filoni.<ref name="Best of Jedi" /> When Filoni penned the episode's script, he created<ref name="Tells all" /> [[Lang]],<ref name="Chapter 13">{{TheMandalorian|Chapter 13: The Jedi}}</ref> an experienced gunslinger to go up against the Mandalorian. When he was writing the [[Calodan|setting]] for the episode, he drew inspiration from the [[fire]]s in [[Wikipedia:California|Northern California]] where he lived. He thought the [[planet]] would be really haunting and have a foreboding feeling. When Filoni wrote the scene in which Tano reveals Grogu's history, he shaped it around when [[Obi-Wan Kenobi]] told [[Luke Skywalker]] about his father in ''A New Hope''. He had to stop himself from having Tano call Grogu a "Yoda baby."<ref name="Tells all" /> Filoni pointed to [[Wikipedia:Gandalf|Gandalf]] from [[Wikipedia:The Lord of the Rings (film series)|''The Lord of the Rings'']] as a touchpoint for Tano, which factored into Dawson's portrayal of the Jedi. He talked about classic [[Wikipedia:Samurai|samurai]] films such as [[Wikipedia:Yojimbo|''Yojimbo'']], and he described Tano as someone wandering [[the galaxy]] helping people in need.<ref name="Best of Jedi" />

Filoni debated having Dawson wear contacts, and she tried them on to show the difference it made. Dawson insisted on wearing contacts to match Tano's blue eyes.<ref name="Best of Jedi" /> Filoni wanted Tano's facial markings to be subtle and look very natural instead of looking like face paint in ''The Clone Wars''.<ref name="Tells all" /> When Dawson showed up on the set in costume, Filoni felt relieved that he would not need to worry about Tano, allowing him to focus on everything else. When Dawson noticed that the horns and [[lekku|headtails]] were shorter than in the animated shows, Filoni assured her it was necessary for stunts.<ref name="Ahsoka for life">{{WebCite|url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2022/05/rosario-dawson-ahsoka-tano-star-wars-series|text=''Star Wars'': Rosario Dawson Aims To Play Ahsoka Tano For Life|author=Breznican, Anthony|date=2022-05-20|work=[[Wikipedia:Vanity Fair (magazine)|''Vanity Fair'']]|archivedate=20220520134642}}</ref> Filoni knew the actress who plays the [[Morgan Elsbeth|Magistrate]] would have to be someone who can instantly challenge Tano. [[Diana Lee Inosanto]] played the [[Magistrate]], and Filoni found her skill and choreography experience to be a great advantage. Filoni felt that the episode had a good match-up of Tano and the Mandalorian against the Magistrate and her gunslinger.<ref name="Tells all" /> According to property master [[Josh Roth]], [[Ahsoka Tano's white lightsabers]] kept getting bigger because of the batteries and receivers inside them. Filoni said the [[lightsaber]]s could not look like that, so they ended up being powered by an external battery.<ref name="Ahsoka transformation" />

During the [[The Walt Disney Company]]'s Investor Day event on [[December 10]], [[2020]], Kathleen Kennedy announced that the ''[[Star Wars: Ahsoka]]'' television series will be written by Filoni and executive produced by Filoni and Jon Favreau. It was also announced that they will executive produce the show ''[[Rangers of the New Republic]]''.<ref name="Announcement">{{SW|url=news/future-lucasfilm-projects-revealed|text=Future Lucasfilm Projects Revealed}}</ref>

Filoni worked as an executive producer,<ref name="Stranger">{{BOBF|Chapter 1: Stranger in a Strange Land}}</ref> director, and writer for ''[[Star Wars: The Book of Boba Fett]]'' television series.<ref name="Desert">{{BOBF|Chapter 6: From the Desert Comes a Stranger}}</ref> He felt it was important to include [[Mark Hamill]] in the process of portraying Luke Skywalker to make sure he is authentic. Filoni shared insights he learned from George Lucas while Hamill shared insights from his experience playing Skywalker.<ref name="Filoni joins SWCA" />

During early production of the ''[[Star Wars: Obi-Wan Kenobi]]'' television series, Filoni attended a meeting with director [[Deborah Chow]] and propmaster [[Brad Elliott]] to discuss translating the animated [[Double-bladed spinning lightsaber|Inquisitor lightsabers]] into realistic props. Filoni advised the propmaster to imagine the live-action came first, and the animated material was an exaggerated version of that. This allowed Elliott to keep the form factor of the props established in animation and to bring them in line with props made for [[human]] [[Limb|hands]].<ref name="Weapons of Inquis">{{SW|url=news/inside-the-lucasfilm-archive-obi-wan-kenobi-props-2|text=Inside the Lucasfilm Archive: Weapons of the Inquisitorius from the ''Obi-Wan Kenobi'' Limited Series}}</ref>

When Filoni was traveling on an airplane, he wrote little shorts, which he only let Carrie Beck and [[Athena Yvette Portillo]] read. They liked the stories and asked Filoni if he would like to make the stories. He agreed to work on the project, and Beck and Portillo found the resources and people to make the animated series [[Star Wars: Tales of the Jedi (television series)|''Star Wars: Tales of the Jedi'']]. Filoni enjoyed reconnecting with the animation team and working with Ashley Eckstein, [[Matt Lanter]], and [[James Arnold Taylor]] again.<ref name="Filoni joins SWCA" />

When Filoni was writing all of the episodes for ''Ahsoka'', he worked on and connected several charts, outlines, and graphs. Jon Favreau enhanced the way Filoni writes by indicating things from the perspective of someone not deep in animation and Ahsoka Tano's history. After Filoni completed a draft, he would first send it to Beck, who worked with him on the project.<ref name="Ahsoka for life" /> Filoni and Favreau met actress [[Natasha Liu Bordizzo]] months after she sent a self-tape. After Bordizzo was cast as the Mandalorian [[Sabine Wren]]<ref name="Day Shift">{{WebCite|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-features/day-shift-natasha-liu-bordizzo-star-wars-sabine-wren-1235202889|text='Day Shift' Star Natasha Liu Bordizzo Is Living Her 'Star Wars' Dream as Sabine Wren|author=Davids, Brian|date=2022-08-20|work=[[Wikipedia:The Hollywood Reporter|''The Hollywood Reporter'']]|archivedate=20221016144800}}</ref> in [[2021]],<ref name="Rosario Natasha SWCA">{{SWYouTube|8RZiIf_oEAc|Rosario Dawson and Natasha Liu Bordizzo takes the stage at SWCA 2022 &#124; Star Wars Celebration Live!}}</ref> Filoni and Favreau told her it would be great if she watched ''Star Wars Rebels'', but they said the live-action version would always be a new thing that finds its own truth.<ref name="Day Shift" />

''Ahsoka'' started production on [[May 9]], [[2022]].<ref name="Ahsoka begins">{{SW|url=news/ahsoka-begins-production|text=''Ahsoka'', the Upcoming Disney+ Original Series, Begins Production}}</ref> Filoni oversaw the show<ref name="Rebellion televised" /> and was on set every day.<ref name="Rosario Natasha SWCA" /> He started to understand that people who watched ''The Clone Wars'' as kids are excited about things they grew up with. Filoni wanted Tano to drive towards a goal instead of going on singular adventures.<ref name="Rebellion televised" /> He felt that continuing the story of characters from ''Star Wars Rebels'' was the most honest thing he could do.<ref name="Filoni joins SWCA" /> At Lucasfilm's Studio Showcase at [[Celebration Anaheim (2022)|Celebration Anaheim]], [[2022]], Filoni said that production on ''Ahsoka'' was going great, and he and Favreau played a video message from [[Rosario Dawson]] in her full costume from the show's set.<ref name="20 Highlights">{{SW|url=news/swca-2022-20-highlights-from-lucasfilms-studio-showcase|text=SWCA 2022: 20 Highlights from Lucasfilm's Studio Showcase}}</ref> At the Mando+ panel, Filoni and Favreau showed the first footage from ''Ahsoka'' and introduced Bordizzo as Wren in the series.<ref name="MandoPlus">{{SW|url=news/swca-2022-mando-plus-panel|text=SWCA 2022: Sabine Wren Joins the Cast of ''Ahsoka'' and 6 Other Favorite Moments from the Mando+ Panel}}</ref>

===Chief Creative Officer and President===
{{Quote|He's very talented and immersed in ''Star Wars''. He and Pablo Hidalgo are like the walking encyclopedias inside the company. They always have the answer when you ask, 'Hey, can I do this with a lightsaber?' Or, 'What colors were the sabers?' Or anything else you need to know with what has come before.|Kathleen Kennedy in 2026|<ref name="Exit interview">{{WebCite|url=https://deadline.com/2026/01/kathleen-kennedy-exit-interview-1236665253/|text=A Shift In The Force For Lucasfilm’s Kathleen Kennedy: The Exit Interview|author=Mike Flemming Jr.|date=2026-01-15|work=[[Wikipedia:Deadline Hollywood|''Deadline'']]|archivedate=20260115233219}}</ref>}}
In a [[November 21]], [[2023]] interview after the release of ''Ahsoka'', Filoni revealed he had been given the position of Chief Creative Officer of Lucasfilm. In this role, he will work closely with Kennedy and veteran producer [[Carrie Beck]].<ref name="VanityFairCCO" />

On [[January 15]], [[2026]], Lucasfilm publicly announced that Kathleen Kennedy would step down as president and leave the company<ref name="Departure">{{SW|url=news/lucasfilm-leadership|text=Lucasfilm and the Walt Disney Studios Announce Leadership Transition}}</ref> the following months<ref name="Exit interview" /> and that she will be succeeded by Filoni as President together with<ref name="Departure" /> former Lucasfilm Executive Vice President and General Manager<ref name="Exit interview" /> [[Lynwen Brennan]] as Co-President.<ref name="Departure" /> Kennedy's departure and Filoni and Brennan as her successors had been planned by her together with Disney's [[Bob Iger (chief executive officer)|Bob Iger]] and [[Alan Bergman]] for two years prior.<ref name="Exit interview" />

==Personal life==
