'''1977 Topps ''Star Wars'' Series 1''' was the first ''[[Star Wars]]'' trading card set released by [[Topps]] in [[June]] [[1977]]. The set was based on [[Star Wars: Episode IV A New Hope|''Star Wars'': Episode IV ''A New Hope'']], and featured stills from the film, promotional photos and behind the scenes photos, all within a blue starfield border. Backs of the cards featured "Story Summary" or "Movie Facts" themes, or included a portion of a larger image which could be pieced together with other cards like a puzzle. When combined, the cards would make two images. One was a photo from the film of [[Luke Skywalker/Legends|Luke Skywalker]], [[Chewbacca/Legends|Chewbacca]], [[Han Solo/Legends|Han Solo]], and [[Obi-Wan Kenobi/Legends|Obi-Wan Kenobi]] in the ''[[Millennium Falcon/Legends|Millennium Falcon]]''. The second was the [[Greg Hildebrandt|Hildebrandt]] [[Tim Hildebrandt|Brother's]] poster for ''A New Hope'', as redone by artist Augie Napoli. A sub-set of sticker cards was included. The set was followed by [[1977 Topps Star Wars Series 2|1977 Topps ''Star Wars'' Series 2]].

==Development==
===Conception===
{{Quote|Before long, Topps representative Dave Friedman finally told 20th Century-Fox that we were indeed interested in licensing ''The Star Wars''.|Topps editor Gary Gerani|<ref name="Volume One" />}}
In preparation for the release of [[Star Wars: Episode IV A New Hope|''Star Wars'': Episode IV ''A New Hope'']], [[20th Century Studios|20th Century Fox]] executive Marc Pevers came to [[Topps|The Topps Company]] to pitch the idea to create a set of trading cards to promote the film. Employees had originally been skeptical on the pitch but eventually, Topps president Arthur Shorin warmed up to the idea, and company representative Dave Friedman notified Fox that Topps was interested in licensing ''[[Star Wars]]''.<ref name="Volume One" />

===Production===
{{Quote|For the first series of Star Wars cards, images were mostly printed from standard 35 mm slides. These were forwarded to our headquarters in Brooklyn, and there was nothing especially exotic about the content.|Gary Gerani|<ref name="Volume One" />}}
The images for the cards were printed on 35mm [[Wikipedia:Reversal film|slides]]. The blue starfield backgrounds used for the set were taken straight from their [[Wikipedia:Star Trek|''Star Trek'']] sets, the only difference being that an image of an [[X-wing starfighter/Legends|X-wing starfighter]] replaced an image of the [[Wikipedia:USS Enterprise (NCC-1701-D)|''USS Enterprise'']]. The sixty-six cards of the set depicted stills, promotional images, and behind-the-scenes photos from the [[original trilogy]] film [[Star Wars: Episode IV A New Hope|''Star Wars'': Episode IV ''A New Hope'']]. Additionally, eleven sticker cards were produced to accompany the main set. Art director Ben Solomon purposefully colored the background of card 64, titled "Governor of Imperial Outlands," green, so that the head of Grand Moff Tarkin would stand out.<ref name="Volume One" />

Backs of the cards featured "Story Summary" or "Movie Facts" sections which provided lore and behind-the-scenes information respectively. The back of some cards included a portion of a larger image which could be pieced together with other cards as a puzzle. When combined, the cards would make two separate images. One was a scene from the film of [[Farmer/Legends|farm boy]] Luke Skywalker, [[Smuggler/Legends|smugglers]] Chewbacca and Han Solo, and [[Jedi Master/Legends|Jedi Master]] Obi-Wan Kenobi in the cockpit of Solo's [[Starship/Legends|ship]], the ''Millennium Falcon''. The second was the [[Greg Hildebrandt|Hildebrandt]] [[Tim Hildebrandt|Brother's]] poster for ''A New Hope'', as redone by artist Augie Napoli. The cards were shipped to the Topps headquarters in [[Wikipedia:Brooklyn|Brooklyn]], [[Wikipedia:New York City|New York City]] to be released<ref name="Volume One" /> in [[June]] of [[1977]].<ref name="Cargo Bay Series 1" />

==Release and reception==
{{Quote|''Charlie's Angels'' was hot that year, too. It was a race to see which set of cards could sustain more sets.|Gary Gerani|<ref name="Insider 53">{{InsiderCite|53|Getting to the Bottom of the Topps Star Wars Trading Cards|Getting to the Bottom of the Topps ''Star Wars'' Trading Cards}}</ref>}}
The set was a hit for Topps,<ref name="Insider 53" /> bringing in sales like never seen before.<ref name="Volume One" />

==Continuity==
{{Quote|It should really be spelled 'lightsaber,' but this was relatively early on, and even the ''Star Wars'' Corporation experts weren't certain about the proper presentation of certain exotic words/terms...though we learned quite quickly that there are two e's in 'Wookiee.'|Gary Gerani|<ref name="Volume One" />}}
Card 2 titled, "[[C-3PO/Legends|See-Threepio]] and [[R2-D2/Legends|Artoo-Detoo]]," was originally just supposed to be a card of C-3PO. However, after a disagreement with Ben Solomon, both droids were added. The caption of card 53, titled "Battle in outer space!", is a homage to the [[1950s|1959]] [[Wikipedia:Cinema of Japan|Japanese]] [[Wikipedia:Science fiction film|sci-fi film]], [[Wikipedia:Battle in Outer Space|Battle in Outer Space]].<ref name="Volume One" /> In story summary 3 on the back of card 17 "Lord Vader threatens Princess Leia!," C-3PO is misspelled as "3-CPO." In card 7, titled "The villainous Darth Vader,"<ref name="Volume One" /> an image is used from a [[Content cut from Star Wars|deleted scene]] featuring [[Anakin Skywalker/Legends|Darth Vader]] and [[Moradmin Bast/Legends|Moradmin Bast]].<ref name="blu-ray">''[[Star Wars: The Complete Saga]]''</ref> Card 20, titled "Hunted by the Sandpeoples!," incorrectly states that [[Tusken Raider/Legends|Tusken Raiders]] hunted [[Human/Legends|humans]]. In card 64, [[Grand Moff/Legends|Grand Moff]] [[Wilhuff Tarkin/Legends|Tarkin]] is referred to as the "Governor of the [[Outer Rim Territories/Legends|Imperial Outlands]]."<ref name="Volume One" /> The referenced "Imperial Outlands" would later go on to be known as the [[Outer Rim Territories/Legends|Outer Rim Territories]].<ref name="TVD">''[[Star Wars: The Visual Dictionary]]''</ref> Multiple cards incorrectly refer to the [[Lars homestead/Legends|Lars homestead]] as a control room.<ref name="Volume One" />

==Legacy==
The set was followed by [[1977 Topps Star Wars Series 2|1977 Topps ''Star Wars'' Series 2]], released alongside the first one.<ref name="Cargo Bay Series 2">{{CargoBay|url=browse/215|text=Topps Star Wars Series 2}}</ref> In [[2015]], Topps released the 1977 Topps ''Star Wars'' Series 1 Remastered Edition on their digital application ''[[Star Wars: Card Trader]]''. Between [[June 8]] and [[August 12]], the cards were released one per day.<ref name="CardTrader">{{ToppsDigital|subdomain=www|url=swcardtrader/article/5575fc3ecc1575215b82dbca|text=From Cardboard to Card Trader, it's 1977 Series 1|date=2015-06-08|nolive=1}}</ref> The cards were all showcased and commented on in the collection art book ''[[Star Wars: The Original Topps Trading Card Series, Volume One]]'', written by [[Gary Gerani]] and published by [[Abrams Books]]<ref name="Volume One" /> on [[November 17]], [[2015]].<ref name="VolumeOneDate">{{Abrams|url=product/star-wars_9781419711725|text=Star Wars (Hardcover)}}</ref>
