'''Funeral Theme''' is a musical motif composed by [[John Williams]] that appears in the soundtracks of [[Prequel trilogy|all]] [[Original trilogy|three]] [[Sequel trilogy|trilogies]] of the [[Star Wars saga|''Star Wars'' saga]].

It is first heard briefly after [[Yoda]] transforms into the [[Cosmic Force]] in [[death]] in [[Star Wars: Episode VI Return of the Jedi|''Return of the Jedi'']], released in [[1983]]. A longer choir is added to the motif during the [[Funeral of Qui-Gon Jinn]] in the [[1999]] film [[Star Wars: Episode I The Phantom Menace|''The Phantom Menace'']], and further developed in [[Star Wars: Episode III Revenge of the Sith|''Revenge of the Sith'']] during the death of [[Padmé Amidala Naberrie|Padmé Amidala]], [[Marriage|wife]] to [[Anakin Skywalker]] and [[Parent|mother]] to the twins [[Luke Skywalker|Luke]] and [[Leia Skywalker Organa Solo|Leia]], and the "rebirth" of Anakin as [[Darth]] Vader in his [[Darth Vader's armor/Legends|suit]]. It is then played throughout the ending sequence of the [[2005]] film without a choir, as the [[Padmé Amidala's funeral|funeral of Padmé Amidala]] takes place and when Vader and [[Darth Sidious]] observe the beginning of the [[DS-1 Orbital Battle Station/Legends|Death Star's]] construction. A rendition of the funeral leitmotif is also used in the the track "[[Star Wars: The Rise of Skywalker (soundtrack)|The Final Saber Duel]]" for the [[2019]] film [[Star Wars: Episode IX The Rise of Skywalker|''The Rise of Skywalker'']], signifying the [[Force projection|sacrifice]] of Leia Skywalker Organa Solo. However, the motif was lifted from the final cut of the film.

In Episode I's rendition, there was also an ominous musical cue that chimes twice that occurs around the time that [[Mace Windu/Legends|Mace Windu]] wonders whether [[Darth Maul/Legends|Darth Maul]] was the [[Sith Master/Legends|Sith Master]] or the [[Sith apprentice/Legends|apprentice]], which was meant to hint that Palpatine was the other [[Sith Lord/Legends|Sith Lord]].
