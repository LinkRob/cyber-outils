'''Andy Serkis''' (born [[April 20]], [[1960s#1964|1964]]) is an [[Wikipedia:English|English]] [[Wikipedia:Actor|actor]] who played [[Snoke]] in the film [[Star Wars: Episode VII The Force Awakens|''Star Wars'': Episode VII ''The Force Awakens'']] and reprised the role in [[Star Wars: Episode VIII The Last Jedi|''Star Wars'': Episode VIII ''The Last Jedi'']]. He then lastly played [[Darth Sidious|Palpatine's]] imitation of Snoke in [[Star Wars: Episode IX The Rise of Skywalker|''Star Wars'': Episode IX ''The Rise of Skywalker'']]. His involvement was first announced on [[April 29]], [[2014]].<ref name="Cast">{{SW|url=news/star-wars-episode-vii-cast-announced|text=Star Wars: Episode VII Cast Announced}}</ref> He later portrayed [[Kino Loy]] in the [[2022]] [[Disney+]] television series ''[[Star Wars: Andor]]'', making his first appearance in the eighth episode, "[[Narkina 5 (episode)|Narkina 5]]."

Serkis is best known for his various motion capture roles, such as [[W:c:lotr:Gollum|Gollum/Sméagol]] in ''[[Wikipedia:The Lord of the Rings (film series)|The Lord of the Rings]]'' and ''[[Wikipedia:The Hobbit (film series)|The Hobbit]]'', the [[W:c:kingkong:Kong 2005|titular character]] in [[Wikipedia:Peter Jackson|Peter Jackson's]] [[2005]] film ''[[Wikipedia:King Kong (2005 film)|King Kong]]'', [[W:c:planetoftheapes:Caesar (CE)|Caesar]] in ''[[Wikipedia:Rise of the Planet of the Apes|Rise of the Planet of the Apes]]'', ''[[Wikipedia:Dawn of the Planet of the Apes|Dawn of the Planet of the Apes]] ''and ''[[Wikipedia:War for the Planet of the Apes|War for the Planet of the Apes]],'' and [[W:c:tintin:Captain Archibald Haddock|Captain Haddock]] in ''[[Wikipedia:The Adventures of Tintin (film)|The Adventures of Tintin]]''. He is also notable for roles such as [[W:c:mcu:Ulysses Klaue|Ulysses Klaue]] in the [[Wikipedia:Marvel Cinematic Universe|Marvel Cinematic Universe]] and [[W:c:the-batman-universe:Alfred Pennyworth|Alfred Pennyworth]] in the [[2022]] film ''[[Wikipedia:The Batman (film)|The Batman]]'', as well as portraying [[Wikipedia:Knull|Knull]] and directing the [[2021]] movie ''[[Wikipedia:Venom: Let There Be Carnage|Venom: Let There Be Carnage]]''.

==Biography==
===Sequel trilogy===
Andy Serkis was born on [[April 20]], [[1960s#1964|1964]].<ref name="A Sneak Peek at Disney" /> He played [[Snoke]] in the ''[[Star Wars]]'' [[sequel trilogy]] films [[Star Wars: Episode VII The Force Awakens|''Star Wars'': Episode VII ''The Force Awakens'']]<ref name="Episode 7">{{Film|VII}}</ref> and [[Star Wars: Episode VIII The Last Jedi|''Star Wars'': Episode VIII ''The Last Jedi'']]<ref name="Episode 8">{{Film|VIII}}</ref> using [[Wikipedia:Motion capture|performance capture]] [[Wikipedia:Visual effects|VFX]].<ref name="Narkina 5 Guide">{{SW|url=andor-s1-e8-trivia-gallery|text="Narkina 5" Trivia Gallery &#124; Andor}} {{C|Slide 2}}</ref> He thought the [[Star Wars Anthology Series|anthology]] film ''[[Rogue One: A Star Wars Story]]'' was a great film,<ref name="Emotional">{{WebCite|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-features/andor-andy-serkis-on-episode-10-1235257860|text=‘Andor' Star Andy Serkis Talks Episode 10's Emotional Moment and Dreading Snoke-Kino Fan Theories|author=Davids, Brian|date=2022-11-09|work=[[Wikipedia:The Hollywood Reporter|''The Hollywood Reporter'']]|archivedate=20221109161046}}</ref> and he liked how it felt like real grit and sweat.<ref name="Tragedy">{{SW|url=news/andy-serkis-andor-interview|text=The Tragedy of Kino Loy: Andy Serkis on His Return to ''Star Wars'' in ''Andor''}}</ref> Serkis was a huge fan of the film.<ref name="Emotional" /> Serkis later provided Snoke's voice in the film [[Star Wars: Episode IX The Rise of Skywalker|''Star Wars'': Episode IX ''The Rise of Skywalker'']].<ref name="Episode 9">{{Film|IX}}</ref>

===''Andor'' television series===
{{Quote|I hope you're prepared to hear 'One way out' for the rest of your life.|[[Amy Ratcliffe]]|<ref name="SWC 2023">{{SWYouTube|hil5cr-tLxY|Star Wars Celebration LIVE! 2023 &ndash; DAY 3|date=2023-04-09}}</ref>}}
[[Tony Gilroy]],<ref name="Career Highlight">{{WebCite|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-features/andor-episode-8-toby-haynes-forest-whitaker-stellan-skarsgard-1235250954|text=‘Andor' Director Toby Haynes Talks That Forest Whitaker-Stellan Skarsgard Scene: “It Was a Career Highlight”|author=Davids, Brian|date=2022-10-28|work=[[Wikipedia:The Hollywood Reporter|''The Hollywood Reporter'']]|archivedate=20221029050743}}</ref> the executive producer and writer of the ''[[Star Wars: Andor]]'' television series,<ref name="Kassa">{{Andor|Kassa (episode)}}</ref> talked to Serkis for a while<ref name="Career Highlight" /> and explained what he wanted to do with the character [[Kino Loy]] and a particular segment of the world.<ref name="Emotional" /> Gilroy tried to line Serkis up for the role, but he did not know if the actor would do it or not. His casting was not affected by his previous role as Snoke.<ref name="Career Highlight" /> Serkis' only problem was he thought his appearance would cause uproar and confusion for the audience, and they would believe there is a connection between Snoke and Loy. He thought the idea of Loy was interesting and intoxicating because he is at the opposite side of the spectrum as Snoke and shows the [[human]] perspective of someone coping with the [[Galactic Empire]] crushing their soul.<ref name="Emotional" /> One of the reasons Serkis was eager for the role was because he wanted to play a character opposite [[Diego Luna]], the actor who played [[Cassian Jeron Andor|Cassian Andor]].<ref name="Tragedy" /> After getting through the quagmire around the Snoke theories, Serkis quickly committed to the role.<ref name="Emotional" />

[[File:Andor_Kino_Loy_Poster.jpg|thumb|left|160px|Andy Serkis as Kino Loy in his character poster]]
Serkis said he really fell in love with his character. After Serkis received the pitch from Gilroy and read the scripts for the three-episode arc,<ref name="Tragedy" /> he created a backstory for Loy in which he had a family and was a [[factory]] worker, shop steward, and a foreman. He stood up for workers' rights, but he was seen as a troublemaker and unpatriotic in a way. After ending up in prison, he forgot about his cause and became a tough shell of his former self, only wanting to get through the day.<ref name="Emotional" /> Loy also wanted to get out and return to his family.<ref name="Unveils">{{WebCite|url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2022/10/andy-serkis-andor-kino-loy|text=Meet Kino Loy: Andy Serkis Unveils His New ''Star Wars'' Character|author=Breznican, Anthony|date=2022-10-26|work=[[Wikipedia:Vanity Fair (magazine)|''Vanity Fair'']]|archivedate=20221026073045}}</ref> Serkis wanted the backstory to show that the Empire knocked the integrity out of Loy.<ref name="Emotional" /> He believed that Loy was brainwashed by the prison system, but he still knew he was imprisoned unfairly. The actor equated the prison to places around the world where people are in servitude or forced labor to build something. He figured that Loy initially clashed with Cassian Andor because Andor's wildness and untamed quality juxtaposed his own tameness.<ref name="Unveils" /> Serkis was excited by Loy and Andor gradually becoming united in the show. He figured that Loy's initial toughness was intentionally established so that it could break down gradually as he finds himself.<ref name="Tragedy" />

Serkis was not self-conscious about sounding like Snoke. Serkis had big ideas on what he wanted to do with Loy in the series. In one of the rehearsals,<ref name="Career Highlight" /> Serkis tried his father's mild [[Wikipedia:Middle East|Middle Eastern]] accent, which he thought was grounded and worked well. It was decided that the accent would overcomplicate things<ref name="Emotional" /> and did not fit. Director [[Toby Haynes]] and the crew wanted every actor to use their own accent to provide realism, so the accent was dropped in favor of Serkis' own accent. Serkis also tried to find the correct kind of tonal quality that fit Loy's class level.<ref name="Career Highlight" />

[[File:Narkina_5_bts_filming.png|thumb|right|230px|Andy Serkis and Diego Luna in the Narkina 5 set]]
According to executive producer [[Sanne Wohlenberg]], the scenes in the Narkina-5 prison were the last to be shot for ''Andor'' Season One.<ref name="SWE Roundtable">{{YouTube|video=CZq4wKUM410|text=Were the Prison Episodes TOO Dark for Star Wars?|channel=@StarWarsExplained|name=Star Wars Explained|quote=The prison episodes of Star Wars Andor were very bleak for a Star Wars story. I got the chance to ask the writer of the arc about the balance between darkness and hope. Here is what he had to say.}}</ref> Serkis talked about the films [[Wikipedia:The Shawshank Redemption|''The Shawshank Redemption'']] and [[Wikipedia:Cool Hand Luke|''Cool Hand Luke'']] on set, but the actors could not have any personal effects.<ref name="Unveils" /> The actors would walk on metal plates for weeks with bare feet, which drained their energy. Serkis said the set, costume design, and prison outfits worked on him psychologically and took away his sense of identity.<ref name="Messed head">{{WebCite|url=https://collider.com/andy-serkis-andor-prison-set-psychology-comments|text='Andor': Andy Serkis on How the Prison Set Messed With His Head|author=Amin, Arezou|date=2022-11-04|work=[[Wikipedia:Collider (website)|''Collider'']]|archivedate=20221104174549}}</ref> He felt that the set design was phenomenal in the way that it make him feel like he was in a strange kind of experiment.<ref name="Tragedy" /> There were many scenes where Serkis and other barefoot actors would spend hours waiting in long tubes in the prison. The tubes would get smelly and rank because the doors were sealed at the ends, so the actor viewed the experience like walking into a big test tube.<ref name="Emotional" />

Serkis and the actors would have to stand because there was no room for private space, making him feel incarcerated.<ref name="Messed head" /> He found it incredible to intimately get to work with Diego Luna due to viewing him as a major talent and gifted actor and storyteller.<ref name="Tragedy" /> Serkis enjoyed performing the "never more than twelve" line, viewing it as a beautifully written transitional moment for Loy. He believed that Loy had a difficult time giving the rousing speech<ref name="Emotional" /> in the episode "[[One Way Out]]"<ref name="One Way Out">{{Andor|One Way Out}}</ref> because he knew he could not swim away from the prison. Serkis thought that Loy had a wonderful arc, and he enjoyed going on a journey with his character.<ref name="Emotional" /> Serkis attended [[Celebration Europe (2023)|Celebration Europe]] in [[April]], [[2023]].<ref name="SWCE">{{SW|url=news/swce-2023-celebrity-guests|text=Star Wars Celebration Europe 2023 Announces First Celebrity Guests}}</ref>

Although in a press release released by Walt Disney Studios after [[Celebration Europe (2023)]], Serkis was credited as returning for [[Andor Season 2|''Andor'' Season 2]]<ref name="Cast Getty">{{Getty|subdomain=assets|url=bf-boulder-whitelabelbucket-getty-prod/r58vvshrv5xcqqbzgzqmjtnx/v/1093969948/original/4.11.23%20SW%20Celeb%20Tues%20Wrap%20Up%20Press%20Blast.pdf|text=SW Celeb Tues Wrap Up Press Blast|date=2023-04-11}}</ref> he remained absent for the remainder of the series.<ref name="S2">[[Andor Season 2|''Andor'' Season 2]]</ref><ref name="THR">{{WebCite|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-news/andor-finale-twists-tony-gilroy-interview-1236212162|text='Andor' Creator Tony Gilroy on Series Finale's Biggest Moves and ‘Rogue One’ Links|date=2025-05-14|author=Hibberd, James|work=[[Wikipedia:The Hollywood Reporter|''The Hollywood Reporter'']]|archivedate=20250515010833}}</ref>
