'''David "Max" Maxwell''' is a [[Wikipedia:Video game developer|video game developer]] who was one of the mission builders of the [[LucasArts|LucasArts Entertainment Company]] [[Star Wars video games|video game]]s [[Star Wars: X-Wing (video game)|''Star Wars: X-Wing'']] and [[Star Wars: TIE Fighter (video game)|''Star Wars: TIE Fighter'']]. A [[Wikipedia:Computer science|Computer Science]] [[Wikipedia:Graduation|graduate]] of [[Wikipedia:Columbia Basin College|Columbia Basin College]], he initially joined Lucasfilm Games as a tester, before joining [[Lawrence Holland]]'s [[Totally Games|Peregrine Software]]. He has since taught game design courses at the [[Wikipedia:College of Marin|College of Marin]] and [[Wikipedia:Stanford University|Stanford University]].

==Biography==
David Maxwell was born in [[Wikipedia:San Francisco, California|San Francisco]] and spent his early years in [[Wikipedia:San Rafael, California|San Rafael]]. He did his [[Wikipedia:Higher education|higher education]] at [[Wikipedia:Columbia Basin College|Columbia Basin College]] in [[Wikipedia:Washington|Washington]] and [[Wikipedia:Graduation|graduated]] with a [[Wikipedia:Academic degree|degree]] in [[Wikipedia:Computer science|Computer Science]].<ref name="Bio" />

Maxwell began working with [[LucasArts|Lucasfilm Games]] in [[1991]]<ref name="Resume" /> as a technician in the [[Wikipedia:Quality assurance|Quality Assurance]] department. His early work with the company involved testing [[Star Wars video games|video game]]s, including [[Wikipedia:Indiana Jones and the Fate of Atlantis|''Indiana Jones and the Fate of Atlantis'']], [[Star Wars (1991 video game)|''Star Wars'']], [[Star Wars: The Empire Strikes Back (1991 video game)|''The Empire Strikes Back'']], [[Wikipedia:Defenders of Dynatron City|''Defenders of Dynatron City'']] and [[Wikipedia:Secret Weapons of the Luftwaffe|''Secret Weapons of the Luftwaffe'']],<ref name="XW:TOSG">''[[X-Wing: The Official Strategy Guide]]''</ref> along with its [[Wikipedia:Expansion pack|expansion packs]]. Maxwell did not work exclusively for Lucasfilm, working also as a tester for [[Wikipedia:Spectrum HoloByte|Spectrum HoloByte]] around this time.<ref name="Resume" /> When fellow Lucasfilm developers [[Lawrence Holland]] and [[Edward Kilham]] began working on the ''[[Star Wars]]'' [[Wikipedia:Flight simulator|flight simulator]] [[Star Wars: X-Wing (video game)|''Star Wars: X-Wing'']] early in the [[1990]]s, Maxwell joined their team as a mission builder. Around this time he acquired the nickname "Max."<ref name="XW:TOSG" />

Working with his longtime friend [[David Wessman]], Maxwell was responsible for designing the missions in the game, and the two also served as the lead testers.<ref name="XW">[[Star Wars: X-Wing (video game)|''Star Wars: X-Wing'']]</ref> Maxwell and Wessman attempted to make the missions progress differently based on what the player did. To this end, certain events were triggered specifically by players attacking certain targets. Some missions also featured multiple endings. For example, failing in the mission "Protect a Disabled X-wing" resulted in the arrival of the [[Imperial I-class Star Destroyer/Legends|''Imperial I''-class Star Destroyer]] [[Intrepid (Imperial I-class)|''Intrepid'']] to capture a [[T-65 X-wing starfighter/Legends|T-65 X-wing starfighter]], while winning saw the [[Starfighter/Legends|starfighter]] rescued by [[Alliance to Restore the Republic/Legends|Rebel]] [[CR90 corvette/Legends|CR90 corvettes]]. However, it was possible to do the mission in such a way that both would arrive simultaneously, resulting in a battle between the [[Capital ship/Legends|capital ships]].<ref name="XW:TOSG" />

[[File:MaxwellWessman.jpg|thumb|left|220px|David Maxwell and David Wessman]]
As an avid simulator pilot, Maxwell tended to make difficult missions and had to learn to make them more approachable for less experienced players&mdash;this led him to be concerned that the game would prove too easy. One of his favorite missions, "Guard Vital Supply Depot," saw the player using Maxwell's starfighter of choice, the [[RZ-1 A-wing interceptor/Legends|A-wing interceptor]], to defend a Rebel weapons cache from attack by the [[Galactic Empire/Legends|Imperial]] [[EF76 Nebulon-B escort frigate/Legends|EF76 Nebulon-B escort frigate]] ''[[Retsub]]''. When the mission proved too difficult, Maxwell implemented three versions of it, varying the difficulty by having the [[Frigate/Legends|frigate]] start in different locations. The original version remained and, though many players considered it impossible, Maxwell maintained that drawing the frigate's fire to the player would allow the weapons cache to survive.<ref name="XW:TOSG" />

Following the release of ''X-Wing'' in [[February]] [[1993]], Maxwell worked with Wessman and [[Rusel DeMaria]] to write ''[[X-Wing: The Official Strategy Guide]]''. The book featured the story of the player character, [[Keyan Farlander/Legends|Keyan Farlander]], as written by DeMaria. Maxwell and Wessman contributed mission strategies and general tactics, along with most of the [[Wikipedia:Screenshot|screenshots]] in the book.<ref name="XW:TOSG" /> He continued his involvement with ''X-Wing'', designing levels for the game's two expansion packs, [[Star Wars: X-Wing Tour of Duty: Imperial Pursuit|''Imperial Pursuit'']] and ''[[Star Wars: X-Wing Tour of Duty: B-Wing]]''.<ref name="Resume" />

After Holland formed [[Totally Games|Peregrine Software]] in [[1994]], Maxwell joined him there in working on the sequel to ''X-Wing'', [[Star Wars: TIE Fighter (video game)|''Star Wars: TIE Fighter'']]. On ''TIE Fighter'', Maxwell once more worked on designing the missions&mdash;which he still found too easy<ref name="TF:TOSG">''[[TIE Fighter: The Official Strategy Guide]]''</ref>&mdash;with David Wessman, and also joined Wessman, Holland and Kilham in developing the overall story for the game.<ref name="TF">[[Star Wars: TIE Fighter (video game)|''Star Wars: TIE Fighter'']]</ref> He later contributed missions to the game's expansion packs, [[Star Wars: TIE Fighter: Defender of the Empire|''Defender of the Empire'']] and ''Enemies of the Empire'', the latter only included on the [[Star Wars: TIE Fighter Collector's CD-ROM|''Collector's CD-ROM'']] edition of the game.<ref name="Resume" /> Maxwell also worked with Wessman once more in submitting strategies and screenshots for the subsequent ''[[TIE Fighter: The Official Strategy Guide]]'', which also featured the story of [[Maarek Stele/Legends|Maarek Stele]] by DeMaria.<ref name="TF:TOSG" />
