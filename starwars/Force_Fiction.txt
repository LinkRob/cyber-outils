"'''Force Fiction'''" is a story by [[Kevin Rubio]]. It appeared in ''[[Star Wars Tales 7]]'' and was collected in ''[[Star Wars Tales Volume 2]]'' and ''[[Star Wars Legends Epic Collection: Rise of the Sith Vol. 2]]''.

==Plot summary==
[[Mace Windu/Legends|Mace Windu]] and [[Yoda/Legends|Yoda]] debate the possibility of training [[Anakin Skywalker/Legends|Anakin Skywalker]] and expressing their opinions about [[Chosen One/Legends|the Prophecy]] about the one, who will bring balance to [[The Force/Legends|the Force]]. While eating at a [[fast food]] restaurant, they are resorting to food-based analogies to make their points. At one point, Yoda uses his mind control powers on the waitress to make the meal free, to protect them from "evil price hikes." They also fend off a robbery. At the end of the story, [[Supreme Chancellor/Legends|Supreme Chancellor]] [[Palpatine/Legends|Palpatine]] joins them and expresses his opinion that Skywalker would make a fine [[Jedi/Legends|Jedi]].

==Continuity==
The title and the premise of the story are based on the frame story one of [[Samuel L. Jackson]]'s most famous movie roles, Jules Winnfield in [[Wikipedia:Pulp Fiction (film)|''Pulp Fiction'' (1994)]]. Windu recites variations of several lines which Winnfield spoke in the earlier film.

It is interesting to note that in the movies, it is Windu who is reluctant of training Anakin, he isn't even convinced that the boy is the "chosen one". This situation is reversed on Rubio's comic book as Mace is trying to convince Yoda that Anakin Skywalker will bring balance to the Force and should be trained. The fact that Yoda is using a purple lightsaber, while this is exclusively Windu's weapon in the movie, might indicate that Rubio is playing along with the roles of the characters.

Yoda's lightsaber in this comic is golden. It was established that Yoda gave a golden lightsaber to [[Qu Rahn/Legends|Qu Rahn]] in ''[[The Dark Forces Saga]]''. [[Abel G. Peña]] alluded to that saber being the one seen in this comic on the [[Jedi Council Forums]].<ref name="JCF">{{JCFCite|url=posts/20211166|board=Literature|thread=Kyle Katarn's Tale (Dark Forces Articles by Abel & Jason)|user=[[Abel G. Peña|Halagad_Ventor]]|date=2005-06-11|time=2:25 AM|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20200801144657/https://boards.theforce.net/threads/kyle-katarns-tale-dark-forces-articles-by-abel-jason.19114424/page-16}}</ref>

Identifiable characters are eating at five of the six tables visible in the background:
*[[Wikipedia:Buzz Lightyear|Buzz Lightyear]] from [[Wikipedia:Toy Story|''Toy Story'']] can be seen in the background at a table with the [[Wikipedia:Tex (THX)|THX Robot]].
*[[Wikipedia:Robby the Robot|Robby the Robot]] from [[Wikipedia:Forbidden Planet|''Forbidden Planet'']] is alongside the robot [[Wikipedia:Gort (The Day the Earth Stood Still)|Gort]] from [[Wikipedia:The Day the Earth Stood Still|''The Day the Earth Stood Still'']] at a second table.
*[[Wikipedia:The Beatles|The Beatles]] in their Sgt. Pepper outfits are at another table.
*A fourth table hosts [[George Lucas]], [[Rick McCallum]], and [[Jar Jar Binks/Legends|Jar Jar Binks]].
*The fifth of the tables with visible patrons hosts [[Kevin Rubio]] and [[Lucas Marangon]], the creators of this story.
*Two unidentified Wookiees are eating at the sixth of the visible tables.

The "Bob's Big Boy" statue from "[[A Death Star Is Born]]" can also be seen in the background of several panels.

At one point, Yoda, when demanding to know why Mace Windu would consider recruiting Anakin Skywalker into the Jedi, sarcastically asks whether its because Anakin has "more Whos in his Who-ville" than the other Jedi, which is both a play on the characters and place, respectively, from the Dr. Seuss stories, and a subtle reference to Anakin Skywalker's abnormally high midichlorian count.
