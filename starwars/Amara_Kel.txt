'''Amara Kel''', nicknamed "'''Shadow''',"<ref name="FACPOV" /> was a [[human]]<ref name="USW" /> [[Imperial Starfighter Pilot|TIE fighter pilot]] of the [[Galactic Empire]] as [[Theta Four]] in [[Theta Squadron (Galactic Empire)|Theta Squadron]]. She was more commonly referred to by her fellow [[pilot]]s by her nickname. During the [[Galactic Civil War]], she was romantically involved with another member of her squadron, "[[Howlrunner (Theta Squadron)|Howlrunner]]," or "Howl" for short.<ref name="FACPOV" />

==Biography==
{{Quote|Thanks!''"<br />"''N&hellip;no problem.|"Howlrunner" and Kel|<ref name="FACPOV" />}}
A conscript on behalf of the [[Galactic Empire]], Amara Kel took an intake exam, the results of which led to her being placed into the [[Imperial Navy]]. At some point, Kel adopted the callsign "Shadow." Kel developed a system of rules for survival as a [[TIE/ln space superiority starfighter|TIE fighter]] [[Imperial Starfighter Pilot|pilot]], including don't get attached, don't be a hero, and not to attack enemies head-on. Kel also got to know a TIE fighter pilot who was promoted to piloting a [[Lambda-class T-4a shuttle|Lambda shuttle]], a safe, prestigious position coveted by other TIE pilots. After a month, they began to miss the rush and transferred back to a line squadron, a decision Kel did not understand.<ref name="FACPOV" />

By [[2 ABY]], Kel had flown at least three tours of duty and was stationed aboard the ''Avenger'' as a [[TIE/ln space superiority starfighter|TIE fighter]] [[Imperial Starfighter Pilot|pilot]], assigned to [[Theta Squadron (Galactic Empire)|Theta Squadron]] as [[Theta Four]]. One day at mess, another TIE pilot, "[[Howlrunner (Theta Squadron)|Howlrunner]]," reported to join [[Theta Squadron]]. Infatuated by Howl's looks, Kel held back out of anxiety and waited to approach the other woman, who, that night, shot down several offers to bunk together.<ref name="FACPOV" />

On an early patrol shift, Kel inquired what a Howlrunner was. When Howl replied that it was a beast was from Kamar, Kel asked if Kamar was her homeworld, but Howl informed Kel that she had only chosen the name because it sounded cool, similar to others in basic. Having pegged Howl as a goofball, Kel laughed aloud but reminded herself of rule number one. Later, Howl asked Kel about her callsign, a question new to her. Kel, unsure, responded that nobody noticed a shadow.<ref name="FACPOV" />

A week later, taking action on information provided by [[Imperial Intelligence]], the ''Avenger'' attacked a Rebel supply base housed inside a Clone Wars-era deep-space installation that had been retrofitted into a battle station. Unaware of the upgrades until the assault, Captain [[Lorth Needa]] was forced to back away and deployed bombers and sixty TIE fighters, including Theta Squadron and Kel. Kel, who felt the attack was "junked" from the start, clipped one enemy starfighter. However, the Imperial lieutenant was killed, causing the skirmish to devolve into a dogfight that favored the Rebels.<ref name="FACPOV" />

Sliding behind a trio of A-wings, Kel destroyed one of them and caused the others to split up. As one of the fighters tried to pursue her, Kel executed a tight arc, generating overwhelming force that could be felt through the inertial dampeners and throwing it off her tail. Kel shot down the A-wing, which exploded in a fireball against the space station's side. As Kel observed the losing battle, Howl contacted her for help, in pursuit of the enemy leader, a Rebel veteran in an X-wing. Kel readied to tag the X-wing so Howl could break off, but Howl instructed her to initiate a left skid, imploring Kel's trust. Kel obeyed, throwing her TIE into a hard spin in time to see Howl execute an upside-down Koiogran crossed with a twist, leaving the Rebel vulnerable and in Kel's sights. Barely aiming, Kel opened fire, destroying the X-wing and its astromech. Howl nonchalantly thanked Kel, who, remaining stunned in disbelief of Howl's expertise, shakily accepted the praise.<ref name="FACPOV" />

Ordered to the ''Avenger'' five minutes later, Kel docked her TIE fighter fifteen after that. After five more, Kel and Howl showered together. Water scalding, bodies slick, the two shared thorough, frantic kisses, engaging in intercourse.<ref name="FACPOV" />

Howl's flying taught Kel to love the TIE fighter as a maneuverable starfighter, a sentiment that became Kel's fourth rule. Kel also taught Howl her first rule of non-attachment. After rule four, Kel came up with rule five, "Never eject."<ref name="FACPOV" />

==Personality and traits==
{{Quote|Rule number one: Don't get attached.|Amara Kel's Rules for TIE Pilot Survival (Probably)|<ref name="FACPOV" />}}
Amara Kel was a<ref name="FACPOV" /> human<ref name="USW" /> female.<ref name="FACPOV" />

Kel felt taxation and the Imperial Navy postal service losing its mail were universal constants. She considered pilots, like the starfighters they flew, to be expendable. Kel thought of pilots who had not yet completed a tour of duty as cloudflies. Kel was physically attracted to Howl, admiring her dark hair and lips.<ref name="FACPOV" />

Kel kept a list of rules for survival as a TIE fighter pilot, though emphasized that adherence did not guarantee success. Kel's first rule was not to form attachments. In Kel's opinion, becoming attached to a starfighter and modifying it led to over-reliance on its specificities, then being killed if caught without it. Kel did not get attached to officers and believed that high turnover was a good thing, as good officers got promoted but bad ones got those under their command killed. Kel allowed for fraternization with squadmates, including casual sexual relationships, but discouraged attachments, as trying to save a squadmate's life would  result in death and a condolence letter subsequently lost by the Navy post.<ref name="FACPOV" />

Rule number two was "Don't be a hero." Kel found that cadets, through ingrained, Academy-taught loyalty or perhaps sheer exuberance, took Imperial service too seriously and attempted highly dangerous maneuvers that got them killed, the leading cause of death among fighter pilots. Kel advised flying not slowly, another way to get killed, but cautiously. Rule number three, "Don't go at them head-on," was a direct contradiction of the Empire's tactics guide. Despite the Imperial focus on maximum firepower, Kel reasoned that Rebel starfighters were well-funded and more well-equipped, possessing shields, astromechs, and additional weapons that TIE fighters lacked. The Empire could trade TIEs against Rebel X-wings at a two-to-one ratio and come out ahead, but Kel refused to be among those two casualties.<ref name="FACPOV" />

After watching Howl fly, Kel learned rule number four: "Learn to love your machine." Despite her misgivings about the TIE/ln, an "ugly piece-of-junk made-by-the-lowest-bidder mass-production death trap," Kel considered it beautiful; a starfighter defined by  finesse, its stripped-down frame granted unmatched handling and incredible maneuverability. Rule number five, "Never eject," applied while in battle or deep space. Though lightly-armored, TIE fighters still offered their pilots greater protection than a debris-vulnerable Imperial flight suit and shielded against radiation. Also, as the Navy did not always take efforts to retrieve its pilots, not ejecting increased the odds of being found by a salvage ship. After violating rule number one to save Howl's life, Kel developed rule number six: "If you are going to get attached to somebody, make sure it's a girl who flies like an ash angel hopped up on death sticks."<ref name="FACPOV" />
