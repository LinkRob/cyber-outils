'''Deborah Chow''' is a [[Wikipedia:Canadians|Canadian]] filmmaker best known for the [[2010]] film [[Wikipedia:The High Cost of Living|''The High Cost of Living'']] and her work on the [[Wikipedia:Marvel Cinematic Universe|Marvel Cinematic Universe]] television series [[Wikipedia:Jessica Jones (TV series)|''Jessica Jones'']] in [[2018]]. She directed two episodes of the live-action ''[[Star Wars]]'' television series ''[[Star Wars: The Mandalorian]]'' and all six episodes of the [[Disney+]] series ''[[Star Wars: Obi-Wan Kenobi]]''.

==Biography==
===''The Mandalorian''===
{{Quote|My dad was Chinese, and he was a huge movie fan, when I grew up he was watching Hong Kong action films. So it kind of gets that reference. I tried to bring out a little ''Hard Boiled'' with the baby. It was kind of an amazing thing because it was like coming back to classic cinema and filmmaking. So there's definitely a lot of my dad in that episode. Sadly he didn't get to see this. But he would be very proud. He would probably also be in shock.|Deborah Chow, on directing ''The Mandalorian''|<ref name="Mando 3 interview">{{WebCite|url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2019/11/deborah-chow-director-episode-three-the-mandalorian|text=''The Mandalorian'': Deborah Chow Reveals the Inspiration For the Baby Yoda Rescue|author=Anthony Breznican|date=2019-11-22|work=[[Wikipedia:Vanity Fair (magazine)|''Vanity Fair'']]|archiveurl=https://archive.is/TeAQu}}</ref>}}
Deborah Chow directed "[[Chapter 3: The Sin]]" of the [[The Mandalorian Season One|first season]] of the [[Disney+]] television series ''[[Star Wars: The Mandalorian]]'',<ref name="The Sin">{{TheMandalorian|Chapter 3: The Sin}}</ref> drawing inspiration from [[Wikipedia:Akira Kurosawa|Akira Kurosawa's]] 1961 Japanese ronin samurai film [[Wikipedia:Yojimbo|''Yojimbo'']] and [[Wikipedia:John Woo|John Woo's]] 1992 Hong Kong cop-and-kid film [[Wikipedia:Hard Boiled|''Hard Boiled'']] for the dynamics between the titular [[Mandalorian]], [[Din Djarin]], and "the Child," [[Din Grogu|Grogu]]. Chow particularly tried to bring out the spirit of Hong Kong action films that her late father loved to watch.<ref name="Mando 3 interview" /> She also directed "[[Chapter 7: The Reckoning]]" of ''The Mandalorian''<ref name="The Reckoning">{{TheMandalorian|Chapter 7: The Reckoning}}</ref> and played the [[pilot]] [[Sash Ketter]] in "[[Chapter 6: The Prisoner]]."<ref name="The Prisoner">{{TheMandalorian|Chapter 6: The Prisoner}}</ref> Chow was later approached to return as Ketter in the [[The Mandalorian Season Two|second season]] episode "[[Chapter 10: The Passenger]]" for a scene where she would have appeared alongside [[Paul Sun-Hyung Lee]]'s [[New Republic]] pilot [[Carson Teva]], but her commitments to ''[[Star Wars: Obi-Wan Kenobi]]'' prevented her from reprising her role due to her busy schedule, so [[Dave Filoni]] filled-in for Chow by returning as [[Trapper Wolf]].<ref name="SWE">{{YouTube|video=pX_vHoz_dMk|text=Paul Sun-Hyung Lee Is My New Best Friend - Comm Chatter #7|channel=@StarWarsExplained|name=Star Wars Explained|quote=Last week I sat down to talk Star Wars, cosplay, The Mandalorian, and general nerdiness with Carson Teva himself, Paul Sun-Hyung Lee!|archivedate=20210106072737}}</ref> Chow eventually reprised her role as Ketter in the [[The Mandalorian Season Three|third season]] episode "[[Chapter 21: The Pirate]]."<ref name="The Pirate">{{TheMandalorian|Chapter 21: The Pirate}}</ref>

===''Obi-Wan Kenobi'' television series===
{{Quote|And for me also taking on [George Lucas'] legacy, it's an honor to take these characters and be able to tell a new story with them. So I wanted to be very respectful to what exists and not change anything that George intended originally or anything like that.|Deborah Chow discussing directing ''Obi-Wan Kenobi''|<ref name="Kenobi retcon">{{WebCite|url=https://ew.com/tv/obi-wan-kenobi-darth-vader-star-wars-line|text=How ''Obi-Wan Kenobi'' changes the meaning behind a classic ''Star Wars'' line|author=Ross, Dalton|date=2022-03-26|work=''[[Wikipedia:Entertainment Weekly|Entertainment Weekly]]''|archivedate=20220426153238}}</ref>}}
When the [[Obi-Wan Kenobi]] [[Star Wars Anthology Series|anthology]] film evolved into a television series, the original director, [[Stephen Daldry]], departed and Deborah Chow joined the project to keep the series cinematic in scope.<ref name="Rebellion televised">{{WebCite|url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2022/05/star-wars-cover-the-rebellion-will-be-televised|text=''Star Wars:'' The Rebellion Will Be Televised|author=Breznican, Anthony|date=2022-05-17|work=[[Wikipedia:Vanity Fair (magazine)|''Vanity Fair'']]|archivedate=20220517110251}}</ref> On [[September 27]], [[2019]], [[StarWars.com]] announced that Deborah Chow would direct the ''Obi-Wan Kenobi'' television miniseries. [[Lucasfilm]] president [[Kathleen Kennedy]] said that she and the crew wanted a director who can "explore both the quiet determination and rich mystique of Obi-Wan in a way that folds seamlessly into the ''[[Star Wars]]'' saga." Kennedy said that she was confident that Chow was the right director because of how she developed characters in ''The Mandalorian''. It was also announced that the series would be written by [[Hossein Amini]]. Chow, Amini, and [[Ewan McGregor]] were going to serve as executive producers alongside Kennedy.<ref name="SW Deborah">{{SW|url=news/deborah-chow-obi-wan-kenobi-series|text=Deborah Chow to Direct Obi-Wan Kenobi Series Exclusively on Disney+}}</ref> Chow felt honored to be able to enter the mythology and to inherit [[the galaxy]] with returning characters.<ref name="Return">''[[Obi-Wan Kenobi: A Jedi's Return]]''</ref>

After Amini was replaced by writer [[Joby Harold]], Chow and Harold crafted a new story that took place [[9 BBY|10 years]] after [[Star Wars: Episode III Revenge of the Sith|''Star Wars'': Episode III ''Revenge of the Sith'']].<ref name="17 year">{{WebCite|url=https://ew.com/tv/obi-wan-kenobi-ewan-mcgregor-hayden-christensen-cover-story|text=Inside the 17-year journey to reunite Ewan McGregor and Hayden Christensen for ''Obi-Wan Kenobi''|author=Ross, Dalton|date=2022-03-10|work=''[[Wikipedia:Entertainment Weekly|Entertainment Weekly]]''|archivedate=20220310151626}}</ref> Chow's starting place was that she wanted to do something that was character-based and character-driven because that was the benefit of having more time to tell a character story in a limited series.<ref name="Return of Vader">{{WebCite|url=https://ew.com/tv/obi-wan-kenobi-director-explains-darth-vader-return|text=''Obi-Wan Kenobi'' director explains the return of Darth Vader|author=Ross, Dalton|date=2022-03-24|work=''[[Wikipedia:Entertainment Weekly|Entertainment Weekly]]''|archivedate=20220324161912}}</ref> She also wanted to show how Kenobi in ''Revenge of the Sith'' changed into the peaceful Kenobi seen in [[Star Wars: Episode IV A New Hope|''Star Wars'': Episode IV ''A New Hope'']]. She found it difficult to balance telling her own story and what director [[George Lucas]] did with the [[original trilogy]]. She was drawn to the father-daughter story because her dad was a pivotal person in her life.<ref name="Return" /> For reference, Chow looked at atmospheric and poetic films like [[Wikipedia:The Proposition (2005 film)|''The Proposition'']] and [[Wikipedia:The Assassination of Jesse James by the Coward Robert Ford|''The Assassination of Jesse James by the Coward Robert Ford'']].<ref name="Rotten Tomatoes TV">{{OfficialYouTube|channel=@RottenTomatoesTV|name=Rotten Tomatoes|link=Wikipedia:Rotten Tomatoes|video=K-BiOMIR6NM|text='Obi-Wan Kenobi' Star Ewan McGregor Explains Why He Sounds So 'Alec Guinness-y' &#124; Rotten Tomatoes TV|archivedate=20220527155120}}</ref> She also felt like she absorbed a lot of the speed and imagination of [[Wikipedia:Asia|Asian]] [[Wikipedia:Martial arts|martial arts]] movies. Chow looked at the fighting in the prequels and started to make it bigger and add some modern elements to it.<ref name="Return" /> Chow wanted a mixture of characters who are important in Kenobi's life and completely new characters.<ref name="Cameo teases">{{WebCite|url=https://www.gamesradar.com/obi-wan-kenobi-cameos-director-star-wars-interview|text=''Obi-Wan Kenobi'' director explains the return of Darth Vader|author=Shepherd, Jack|date=2022-04-26|work=[[Wikipedia:GamesRadar+|GamesRadar]]|archivedate=20220426160401}}</ref> The approach was always to create one full story that had a beginning, middle, and end. Kenobi was meant to be a lost and broken man who felt responsible for what happened to [[Anakin Skywalker]] in ''Revenge of the Sith''. The series was designed to focus on Kenobi's journey from pain to a modicum of peace.<ref name="17 year" /> Chow wanted the series to focus on who Ben Kenobi is instead of his Jedi identity.<ref name="Return" /> Chow said the crew inherited some of the original plot, but they made significant changes and added a few different elements.<ref name="17 year" />

Chow was very mindful of filling in gaps in the ''Star Wars'' timeline. She felt honored to take George Lucas's characters and be able to tell a new story with them. Because of this, Chow wanted to be very respectful to existing material and to not change anything that Lucas originally intended. Despite this, Chow felt that the show had room for interpretation. Ewan McGregor, the actor who played Kenobi in the series, enjoyed working with Chow and said she's an amazing director. He added that she had such quiet authority and understood the world and always knew what she wanted. During the pre-production process, Chow managed to merge the new world of the show with the worlds established in the previous trilogies. She took things that were made on the set in [[1975]] and linked it to what actors said in [[2003]] and made it all work.<ref name="Kenobi retcon" />

Chow wanted [[Darth]] Vader to return because he was important to Kenobi in his life.<ref name="Return of Vader" /> She believed that there is a "love-story dynamic" between Kenobi and Vader, so she felt like it was difficult to not include the person who left Kenobi in a state of anguish. Chow was intrigued by the idea that Kenobi might still care deeply about Vader despite what he had become.<ref name="Rebellion televised" /> Eventually, it was decided that Vader would appear and he would be played by [[Hayden Christensen]], the actor who played Skywalker in the [[prequel trilogy]]. Writer Joby Harold thought it was important to have Christensen be a part of the project because he was a massive part of the character. Harold said that the whole crew made it a priority to handle Vader with as much care as possible "to honor one of the greatest villains and antagonists in the history of entertainment."<ref name="17 year" /> In the fall of 2019, Chow went to Christensen's farm to make her pitch in person. Chow talked about the project and her vision for it.<ref name="17 year" /> She promised that Vader would add a new dimension that could reframe the way fans look at Vader and Kenobi's [[lightsaber duel]] in ''A New Hope''.<ref name="Rebellion televised" /> Christensen thought it sounded wonderful and was excited to return to ''Star Wars''.<ref name="17 year" />

When Christensen first came on the set, Chow thought it was intense to have an iconic character and that she was able to direct and film new scenes with him.<ref name="17 year" /> Chow also found it interesting because she started to see the different generations that connect to ''Star Wars''. Chow enjoyed working with Christensen and thought he was wonderful.<ref name="Return of Vader" /> Many of Christensen's conversations with Chow were about him wanting to "convey this feeling of strength, but also coupled with imprisonment," which he thought was an interesting space to explore. When Chow became the showrunner, she championed a rematch between Kenobi and Vader as Lucasfilm contemplated going that route. In [[March]] [[2020]], Vader's return was finalized.<ref name="Rebellion televised" /> Chow and the crew made sure that Vader was not overused in order to keep the character's mystique.<ref name="Rotten Tomatoes TV" />

Chow and Harold described [[Inquisitor]] [[Reva Sevander|Reva]] as ruthless and ambitious. She found it exciting to bring a female villain into the show and to have a [[Dark side of the Force|dark side]] woman in a significant role.<ref name="17 year" /> Chow wanted Reva to be played by a young woman that the audience would believe could take on Darth Vader. Chow liked [[Moses Ingram]]'s strength, intelligence, and charisma, so she was excited when she and the crew casted her in the part of Reva.<ref name="Return" /> After Ingram spent a week auditioning, Chow told her that she got the part in a [[Wikipedia:Zoom Video Communications|Zoom]] call.<ref name="7 Questions">{{OfficialYouTube|channel=@VanityFair|name=Vanity Fair|video=kWpCRYBq6kM|text=Ewan McGregor, Hayden Christensen & Moses Ingram Answer 7 Star Wars Questions &#124; Vanity Fair|archivedate=20220518174951}}</ref> Chow liked that Reva was not from the animated series and she thought Ingram did a fantastic job playing the character.<ref name="Reva Hair">{{WebCite|url=https://ew.com/tv/star-wars-obi-wan-kenobi-moses-ingram-reva-hair|text=How Moses Ingram made a ''Star Wars'' Halloween character for Black girls on ''Obi-Wan Kenobi''|author=Ross, Dalton|date=2022-04-06|work=''[[Wikipedia:Entertainment Weekly|Entertainment Weekly]]''|archivedate=20220406163044}}</ref> Chow and Ingram had many conversations about what the right hair might be for Reva. Ingram hoped that her look for Reva's hair would allow Black children with [[Wikipedia:Afro-textured hair|kinky hair]] to wear their natural hair in [[Wikipedia:Halloween costume|Halloween costumes]]. Ingram said that Chow was very great with moving from the initial vision to what they ended up with.<ref name="17 year" /> Chow said that Darth [[Maul]] was never in any part of the series because Dave Filoni concluded Maul's story<ref name="Maul not in series">{{WebCite|url=https://www.ign.com/articles/darth-maul-obi-wan-was-never-going-to-be|text=Darth Maul Was Never Going to be in Obi-Wan Kenobi|author=Muir-Taylor, Casey|date=2022-04-25|work=[[Wikipedia:IGN|''IGN'']]|archivedate=20220425212706}}</ref> in the ''[[Star Wars Rebels]]'' episode "[[Twin Suns (episode)|Twin Suns]]."<ref name="Twin Suns">{{Rebels|Twin Suns (episode)}}</ref> She also worried that having Maul and Vader in the series would have been "a little bit much."<ref name="Maul not in series" />

When costume director [[Suttirat Anne Larlarb]] first met Chow, the director said she liked how Larlarb talked about culture and history as it relates to costume. She added that Larlarb's approach to the show [[Wikipedia:American Gods (TV series)|''American Gods'']] would be very similar to Kenobi uncovering all of the cultural stones necessary to create the [[Planet|worlds]] seen in the series. Larlarb was then hired as the series' costume designer.<ref name="Suttirat interview">{{WebCite|url=https://www.costumedesignersguild.com/obi-wan-kenobi-suttirat-larlarb|text=Interview with Suttirat Larlarb|author=Foss, Gary|date=2022-06-13|archivedate=20220803191837}}</ref> One day, the actor [[Kumail Nanjiani]] received a call from his agents and after waiting an hour, the callers told Nanjiani that he was wanted in the''Obi-Wan Kenobi'' television series. The callers told Nanjiani they could not tell him about his character or anything else, and they asked him to speak with Chow. In a [[Wikipedia:Zoom Video Communications|Zoom]] meeting, Chow pitched the idea to Nanjiani to convinvce him to join the project. Nanjiani quickly agreed to joining and he said it was a very easy decison.<ref name="Kumail Nanjiani interview">{{WebCite|url=https://ew.com/tv/obi-wan-kenobi-kumail-nanjiani-haja-star-wars|text=Kumail Nanjiani shares first details of ''Obi-Wan Kenobi'' con-man character Haja|author=Ross, Dalton|date=2022-05-20|work=''[[Wikipedia:Entertainment Weekly|Entertainment Weekly]]''|archivedate=20220311122528}}</ref> Chow also spoke with actress [[Indira Varma]] about joining the show. Varma felt like she could trust Chow's sensibilities, both to value emotion over action and to uplift female voices. When they spoke, Varma liked how Chow wanted the show to be character driven and Chow asked for her input.<ref name="Indira Varma Interview">{{WebCite|url=https://www.elle.com/culture/movies-tv/a40151808/who-is-tala-obi-wan-kenobi-indira-varma-star-wars|text=Indira Varma On Her Obi-Wan Kenobi Character Reveal: "Tala Has A Lovely Arc"|author=Pucket-Pope, Lauren|date=2022-06-01|work=[[Wikipedia:Elle (magazine)|''Elle'']]|archivedate=20220606034947}}</ref> Chow also offered the role of a young [[Leia Skywalker Organa Solo|Leia Organa]] to the actress [[Vivien Lyra Blair]].<ref name="Vivien Facebook">{{Facebook|username=vivienlyrablairofficial|url=photo/?fbid=461414942460436&set=a.181989547069645|name=Vivien Lyra Blair|date=2022-05-29|quote=Like most little girls I was drawn to Leia. 💫 When I went to #disneyland and flew the Millennium Falcon for the first time around 6 years old I was hooked on the whole galaxy! ✨ So when Deborah offered me the role I was speechless. She had to ask me for sure if I was saying yes! Then I got to spend the summer playing with droids, meeting an amazing crew, acting with a cast that made me feel special and respected and learning so much. And now I've met the fans who've made this journey even more incredible! 👀 The memories of this #obiwankenobi adventure will always be a part of me. Thank you to everyone that helped make this happen and thank you for believing in me😻|archiveurl=https://archive.ph/2qER9}}</ref> She loved that the series would acknowledge that Organa played a pivotal role in ''Star Wars''.<ref name="Return" />

Chow said that the strongest connective tissue for the crew is to the prequels because it is where the series' characters come from and it is where their stories started.<ref name="17 year" /> [[Bonnie Maree Piesse|Bonnie Piesse]], the actress who played [[Beru Whitesun Lars|Beru Lars]] in the prequels, received a message from Chow to chat, but she at first did not recognize it as having to do with work. She subsequently ignored the message for almost a week until realizing the message was for casting. Piesse then joined a call from [[Wikipedia:Portugal|Portugal]] and Chow convinced her to return to the role of Beru Lars.<ref name="Bonnie Piesse interview">{{WebCite|url=https://www.elle.com/culture/movies-tv/a40084975/bonnie-piesse-nxivm-star-wars-obi-wan-kenobi|text=Bonnie Piesse Escaped The Cult NXIVM. Now, She's Back In The Star Wars Universe.|author=Pope-Puckett, Lauren|date=2022-05-24|work=[[Wikipedia:Elle (magazine)|''Elle'']]|archivedate=20220524141703}}</ref> Chow was very excited to bring [[Joel Edgerton]] and Piesse back to play [[Owen Lars|Owen]] and Beru Lars, respectively, and she said their return was part of what made the series special.<ref name="17 year" /> She was also thankful that George Lucas casted Edgerton as Owen.<ref name="First look">{{WebCite|url=https://ew.com/tv/obi-wan-kenobi-first-look-photos|text=See exclusive first-look photos from ''Obi-Wan Kenobi''|author=Ross, Dalton|date=2022-03-09|work=''[[Wikipedia:Entertainment Weekly|Entertainment Weekly]]''|archivedate=20220311122528}}</ref> In early [[June]], actor [[Grant Feely]] had a director's callback with Chow. He later flew to [[Wikipedia:Los Angeles|Los Angeles]] to do an in-person audition with Chow. In early [[July]], Chow called Feely to offer him the role of young [[Luke Skywalker]] in the series.<ref name="Rival">{{WebCite|url=https://www.rivalmagazinela.com/rival-online/the-force-is-with-grant-feely|text=The Force is With Grant Feely|author=Mariana González|work=[https://www.rivalmagazinela.com Rival Magazine LA]|archivedate=20220805204647}}</ref> Chow spoke with [[Liam Neeson]], the actor who played [[Qui-Gon Jinn]] in the prequels, and told him that McGregor would reprise his role as Kenobi in a series. She explained what Jinn would do in the series, and Leeson then said he was definitely onboard.<ref name="Return" /> Chow chose actress [[Ming Qiu]] to play<ref name="Ming Instagram">{{Instagram|username=sunmoonl|url=p/CfP4JErp1he|name=[[Ming Qiu]]|date=2022-06-25|quote=I'm grateful to have chance to play Jedi master Minas Veiti on Obi Wan. Thank you Deborah for choose me, our awesome cast and crew, our stunt coordinator Jonathan Eusebio, my jedi younglings!|archivedate=20221203035845}}</ref> [[Jedi Master]] [[Minas Velti]] in "[[Part I]]."<ref name="Part I">{{Kenobi|Part I}}</ref> Chow explained that a large chunk of the show took place on [[Tatooine]] because of the nature of where the starting place was for the story, but she promised the show would visit new worlds.<ref name="17 year" />

When Joby Harold was writing fight scenes, such as Kenobi and Vader's [[Battle on Mapuzo|duel on Mapuzo]], he tried to find a way for character to come through action. He wrote the choreography from a character point of view, which got handed off to stunt coordinator [[Jonathan (Jojo) Eusebio|Jojo Eusebio]] who developed the fight sequence with Chow.<ref name="Leia Secret">{{WebCite|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-features/obi-wan-kenobi-leia-disney-1235160356|text='Obi-Wan Kenobi' Writer Joby Harold Relieved Leia Was Kept Secret|author=Davids, Brian|date=2022-06-07|work=[[Wikipedia:The Hollywood Reporter|''The Hollywood Reporter'']]|archivedate=20220607180506}}</ref> Chow wanted to make Vader's arrival in the [[Mapuzo mining village]] to be similar to a [[Wikipedia:Horror film|horror movie]] where the monster would appear and then disappear.<ref name="Return" /> Lucasfilm art director [[Doug Chiang]], Chow, and other crew members met virtually to discuss what purpose [[KP-1]], the [[astromech droid]] desgined by [[Camille Manet]], could serve in the show. Chow suggested that the droid could be put in the "pseudo rebel base" and have some functionality. She told the builders to think of the droid's personality as "a scrappy little fighter."<ref name="BMD Contest">{{SWYouTube|QrSKKyWhZvU|Build My Droid Contest &#124; Obi-Wan Kenobi &#124; Disney+}}</ref> Chow and the crew looked at references of old whaling stations for the [[Tibidon Station|Sandwhale Farm]], and it was her favorite set.<ref name="Return" /> Propmaster [[Brad Elliott]] and his team were led by Chow.<ref name="Elegant weapon">{{SW|url=news/inside-the-lucasfilm-archive-obi-wan-kenobi-props-1|text=Inside the Lucasfilm Archive: An Elegant Weapon and Other Jedi Artifacts from the ''Obi-Wan Kenobi'' Limited Series}}</ref> She worried that the [[wupiupi]] props were too large for swift hand-to-hand transactions, so the coins were scaled down slightly with the same art as the first design in [[Star Wars: Episode I The Phantom Menace|''Star Wars'': Episode I ''The Phantom Menace'']].<ref name="Elegant weapon">{{SW|url=news/inside-the-lucasfilm-archive-obi-wan-kenobi-props-1|text=Inside the Lucasfilm Archive: An Elegant Weapon and Other Jedi Artifacts from the ''Obi-Wan Kenobi'' Limited Series}}</ref> When director Chow visited the creature shop, she suggested that the [[Unidentified Boosodian vendor|Boosodian's]] head could belong to someone who would be amazing at stopping shoplifters. A crew member suggested that the character could own a [[Chiu Mo Threads|store]] and would be able to watch everyone, which Chow agreed with.<ref name="Return" />

[[Daiyu]] was one of Chow's favorite planets. Some of the earliest references were places like the [[Wikipedia:Bangkok|Bangkok]] night market and the city [[Wikipedia:Hong Kong|Hong Kong]] at night; Chow took inspiration from the Hong Kong films of director [[Wikipedia:Wong Kar-wai|Wong Kar-Wai]] and similar works.<ref name="Return" /> Chow told Feely to not watch any ''Star Wars'' movies so that he could act like a regular boy instead of [[Mark Hamill]]'s portrayal of the character in the original trilogy.<ref name="Rival" /> Chow wanted [[Darth Sidious|Palpatine]] to tell Vader that his obsession with Kenobi is a weakness. She told [[Ian McDiarmid]] to film his scene as Palpatine between being on an edge and having some lines be more casual. On the 125th day of shooting, filming ended and Chow thanked the cast and the crew for contributing to the series.<ref name="Return" /> After the show was finished, Chow wanted to take a vacation from directing.<ref name="Rotten Tomatoes TV" /> She was interviewed for ''[[Obi-Wan Kenobi: A Jedi's Return]]'', a documentary about the making of ''Obi-Wan Kenobi''. She was also an executive producer for the documentary.<ref name="Return" />
