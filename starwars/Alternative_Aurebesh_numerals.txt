An alternate form of '''Aurebesh numerals''' was used in [[the galaxy]]. Completely different from regular [[Aurebesh]], the numbers were used on [[At Achrann]], [[At Attin]], [[Coruscant]], [[Daiyu]], [[Kessel]], [[Lothal]], [[Nevarro]], [[Nur]], [[Plazir-15]], [[Tatooine]] and [[Duro]]; on [[credit chip]]s, [[credit ingot]]s and in [[Data visor|Tech's visor]]; by the [[Galactic Empire]], their [[Imperial Senate|senate]] and their [[Imperial Security Bureau|security bureau]], and the New Republic; on the console of the ''[[Razor Crest]]'', on a monitor in the ''[[Providence]]'' and [[T-6 1974|Ahsoka Tano's shuttle]], on the ''[[Eye of Sion]]''<nowiki>'</nowiki>s special [[galactic coordinates]] calculation monitors, on the [[cockpit]] monitor of the ''[[Onyx Cinder]]'', in the [[rank insignia of the First Order]], and in the [[Yacht|Star Yacht]] ''[[Libertine]]''<nowiki>'</nowiki>s databank.

==Description==
The numerals were completely different from regular [[Aurebesh]] numbers, using an alternate numerical system based on dots and lines.<ref name="TLJ" />

==History==
===Republic Era===
Around [[233 BBY]],<ref name="Date">{{YJADate|107B}}</ref> these digits were inscribed on a set of [[Galactic Republic|Republic]] [[credit chip]]s that [[Ansen Strung]], a [[bounty hunter]], used to purchase [[sap beverage]]s for himself and a band of [[Jedi Initiate]]s who had assisted him in locating a [[Figgles|missing kibbin]]. The credits bore the numbers "8 4 2."<ref name="Kibbin">{{YJA|The Missing Kibbin}}</ref>

The alternative numerals "203," written in reverse like a mirror reflection, showed up on a large screen in a [[Unidentified police station|police station]] on [[Duro]].<ref name="A Good Turn" /> A [[computer]] that [[Deputy]] [[Vance]] used to search for information on [[Cad Bane]] displayed a variety of alternative numbers: "30" appeared in the top right corner as a mirror image, and "2" was shown in both the lower left and upper right corners. The "2" in the lower left was also reversed, while the one in the upper right was in its standard form.<ref name="One Good Deed">{{TOTU|One Good Deed}} {{C|Note that what initially seems like extra digits appears in the upper and lower left hand corners, as well as the upper right hand corner. However, upon closer examination, these symbols do not correspond to any known alternative numerals.}}</ref>

[[File:Alternate_numerals_Marauder.png|thumb|left|300px|Alternative numerals on a monitor in the ''Marauder'']]
At the [[time]] of the [[Clone Wars]], alternative numerals were engraved on [[credit ingot]]s used by a [[Mooga smuggling ring|band]] of [[Moogan]] [[smuggler]]s to bribe a [[Unidentified Mandalorian customs official|corrupt customs officer]] on [[Mandalore]]. The [[Color|silvery]] ingots read "8 88," while the golden ones said "{{Cr}}84."<ref name="Corruption">{{TCW|Corruption (episode)}}</ref> 

During the Clone Wars, alternative digits appeared on the credit chips used by [[Jedi Master]] [[Yoda]] to pay [[Anthen]] for information on a mysterious [[Confederacy of Independent Systems|Separatist]] weapon project called [[Operation: Titan]].<ref name="Yoda 7">[[Yoda 7|''Yoda'' 7]]</ref> 

A [[map]] of destinations on the [[subtram]] in the [[Coruscant Underworld]] during the war also used these numerals.<ref name="Trivia">{{SW|url=series/clone-wars/to-catch-a-jedi-trivia-gallery|text="To Catch a Jedi" Trivia Gallery}} {{C|Slide 4}}</ref>

===Imperial Era===
After the Clone Wars ended, the numbers were used on monitors in the [[repulsorpod]]s in the [[Imperial Senate]], although regular [[Aurebesh]] numbers were also in use.<ref name="Truth" />

Also during this time period, [[Clone Force 99]] member [[Tech]] wore a [[Data visor|special visor]] that could be used to scan his surroundings. When used in [[Cid's mine]], the scan resulted in "[[Ipsium|IPSIUM]] DETECTED," followed by a list of alternate numerals.<ref name="Crossing" />

A set of credit chips in a case that [[Omega]] handed to [[Lanse Crowder]] as payment for a [[Unidentified artifact (Phee Genoa)|tree-shaped artifact]], had "845" engraved on them in these numerals. During the exchange, [[Hunter]] was playing a game of [[sabacc]] with a [[Bith]], with the credit chips lying on the table saying "9 99," using the same numbers.<ref name="Pabu">{{TBB|Pabu (episode)}} {{C|Note that most of the [[credit chip]]s that [[Omega]] hands to [[Lanse Crowder]] are lying upside-down in the case, while the rest are sideways}}</ref>

[[File:Kessel_gate.png|thumb|right|200px|Door in the spice mines of Kessel, reading "D7 X371K"]]
The control panel in the cockpit of the ''[[Marauder (Omicron-class)|Marauder]]'' displayed these numbers as Tech was teaching Omega how to fly. When Tech had to alert the rest of the team with some serious news, two lengthy chains of digits emerged on an alternate screen.<ref name="Tipping">{{TBB|Tipping Point}}</ref> The same data appeared on the screen while Tech briefed the rest of Clone Force 99 about his search through the [[Galactic Republic|Republic]] and [[Galactic Empire|Imperial]] files for information about the [[Advanced Science Division]].<ref name="Summit">{{TBB|The Summit}}</ref>

As [[CX-2]] tracked [[Phee Genoa]]'s [[starship]], ''[[Providence]]'', a [[Color|blue]] monitor aboard Genoa's ship displayed a long list of Aurebesh numbers.<ref name="Point">{{TBB|Point of No Return (The Bad Batch)}}</ref> 

Several [[Standard year|years]] later, a sign over a door in the [[spice mines of Kessel]] used these numerals, reading "D7 X371K."<ref name="Solo" /> They also appeared on monitors in [[Daiyu spaceport]],<ref name="Kenobi2" /> and on blueprints of [[Fortress Inquisitorius]] available on the underwater stronghold's [[computer]]s.<ref name="Kenobi4" />

Prior to the outbreak of the [[Galactic Civil War]], these numerals were used in ''[[7189]]'', a wall mural in the [[Capital City]] of [[Lothal]].<ref name="Empire Day" />

During a [[Imperial Senate|Senate]] session in the aftermath of the [[Ghorman Massacre]]<ref name="Welcome to the Rebellion" /> in [[2 BBY]],<ref name="Date Welcome">{{Andor|Messenger}} dates the third arc of [[Andor Season 2|''Andor'' Season 2]] to [[2 BBY]]</ref> a large circular monitor encircling the [[Imperial Security Bureau control center]] used alternative numbers, with one readout saying "1 2 3 4 5 6 7" and another one reading "0 2 5 5 0 7 5 1 0 0."<ref name="Welcome to the Rebellion" />  

During the Galactic Civil War, the numbers "042 1792-46" appeared on the display of a pair of [[binoculars]] used by [[Kes Dameron]] to spy on a [[Scarabi]] safe house during the rescue of [[Alliance to Restore the Republic|Rebel Alliance]] leader [[Mon Mothma]].<ref name="Star Wars 2020 46">[[Star Wars (2020) 46|''Star Wars'' (2020) 46]]</ref>

===New Republic Era===
====Fall of the Empire====
{{Quote|There are old carvings. Coordinates for other planets. Perhaps one of them is your home?|[[Hayna]] describing the Fallen Sanctum to the Skeleton Crew|<ref name="No At Attin" />}}
After the fall of the [[Galactic Empire]], the console aboard [[Din Djarin|Din Djarin's]] [[ST-70 assault ship|ship]], the ''[[Razor Crest]]'', used the same kind of numbers.<ref name="Man2">{{TheMandalorian|Chapter 2: The Child}}</ref> They also appeared on a red monitor on the console of [[Din Djarin's N-1 starfighter|his N-1 starfighter]], which said "ALT 51" and "PSI 11";<ref name="BF5">{{BOBF|Chapter 5: Return of the Mandalorian}}</ref> and on a small blue monitor on the opposite side of the console, which listed every number from 1 to 9. Curiously, the latter was upside-down.<ref name="Man17">{{TheMandalorian|Chapter 17: The Apostate}}</ref>

After [[Boba Fett]] [[Attack on Jabba's Palace|claimed]] [[Boba Fett's Palace|Jabba's throne]], the [[Aqualish]] delegate [[Garfalaquox]] presented Fett with a box of credit chips, two of which were emblazoned with the credit symbol ({{cr}}) and an upside-down version of the alternative numeral 3.<ref name="BF1" />

Shortly afterward, the monitor on the control panel of a [[Six-O-Two Mitigator]] used by the [[New Republic Amnesty Program]], listed every alternative numeral from 1 to 9, which represented different voltage levels.<ref name="Manda19">{{TheMandalorian|Chapter 19: The Convert}}</ref>

The plaque on the [[statue of IG-11]] in [[Nevarro City]] on [[Nevarro]] used these digits, while a [[Nevarro City cafe|local cafe's]] prices were written in these numerals.<ref name="Pirate" />

[[File:Plazir-15_camera.png|thumb|left|300px|Security camera footage from Plazir-15, using alternative Aurebesh numerals]]
The [[security camera]] monitoring room in the [[peacekeeping zone]] on [[Plazir-15]] used alternative numerals, with "CAMERA 7709-739" appearing on on three different monitors, while another monitor said "[[Sector 7G|SECTOR 7G]]." The also appeared on the business sign of [[Premium Droid Rentals]], which said "D7X"; on [[spark pad]]s, which read "2311 PLAZIR 86"; and on the console used by the [[Unidentified lab tech (Plazir-15)|lab tech]].<ref name="Guns" />

When [[Sabine Wren]] tried to crack into a [[HK-87 assassin droid|HK-87 assassin droid's]] [[memory bank]]s, the inscription on both sides of the [[datapad]] she used said "ABC 1112 34," using these numerals.<ref name="Toil and Trouble">{{Ahsoka|Part Two: Toil and Trouble}}</ref>

When [[Ahsoka Tano]] sent Wren to man [[T-6 1974|their shuttle's]] tail gun during an [[Dogfight in the Denab system|attack]], these digits appeared on a monitor that Wren turned on in the [[gunner]] seat, reading "1 2 3 4 5 6."<ref name="Time to Fly">{{Ahsoka|Part Three: Time to Fly}}</ref>

The ''[[Eye of Sion]]''<nowiki>'s</nowiki> monitors used a special system to determine the [[hyperspace]] [[Galactic coordinates|coordinates]] for its journey to [[Peridea]]; these were calculated using this numerical system.<ref name="Part Four">{{Ahsoka|Part Four: Fallen Jedi}}</ref> As the ship approached Peridea, these numbers appeared alongside regular Aurebesh letters on the monitor used by the [[Star Navigator droid|Star Navigator Droid]].<ref name="Part Six">{{Ahsoka|Part Six: Far, Far Away}}</ref>

At [[Hera Syndulla|Hera Syndulla's]] hearing in the [[New Republic]]'s [[Defense Council building]] on [[Coruscant]], a large panel was mounted on the wall. The headline on the left said "NEW REPUBLIC," and below that, there were red and blue cells labeled with alternative Aurebesh numerals. The headline on the right said "HALL OF JUSTICE DOCKET." The scrolling Aurebesh below the headline appeared to be a list of case files: Most of the names appeared to be nonsense, but a few lines on the list had the word "CASE" beside a random mixture of letters and alternative numerals.<ref name="Part Seven" />

====Rise of the First Order====
Shortly before the outbreak of the [[First Order-Resistance War]], the logo of [[8Key]] contained the number 8 written in this script.<ref name="Design Origin">{{SW|url=news/designing-star-wars-resistance|text=Designing ''Star Wars'': ''Star Wars Resistance''|author=[[Kristin Baver]]|date=2019-03-11}} {{C|Slide 6}}</ref> The business was advertised on the chassis of [[R4-G77]],<ref name="Unmotivated">{{Resistance|Unmotivated}}</ref> an [[astromech droid]] owned by [[Hype Fazon]], a [[racer]] [[pilot]] on the ''[[Colossus]]'' [[Fuel|refueling]] [[Space station|station]].<ref name="Recruit">{{Resistance|The Recruit}}</ref>

During the war, [[rank insignia of the First Order]] utilized this system, with the numbers appearing in the insignias for [[sergeant]] ("[[Ro]] 8") and [[squad leader]] ("[[Hal (Imperial officer)|Hal]] 4").<ref name="VD" /> The "Hal 4" insignia was worn by [[Lank Paze]], while the "Ro 8" insignia could be seen on [[Armitage Hux's First Order monitor]]. When [[Resistance pilot]] [[Poe Dameron]] was communicating with [[General]] [[Armitage Hux|Hux]] over the [[intercom]] during the [[Battle of D'Qar|evacuation of D'Qar]], a computer monitor aboard the ''[[Finalizer]]'', which showed an Aurebesh transcription of Dameron's words, was filled with digits presented in this alternative format. The databank of the ''[[Libertine]]'', which [[DJ]] gained access to after he stole the [[Yacht|Star Yacht]] in [[Canto Bight]], used these numerals to list prices in the [[Korfé Bennux-Ai|owner's]] catalog of [[starfighter]]s for sale.<ref name="TLJ" />

[[R-3X|Rex]], an [[RX-series pilot droid]] who provided [[music]]al entertainment in [[Oga's Cantina]] on [[Batuu]], had an inscription, "[[R-3X|RX24]]," on his chest, using alternative numerals.<ref name="D23">{{D23|url=creatures-droids-and-aliens-meet-the-fantastical-figures-of-star-wars-galaxys-edge|text=Creatures, Droids, and Aliens: Meet the Fantastical Figures of ''Star Wars'': Galaxy's Edge}}</ref>

==Behind the scenes==
{{Quote|I can't recall which came first, Stock Ships or Gamemaster Screen Revised, but I worked on both and both have that chart.|Pablo Hidalgo on [[Wikipedia:Twitter|Twitter]]|<ref name="Hidalgo New Aurabesh">{{Twitter|pabl0hidalgo|status/1535405496089186307|[[Pablo Hidalgo]]|date=2022-06-10|quote=I can't recall which came first, Stock Ships or Gamemaster Screen Revised, but I worked on both and both have that chart.|archiveurl=https://archive.ph/J6QST}}</ref>}}
[[File:New_Aurabesh_numbers.png|thumb|right|300px|A representation of the alternative numerals]][[Aurebesh]] was originally created by [[Stephen Crane]] of [[West End Games]], for the [[1993]] book ''[[Star Wars Miniatures Battles Companion]]''. Crane based the alphabet on an unidentified script seen on the on the monitor readouts on the [[DS-2 Death Star II Mobile Battle Station|second Death Star]] at the beginning of the [[1983]] movie {{Film|6}}. However, Crane never created numbers for the Aurebesh alphabet, because the Death Star monitors in film show regular Arabic numerals.<ref name="Aurebesh Soup">{{WebCite|url=http://www.echostation.com/features/aurebesh.htm|text=Aurebesh Soup: ''Recipe for a Star Wars Font Phenomenon''|author=[[Stephen Crane]]|date=2000-10-21|work=Echo Station|quote=The evolution of the Star Wars alphabet told by the guy who designed the font for West End Games.|archivedate=20160520153232}}</ref>

However, an unrelated numerical system made up of dots and lines first appeared in the West End Games sourcebook ''[[Platt's Starport Guide]]'', which features a graphic called "Current [[Transponder code/Legends|transponder code]] profile," with the numerals along both axes of a graph. Unlike later appearances, numerals for 0 through 16 are represented.<ref name="Platt">''[[Platt's Starport Guide]]''</ref> The graph was reused for the West End Games publication ''[[Star Wars Gamemaster Screen, Revised]]'',<ref name="Screen Revised">''[[Star Wars Gamemaster Screen, Revised]]''</ref> and the West End Games sourcebook ''[[Stock Ships]]''.<ref name="Stock Ships">''[[Stock Ships]]''</ref><ref name="Hidalgo New Aurabesh" />

These numerals were digitized by ''Star Wars'' fan Peter Schuster in [[1998]], as part of an alternate Aurebesh font that Schuster named New Aurabesh,<ref name="New Aurabesh">{{WebCite|url=https://ko-fi.com/post/New-Aurabesh-A-Casual-Interview-with-Peter-Schu-Q5Q41QAX6|text="New Aurabesh": A Casual Interview with Peter Schuster|date=2020-05-19|work=Ko-fi|archivedate=20220509195609}}</ref> which can be downloaded online.<ref name="New Aurabesh font">{{WebCite|url=https://aurekfonts.github.io/?font=NewAurabesh|text=New Aurabesh (free)|work=AurekFonts|archiveurl=https://archive.ph/z1KU2|archivedate=May 9, 2022}}</ref> Although most [[canon]] appearances of Aurebesh use Arabic numbers, the alternative font has also appeared in various ''Star Wars'' media, although it has never been named.

[[File:Subtram_map.jpg|thumb|left|300px|A subtram map created for "To Catch a Jedi," using alternate Aurebesh numerals]]
In ''Star Wars'' canon, these numerals first appeared on the [[credit ingot]]s used to bribe a [[Unidentified Mandalorian customs official|corrupt customs officer]] in the ''[[Star Wars: The Clone Wars]]'' episode "[[Corruption (episode)|Corruption]]."<ref name="Corruption/> They also appear on a [[map]] of destinations on the [[subtram]] in the [[Coruscant Underworld]], created for the ''[[Star Wars: The Clone Wars]]'' episode "[[To Catch a Jedi]]."<ref name="Trivia" /> However, although the map does appear on-screen in the episode, it is too far away to decipher.<ref name="Catch">{{TCW|To Catch a Jedi}}</ref>

The only ''Star Wars'' canon media to map any of the alternate numbers are the [[2014]] Episode Guide for ''[[Star Wars Rebels: Spark of Rebellion]]'' on [[StarWars.com]], whose Concept Art Gallery includes a translation for a [[7189|sign]] on [[Lothal]], containing the numbers 7, 1, 8 and 9;<ref name="SW">{{SW|url=series/spark-of-rebellion-concept-art-gallery|text="Spark of Rebellion" Concept Art Gallery}} {{C|Slide 12}}</ref> and the [[2017]] reference book ''[[Star Wars: The Visual Encyclopedia]]'', which presents an Aurebesh font which includes two examples of Aurebesh numbers, 3 and 7.<ref name="Visual Encyclopedia">''[[Star Wars: The Visual Encyclopedia]]''</ref> Both these sources use West End Games' alternate font.

Concept art for the ''[[Star Wars Resistance]]'' episode "[[Descent (episode)|Descent]]" on [[StarWars.com]] reveals an unused design for two [[First Order officer]]s wearing a [[rank insignia of the First Order|rank insignia]] on their sleeve, which says "[[Hal (Imperial officer)|HAL]] 4."<ref name="Descent Concept">{{SW|url=star-wars-resistance-descent-concept-art-gallery|text="Descent" Concept Art Gallery}} {{C|Slide 3, 5}}</ref> However, the insignia do not appear in the finished episode.<ref name="Descent">{{Resistance|Descent (episode)}}</ref>

Costume concept art by [[Glyn Dillon]] in the behind-the-scenes book ''[[The Art of Star Wars: The Rise of Skywalker]]'' shows [[Finn]] wearing a [[Color|yellow]] [[jacket]] with Aurebesh on the back, reading "FN 2187," "FINN"/"[[Rose Tico|TICO]]." The inscription is written in an [[Unidentified Aurebesh script (battle helmet)|alternative Aurebesh font]] and alternative numerals.<ref name="Rise Art">''[[The Art of Star Wars: The Rise of Skywalker]]''</ref>
