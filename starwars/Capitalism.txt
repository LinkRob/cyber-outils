'''Capitalism''' was an economic model used within [[the galaxy]] based around private ownership and profit. Institutions and individuals that supported capitalism were known as '''capitalists'''.

==Description==
{{Quote|Profit is powerful. Financial reward&mdash;and the illusion of competition&mdash;can motivate in ways force cannot.|[[Denetrius Vidian]]|<ref name="Bottleneck">{{StoryCite|story=Bottleneck (short story)|book=The Rise of the Empire}}</ref>}}
Capitalism, its supporters known as capitalists, was an economic model used in [[the galaxy]]. It dealt with the generation of profit under privately-operated corporations. Capitalist institutions such as the [[Trade Federation]]&mdash;which stood as an example of corruption and greed-based capitalism&mdash;were able to forge monopolies in which they cut out competition, an act regarded as criminal under the capitalist model but which was, nonetheless, still possible if not opposed. The Federation, [[Techno Union]], [[InterGalactic Banking Clan]], [[Commerce Guild]], and [[Corporate Alliance]] were considered examples of unbridled capitalism.<ref name="Prelude to the Clone Wars">{{EncyclopediaCite|Prologue to the Clone Wars and the First Battle of Geonosis}}</ref>

==History==
===Rise of the corporations===
In [[the galaxy]], powerful [[megacorporation]]s such as the Trade Federation and Techno Union arose<ref name="Seize the Galaxy">{{FantasyFlightGames|url=en/news/2019/1/8/seize-the-galaxy|text=Seize the Galaxy}}</ref> with influence over the economy and [[politics]]<ref name="Heroes">{{TCW|Heroes on Both Sides}}</ref> while smaller companies also tried to make their mark.<ref name="Tarkin">[[Tarkin (novel)|''Tarkin'']]</ref> Around [[162 BBY]],<ref name="Phasma date 1">''[[Star Wars: Timelines]]'' dates the events of [[Phasma (novel)|''Phasma'']] to [[34 ABY]]. As ''Phasma'' establishes that the [[Con Star Mining Corporation]] arrived on [[Parnassos]] one hundred and eighty-six years before chapters set ten years before the present day events, the company must have arrived on Paranassos in [[162 BBY]].</ref> the capitalist<ref name="Return to a Shattered Planet" /> [[Con Star Mining Corporation]] arrived on the planet [[Parnassos]]<ref name="Phasma">[[Phasma (novel)|''Phasma'']]</ref> in the [[Unknown Regions]]<ref name="Traveler's Guide">''[[Star Wars: Galaxy's Edge: Traveler's Guide to Batuu]]''</ref> and began a strip-[[mining]] operation, devastating the planet in pursuit of profit. However, a cheaply built<ref name="Return to a Shattered Planet" /> and poorly upkept nuclear reactor ultimately spelt the end for Con Star's presence on the planet; its explosion fully devastated the environment and inspired the company to abandon Parnassos, leaving the population behind.<ref name="Phasma" />

[[File:Safe_In_Our_Hands.png|thumb|left|250px|''[[Safe In Our Hands]]'', a pro-Trade Federation poster]]
Elsewhere in the galaxy, the capitalist Trade Federation, Techno Union, InterGalactic Banking Clan, Commerce Guild, and Corporate Alliance cemented great power for themselves yet still desired greater profits. These companies shaped what wealth and prosperity looked like for the entire galaxy. Four of those companies proved to be vital to the survival of entire [[star system]]s away from the [[Core Worlds]], particularly systems in the [[Outer Rim Territories]],<ref name="Prelude to the Clone Wars" /> which largely existed outside of the [[Galactic Republic]]'s jurisdiction.<ref name="Episode I">{{Film|I}}</ref> These companies formed often-criminal monopolies<ref name="Prelude to the Clone Wars" />&mdash;such as the Trade Federation, which established a monopoly over Outer Rim shipping and tried to do the same in the [[Free Trade Zone]]s<ref name="Tarkin" />&mdash;that the exploited systems could not afford to break, lest those systems lose the only organization they found themselves with.<ref name="Prelude to the Clone Wars" /> Corruption also grew in the Republic itself. Corporations represented in the [[Galactic Senate]] pushed for their own interests while self-interested senators<ref name="Corruption">{{TCW|Corruption (episode)}}</ref> attempted to profit off their seats in government.<ref name="Justice">{{TOTJ|Justice (episode)}}</ref><ref name="TOTJ">{{TOTJ|Choices}}</ref>

In time, all while they were increasing in power and influence, the Trade Federation, Techno Union, InterGalactic Banking Clan, Commerce Guild, and Corporate Alliance&mdash;all of which were targeted and empowered by the [[Dark Lord of the Sith]] [[Darth Sidious]], who sought to use these institutions to help overthrow the Republic&mdash;allied together in the name of greater profit. At that same time, these companies were growing annoyed by the restrictions the Senate attempted to put on their businesses. Overall, however, the Republic was ineffective against the influence of the major corporations, with entire [[Supreme Chancellor]]s rising and falling out of power while failing to uphold the promise that they could bring the capitalist alliances into line. Whatever protections the Senate did attempt to enforce were pioneered by Sidious in his alter ego of [[Senator]] Sheev Palpatine.<ref name="Prelude to the Clone Wars" /> Those Senate actions that did pass generated anger on the part of the corporations, playing into his plans. One particular measure was the taxation of the [[Free Trade Zone]]s<ref name="Episode I" /> under [[Prop 31-814D]].<ref name="Propaganda">''[[Star Wars Propaganda: A History of Persuasive Art in the Galaxy]]''</ref>

All the while, pirate attacks against the corporations had led to the Republic giving the galaxy's capitalist alliances permission to use militaries to defend their interests. In total truth, those attacks&mdash;and the ineffectiveness of the many Supreme Chancellors who came and passed&mdash;came because of the [[Grand Plan]] of the [[Sith]] to topple the Republic. The paranoia and profit-focus of the corporations led to a reliance on [[droid]] [[Army|armies]], pushing the the Trade Federation, Techno Union, InterGalactic Banking Clan, Commerce Guild, and Corporate Alliance further together. The first to directly affected by Sidious' influence was the Trade Federation,<ref name="Prelude to the Clone Wars" /> which launched the [[Invasion of Naboo]] in [[32 BBY]]<ref name="SWGA">''[[Star Wars: Galactic Atlas]]''</ref> in protest of Prop 31-814D.<ref name="Episode I" /> However, the incident was also something of a test by the Federation to see how the Republic would respond<ref name="Rise">''[[Rise of the Separatists]]''</ref> and had come at the order of Sidious, who used the incident to [[Supreme Chancellor election, 32 BBY|arise to the position of Supreme Chancellor]].<ref name="Episode I" />

===The Clone Wars===
With the failure of the Naboo Crisis, the Federation's monopoly over the Outer Rim was broken, which helped [[Eriadu Mining and Shipping]] to rise in power.<ref name="Tarkin" /> While the Federation would ultimately retain a great deal of power and its Senate seat,<ref name="Heroes" /> it endured a form of political exile for a time, during which it found allies in its fellow megacorporations and Outer Rim planets.<ref name="RiseFall">''[[Star Wars: The Rise and Fall of the Galactic Empire]]''</ref> With the Republic's failure to respond to the Naboo Crisis in any meaningful way, many planets began to cut their own deals with the Federation despite their prior distaste for the organization. At the urging of the megacorporations, star systems began to secede in spirit from the Republic until this separatist movement began in earnest with the [[Raxus Address]]<ref name="Rise" /> of [[24 BBY]],<ref name="Date">''[[The Star Wars Book]]'' dates the Raxus Address to twenty-four years before [[Star Wars: Episode IV A New Hope|''Star Wars'': Episode IV ''A New Hope'']], which corresponds to [[24 BBY]] according to ''[[Star Wars: Timelines]]''.</ref> a speech given by the former [[Jedi Master]] [[Dooku]] that marked the establishment of the breakaway [[Confederacy of Independent Systems]] (CIS) and the start of the [[Separatist Crisis]].<ref name="Rise" />

[[File:Tambor_on_Ryloth.png|thumb|right|300px|Capitalist institutions like the Techno Union supplied military power to the Confederacy of Independent Systems and stole power from the civilian government.]]
Secretly, Dooku was the [[Sith apprentice]] of Sidious and was working with his master to lead the galaxy to war to continue the Sith Grand Plan.<ref name="EpII">{{Film|II}}</ref> Dooku had secretly allied with the Federation and other titans of galactic industry before his address<ref name="Rise" /> and, in [[22 BBY]],<ref name="SWGA" /> invited them to a [[Geonosian accord|business meeting]] on the planet [[Geonosis]]<ref name="Umbara Commentary">[[Star Wars: The Clone Wars The Complete Season Four|''Star Wars: The Clone Wars'' The Complete Season Four]], "Darkened World of Umbara" Commentary</ref> that also marked the formal establishment of the Confederacy.<ref name="Helmet Collection 52">{{HelmetCollectionCite|52|Databank A-Z}}</ref> That meeting would soon spiral into the [[First Battle of Geonosis]], marking the beginning of the [[Clone Wars]].<ref name="EpII" /> Dooku's plans for his government put business and capitalism first and foremost<ref name="The Separatists">{{EncyclopediaCite|The Separatists}}</ref> despite the ideals of the actual base of Separatist citizens.<ref name="MOF">''[[Reign of the Empire: The Mask of Fear]]''</ref> Dooku's plans attracted the attention of [[Passel Argente]], [[Magistrate]] of the [[Corporate Alliance]], who was instantly attracted to the Separatist cause thanks to Dooku's plans. Inspired to leave the Republic without any hint of hesitation, Argente added the [[Corporate Alliance Policy Administration Directorate]] forces into Dooku's army<ref name="The Separatists" /> at the Geonosis meeting.<ref name="EpII" />

Before the secession, Separatist believers like [[Tychon Nulvolio]] had hoped that they would be able to convince their corporate sponsors to obey their interests through the zeal of their populations. In practice, however, the corporations&mdash;along with the [[Confederacy military]]&mdash;stole power from the civilian government. Separatist believers, such as [[Soujen Vak-Nhalis]], believed that the CIS had made a terrible deal that entailed selling itself out to corporate masters, but believers like Vak-Nhalis hoped that the agreement might, one day, prove wise if it led to Separatist victory and independence. All the while, the corporations declared neutrality in the war<ref name="MOF" /> so as to engage in war profiting by selling their services to both the Republic and Confederacy.<ref name="Umbara Commentary" /> However, the companies actively lifted up and supported the Separatist cause, with their allegiance to the CIS little more than an open secret<ref name="MOF" /> by the war's end.<ref name="Propaganda" /> The corporations supplied their droid armies to the Separatist cause<ref name="EpII" /> and purchased the loyalty of entire planets.<ref name="MOF" />

===Imperial Era===
At the end of the Clone Wars, the CIS was defeated while the Republic was transformed into the [[Galactic Empire]] by the victorious Sidious. As Palpatine, he maintained an image of political [[Populist|populism]], in which he compared successful business people to criminals and, seemingly, cracked down on corporations. In reality, however, Palpatine's policies were [[Kleptocracy|kleptocratic]] and involved a rearranging of wealth into the hands of those who he wanted to hold it. Successful businessmen like [[Arvik Cornade]], despite Palpatine's public statements, were set to profit under his new empire, as was noticed by [[Senator]] [[Bail Prestor Organa]]. Meanwhile, the corporations that had supported the Separatists dropped their sponsorship of the defeated CIS and begged for forgiveness from the Empire in order to continue operating.<ref name="MOF" /> While the Trade Federation<ref name="BustCollection13">{{BustCollectionCite|13|Star Wars Universe}}</ref> and Techno Union were nationalized<ref name="Uprising">''[[Star Wars: Uprising]]''</ref> despite their efforts to surrender,<ref name="MOF" /> most of the companies that had supported the Separatist armed forces were allowed to continue into the new [[Imperial Era]]<ref name="SWBook">''[[The Star Wars Book]]''</ref> and continued to look for ways to influence affairs to their ends.<ref name="The Clone Conspiracy">{{TBB|The Clone Conspiracy}}</ref>

[[File:FirstDance-Harvest.png|thumb|left|300px|The wealthy enjoyed great influence and profit under Imperial rule.]]
For all of Palpatine's populist rhetoric,<ref name="MOF" /> it was the wealthy and a number of corporations that profited during Palpatine's rule,<ref name="RiseFall" /> just as Organa had predicted.<ref name="MOF" /> Overall, the Empire made it a policy to be kind to the galaxy's wealthy, keeping them comfortable.<ref name="Star Wars 46">[[Star Wars (2020) 46|''Star Wars'' (2020) 46]]</ref> Such was done at the expense of the galaxy's average workers, particularly those in the Outer Rim,<ref name="RiseFall" /> such as the [[Scrapper Guild]] employees of the planet [[Bracca]]. Once a planet of great engineers, the guild employees were given lesser pay and worked under more demanding conditions during Imperial rule.<ref name="Jedi Fallen Order">''[[Star Wars Jedi: Fallen Order]]''</ref> The exploitation of average workers allowed for the uplifting of the Imperial government and the wealthy, particularly those in the Core Worlds. In return for continued preferential treatment, companies and wealthy figures would effectively sponsor the Imperial project, funding new starships in the [[Imperial Navy]] and supporting the advancement of promising [[officer]]s.<ref name="RiseFall" /> The wealthy also mingled with Imperial officers at parties while average working citizens on the other side of the galaxy lived in fear.<ref name="One Year Later">{{Andor|One Year Later}}</ref>

However, the Empire also made sure to establish a fear of total nationalization under the [[Imperialization]] process; should such an act be seen as supportive of the Imperial project or deployed to punish anti-Imperial actors, Imperial officials had the right to nationalize private interests. As such, a fear of nationalization spread across the business community, whose members willingly partnered with the Empire and brutally enforced Imperial policy so as to maintain a positive relationship with the state. While the historian [[Beaumont Kin]] theorized the Empire desired total control of the economy in due time, Imperial officials took great joy in watching business interests willing give into Imperial demands so as to maintain their independence.<ref name="RiseFall" /> Meanwhile, the average people of the galaxy were left in a state that left them struggling<ref name="Jedi: Survivor">''[[Star Wars Jedi: Survivor]]''</ref> by 9 BBY.<ref name="9BBY">{{JediSurvivorDate}}</ref> In response, Imperial [[propaganda]] worked to create the image of a far more successful society than that which actually existed, hoping to cover up the economic realities of Imperial rule. Senator [[Daho Sejan]] would claim in a recorded speech that [[terrorist]]s hiding amongst the population were those at fault for the difficulties of everyday life.<ref name="Jedi: Survivor" />

===New Republic Era===
{{Quote|It might be thought that, given the close ties between the wealthy elite and Palpatine's Empire, that some form of investigations and prosecutions would have taken place following the Galactic Civil War. But it seems that the same wealth that insulated the likes of the Tagges from the Empire worked just as well against the New Republic. Notable figures who had prospered under Palpatine, such as Wayulia Tagge-Simoni, were still active in galactic affairs and politics right up to the recent First Order-Resistance conflict.|Beaumont Kin, ''The Rise and Fall of the Galactic Empire''|<ref name="RiseFall" />}}
Wealthy figures who spent time on [[Canto Bight]] profited off the emerging [[First Order]] by selling it arms.<ref name="Episode VIII">{{Film|VIII}}</ref> Engaging in outright war profiteering, many of the wealthiest on Canto Bight also took to selling arms to the [[Resistance]] to profit off both sides. After the [[First Order-Resistance War]], Beaumont Kin took notice in his historical book ''[[The Rise and Fall of the Galactic Empire]]'' of how the Empire had interacted with the economy and how wealthy figures from before the [[Galactic Civil War]] had used their money to escape consequence.<ref name="RiseFall" /> 

==Behind the scenes==
{{Quote|And let me remind you of our absolute commitment to capitalism&hellip; of the lower taxes, the reduced tariffs, and the eventual abolition of all trade barriers. Signing this treaty will bring you profits beyond your wildest imagination.|Count Dooku, in the ''Attack of the Clones'' script|<ref name="EpIIScript">{{WebCite|url=https://assets.scriptslug.com/live/pdf/scripts/star-wars-episode-ii-attack-of-the-clones-2002.pdf|text=''Star Wars'' Episode II ''Attack of the Clones''|author=[[George Lucas]], [[Jonathan Hales]]|archivedate=20250324014207}}</ref>}}
[[File:SecessionistMovement.png|thumb|right|300px|Count Dooku addresses the Separatist Council in ''Attack of the Clones'' in a scene that originally outright named capitalism as an economic model.]]
Capitalism is a real world economic model<ref name="StarWarsFordham">{{WebCite|url=https://fordhampoliticalreview.org/star-wars-america-from-a-certain-point-of-view|text=Star Wars: America from a Certain Point of View|author=Capucine Fourleignie Baufumé |date=2023-11-06|archivedate=20250117045310}}</ref> that was first referenced within ''[[Star Wars]]'' [[canon]] in the short story "[[Black Spire: Return to a Shattered Planet]]<ref name="Return to a Shattered Planet" /> before being outright identified in the encyclopedia "[[Prologue to the Clone Wars and the First Battle of Geonosis]]."<ref name="Prelude to the Clone Wars" /> Previously, capitalism was outright mentioned within the script for<ref name="EpIIScript" /> the [[2002]] film<ref name="EpII" /> [[Star Wars: Episode II Attack of the Clones|''Star Wars'': Episode II ''Attack of the Clones'']] by [[George Lucas]] and [[Jonathan Hales]]; dated [[July 4]], [[2000]], the script saw Count Dooku promise the corporate leaders of the Separatist Council that his Confederacy of Independent Systems would have an "absolute commitment to capitalism" that would "bring [them] profits beyond [their] wildest imagination." Specifically, Dooku stated the corporations would enjoy lower taxes, reduced tariffs, and, in due time, the abolition of trade barriers of any kind.<ref name="EpIIScript" />

While this quote was not featured in the final scene,<ref name="AOTC novel">[[Star Wars: Episode II Attack of the Clones (novelization)|''Star Wars'': Episode II ''Attack of the Clones'' novelization]]</ref> it was retained in the film's [[Star Wars: Episode II Attack of the Clones (novelization)|novelization]] by [[R. A. Salvatore]].<ref name="AOTC novel" /> The novelization was released on [[April 23]], 2002, several weeks before the film's own debut on [[May 16]].<ref name="YearByYear">''[[Star Wars Year By Year: A Visual History, New Edition]]''</ref> The line also retained in the film's [[Attack of the Clones (Mighty Chronicles)|Mighty Chronicles adaptation]] by [[John Whitman]]<ref name="Mighty Chronicles">[[Attack of the Clones (Mighty Chronicles)|''Attack of the Clones'' Mighty Chronicles adaptation]]</ref> released on that same April 23<ref name="DateMighty">{{SWArchive|url=episode-ii/release/publish/news20020211.html|text=Book Preview: Mighty Chronicles}}</ref> and the [[Star Wars: Episode II — Attack of the Clones 3|third issue]] of the film's [[Star Wars: Episode II — Attack of the Clones (comic series)|comic book adaption]] by [[Henry Gilroy]],<ref name="ATOC3">[[Star Wars: Episode II — Attack of the Clones 3|''Star Wars: Episode II — Attack of the Clones'' 3]]</ref> which was released on [[May 1]], 2002.<ref name="Dark Horse">{{DarkHorse|url=Comics/00-474/Star-Wars-Episode-II----Attack-of-the-Clones-3|text=Star Wars: Episode II -- Attack of the Clones #3}}</ref> All of these adaptions now fall into the [[Star Wars Legends|''Star Wars'' Legends]] continuity.<ref name="Legends">{{SW|url=news/the-legendary-star-wars-expanded-universe-turns-a-new-page|text=The Legendary ''Star Wars'' Expanded Universe Turns a New Page}}</ref> Capitalism continued to outright mentioned throughout various Legends works.<ref name="Knights44">[[Knights of the Old Republic 44|''Knights of the Old Republic'' 44]]</ref><ref name="MedStar">[[MedStar I: Battle Surgeons]]</ref>

[[File:InvisibleHandROTS.png|thumb|left|300px|The name of General Grievous's flagship, the ''Invisible Hand'', is also the name of a theory for how real world market forces operate.]]
In both Legends<ref name="RotsDeluxe">[[Star Wars: Revenge of the Sith (novelization)|''Star Wars'': Episode III ''Revenge of the Sith'' novelization]], [[2025]] Deluxe Edition</ref> and canon, the flagship of [[General]] [[Grievous]] is identified as the ''[[Invisible Hand]]'',<ref name="SWBook" /> a name it shares with the capitalist theory of an "[[Wikipedia:Invisible hand|invisible hand]]" of the market, as pioneered by economist [[Wikipedia:Adam Smith|Adam Smith]].<ref name="StarWarsFordham" /> In [[2025]], author [[Matthew Stover]] revealed he came up with the name ''Invisible Hand'' for the warship as "sort of an inside joke." To Stover, the name reflects both Palpatine's role as the secret puppet master of the Clone Wars and also stands "as a subtle dig at the conservatives who idolize Adam Smith." According to Stover, he made that point to compare the Trade Federation to the robber barons of real world history, whom he wrote the Trade Federation officials "are so clearly modeled on."<ref name="RotsDeluxe" />
