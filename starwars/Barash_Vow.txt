The '''Barash Vow''', or '''Barash''' for short, was an oath taken by [[Jedi]] who completely refrained from all activities related to the [[Jedi Order]] as a form of penitence, disengaging from anything but [[the Force]] itself and committing themselves to gain ultimate communion with it. [[Jedi Master]] [[Barash Silvain]] originated the practice, and the vow was named after her. Although most Jedi who swore the vow did so as a method of atonement, some did it in order to re-focus themselves on the Force.

Following the creation of the practice, many more Jedi took the vow over the [[Standard year|years]]. In [[232 BBY]], the [[Jedi Knight]] [[Dez Rydan]] took the vow to re-focus himself on the Force after a traumatic experience with the [[Dark side of the Force|dark side]]-steeped [[Drengir]]. After an incident on [[Brendok]] around [[148 BBY]], Jedi Master [[Torbin]] took the vow and remained in a [[Meditation|meditative]] state for many years. In the late [[Republic Era]], the [[Jedi Master]] [[Kirak Infil'a]] had taken the vow and survived [[Order 66]] due to his adherence to the practice. During the years of his [[exile]] on [[Tatooine]], [[Obi-Wan Kenobi]] took and kept the ways of the vow.

==History==

[[File:BarashPortrait-TheBlade-TPB.png|thumb|left|275px|Jedi Master Barash Silvain (pictured) was the namesake and originator of the Barash Vow.]]
In [[382 BBY]],<ref name="Date">The present day events of ''[[Star Wars: The High Republic — The Blade]]'' take place between the [[wedding of Phan-tu Zenn and Xiri A'lbaran]] and [[Porter Engle]] [[Siege of Bardotta|earning]] his title the "Blade of Bardotta," both of which are dated to [[382 BBY]] by ''[[Star Wars: The High Republic Character Encyclopedia]]''.</ref> [[Jedi Master]] [[Barash Silvain]] accompanied her adopted brother Master [[Porter Engle]] on a [[Mission on Gansevor|mission]] to the [[planet]] [[Gansevor]] to resolve a [[Siege of Firevale|conflict]] between the [[City|cities]] [[Firevale]] and [[Bethune]]. Silvain was tricked by [[Princess]] [[Sicatra]] of Bethune into escalating the conflict, as the Jedi Master was swayed by her own biases and did not listen to the Force. After Silvain realized her mistake and the conflict was resolved, she swore to Engle that she would take no action on behalf of the Jedi Order and isolate herself until she could trust herself and the Force again. Barash Silvain's choice became the practice known as the Barash Vow,<ref name="The Blade 4" /> which was named after her.<ref name="Chronicles of the Jedi">''[[Star Wars: The High Republic: Chronicles of the Jedi]]''</ref> From the founding of the Order to its [[Great Jedi Purge|doom]], over fourteen thousand Jedi pursued Barash.<ref name="Darth Vader 2">[[Darth Vader (2017) 2|''Darth Vader'' (2017) 2]]</ref>

Although primarily a method of atonement for terrible mistakes, some [[Jedi]], such as [[Jedi Knight]] [[Dez Rydan]], took the vow in order to re-focus themselves on the Force.<ref name="Into the Dark">''[[The High Republic: Into the Dark]]''</ref> In [[232 BBY]],<ref name="Timelines">''[[Star Wars: Timelines]]''</ref> After a traumatic experience as a prisoner of the [[Dark side of the Force|dark side]]-steeped [[Drengir]], Rydan chose to swear the vow and leave for a contemplation world. Rydan hoped to both recover from the damage the Drengir had inflicted on him and refocus his path on service instead of action and excitement.<ref name="Into the Dark" />

[[File:TorbinLevitation-RevengeJustice.png|thumb|right|250px|While taking the vow, Torbin remained in a state of silent levitation for over 10 years.]]
Jedi Master [[Torbin]] took the Barash Vow in response to an [[Mission to Brendok|incident]] on [[Brendok]]<ref name="RevengeJustice">{{Acolyte|Revenge / Justice}}</ref> around [[148 BBY]].<ref name="Acolyte Date">{{TheAcolyteDate}} As the series establishes that the incident on Brenok occurred sixteen years before its events, it must have occurred around [[148 BBY]].</ref> As part of his vow, he eventually entered a silent [[Levitation|levitating]] [[meditation]] in which the Force deflected any physical interaction with him. After over a [[Standard decade|decade]] in this state,<ref name="RevengeJustice" /> in around [[132 BBY]],<ref name="Acolyte Date" /> he was [[Skirmish on Olega|confronted]] by [[assassin]] [[Mae-ho Aniseya|Mae Aniseya]], who warned him that she would [[Death|kill]] him if he did not confess the crime he committed on Brendok to the [[Jedi High Council]]. Seeking forgiveness, Torbin broke his meditative state and killed himself by consuming a concoction provided by Aniseya, which contained the [[poison]]ous substance [[bunta]].<ref name="RevengeJustice" />

During the late [[Republic Era]], Jedi Master [[Kirak Infil'a]] was a Barash-taker who was not killed by the execution of [[Order 66]]<ref name="Darth Vader 2" /> in [[19 BBY]]<ref name="Timelines" /> due to his adherence to the vow. Knowing that the vow required disengagement from anything but the Force and thus would not allow Infil'a to react to the event, [[Sith Lord]] [[Anakin Skywalker|Darth Vader]] hunted him down,<ref name="Darth Vader 2" /> finding him on the planet [[Al'doleem]]. There, Infil'a spent his Barash meditating while levitating on top of the sacred Jedi [[mountain]] [[Mount Pasvaal|Pasvaal]]. While [[Lightsaber duel|sparring]] with his [[droid]] [[Arex]], Infil'a reaffirmed his decision not to go into hiding and sensed Darth Vader's imminent [[Mission to Al'doleem|arrival]] in the Force. After Infil'a stared down Vader from the mountain and resisted the [[Sith Lord]]'s Force attacks, Vader confirmed that he had killed the other Jedi and planned to kill Infil'a as well. In response, Infil'a declared his Barash complete, deciding that his new path ended with killing Vader.<ref name="Darth Vader (2017) 3" /> However, their duel ended with Vader killing him instead.<ref name="Darth Vader (2017) 4">[[Darth Vader (2017) 4|''Darth Vader'' (2017) 4]]</ref>

[[Obi-Wan Kenobi]] took and kept to the ways of the vow during the years of his [[Exile (punishment)|exile]] on [[Tatooine]] until leaving the planet to heed [[Princess]] [[Leia Skywalker Organa Solo|Leia Organa's]] call for aid with his former apprentice's son, [[Luke Skywalker]].<ref name="Family at War">''[[Skywalker: A Family at War]]''</ref>

==Behind the scenes==
{{Quote|The Vow is taken by Jedi who feel they have done something deeply wrong &ndash; particularly a misinterpretation of what they believed the Force wanted them to do &ndash; and while they don't leave the Order, they decide to sit and listen to the Force until they better understand themselves and what it's saying.|Charles Soule|<ref name="Instagram">{{Instagram|username=charlesdsoule|url=p/C71lc2Hx9oe|name=Charles Soule|date=2024-06-05|quote=The Barash Vow! It's always amazing when anything I make up in a comic or a novel finds its way into other kinds of storytelling, but this one was really special. As seen in THE ACOLYTE, Episode 2 - the Barash Vow, which we first saw taken by a character named Kirak Infil'a in Darth Vader #2, written by me and drawn by @giuseppe_camuncoli. The Vow is taken by Jedi who feel they have done something deeply wrong - particularly a misinterpretation of what they believed the Force wanted them to do - and while they don't leave the Order, they decide to sit and listen to the Force until they better understand themselves and what it's saying. It's named after Barash Silvain, a Jedi in the High Republic who took the Vow after a particularly egregious error. Jedi did this before her (and certainly after,) but her name became linked with it from that point forward. You can see that story in The Blade, a four-issue comic miniseries about Barash and her adopted brother Porter Engle, written by me and drawn by @mark_castiello and @jethro_morales Was really nice to see. #todaysgoodthing|archiveurl=https://archive.is/DHW2v}}</ref>}}
The Barash Vow first appeared in the [[Darth Vader (2017) 2|second issue]] of the [[Star Wars comic books|comic series]] [[Star Wars: Darth Vader (2017)|''Star Wars: Darth Vader'' (2017)]], which was written by [[Charles Soule]]<ref name="Darth Vader 2" /> and released on [[June 21]], [[2017]].<ref name="Marvel">{{Marvel|url=comics/issue/63176/darth_vader_2017_2|text=Darth Vader (2017) #2}}</ref> According to Soule, a Jedi undertaking a Barash Vow would not take on a [[Padawan]] learner.<ref name="Mad cool">{{Twitter|CharlesSoule|status/1020075056989261824|[[Charles Soule]]|quote=I don't think someone who has taken the Barash Vow would think they had any business taking a padawan. {{C|In response to: "Hello Mr. Soule. I'm a fan of your work. I have a question on the Darth Vader comic Jedi Kirak Infil'a. I know he had taken a vow of disengaging from the Jedi to focus on the Force but is it possible for him to still take on a padawan during his vow?"}}|archivedate=20190919004806}}</ref> ''[[Star Wars: The High Republic — The Blade]]'' depicts the Barash Vow being created after the [[mission on Gansevor]],<ref name="The Blade 4" /> however ''[[Star Wars: The High Republic: Chronicles of the Jedi]]'' places it after the [[Siege of Bardotta]].<ref name="Chronicles of the Jedi" /> This article assumes the former to be the correct flow of events.

Soule was quite pleased to see the Barash Vow make its live action debut in ''[[Star Wars: The Acolyte]]'' episode "[[Revenge / Justice]]." On Twitter<ref name="Soule Happy">{{Twitter|CharlesSoule|status/1798357397263819102|[[Charles Soule]]|quote=Particularly liked this part of Acolyte Episode 2.|archiveurl=https://archive.ph/ExK6L}}</ref> and Instagram, Soule expressed his enjoyment of the Barash Vow's appearance in ''The Acolyte'', recounting the Vow's origins both in and out-of-universe. Soule also claimed that the practice had been adopted by Jedi before and after its namesake, Barash Silvain, a fact that has yet to be stated by any [[canon]] source.<ref name="Instagram" />
