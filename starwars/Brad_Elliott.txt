'''Brad Elliott''' was the property master for the ''[[Star Wars: Obi-Wan Kenobi]]'' television series. Elliott and his team worked on several props for the series, including [[Double-bladed spinning lightsaber|Inquisitor lightsabers]], [[Obi-Wan Kenobi's third lightsaber|Obi-Wan Kenobi's lightsaber]], [[wupiupi]] [[coin]]s, a power [[Hammer (tool)|hammer]], a [[thermal detonator]], [[spice]] canisters, [[Tala Durith]]'s [[holster]], and a [[scomp link]] for the [[TR-286]] [[Transport eight|cargo transport shuttle]].

==Biography==
Brad Elliott first saw ''[[Star Wars]]'' when he was six years old and he walked out of the movie theater in awe.<ref name="Weapons of Inquis">{{SW|url=news/inside-the-lucasfilm-archive-obi-wan-kenobi-props-2|text=Inside the Lucasfilm Archive: Weapons of the Inquisitorius from the ''Obi-Wan Kenobi'' Limited Series}}</ref> Elliott was the propmaster for the ''[[Star Wars: Obi-Wan Kenobi]]'' television series.<ref name="Elegant weapon">{{SW|url=news/inside-the-lucasfilm-archive-obi-wan-kenobi-props-1|text=Inside the Lucasfilm Archive: An Elegant Weapon and Other Jedi Artifacts from the ''Obi-Wan Kenobi'' Limited Series}}</ref> For preparation, he rewatched all of the movies that took place before ''Obi-Wan Kenobi'' to acquire a deeper understanding of the aesthetic that the series would be evolving from. This helped reframe his interpretation of legacy props such as [[Obi-Wan Kenobi's third lightsaber|Obi-Wan Kenobi's lightsaber]]. Elliott used the resources provided by<ref name="Weapons of Inquis" /> [[Lucasfilm]] creative executive<ref name="Elegant weapon" /> [[Pablo Hidalgo]] and he constantly referred to the works of [[Ralph McQuarrie]] for inspiration. He relied heavily on the same techniques the prop builders used in [[Star Wars: Episode IV A New Hope|''Star Wars'': Episode IV ''A New Hope'']]. His team used pieces from the [[1920s]] through the [[1960s]] to ensure that the props felt like they belonged in ''Star Wars''. Elliott and his team were led by executive producer and director [[Deborah Chow]].<ref name="Weapons of Inquis" />

In an early meeting with Chow and [[Dave Filoni]] to discuss translating the animated [[Double-bladed spinning lightsaber|Inquisitor lightsabers]] into realistic props, Filoni advised the propmaster to imagine the live-action came first, and the animated material was an exaggerated version of that. This allowed Elliott to keep the form factor of the props established in animation and to bring them in line with props made for [[human]] [[Limb|hands]]. All four [[Inquisitor]] [[lightsaber]]s were shifted to accommodate the actors portraying the roles, including [[Rupert Friend]] and [[Sung Kang]]. Elliott and his team were careful to minimize the more fragile looking parts of the animated designs and they made sure that the size of the rings and handles would fit their intended character.<ref name="Weapons of Inquis" /> Elliott said that Kenobi's lightsaber was the trickiest thing for his team to figure out because the lightsaber from [[Star Wars: Episode III Revenge of the Sith|''Star Wars'': Episode III ''Revenge of the Sith'']] differed in many details from the lightsaber seen in ''A New Hope''.<ref name="Elegant weapon" />

During creation of [[wupiupi]] props, Elliott's team only had one image for reference from [[Star Wars: Episode I The Phantom Menace|''Star Wars'': Episode I ''The Phantom Menace'']], which showed that the coin is slightly larger than an [[Wikipedia:Half dollar (United States coin)|American half dollar]]. Deborah Chow worried that the size was too large for swift hand-to-hand transactions, so the coins were scaled down slightly with the same art as the first design. Elliott and Pablo Hidalgo discussed the coins' look and their proper conversion rate with [[trugut]]s and [[credit]]s. This allowed the two to figure out the cost of some common goods in ''Star Wars'' and a minimum wage for the [[Tibidon Station|whale-carving station]] that [[Obi-Wan Kenobi]] worked at. It was decided that a full shift paid one trugut and three wupiupi.<ref name="Elegant weapon" /> The team made at least four prop coins which were made to look very detailed.<ref name="Props">{{ThisWeek|5dYCIFU5q6Y|Obi-Wan Kenobi Props, LEGO Star Wars Summer Vacation, and More!}}</ref>

The script described [[NED-B]]'s [[Hammer (tool)|hammer]] as a power hammer. Elliott wasn't sure what that meant, so he took design inspiration from the guards on [[Khetanna|Jabba the Hutt's skiff]]. He added a similar looking power unit and some rigid tubing to suggest that it creates extra impact with a power assist. The [[thermal detonator]] used by [[Tala Durith]] took inspiration from the one held by [[Leia Skywalker Organa Solo|Leia Organa]] in [[Star Wars: Episode VI Return of the Jedi|''Star Wars'': Episode VI ''Return of the Jedi'']]. Elliott bought a [[Wikipedia:Corgi_International#Master_Replicas|Master Replicas]] thermal detonator and then dismantled it. The propmaster's team rewired the electronics to behave the way Chow wanted it to work in the series. Elliott found that using real merchandise was more efficient than having to resculpt, remold, and repaint something new. The [[spice]] canisters were crafted in the same shape and style as [[Jabba Desilijic Tiure|Jabba the Hutt's]] [[hookah pipe]]. Elliott said that the idea was that there is a pressurized canister that vents the spice when a button is pushed.<ref name="Brewing rebellion">{{SW|url=news/inside-the-lucasfilm-archive-obi-wan-kenobi-props-3|text=Inside the Lucasfilm Archive: A Brewing Rebellion in the ''Obi-Wan Kenobi'' Limited Series}}</ref>

The propmakers were assigned with designing a [[holster]] that could fit young Leia Organa and the adult Tala Durith. Elliott found this to be challenging because of the actors' height differences and it had to hold [[L0-LA59|Lola]], Organa's [[droid]], and Durith's [[blaster]]. Durith's holster was designed as a drop holster. For Organa, the team took the drop portion off of the gun belt and it fit her perfectly. To fill the space needed to accommodate Lola, a [[Han Solo]]-style [[scope]] was added to Durith's blaster. Elliott said it was very satisfying when the holster ended up working. In the series' script, the [[TR-286]] [[Transport eight|cargo transport shuttle]] was described as an "automated transport," so Elliott decided it was illogical to have a launch key designed for humans. The team decided to use a rigged [[scomp link]] [[computer]] interface device that had its own circuit board to simulate what an [[astromech droid]] would use to start the [[transport]]'s launch sequence and program the route.<ref name="Brewing rebellion" />
