'''Cavan Scott''' (born [[April 18]], [[1973]])<ref name="Birthdate" /> is an author known for writing four of the novels in the ''[[Star Wars: Adventures in Wild Space]]'' young-readers series,<ref name="Site" /> plus several issues of [[IDW Publishing|IDW's]] [[Star Wars Adventures (2017)|''Star Wars Adventures'']] and the miniseries ''[[Star Wars Adventures: Tales from Vader's Castle]]''. He also worked on ''[[Star Wars: The High Republic]]'' with [[Claudia Gray]], [[Charles Soule]], [[Daniel José Older]], and [[Justina Ireland]].<ref name="Celebration">{{StarWarsShow|sP6SaKn2WuM|Star Wars Celebration Chicago 2019 Live Stream - Day 4 &#124; The Star Wars Show LIVE!}}</ref>

==Biography==
===Early life===
Cavan Scott was born on [[April 18]], [[1973]].<ref name="Birthdate" /> In [[2022]], Scott named the ''[[Star Wars: Darth Bane Trilogy|Darth Bane Trilogy]]'' and the comic series ''[[Star Wars: Republic]]'', ''[[Star Wars: Dark Empire|Dark Empire]]'' and ''[[Star Wars: Tales of the Jedi (comic series)|Tales of the Jedi]]'' as his favourite stories from [[Star Wars Legends|''Star Wars'' Legends]]. He also said that he'd love to continue the comic series ''[[Star Wars: Agent of the Empire|Agent of the Empire]]'' if it were still canon.<ref name="Favourite Leg">>{{Twitter|username=cavanscott|url=status/1552311007610933254|name=[[Cavan Scott]]|quote=Off the top of my head, the Bane Trilogy, Republic (my all time favourite SW comic to this day), Dark Empire, Tales of the Jedi. Also, a special mention to Agent of the Empire which, if we were still writing the EU, I would LOVE to continue.|date=2022-07-22|archiveurl=http://web.archive.org/web/20220727173023/https://twitter.com/seanmason/status/1552325270186905600|suppress=1|nolive=1}} {{C|In response to "@cavanscott I’m curious - what are your favourite legends era stories, books or comics?"}}</ref>

===''Star Wars''===
====''Adventures in Wild Space''====
Scott's first assignment with the ''[[Star Wars]]'' franchise first came when he and [[Tom Huddleston]] were contacted by [[Egmont UK Ltd]] and [[Lucasfilm]] to write ''[[Star Wars: Adventures in Wild Space]]'', a seven-part junior novel series published by Egmont in the [[Wikipedia:United Kingdom|United Kingdom]] and by [[Disney–Lucasfilm Press]] in the [[Wikipedia:United States of America|United States of America]]. With nothing more but a general idea for the set-up, Scott spun out the characters for what ultimately became ''[[Adventures in Wild Space: The Escape]]'', the first installment in the series.<ref name="ERDG Cavan Scott Interview">{{Blogspot|subdomain=elrecovecodelgeek|url=2019/11/entrevista-cavan-scott-y-he-aqui.html|name=El Recoveco del Geek|text=Interview: Cavan Scott|date=2019-11-27}}</ref> For Scott, there was a certain level of content that distinguished what was for younger readers, but he had no problem as he believes that there's a certain point where the children must be treated like grown-ups by telling them an engaging and interesting story. Additionally, Scott considered that creating his own characters was less stressing than writing already existing and cemented characters.<ref name="Refuge Interview">{{YouTube|video=A7fcXlV8sGg|text=We Interview Cavan Scott! - Adventures in Star Wars (and Other Writings)|channel=channel/UCaUfRuWVB1G6LZ_qdyZ0HcA|name=The Star Wars Refuge|archivedate=20200814164701}}</ref>

Among the characters Scott created for the series were [[Milo Graf|Milo]] and [[Lina Graf]], the protagonists, and [[Captain]] [[Visler Aqorian Korda III|Visler Korda]], one of the antagonists.<ref name="ERDG Cavan Scott Interview" /> Before getting into the project, Scott wrote an audition piece which differed from the finished product. One of the revisions was changing Lina Graf from a [[Twi'lek]] to a [[human]].<ref name="Being Cavan Scott">{{WebCite|url=https://thefutureoftheforce.com/2018/06/06/interview-being-cavan-scott|text=Being Cavan Scott|author=Brad Tracy|date=2018-06-06|archivedate=20190315205636}}</ref> During the writing progress, Lucasfilm requested Scott to tap into the events of ''[[Star Wars Rebels]]'', which aired on [[Disney XD]] by that time. While writing ''The Escape'', the [[Lucasfilm Story Group]] asked Scott to rework the beginning under no specific explanation; with Scott discovering months later that his novel started pretty much like [[Capture of Galen Erso|the beginning]] of the [[Star Wars Anthology Series|Anthology]] film ''[[Rogue One: A Star Wars Story]]'' which released in [[December]] [[2016]].<ref name="Refuge Interview" />

''Adventures in Wild Space: The Escape'' proved to be a success.<ref name="Amazon-US The Escape">{{Amazon|url=Star-Wars-Adventures-Wild-Space-ebook/dp/B01N8XROIS|text=Star Wars Adventures in Wild Space: The Escape: Prelude|archivedate=20170426210313}}</ref> As a result, Scott was hired to write a [[Adventures in Wild Space: The Snare|three]] [[Adventures in Wild Space: The Steal|more]] [[Adventures in Wild Space: The Cold|follow-ups]] while Huddleston wrote [[Adventures in Wild Space: The Nest|the]] [[Adventures in Wild Space: The Dark|other]] [[Adventures in Wild Space: The Rescue|three]]. It was after the first book's release, however, that Scott and Huddleston met in person for the first time.<ref name="Refuge Interview" /> Something Scott actually expected upon the series' conclusion was the growing popularity of the Graf children, leading to do the suggestion of seeing them grown up much later.<ref name="ERDG Cavan Scott Interview" /> Scott's suggestion turned out to be true, and he was really surprised upon later finding a mention to the Graf siblings in [[Emil Fortune]]'s reference book ''[[Star Wars: Galactic Atlas]]'' during [[Celebration Europe (2016)|Celebration Europe]], as it meant the Grafs had survived to the [[New Republic Era|era of the current films]]. Scott deemed fantastic at the possibility for the readers to grow up with the characters, like it happened to [[Han Solo]], [[Leia Skywalker Organa Solo|Leia Organa]] and [[Luke Skywalker]] with the release of the [[sequel trilogy]].<ref name="Refuge Interview" />

====More stories for the galaxy far, far away====
By the time Cavan had just written the [[W:c:memory-alpha:Star Trek|''Star Trek'']] comic book anthology series [[W:c:memory-alpha:Star Trek: Waypoint|''Star Trek: Waypoint'']], he was suggested by Sarah Gaydos, the ''Star Trek'' editor on [[IDW Publishing]], to submit a number of storylines for [[Star Wars Adventures (2017)|''Star Wars Adventures'']], another anthology comic book series that was in development as IDW to be released in late [[2017]].<ref name="ERDG Cavan Scott Interview" /> Scott found the opportunity wonderful yet terrifying because he didn't know what he was gonna get, but accepted it. He was attached to work with [[Derek Charm]], with whom he had never worked previously, but both went to knock things from each other over time.<ref name="Refuge Interview" /> Said and done, Scott was invited to meet the other creative teams, and during one session, Scott submitted a number of storylines, including the one that developed in "[[Better the Devil You Know, Part I]]," starring [[Rey Skywalker|Rey]].<ref name="ERDG Cavan Scott Interview" />

The series was officially announced on [[Celebration Orlando]] for a [[September]] 2017 release date, with Scott attached to write the [[Star Wars Adventures (2017) 1|first issue]] to follow up a [[Star Wars Adventures Ashcan|preview issue]] by [[Landry Q. Walker]].<ref name="Adventures Creative Team">{{WebCite|url=https://www.cbr.com/star-wars-adventures-storylines-creative-teams-reveal-exclusive|text=Star Wars Adventures: First Storylines, Creative Teams Revealed|author=Albert Ching|date=2017-06-14|archivedate=20190110214816}}</ref> When writing the first issue, Scott came up with the idea to introduce [[Emil Graf]], the grandson of Milo Graf, as the story's narrator<ref name="ERDG Cavan Scott Interview" /> to replace [[C-3PO]] and [[Magreda]] in that role, as someone who knew everyone in [[the galaxy]]. Lucasfilm suggested to name the character after Emil Fortune as a tribute, which Scott complied, as well to the creation of new characters like [[CR-8R]], [[BB-00]] and [[Noni]]. CR-8R became Scott's favorite character for his sarcastic and sassy personality whereas BB-00 was named after the nickname of his youngest daughter.<ref name="Refuge Interview" /> Scott commented that the response to the series was phenomenal and helped to introduce ''Star Wars'' and comic books to a new generation, much like how the original [[Marvel Comics]]' [[Star Wars (1977)|''Star Wars'']] line introduced Scott to the franchise.<ref name="Being Cavan Scott" /> Since the series' beginning, Scott campaigned to bring back the character of [[Jaxxon/Legends|Jaxxon]] from the now-rebranded [[Star Wars Legends|''Star Wars'' Legends]] continuity into the new [[canon]] continuity established by Lucasfilm since [[2014]]. Initially dismissing the possibility, Scott was surprised when the ''Adventures'' editorial team called him to ask him if he would like to write an story featuring Jaxxon. Scott leaped at the opportunity immediately and [[Jaxxon T. Tumperakki|Jaxxon]] made a return to the ''Star Wars'' canon in Scott's "[[The Lost Eggs of Livorno]]," the secondary story of [[Star Wars Adventures Annual 2018|''Star Wars Adventures Annual'' 2018]].<ref name="ERDG Cavan Scott Interview" />

Towards the end of 2017, Scott joined the project of ''[[From a Certain Point of View]]'', an anthology book with forty-three different stories centered on several characters from the [[1977]] original film [[Star Wars: Episode IV A New Hope|''Star Wars'': Episode IV ''A New Hope'']] to celebrate the film's 40th anniversary.<ref name="FaCPoV Announcement">{{SW|url=news/star-wars-from-a-certain-point-of-view-anthology-book-celebrates-40-years-of-a-galaxy-far-far-away|text=''Star Wars: From A Certain Point of View'' Anthology Book Celebrates 40 Years of a Galaxy Far, Far Away}}</ref> When asked which character and moment he wanted to choose, Scott replied that he wanted to focus on [[Obi-Wan Kenobi]]'s [[Rescue of Princess Leia|death]]. The wise [[Jedi Master]] originally portrayed by [[Alec Guinness]] meant so much for Scott as Kenobi had been the first ''Star Wars'' action figure Scott ever had when his grandmother bought him when he was little. Though he thought that another author had already chosen that moment, editor [[Elizabeth Schaefer]] replied that no one had asked to tackle the moment. To prepare for writing it, Scott rewatched the [[prequel trilogy]] and thought about possible flashbacks of Luke Skywalker's childhood.<ref name="Being Cavan Scott" /> The idea that Obi-Wan left toys for Luke when he was a child was Scott's own, basing it on the ''Star Wars'' [[Kenner Products|Kenner]] toys he played with as a kid. Scott also felt that having Obi-Wan revising his past, present and future created the sense that he was literally going to step into [[Netherworld of the Force|a larger world]]. Scott overall liked the experience of writing the story as he could finally wrote a ''Star Wars'' tale for adults.<ref name="Refuge Interview" />

In [[2018]], in anticipation of the then upcoming release of ''[[Solo: A Star Wars Story]]'', the second Anthology film, Scott was asked by Lucasfilm to helm a [[Wikipedia:Choose Your Own Adventure|''Choose Your Own Adventure'']]-esque book series focusing on Han Solo and [[Chewbacca]]. The project originally started as a normal book series with straight chapters; the idea to make them gamebooks came quite late in the planning process.<ref name="ERDG Cavan Scott Interview" /> Scott had no problems with the change, as he had loved the [[Wikipedia:Fighting Fantasy|''Fighting Fantasy'']] gamebooks written by [[Wikipedia:Ian Livingstone|Ian Livingstone]] and [[Wikipedia:Steve Jackson (British game designer)|Steve Jackson]]. Prior to this, however, Cavan had never done a gamebook after a failed attempt in the mid-[[2000]]s to make one centered on [[Wikipedia:Pirates of the Caribbean (film series)|''Pirates of the Caribbean'']]. Ironically, much like it happened with the first installment of ''Adventures in Wild Space'', Scott's original outline inadvertently resembled too much the upcoming Anthology film of that year so he had to tweak some changes. While making the series, Scott provided a series of short but straightforward illustration briefs for [[Elsa Charretier]], the illustrator of the gamebooks.<ref name="Choose Your Destiny Interview">{{SW|url=news/choose-han-solos-destiny-writer-cavan-scott-and-artist-elsa-charretier-discuss-their-charming-new-book|text=Choose Han Solo's Destiny: Writer Cavan Scott and Artist Elsa Charretier Discuss Their Charming New Book}}</ref> The idea to focus in all four ''Star Wars'' eras came directly from Lucasfilm.<ref name="ERDG Cavan Scott Interview" />

====''Vader's Castle''====
While promoting the launch of ''Star Wars Adventures'' during [[Wikipedia:San Diego Comic-Con|San Diego Comic-Con]] 2017, Cavan was approached by [[Michael Siglain]] with the concept of making an ''Star Wars'' anthology of all-age horror-inspired stories. Scott and Siglain shared a love to classic horror films from those of the [[Wikipedia:Universal Classic Monsters|Universal Monsters]] to the ones of [[Wikipedia:Hammer Film Productions|Hammer Films]]. Scott wanted the series to have a similar feel to that of [[Wikipedia:Amicus Productions|Amicus Productions]]' [[1960s#1965|1965]] classic [[Wikipedia:Dr. Terror's House of Horrors|''Dr. Terror's House of Horrors'']], one of Scott's favorite horror films which starred [[Peter Cushing]] and [[Christopher Lee]], the actors who played [[Grand Moff]] [[Wilhuff Tarkin]] and [[Count]] [[Dooku]] in [[Original trilogy|original]] and prequel trilogies respectively. Thus, the idea for ''[[Star Wars Adventures: Tales from Vader's Castle]]'' was born. Derek Charm teamed up with Scott again to illustrate the series while Story Group's [[Matt Martin]] made sure that the horror content would be still suitable for all ages.<ref name="Vader's Castle Announcement">{{SW|url=news/sdcc-2018-tales-from-vaders-castle|text=SDCC 2018: IDW's ''Tales From Vader's Castle'' To Bring The Galactic Frights This October &ndash; Exclusive!}}</ref>

In the earliest stages of development, ''Tales from Vader's Castle'' was intended to be set around the [[34 ABY|same time]] as the [[2015]] film [[Star Wars: Episode VII The Force Awakens|''Star Wars'': Episode VII ''The Force Awakens'']], so Scott suggested a number of appropriate characters from the sequels' era, like [[Poe Dameron]]. However, it was then decided to set it instead around the same time as ''Rogue One''. As a result, the characters had to change and the Poe story Scott originally pitched was replaced with one starring Han Solo.<ref name="ERDG Cavan Scott Interview" /> Similarly, Scott was unable to write the [[Nightsisters]] into the [[Star Wars Adventures: Tales from Vader's Castle 3|third issue's]] story starring Han given the timeline's changes. Likewise, the story of the [[Star Wars Adventures: Tales from Vader's Castle 4|fourth issue]] featuring the [[Ewok]]s was intended to star [[Wicket Wystri Warrick|Wicket W. Warrick]] and [[Teebo]], but the timeline displacement caused Scott to change them for [[Chirpa]] and [[Logray]] respectively, a change he ultimately liked. While working on the [[Star Wars Adventures: Tales from Vader's Castle 2|second issue]], Scott desisted from parodying [[Wikipedia:Mary Shelley|Mary Shelley's]] 1818 novel [[Wikipedia:Frankestein|''Frankestein'']] and instead parody [[Wikipedia:Bram Stoker|Bram Stoker's]] 1897 novel [[Wikipedia:Dracula|''Dracula'']] due Christopher Lee's legendary portrayal as Count Dracula, even naming the planet [[Bray]] after Hammer's [[Wikipedia:Bray Productions|Bray Studios]]. The story for the [[Star Wars Adventures: Tales from Vader's Castle 5|fifth issue]] came up quite late in development, on the other hand.<ref name="Spooky Secrets">{{SW|url=news/the-spooky-secrets-of-idws-tales-from-vaders-castle|text=The Spooky Secrets of IDW's ''Tales from Vader's Castle''}}</ref>

Scott's original plans for the series was to have a different title for each issue which referenced some iconic horror movie. While the [[Star Wars Adventures: Tales from Vader's Castle 1|first issue's]] title was going to be "The Haunting of [[Hera Syndulla]]," Scott changed it to "The Haunting of the [[Ghost]]" as a reference to [[Wikipedia:Shirley Jackson|Shirley Jackson's]] [[1950s#1959|1959]] gothic horror novel [[Wikipedia:The Haunting Hill House|''The Haunting of Hill House'']]. Other influences for the series included [[The Walt Disney Company|Disney's]] [[2013]] film [[Wikipedia:Frozen (2013 film)|''Frozen'']], which inspired the [[Rendel|setting]] and story of the third issue.<ref name="Spooky Secrets" /> The character of [[Thom Hudd]] was named and partially modeled after Tom Huddleston, Scott's teammate from the ''Adventures in Wild Space'' series.<ref name="ERDG Cavan Scott Interview" /> Additionally, easter eggs to the Legends continuity, like the TV movie ''[[Caravan of Courage: An Ewok Adventure]]'', were included as well. Aside from Charm, Scott and Siglain had several illustrators invited too as guest artists to collaborate each one with an issue: these were [[Chris Fenoglio]], [[Kelley Jones]], [[Corin Howell]], [[Charlie Kirchoff]] and [[Charles Paul Wilson III]].<ref name="Spooky Secrets" /> In anticipation of the series' release in [[October]] 2018, Scott expressed his interest on making ''Tales from Vader's Castle'' a ''Star Wars'' [[Wikipedia:Halloween|Halloween]] tradition which could be revisited year on year.<ref name="Vader's Castle Announcement" />

Before the release of ''Tales from Vader's Castle'', Scott certainly hoped to make a follow-up series despite that he had no way to know how things were going to turn about.<ref name="ERDG Cavan Scott Interview" /> Fortunately, ''Tales from Vader's Castle'' was highly acclaimed and Scott was hired to write ''[[Star Wars Adventures: Return to Vader's Castle]]'', a sequel series.<ref name="Return to Vader's Castle Announcement">{{SW|url=news/swcc-2019-new-tales-from-vaders-castle-series-announced-jaxxon-returns-and-more-from-the-idw-publishing-panel|text=SWCC 2019: New ''Tales from Vader's Castle'' Series Announced, Jaxxon Returns, and More from the IDW Publishing Panel}}</ref> Scott partnered again with [[Francesco Francavilla]] to do the framing of the series, focusing on stories starring [[Maul|Darth Maul]], [[Asajj Ventress]], [[Jabba Desilijic Tiure|Jabba the Hutt]], [[Anakin Skywalker|Darth Vader]] and Grand [[Moff]] Tarkin. Following the well-received inclusion of [[Vaneé]] in ''Tales from Vader's Castle'', Cavan decided to use him as the narrator and Francesco pitched the idea of having Vaneé holding a flaming staff. Given Scott's love for the ''[[Star Wars: The Clone Wars]]'' episode "[[Brothers]]," it was decided to have the [[Star Wars Adventures: Return to Vader's Castle 1|first issue's]] setting be [[Lotho Minor]], featuring [[Darth]] Maul in his disturbing appearance with spider-like [[Cybernetics|robotic legs]].<ref name="Behind Spooky Secrets">{{SW|url=news/behind-the-spooky-scenes-of-idws-return-to-vaders-castle-1-exclusive|text=Behind the Spooky Scenes of IDW's ''Return to Vader's Castle'' #1 &ndash; Exclusive}}</ref>

====''Dooku: Jedi Lost''====
Before returning to [[Fortress Vader|Vader's castle]], Cavan was assigned to handle an special task: write a prequel audiobook to the [[1999]] film [[Star Wars: Episode I The Phantom Menace|''Star Wars'': Episode I ''The Phantom Menace'']] focusing on the early days of Count Dooku, one of the major antagonists of the prequels.<ref name="D:JL Announcement">{{SW|url=news/dooku-jedi-lost-audio-book-announce|text=Discover the Secrets of ''Dooku: Jedi Lost''}}</ref> Scott was unworried about the job, however: he had previously done several audiobooks for other franchises like [[Wikipedia:Doctor Who|''Doctor Who'']] and had heard many soap operas and audio dramas like [[Wikipedia:BBC Radio 4|BBC Radio 4's]] [[Wikipedia:The Archers|''The Archers'']], [[Wikipedia:The Avengers (radio series)|''The Avengers'']] and [[Wikipedia:The Shadow#Radio program|''The Shadow'']] to name a few, so he had been preparing for these kind of jobs for so many years, being like a second nature for him.<ref name="What The Force Interview">{{YouTube|video=agDR3KVnn6s|text=Podcast &#124; Interview with Cavan Scott Author of Dooku: Jedi Lost|channel=channel/UCLJu_UVHQDUmzewiNLs3tpA|name=What The Force|archivedate=20200815075040}}</ref> Ultimately, writing ''Dooku: Jedi Lost'' made Scott a bigger fan of the Dooku character.<ref name="Explores">{{SW|url=news/dooku-jedi-lost-cavan-scott-interview|text=How ''Dooku: Jedi Lost'' Explores a Rising Sith Lord and His Apprentice}}</ref>

For Scott, ''Dooku: Jedi Lost'' was quite a hard story to write at some points, given that he and Lucasfilm talked about the ending quite a lot before even writing the beginning as they planned it. Scott's overall plan was looking at something that would hang the entire story from Dooku's early [[Youngling|childhood]] to the point where he left the [[Jedi Order]]. The depiction of Dooku's past and emotional side was meant for Scott to lead the audience speculate whether the architect of the story is Dooku or [[Darth Sidious]], showing that behind his independence and ego, Dooku was just a mere [[Slavery|slave]] of the mastermind behind the plan who naively believed himself to be the [[Chosen One]], making his story a tragedy. In developing Dooku's origins, aside rewatching the prequels and some episodes of ''The Clone Wars'', Scott was first given a list of potential things to include and some notes of the Story Group about which kind of areas of the ''Star Wars'' canon they could explore or whether the content was too dark or suitable. Among them, was Dooku's relationship with [[Sifo-Dyas]], a [[Jedi Knight]] who was mentioned in passing in the [[2002]] film [[Star Wars: Episode II Attack of the Clones|''Star Wars'': Episode II ''Attack of the Clones'']]. Scott also checked on the [[Dooku/Legends#Jedi (102–32 BBY)|Legends version]] of Dooku's origins.<ref name="TT Cavan Scott Interview">{{WebCite|url=https://tatooinetimes.com/interviews/dooku-jedi-lost-interview-with-cavan-scott|text=DOOKU: JEDI LOST – INTERVIEW WITH CAVAN SCOTT|author=Fabio Fiori|date=2019-06-27|work=[https://tatooinetimes.com ''Tatooine Times'']|archivedate=20200821132015}}</ref>

The only other major character from the films with a substantial role in the audiobook was [[Grand Master]] [[Yoda]], who had been established to have been Dooku's [[Jedi Master]] back during his days as [[Padawan]]. Scott didn't know if Yoda was the right character to spend more time with, feeling that he should just leave glimpses of Yoda throughout the story, but he opted to depict master and apprentice to have some kind of friendship.<ref name="TT Cavan Scott Interview" /> When writing Dooku's and Yoda's last scene, Scott knew it was going to be emotional but he didn't really expect it to be as it was, leading him to go through the scene so many times and struggle with Yoda's characterization in the audiobook.<ref name="What The Force Interview" /> For Asajj Ventress, Scott initially planned to tell the story through Ventress' point of view, but realized that Dooku wasn't that kind of person who would sit down and just tell his stories. Using Ventress meant for Scott an opportunity to offer her views on the things she was discovering about her [[Sith Master]], which is one of the reasons for which he believes that the audiobook's title refers to both Dooku and Ventress.<ref name="TT Cavan Scott Interview" /> Moreover, Scott illustrated the relationship between Dooku and Sifo-Dyas in a way the latter's place in the story is to remind Dooku that they are equal to downplay Dooku's growing arrogance.<ref name="Explores" />

In regards to new characters, Scott introduced [[House Serenno|Dooku's family]]&ndash;specifically his father [[Gora]], his brother [[Ramil]] and his sister [[Jenza]]&ndash;as a way to expand on Dooku's backstory.<ref name="Explores" /> In Scott's opinion, Jenza's purpose in the story was that of serving as Dooku's catalyst of his final embracement to the [[dark side of the Force]] and final test to prove his worth for Dooku and Ventress.<ref name="TT Cavan Scott Interview" /> In regards to [[Rael Averross]], Dooku's apprentice, Scott worked closely with [[Claudia Gray]], whose novel ''[[Master & Apprentice]]'', which was published before ''Jedi Lost'', featured Averross in a major role as well. Scott ultimately deemed such collaboration as particularly beneficial for their works.<ref name="Explores" /> The character of [[Lene Kostana]] was inspired by that of [[Thame Cerulian]] from the Legends continuity, as Scott didn't necessarily want to recanonize Cerulian to explore different ways and was intrigued at the idea of having two masters for Dooku.<ref name="TT Cavan Scott Interview" /> At one point during writing, Scott was asked by [[George Mann]] about the possibility of including a reference to [[Darth Caldoth]], a new [[Sith Lord]] that Mann had created for his then forthcoming ''[[Myths & Fables]]'' novel. Scott complied and a reference of Caldoth was put.<ref name="George Mann">{{SW|url=news/star-wars-myths-fables-george-mann-interview|text=''Star Wars: Myths & Fables'' Author George Mann On Writing Darth Vader and Other Legends}}</ref>

====2020 onward====
Next to his continued work on ''The High Republic'', Scott also wrote the stories read by the character [[AD-3]] to the contestants of the [[2020]] children's game show ''[[Star Wars: Jedi Temple Challenge]]''<ref name="Jedi Temple Challenge">{{SW|url=news/20-jedi-temple-challenge-easter-eggs|text=20 ''Star Wars: Jedi Temple Challenge'' Easter Eggs You Probably Didn't Notice}}</ref> and also wrote various other ''Star Wars'' stories. He returned to writing for Jaxxon with the ''[[Hyperspace Stories Annual—Jaxxon 2023]]'' comic one-shot<ref name="HS">{{SW|url=news/swce-2023-jaxxon|text=Holy Hutch! ''Star Wars: Hyperspace Stories Annual&mdash;Jaxxon 2023'' Revealed}}</ref> and horror themed comic anthologies with the [[2025]] ''[[Star Wars: Tales From the Nightlands]]'' series.<ref name="SDCC 2025">{{SW|url=news/star-wars-san-diego-comic-con-lucasfilm-publishing-panel-2025|text=SDCC 2025: Highlights from the Lucasfilm Publishing Panel}}</ref> Scott also wrote the 2025 [[Star Wars Adventures: Audible Originals|Audible-exclusive]] audiobook ''[[The Jaws of Jakku]]''<ref name="JoJ">''[[The Jaws of Jakku]]''</ref> for for the tenth anniversary of the film ''[[Star Wars: Episode VII The Force Awakens]]''.
