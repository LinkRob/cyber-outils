'''Daron Stinnett''' is a video game designer. He was the project leader on ''[[Star Wars: Dark Forces]]''.

==Biography==
===Early life===
Based in [[Wikipedia:Seattle|Seattle]], Stinnett taught himself programming during his school years creating video games. He had extensive programming experience (including the expansion pack for [[Wikipedia:Falcon 3.0|Falcon 3.0]]) by July [[1993]], when he was hired by [[LucasArts]] to develop a new ''[[Star Wars]]'' [[Star Wars video games|video game]].<ref name="DFguide">''[[Dark Forces Official Player's Guide]]''</ref>

===LucasArts===
Initially Stinnett thought about a flight simulator but turned towards a [[Wikipedia:First-person shooter|first-person shooter]], impressed by the revolutionary [[Wikipedia:Wolfenstein 3D|Wolfenstein 3D]]. As the genre was still an emerging trend, the team he assembled was based simply on talent than on experience. Along with [[Ray Gresko]] and [[Winston Wolff]], he started in late August to develop the [[Jedi Engine|3d engine]] of the game that would become ''Dark Forces''.<ref name="DFguide" />

As a project leader, Stinnett briefly attempted designing a level for the game, but was overwhelmed by the skills it required, namely architecture. It was when relating these problems to a friend in a movie theater when a stranger, overhearing the discussion, pointed out that [[Wikipedia:University of California, Berkeley|University of California, Berkeley]] had a program that "combined architecture and computers". Thus Stinnett hired architectural undergrads [[Ingar Shu]] and [[Matt Tateishi]].<ref name="DFguide" />

Eventually Stinnett found free time to play ''Dark Forces'', from start to finish, only after the release of the game.<ref name="DFguide" />

He and [[George Lucas]] promoted the game at [[Disneyland]] at the opening of the [[w:c:indianajones:Indiana Jones Adventure: Temple of the Forbidden Eye|Indiana Jones Attraction]] there (March 3, 1995).
