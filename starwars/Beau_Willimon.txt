'''Pack Beauregard Willimon''' is an American playwright and screenwriter. He is one of the writers for ''[[Star Wars: Andor]]''. He previously worked on [[Wikipedia:House of Cards (American TV series)|''House of Cards'']].

==Biography==
Beau Willimon was born in [[1977]].<ref name="Grown-up" /> He was one of the writers for the ''[[Star Wars: Andor]]'' television series.<ref name="Breaks down">{{SW|url=news/tony-gilroy-andor-season-1-interview|text=Tony Gilroy Breaks Down ''Andor'' Season 1}}</ref> He was excited to work with writer [[Tony Gilroy]] and be part of ''Andor'' and ''[[Star Wars]]''. He was immediately drawn to the show's strong vision and good writing.<ref name="Grown-up" /> In [[November]] [[2019]],<ref name="Vision to life">{{WebCite|url=https://collider.com/andor-beau-willimon-sanne-wohlenberg-interview|text='Andor's EP Sanne Wohlenberg and Writer Beau Willimon on Bringing Tony Gilroy's Vision to Life|author=Lovitt, Maggie|date=2022-11-10|work=[[Wikipedia:Collider (website)|''Collider'']]|archivedate=20221110211930}}</ref> the first writers' room session with Willimon, fellow writers [[Dan Gilroy]] and Tony Gilroy,<ref name="Breaks down" /> executive producer [[Sanne Wohlenberg]], and production designer [[Luke Hull]]<ref name="Vision to life" /> lasted five or six days.<ref name="Easter eggs">{{WebCite|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/tv/tv-features/andor-tony-gilroy-talks-luthen-rael-monologue-easter-eggs-1235258961|text=‘Andor' Creator Tony Gilroy Talks Luthen Rael's Future and Being Surprised by Certain Easter Eggs|author=Davids, Brian|date=2022-11-09|work=[[Wikipedia:The Hollywood Reporter|''The Hollywood Reporter'']]|archivedate=20221109220924}}</ref> Tony Gilroy brought an 80-page show bible and explained his general approach for the whole show and what he wanted to do with the [[Andor Season 1|first season]].<ref name="Grown-up" /> According to Gilroy, Willimon's skill was plotting and using a whiteboard.<ref name="Easter eggs" /> Willimon questioned what they were trying to do thematically,<ref name="Grown-up" /> and the writers always approached things from an emotional perspective.<ref name="Vision to life" /> Willimon and the writers aimed to create a character-based story that felt true. He questioned how one becomes a rebel and what sacrifices they are willing to make.<ref name="Grown-up" />

The writers knew they were doing the [[Narkina 5 Imperial Prison Complex|prison]]<ref name="Breaks down" /> on [[Narkina 5]],<ref name="Narkina 5">{{Andor|Narkina 5 (episode)}}</ref> and Gilroy was interested in doing a [[Narkina 5 prison escape|prison sequence]] that included [[Cassian Jeron Andor|Cassian Andor]] escaping.<ref name="Vision to life" /> It took the writers a couple days to figure out a unique concept for a prison.<ref name="Breaks down" /> The writers started with making the prison brightly lit and antiseptic so that it would be the opposite of a dark and gritty prison. They built from there to do something fresh. Willimon found the prison much more terrifying than one would expect. After deciding the prison would have an electrified factory floor where prisoners are building things,<ref name="Vision to life" /> the writers realized the guards would need to wear [[Tunqstoid protective boot|rubber boots]].<ref name="Breaks down" /> When the writers developed the characters that appear in the prison, they knew they wanted someone at the center that would be the face of the prison from the perspective of the prisoners, and they would go from trying to finish their shift to being a rebel. They wanted to cast an actor they could have an emotional attachment to.<ref name="Vision to life" />

Gilroy wanted to bring [[Ruescott Melshi|Melshi]] back, but Willimon and the writers only wanted to do it if it was necessary, organic, and emotionally touching. Someone at the meeting suggesting having Melshi be in the prison. In an early iteration of the story, the prisoners would steal the boots to escape.<ref name="Breaks down" /> Willimon said he does not remember if Loy's "I can't swim" line was discussed during the meeting or if it came during the outline and script phase, though he thinks they got to the line in the room. The writers made Loy unable to escape after leading a prison break because they wanted the most heartbreaking thing at the end of Loy's journey. From the beginning, Sanne Wohlenberg helped Willimon and the writers think about what could be practically achieved and made it so.<ref name="Vision to life" /> Willimon and the writers wanted the prison episodes to make Andor feel the oppression of the [[Galactic Empire]] in a serious, sustained, and inescapable way. He viewed Andor as a recruiter for [[Kino Loy]] in a similar way to [[Luthen Rael]].<ref name="Grown-up" />

At the meeting, Gilroy shared his extensive idea of what he wanted to do with [[Syril Karn]]. Willimon had fun discussing and writing about Karn because of his relationship with his mother,<ref name="Vision to life" /> [[Eedy Karn]].<ref name="Aldhani">{{Andor|Aldhani (episode)}}</ref> At the end of the meeting, Gilroy assigned episodes to writers.<ref name="Grown-up" /> According to Gilroy, Willimon probably got the prison break arc because he had not worked on action before.<ref name="Easter eggs" /> Willimon was pleased that he got to work on the prison episodes.<ref name="Grown-up" /> Willimon wrote the episodes "[[Narkina 5 (episode)|Narkina 5]],"<ref name="Narkina 5" /> "[[Nobody's Listening!]],"<ref name="Nobody's Listening!">{{Andor|Nobody's Listening!}}</ref> and "[[One Way Out]]."<ref name="One Way Out">{{Andor|One Way Out}}</ref> Willimon, Tony, and Dan brought in [[Tom Bissell]] to work as a writer for the [[Andor Season 2|second season]]. They all spent around seven days in the writers' room for the season.<ref name="Easter eggs" />
