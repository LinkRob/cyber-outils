A '''chariot''' was a component of the [[board game]] [[shogi]]. While aboard the [[light freighter]] ''[[Poor Crow]]'', the former [[Sith]] known as [[the Ronin]] and the former [[Jedi]] known as [[the Traveler]] played several games of shogi in which the Traveler always began cheating after the first few turns. During one game, the Traveler slid their chariot while the Ronin was not looking, putting them in a position in which their victory was assured.<ref name="Ronin" />

Later, the Ronin played shogi with his [[Gender|daughter]] [[Mirahi]] in [[Shinsui Temple]] on the [[planet]] [[Rei'izu]]. The table on which the board was placed existed simultaneously both within the temple's [[Kyber mirror of Shinsui Temple|kyber mirror]], where Mirahi sat, and outside of it, where the Ronin sat. At one point during the game, Mirahi moved a chariot while telling the Ronin she had expected a previous visitor to the temple to have been him, although it was actually the [[Jedi]] [[the Traveler|Idzuna]].<ref name="Ronin" />

==Behind the scenes==
A chariot appeared in the [[2021]] non-[[canon]] novel ''[[Ronin: A Visions Novel]]'', by [[Emma Mieko Candon]].<ref name="Ronin" /> In the real world, the rook is a piece in the [[Wikipedia:Japan|Japanese]] board game [[Wikipedia:Shogi|shogi]] that is known in [[Wikipedia:Japanese language|Japanese]] as ''hisha'' (飛車), meaning "flying chariot." It can be moved as many spaces as desired forward, backward, or horizontally.<ref name="Shogi">{{WebCite|url=https://ancientchess.com/page/play-shogi.htm|text=How to Play Shogi|work=[https://ancientchess.com anicentchess.com]|archivedate=20250126143047}}</ref>
