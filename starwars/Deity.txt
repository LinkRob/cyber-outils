A '''deity''',<ref name="Endor Bio Gallery">{{SW|url=endor-history-gallery|text=Endor History Gallery}}</ref> also called a '''god''' or '''goddess''', was a being of supernatural powers or attributes, believed in and worshipped by a people. When the primitive [[Ewok]]s of [[Endor]] encountered the golden [[protocol droid]] [[C-3PO]], they mistook him for a god and started worshipping him, despite it being against his programming to impersonate one.<ref name="Episode VI" /> Because of their immense powers, the [[Force wielder]]s of [[Mortis]] were considered to be god-like beings,<ref name="MortisDB">{{Databank|mortis|Mortis}}</ref> and [[Darth Sidious]] once referred to the "[[The Ones|Mortis gods]]" as deities.<ref name="Wolves and a Door">{{Rebels|Wolves and a Door}}</ref>

The [[Kaleesh]] [[captain]] [[Dumas Mar'Ti]] believed in a pantheon of beings she called her gods. According to her, her [[sin]]s were a matter between her and the gods.<ref name="After the Fall">{{StoryCite|story=After the Fall|book=The High Republic: Tales of Light and Life}}</ref> The [[Holy Mother]] was a deity spoken of in the [[Aakaash system]].<ref name="Vow">''[[The Vow of Silver Dawn]]''</ref> Upon seeing [[Asajj Ventress]] in gala [[Clothing|dress]], the enamoured [[Quinlan Vos]] thought she looked like "a goddess of love and war and hope and ecstasy."<ref name="Dark Disciple">''[[Dark Disciple]]''</ref> [[Count]] [[Dooku]] once referred to [[Florrum]] as a "godforsaken [[planet]]."<ref name="TCWGungan">{{TCW|The Gungan General}}</ref> The [[Church of the Force]] and the [[Acolytes of the Beyond]] worshipped aspects of [[the Force]] in lieu of a god.<ref name="TFA VD">''[[Star Wars: The Force Awakens: The Visual Dictionary]]''</ref><ref name="Aftermath">[[Aftermath (novel)|''Aftermath'']]</ref>

[[Trandoshan]]s idolized a goddess known as the [[Scorekeeper]]. For every kill a Trandoshan earned on a [[Hunting|hunt]], the Scorekeeper would reward them with a "[[Jagannath point]]."<ref name="Uprising">''[[Star Wars: Uprising]]''</ref> This system was used as a game of sorts, where Trandoshans would capture unique [[species]] from around [[the galaxy]] and bring them to [[Island Four]] of the [[moon]] [[Wasskah]], which rotated around the Trandoshan's [[homeworld]], [[Trandosha]],<ref name="Padawan Lost">{{TCW|Padawan Lost}}</ref> to see how well one another could appease the goddess when each of them were stretched to their limits.

The [[Delphidian]] [[pirate]] captain [[Sidon Ithano]] donned the [[Color|red]] Kaleesh [[Helmet|mask]]<ref name="Lost Treasure">"[[The Crimson Corsair and the Lost Treasure of Count Dooku]]"</ref> of the trickster god [[Ithano (god)|Ithano]].<ref name="Sinister Secret of the Binary Star">{{InsiderCite|234|Galactic Tales: The Crimson Corsair and the Sinister Secret of the Binary Star}}</ref> The [[Teedo]] worshipped a deity called [[R'iia]], an aggressive deity who would rain their anger down on the [[planet]] [[Jakku]]. They believed that R'iia brought upon the [[Battle of Jakku]], and crashed the [[Imperial-class Star Destroyer|Imperial Star Destroyers]] into the [[desert]]s. R'iia would supposedly breathe harsh [[sandstorm]]s onto the world, which the Teedo called ''[[X'us'R'iia]]''. R'iia was said to bring upon a never-ending storm that was spreading devastation across the world, and that all their anger was because of the sins the Teedo had committed.<ref name="BTA">''[[Before the Awakening]]''</ref> Sometimes, powerful Force users, like Sith, were viewed as gods.<ref name="Star Wars: The Rise of Skywalker: Expanded Edition">''[[Star Wars: The Rise of Skywalker: Expanded Edition]]''</ref>
