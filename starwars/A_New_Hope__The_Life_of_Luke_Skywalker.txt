'''''A New Hope: The Life of Luke Skywalker''''' is a young adult novel written by [[Ryder Windham]]. It was released in [[September]] [[2009]] and follows the same style as previous biographies such as ''[[The Rise and Fall of Darth Vader]]'' and ''[[The Life and Legend of Obi-Wan Kenobi]]''. A preview of ''[[Rebel Force: Target]]'' is included at the back of the book.

==Publisher's summary==
''Hidden away at birth, he would believe himself an orphan. Watched over by a mysterious hermit, he would discover incredible abilities. And by finding hope where others found only evil, he would restore balance to the galaxy''.

''This is the legendary story of Luke Skywalker, from his childhood on Tatooine to his fierce battle with his toughest enemy&mdash;himself.''

==Plot summary==
As the [[Alliance to Restore the Republic/Legends|Rebel Alliance]] brings aid to the ailing [[Planet/Legends|world]] of [[Aridus/Legends|Aridus]], [[Luke Skywalker/Legends|Luke Skywalker]] tries to engage his sister, [[Princess/Legends|Princess]] [[Leia Organa Solo/Legends|Leia]], in a discussion about their [[Parent/Legends|father]], [[Anakin Skywalker/Legends|Darth Vader]]. Leia refuses to dwell upon her distressing lineage, but Luke thinks back to the events of his life that have shaped him and wonders what events shaped Anakin. 

Luke's research uncovers Anakin's victorious [[Boonta Eve Classic/Legends|Boonta Eve Podrace]]. Luke returns to [[Tatooine/Legends|Tatooine]] and visits the old [[Mos Espa Grand Arena/Legends|Mos Espa Grand Arena]], interviewing two of the surviving Podracer [[Pilot/Legends|pilots]], [[Teemto Pagalies/Legends|Teemto Pagalies]] and [[Ody Mandrell/Legends|Ody Mandrell]]. Luke then visits [[Wald's Parts|Wald's junk shop]] (formerly [[Watto/Legends|Watto's]]) and learns that Anakin and [[Shmi Skywalker Lars/Legends|Shmi Skywalker]] were once [[Slavery/Legends|slaves]].

Luke's personal mission is cut short when an emergency distress call from [[Tarnoonga/Legends|Tarnoonga]] reports of a [[Gender/Legends|woman]] wielding a [[Lightsaber/Legends|lightsaber]] rescuing Rebel scouts. Luke rushes to a desolate island on the waterworld and encounters the mysterious woman – it is an old nemesis, the mind witch [[S'ybll]], who has lured Luke into a trap. Luke [[Death/Legends|kills]] S'ybll and reunites with his friends.

==Continuity==
This biography dramatized as flashbacks scenes previously presented in other media and bridged the narrative with new scenes as well. Young [[Luke Skywalker/Legends|Luke]] being stranded in the [[Tatooine/Legends|Tatooine]] [[Desert/Legends|desert]] with his friend [[Windy Starkiller|Windy]] comes from a story told by Luke in the ''[[Star Wars]]'' [[Wikipedia: National Public Radio|National Public Radio]] dramatization ([[1981]]) by [[Brian Daley]], which was later adapted as the comic ''[[Luke Skywalker's Walkabout]]'' by [[Phillip Norwood|Phil Norwood]], published by [[Dark Horse Comics]] ([[1999]]). It would also appear in ''[[The Life and Legend of Obi-Wan Kenobi]]'' ([[2008]]) by [[Ryder Windham]].<ref name="TERC" />

A [[Skyhopper/Legends|skyhopper]] race that nearly ends in tragedy is adapted from the [[Star Wars (1977)|Marvel Comics]] story "Crucible," published in [[Star Wars (1977) 17|''Star Wars'' #17]] ([[1978]]). Luke's daring skyhopper speed run, wherein he threads the stone needle – a natural monument in [[Beggar's Canyon/Legends|Beggar's Canyon]] – is from the first episode of the aforementioned radio drama. The flashbacks set between [[Star Wars: Episode IV A New Hope|Episode IV]] and [[Star Wars: Episode V The Empire Strikes Back|Episode V]] are largely drawn from "Iceworld" and "The Paradise Detour," two installments of the daily [[Star Wars (comic strip)|''Star Wars'' newspaper strip series]] ([[1981]]-[[1984|84]]), written by [[Archie Goodwin]] and illustrated by [[Al Williamson]]. The latter is where [[S'ybll]] first appears.<ref name="TERC" />
