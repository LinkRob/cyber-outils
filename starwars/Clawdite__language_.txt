'''Clawdite''' was a [[language]] spoken by the [[Clawdite]] [[bounty hunter]] [[Zam Wesell]]. After [[Gender|her]] second attempt to [[Plot to assassinate Senator Amidala|assassinate]] [[Naboo]] [[Senator]] [[Padmé Amidala Naberrie|Padmé Amidala]] failed, Wesell was struck in the neck by a [[Mk I Saberdart]] before she was able to reveal the name of her co-conspirator, [[Jango Fett]], to her [[Jedi]] captors. As she lay [[Death|dying]], she [[Changeling|reverted]] to her natural form and shifted to speaking Clawdite<ref name="YouTube" /> before she gave a final [[Sleemo|insult]] in [[Huttese]].<ref name="aotc" />

==Behind the scenes==
The Clawdite language first appeared in the [[2002]] [[prequel trilogy]] film [[Star Wars: Episode II Attack of the Clones|''Star Wars'' Episode II ''Attack of the Clones'']],<ref name="aotc" /> but wasn't named in the [[Canon]] ''[[Star Wars]]'' continuity until the [[2019]] ''Star Wars'' Kids [[Wikipedia:YouTube|YouTube]] video "Every Language in ''Star Wars''".<ref name="YouTube" /> The name originates from the [[Star Wars Legends|''Star Wars'' Legends]] continuity, where it was first used in the [[2003]] ''[[Star Wars Roleplaying Game]]'' supplement ''[[Ultimate Alien Anthology]]'', though Wesell's dialogue in ''Attack of the Clones'' was never labeled as Clawdite in Legends.<ref name="UAA">''[[Ultimate Alien Anthology]]''</ref>

According to sound designer [[Ben Burtt]] the word used by Wesell as she died was "Murishani",<ref name="Hidalgo Tweet Murishani">{{Twitter|pablohidalgo|status/735307566838743040|[[Pablo Hidalgo]]|quote=It's murishani. That's how Ben Burtt spells it. Couldn't tell you where the subtitles got it. Figure it's a transliteration.|nobackup=1}}</ref> which in both Legends
<ref name="phrase book">[[Galactic Phrase Book & Travel Guide (real-life book)|''Galactic Phrase Book & Travel Guide'']]</ref> and Canon is [[Huttese]] for "bounty hunter." However, [[De Agostini]]'s [[Star Wars Helmet Collection 39|''Star Wars Helmet Collection'' 39]]<ref name="Helmet Collection 39">{{HelmetCollectionCite|39|Helmets}}</ref> transcribes the same phrase as "Wee shahnit," which remains untranslated if considered Clawdite rather than Huttese.<ref name="YouTube" />
