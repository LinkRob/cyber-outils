A '''chain code''', also known as a '''chaincode''' or, in the [[Imperial Era|time of the Empire]], an '''Imperial I.D.''', was a type of identification marker that contained biographical information on an individual. Attuned to a subject's specific bio-metrics, these markers cataloged certain features of a person and their history. They were first introduced by the fledgling [[Galactic Empire]]'s [[Vice Admiral]] [[Edmon Rampart]] shortly after the [[Clone Wars]], and continued to be used by the [[New Republic]]. Chain codes were also commonly used by [[bounty hunter]]s, who used the markers in conjunction with devices known as [[tracking fob]]s to [[Hunting|locate]] their targets.

==Description==
{{Quote|What's the chain code?''"<br />"''We can only provide the last four digits.''"<br />"''Their age? That's all you can give me?|Din Djarin and an [[The Client|Imperial client]]|<ref name="Chapter 1" />}}
A type of identification marker,<ref name="POV 222">{{InsiderCite|222|A Certain Point of View}}</ref> chain codes identified<ref name="FACPOV Insider 201">{{InsiderCite|201|A Certain Point of View}}</ref> and described<ref name="POV 222" /> specific bio-metric details of an individual.<ref name="FACPOV Insider 201" /> They also described biographical information relating to the individual,<ref name="POV 222" /> including the subject's age,<ref name="Chapter 1" /> family history,<ref name="Chapter 14">{{TheMandalorian|Chapter 14: The Tragedy}}</ref> and criminal record.<ref name="Chapter 7" /> The chain code, also referred to as an Imperial I.D.,<ref name="Doctor Aphra 19" /> was made up of several numbers that displayed said information, with the final four digits signifying the subject's age.<ref name="Chapter 1" /> Since governments like the [[New Republic]] were known to run an individual's chain code upon securing passage on registered [[starship]]s, it was practice among criminals to lay low in order to avoid having the details of their codes shared.<ref name="Chapter 7" />

[[File:TrackingFobs-TMCh1.png|thumb|left|300px|Tracking fobs were designed to track their victims' chain code.]]
The information within these markers was used by the [[Bounty hunter|enforcers]] of the [[Bounty Hunters' Guild]], who were given access to their targets' codes upon accepting assignments. [[Hunting|Hunters]] made use of devices known as [[tracking fob]]s, short-range [[sensor]]s that were attuned to a code's specifics<ref name="The Star Wars Book" /> and data. A tracking fob allowed a [[hunter]] to zero in on a [[bounty]] and confirm the target's identity.<ref name="POV 222" /> It took an experienced hunter capable of networking for information and stalking prey to fully utilize a tracking fob.<ref name="100 objects">''[[Star Wars 100 Objects]]''</ref> After the hunter had obtained the target, [[Death|dead]] or alive, Guild operatives would run a check on the body to ensure that it matched to the information included within the chain code.<ref name="The Star Wars Book" />

In practice, the use of chain codes on a [[galaxy]]-wide scale, as they were implemented by the [[Galactic Empire]], enabled the reigning government to build up a database of everyone in [[the galaxy]]. In order to register citizens within the chain code network, public [[shuttle]] transport and conversion of old [[Galactic Republic]] [[Galactic Credit Standard|credits]] into Imperial [[currency]] was locked behind a chain code, forcing people to register. Chain codes were contained in [[Datacard|datadiscs]] and a programming device to make chain codes.<ref name="Cut and Run">{{TBB|Cut and Run}}</ref> The Empire established checkpoints known as [[Imperial Informational Station]]s in [[spaceport]]s throughout the galaxy to further this process.<ref name="Cornered">{{TBB|Cornered}}</ref><ref name="Cut and Run Episode Guide">{{SW|url=series/the-bad-batch/cut-and-run-episode-guide|text="Cut and Run" Episode Guide &#124; The Bad Batch}}</ref> During its early days, these codes were challenging, but not impossible, to forge, but this was made more difficult with the passage of time.<ref name="100 objects" /> Despite the [[Battle of Jakku|fall of the Empire]], the New Republic,<ref name="Chapter 7" /> the Bounty Hunters' Guild, and [[Gideon's Imperial remnant]] continued to make use of chain codes.<ref name="Chapter 1" /> As such, the Empire's usage of chain codes continued to have an effect into the [[New Republic Era]].<ref name="POV 222" />

==History==
===Imperial implementation===
{{Quote|It's ironic. Clones wanted names instead of numbers, yet now people are signing up to be given numbers.''"<br />"''It's ingenious. You could create a database to identify anyone in the galaxy.|[[Echo]] and [[Tech]] discuss the Empire's implementation of chain codes|<ref name="Cut and Run" />}}
[[File:Chain_code_Bad_Batch.png|thumb|right|220px|The Lawquanes' chain codes being submitted for inspection]]
Introduced by [[Vice Admiral]] [[Edmon Rampart]],<ref name="SWECGSWG" /> chain codes were used as a form of identification during the [[Imperial Era]]. During the early [[Standard day|days]] of the Galactic Empire<ref name="Cut and Run" /> in [[19 BBY]],<ref name="Cut and Run Date" /> citizens of the newly formed regime were required to register for an Imperial I.D. in order to book passage off world or to convert their Republic credits into Imperial credits.<ref name="Cut and Run" />
Many went along with it not realizing that the Empire was cataloging its own population in order to track their movements, their background, and their everyday activities. Many believed that such information was safe in the hands of their new benevolent government, but some would come to regret that when it was used against them in various trumped-up legal charges.<ref name="Rise and Fall">''[[Star Wars: The Rise and Fall of the Galactic Empire]]''</ref>

Due to the new rule, [[Cloning|clone]] [[Desertion|deserter]] [[Cut Lawquane]] and his [[Lawquane family|family]] had to receive help from [[Clone Force 99]] to forge chain codes for them so they could book passage off of [[Saleucami]].<ref name="Cut and Run" /> Clone Force 99 would later forge multiple chain codes to trade with the [[Vanguard Axis]] only for the deal to fall through as a result of an altercation with the [[Wookiee]] [[Padawan]] [[Gungi]].<ref name="Tribe">{{TBB|Tribe}}</ref> During the early years of the Empire, a [[Unidentified human (Cina system)|human]] in the [[Cina system]] claimed to possess hundreds of legitimate chain codes that they offered to clients in search of safe passage. The [[crime lord]] [[Maul]] took advantage of this, killing the human<ref name="Shadow of Maul 1">[[Shadow of Maul 1|''Shadow of Maul'' 1]]</ref> and using their [[Unidentified starship (Maul)|ship]] and chain codes to infiltrate the [[Mid Rim Territories|Mid Rim]] planet [[Janix]].<ref name="Shadow of Maul 2">[[Shadow of Maul 2|''Shadow of Maul'' 2]]</ref>

With the passage of time, chain codes became more difficult to forge.<ref name="100 objects" /> Around [[16 BBY]],<ref name="16 BBY note">{{MandoDate|14}} In "Chapter 14: The Tragedy," [[Boba Fett]] states in that his chain code was encoded into [[Boba Fett's armor|his armor]] twenty-five [[Standard year|years]] prior to the events of the chapter. Therefore, Boba's chain code was encoded into his [[armor]] around the year [[16 BBY]].</ref> the chain code of bounty hunter [[Boba Fett]] was encoded into a set of [[Mandalorian armor]] formerly worn by Boba's [[Parent|father]], [[Jango Fett]]. This code was written in the [[Mando'a]] [[Mandalorian (writing system)|script]] and contained information on not only the Fetts but [[Jaste|Jango's mentor]] as well.<ref name="Chapter 14" /> During the Imperial reign, [[Bothan]]s forged false chain codes that were implanted into [[Aqualish]] by [[Nautolan]] doctors. There were huge empty strings of chain codes within Imperial administration files. The only information attached to these files explained that the chain codes were initially attached to an individual, and then were later rendered obsolete with the tag "subject purged, code redundant."<ref name="Rise and Fall" />

As the Empire hoped, the usage of chain codes permeated throughout society, and an Imperial I.D. became a requirement to perform many tasks,<ref name="SWECGSWG" /> including those not directly overseen by the galactic government. Registration within private learning institutes, for example, could not be completed without verifying an applicant's chain code. This practice caused trouble at the [[University of Bar'leth]] for incoming [[Alcedian]] student [[Kho Phon Farrus]], whose name at the time did not match the one listed on their I.D. The issue was smoothed over by another student, [[Chelli Lona Aphra]], who convinced the [[Unidentified Gran registrar|registrar]] that the discrepancy was a clerical error.<ref name="Doctor Aphra 19" /> Criminal organizations, such as [[Crimson Dawn]], made use of chain codes as well, requiring their agents to present their I.D.s before boarding the syndicate's [[flagship]], the [[Vermillion (Crimson Dawn)|''Vermillion'']].<ref name="Bounty Hunters 24">[[Bounty Hunters 24|''Bounty Hunters'' 24]]</ref> Not all chose to heed Imperial precedent, however, as establishments like the [[Garkata Fighting Arena]] of [[Nar Shaddaa]] declined to verify their participants' chain codes.<ref name="Star Wars 13">[[Star Wars (2020) 13|''Star Wars'' (2020) 13]]</ref>

===Under the New Republic===
{{Quote|Chain code?''"<br />"''I have a gift for the boss.''"<br />"''Chain code?|Scout trooper AP-1982 and Greef Karga|<ref name="Chapter 7" />}}
[[File:ChainCode-TMCh14.png|thumb|left|220px|Boba Fett's chain code was embedded in his armor written in Mando'a.]]
Despite the practice being rolled out by the Empire,<ref name="Cut and Run" /> the New Republic continued to make use of chain codes during its [[New Republic Era|reign]].<ref name="Chapter 7" /> In [[9 ABY]],<ref name="Mandalorian Date">{{MandoDate|1}}</ref> the [[Mandalorian]] bounty hunter [[Din Djarin]] was [[Hunt for Grogu|hired]] by an [[Gideon's Imperial remnant|Imperial remnant]] to acquire the [[Force-sensitive]] [[Youngling|infant]] [[Din Grogu|Grogu]]. The chain code provided to Djarin was fractured, only pertaining to the child's age, though the hunter [[Mission to Arvala-7|located]] the infant on the [[planet]] [[Arvala-7]] after also being given Grogu's last known positioning.<ref name="Chapter 1" /> Later, Djarin's ally, [[Magistrate]] [[Greef Karga]], was prevented from entering a [[Nevarro City|city]] on the planet [[Nevarro]] without first presenting his chain code to a [[AP-1982|persistent scout trooper]]. Karga turned over a small card, which the [[scout trooper]] scanned with a rod-like device.<ref name="Chapter 7" />

[[Carasynthia Dune|Cara Dune]], a former [[shock trooper]] of the [[Alliance to Restore the Republic]], committed several crimes following the [[Galactic Civil War]] that carried life sentences should she be apprehended by the New Republic. Knowing the consequences of having her chain code run through official channels, she resolved to lay low on [[Outer Rim Territories|Outer Rim]] worlds, like [[Sorgan]] to avoid prosecution.<ref name="Chapter 7" /> After [[Clash on Nevarro|liberating Nevarro]] of Imperials with Djarin and Karga, the magistrate offered Dune a position as his enforcer, promising to take care of whatever "clerical concerns" she had regarding her chain code.<ref name="Chapter 8">{{TheMandalorian|Chapter 8: Redemption}}</ref> After taking up Karga's offer, Dune went on to become a deputized [[marshal]] of the New Republic.<ref name="Chapter 14" />

Many [[Standard year|years]] after his chain code was first encoded into his [[armor]], the [[mercenary]] Boba Fett sought to reclaim [[Boba Fett's armor|his father's equipment]], which was by that point in the hands of Din Djarin. Djarin was strongly opposed to returning the armor to Fett, as the [[Mandalorian Creed]] dictated that it rightfully belonged to the Mandalorian people. In order to convince the hunter that his claim to the armor was justified, Fett showed his chain code to Djarin on the planet [[Tython]], proving to the man that Fett was of Mandalorian heritage.<ref name="Chapter 14" />

==Behind the scenes==
Chain codes were first mentioned within the [[Chapter 1: The Mandalorian|first episode]] of the ''[[Star Wars: The Mandalorian]]'', a [[Disney+]] television series created by [[Jon Favreau]].<ref name="Chapter 1" /> The codes are mentioned several times throughout the [[The Mandalorian Season One|first season]], later appearing onscreen through a [[hologram]] of Boba Fett's code in the [[Chapter 14: The Tragedy|sixth episode]] of the show's [[The Mandalorian Season Two|second season]].<ref name="Chapter 14" /> Chain codes function similarly to real-world [[Wikipedia:Social Security number|Social Security numbers]] in the [[Wikipedia:United States|United States]].<ref name="Cut and Run Episode Guide" />
