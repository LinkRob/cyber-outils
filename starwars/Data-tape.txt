'''Data-tape''',<ref name="TRB">''[[The Rebellion Begins]]''</ref> also spelled as '''datatape''',<ref name="DiD">[[Droids in Distress (book)|''Droids in Distress'' chapter book]]</ref> or '''data tape''',<ref name="Rogue One novel">[[Rogue One: A Star Wars Story (novelization)|''Rogue One: A Star Wars Story'' novelization]]</ref> was a type of physical storage media for different kinds of data. The tape itself, a continuous narrow, flexible strip, was stored on reels inside a protective shell. Monomolecular-switching binary tapes like the ones used by the [[Galactic Empire]] on [[Scarif]] had a 512-million-[[exanode]] capacity.<ref name="ROUVG">''[[Star Wars: Rogue One: The Ultimate Visual Guide]]''</ref>

==History==
The [[Death Star plans|secret plans]] for the Galactic Empire's powerful [[planet]]-destroying [[DS-1 Orbital Battle Station|Death Star]] weapon were stored on data-tapes. These tapes were [[Battle of Scarif|stolen]] and later downloaded into a [[datacard]] by members of the [[Alliance to Restore the Republic|Rebel Alliance]] and spirited to [[Princess]] [[Leia Skywalker Organa Solo|Leia Organa]], who copied the data from them into the memory banks of the [[droid]] [[R2-D2]] in order to deliver them to [[Jedi Master]] [[Obi-Wan Kenobi]] on [[Tatooine]].<ref name="Episode IV" />

{{Tech-stub}}

==Behind the scenes==
The plans for the Death Star were described as being recorded on data-tapes in the [[Star Wars: A New Hope (novelization)|novelization]] of the original ''[[Star Wars]]'' film, released in [[1976]] before the release of the actual film, [[Star Wars: Episode IV A New Hope|''A New Hope'']], known at the time only as ''Star Wars''. At the time, tapes were a modern means of storing information, though for data storage they later became largely obsolete.
