'''Anthony Daniels''' (born [[February 21]], [[1940s#1946|1946]])<ref name="This Week! Ep4" /> is an actor who portrayed and voiced the [[droid]] [[C-3PO]] in the ''[[Star Wars]]'' films. He is the only actor to be credited to appear in all nine ''Star Wars'' episodic films, as well as ''[[Solo: A Star Wars Story]]'', ''[[Rogue One: A Star Wars Story]]'', ''[[Star Wars: The Mandalorian and Grogu]]'' and the animated feature [[Star Wars: The Clone Wars (film)|''Star Wars: The Clone Wars'']].

==Biography==
===The character of C-3PO===
[[File:ADC3PO2.jpg|thumb|left|270px|Daniels posing with the pieces of his C-3PO costume from the original trilogy]]
During the production of the [[Star Wars: Episode IV A New Hope|original ''Star Wars'' film]], Daniels helped [[George Lucas]] develop the character, voice, and mannerisms of C-3PO. Aside from playing the fussy droid in all nine movies, Daniels has reprised the role frequently over the past three decades. He's donned the shiny suit for various promotional work, including hosting ''[[The Making of Star Wars]]''; for appearances on ''[[The Muppet Show]]'' and ''[[Sesame Street]]''; for ''[[The Star Wars Holiday Special]]''; for commercials including [[Kenner Products|Kenner]] toys, an [[Star Wars Anti-Smoking PSA|anti-smoking PSA]], and even a breakfast cereal based on the character; and for [[prequel trilogy|prequel]] tie-ins such as ''Star Wars Connections'' and ''The Science of Star Wars''. He also voiced C-3PO in a [[Star Wars (radio)|radio drama]] based on the [[original trilogy]]; for the Christmas-themed ''[[Christmas in the Stars]]'' album; for the [[The Walt Disney Company|Disney]] theme-park attraction [[Star Tours/Legends|''Star Tours'']]; for the animated film ''The Lego Movie''; and for animated series like [[Star Wars: Droids: The Adventures of R2-D2 and C-3PO|''Droids'']], ''[[Star Wars: Clone Wars]], ''[[Star Wars: The Clone Wars|''The Clone Wars'']], and ''[[Star Wars Rebels]]''. He provided both narration and all character voices for the ''[[Dark Force Rising]]'' and ''[[The Last Command]]'' audio books.

===Other roles===
Daniels has a cameo outside of the C-3PO costume in the [[Outlander Club|Outlander nightclub]] scene early in [[Star Wars: Episode II Attack of the Clones|''Star Wars'': Episode II ''Attack of the Clones'']] as a character named [[Dannl Faytonni]].<ref name="Episode II">{{Film|II}}</ref> Faytonni can also be seen at the [[Galaxies Opera House]] in [[Star Wars: Episode III Revenge of the Sith|''Star Wars'': Episode III ''Revenge of the Sith'']].<ref name="Episode III">{{Film|III}}</ref> He later played the enslaved worker [[Tak]] in ''[[Solo: A Star Wars Story]]''.<ref name="Solo">''[[Solo: A Star Wars Story]]''</ref>

Aside from portraying C-3PO and Dannl Faytonni, Anthony Daniels also played numerous minor characters such as [[CZ-3]] in ''A New Hope''. The exact number of such characters Daniels played is unknown.

===Further ''Star Wars'' and C-3PO work===
He was the only cast member of the original trilogy to voice his character in all three episodes of National Public Radio's dramatizations of that trilogy. (While [[Mark Hamill]] voiced [[Luke Skywalker]] for both ''A New Hope'' and ''The Empire Strikes Back'', and [[Billy Dee Williams]] voiced [[Landonis Balthazar Calrissian|Lando Calrissian]] for ''The Empire Strikes Back'', when the ''Return of the Jedi'' adaptation was recorded many years later, Hamill and Williams were replaced by [[Joshua Fardon]] and [[Arye Gross]], respectively.)

Daniels contributed the foreword to the collected scripts of the ''Return of the Jedi'' radio drama, as their author, [[Brian Daley]], died just as the episodes were being recorded. Daniels' other ''[[Star Wars]]'' writings include [[The Wonder Column|''The New Wonder Column'']] for ''[[Star Wars Insider]]'' magazine and a [[Star Wars Legends|Legends]] comic-book adventure for [[C-3PO/Legends|C-3PO]] and [[R2-D2/Legends|R2-D2]] entitled ''[[The Protocol Offensive]]'', published by [[Dark Horse Comics]].

In ''The Phantom Menace'', Daniels did the voice work for C-3PO but did not physically appear in the movie, as the character was a puppet instead of a costume&mdash;a prototype version of the protocol droid controlled by someone else. Daniels controlled the puppet for 2002's ''Attack of the Clones'', but all prototype scenes were cut, with the droid's first appearance in the movie being Daniels in costume. In this movie and 2005's ''Revenge of the Sith'', he also dubbed the vocal tracks for some scenes that used computer-generated imagery.

[[File:DesertPowerLeibovitz.jpg|thumb|right|300px|Anthony Daniels once again played C-3PO in the desert heat for ''The Rise of Skywalker''.]]
Daniels was the host and narrator of [[Star Wars: In Concert]].

Daniels has returned to voice C-3PO in numerous animated projects,<ref name="White Witch">{{DroidsCite|The White Witch}}</ref><ref name="CW 15">{{CW|15}}</ref> including ''Star Wars: The Clone Wars''. Due to his years of experience with the droid, Daniels was allowed to go through scripts before voice recording on ''The Clone Wars'' with Supervising Director [[Dave Filoni]]. During these sessions, Daniels was allowed to suggest changes to dialogue to ensure it fit with C-3PO's personality. To Filoni, such changes gave the audience a more authentic performance from a legacy actor.<ref name="Evil Plans Featurette">{{SW|url=video/evil-plans-episode-featurette|text="Evil Plans" Episode Featurette}}</ref>

In 2019, Daniels published an autobiography, ''[[I Am C-3PO: The Inside Story]]''.<ref name="SW-From the Pages of Star Wars Insider: Anthony Daniels on His New Memoir and Journey as C-3PO">{{SW|url=news/anthony-daniels-star-wars-insider|text=From the Pages of ''Star Wars Insider'': Anthony Daniels on His New Memoir and Journey as C-3PO}}</ref>
