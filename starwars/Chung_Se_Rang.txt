{{Quote|My motto became, 'live like an X-wing pilot.' Even when they are breaking apart and no one remembers them, they keep going and fight for good.|Chung Se Rang|<ref name="FilmmakerFocus">{{VisionsFocus|Journey to the Dark Head}}</ref>}}
'''Chung Se Rang''', or '''Chung Serang''' ([[Wikipedia:Korean language|Korean]]: 정세랑), (born in [[1984]] in [[Wikipedia:Seoul|Seoul]], [[Wikipedia:South Korea|South Korea]]), is a writer and the screenwriter of [[Studio Mir]]'s "[[Journey to the Dark Head]]," the fifth short film of the [[Star Wars: Visions Volume 2|second volume]] of the ''[[Star Wars: Visions]]'' non-[[canon]] animated anthology television series. She became a fan of the ''[[Star Wars]]'' franchise after viewing the film [[Star Wars: Episode I The Phantom Menace|''Star Wars'': Episode I ''The Phantom Menace'']] while attending middle school, and eventually became inspired by the resilience displayed by ''Star Wars'' [[starfighter]] [[pilot]]s. When Chung, who by then was an acclaimed writer, became the screenwriter for "Journey to the Dark Head," the film's production manager [[Lee Soo Kyung]] was excited to work with her.

Chung's writing process of "Journey to the Dark Head" was inspired by the South Korean [[Wikipedia:Tripitaka Koreana|''Tripiṭaka Koreana'']] collection of [[Wikipedia:Buddhist canons|Buddhist scriptures]], and the development of the character [[Toul]] was informed by reflection on other [[Jedi]] characters in ''Star Wars'' who were undergoing [[Jedi training]]. The writer also suggested that the [[clothing]] worn by the characters in the film be based on historical Korean attire.

==Biography==
===The road to ''Star Wars''===
{{Quote|When I was in middle school, ''Star Wars'': Episode I ''The Phantom Menace'' was in theaters. I remember going to see it with my friends after school. Ever since then, I loved the series.|Chung Se Rang|<ref name="FilmmakerFocus" />}}
[[File:EPI_TPM_poster.png|thumb|right|250px|Chung Se Rang became a fan of ''Star Wars'' after seeing ''Star Wars'': Episode I ''The Phantom Menace''.]]
Chung Se Rang,<ref name="JDH" /> or Chung Serang (written in [[Wikipedia:Korean language|Korean]] as 정세랑), was born in [[1984]] in [[Wikipedia:Seoul|Seoul]], [[Wikipedia:South Korea|South Korea]]. When she<ref name="LTI" /> was attending middle school, Chung and her friends went to see<ref name="FilmmakerFocus" /> the [[1999]] [[prequel trilogy]]<ref name="YearByYear">''[[Star Wars Year By Year: A Visual History, New Edition]]''</ref> film [[Star Wars: Episode I The Phantom Menace|''Star Wars'': Episode I ''The Phantom Menace'']] at a film theater. Since that time she developed a love for the ''[[Star Wars]]'' franchise, formulating the motto, "Live like an [[X-wing starfighter|X-wing]] pilot," based on the perceived resilience the franchise's [[starfighter]] [[pilot]]s display in continuing to fight for good in the face of overwhelming odds and probable [[death]] in [[Space warfare|combat]].<ref name="FilmmakerFocus" /> Chung became a writer, with her writing usually exploring settings that feature violence as an everyday occurrence, yet in which the characters ultimately find hope.<ref name="LTI" />

According to [[Lee Soo Kyung]], the production manager of "[[Journey to the Dark Head]]"<ref name="StudioMirRadioTalk1">{{YouTube|video=N8ZGEgeqZbU|text=(ENG) Studio Mir Radio Talk With Star Wars Visions Volume 2 Crew Part. 1|channel=@StudioMIR2010|name=Studio Mir|date=2023-05-19}}</ref>&mdash;the fifth short film of the [[Star Wars: Visions Volume 2|second volume]] of the ''[[Star Wars: Visions]]'' non-[[canon]] animated anthology television series, which was produced by<ref name="SWCE 2023">{{SW|url=news/swce-2023-star-wars-visions|text=SWCE 2023: ''Star Wars: Visions'' Volume 2 Trailer, Key Art, and Cast Revealed}}</ref> the South Korean<ref name="Studios">{{SW|url=news/star-wars-visions-volume-2|text=''Star Wars: Visions'' Volume 2 Release Date, Studios Revealed}}</ref> [[Studio Mir]] and directed by [[Hyeong Geun Park]]<ref name="JDH" /> and which premiered on [[Disney+]] on [[May 4]], [[2023]]<ref name="SWCE 2023" />&mdash;securing a "top-notch" screenwriter was critical in order to make the most out of the studio's opportunity to tell their own ''Star Wars'' story. At the time, Lee was already aware of Chung's love for ''Star Wars'' as well as her experience in the [[Wikipedia:Science fiction|science fiction]] genre; however, the former was concerned whether it would be possible to negotiate for the latter to become the screenwriter for the film due to Chung by then already being a highly renowned writer.<ref name="StudioMirRadioTalk1" />

===The writing process===
{{Quote|At an age when one's personality has yet to be developed, they see some extreme things, so I wanted to create a character who was lost in fear and skepticism.|Chung Se Rang, on Jedi-in-training and the development of the character Toul|<ref name="FilmmakerFocus" />}}
Nevertheless, Chung did become the screenwriter, responding "very positively" to Lee and visiting the studio. The production manager was very excited to be working with the writer, with the film's producer [[Choi Go Un]] later humorously noting that the wearing of [[Wikipedia:Face masks during the COVID-19 pandemic|face masks]] that was required during the [[Wikipedia:COVID-19 pandemic|COVID-19 pandemic]] allowed Lee to conceal her wide smiling during Chung's visit.<ref name="StudioMirRadioTalk1" /> Lee also later expressed the humorous idea of sending Chung a stuffed doll of the character [[Ara]] if she ever made one, which was an idea she conceived of immediately upon seeing the design for the character.<ref name="Bloopers">{{YouTube|video=w9IcehkHfNU|text=[Exclusive] Journey to the Dark Head BTS Featurette Bloopers!|channel=@StudioMIR2010|name=Studio Mir|date=2023-07-07}}</ref>

[[File:TripitakaKoreana.jpg|thumb|left|300px|In creating the planet Dolgarak, Chung Se Rang was inspired by the real-life ''Tripiṭaka Koreana'' (pictured) in South Korea.]]
Per Lee, the writing process of "Journey to the Dark Head" was fast and smooth-going, with her noting an instance where members of the production team sent in their notes and received a reply from Chung within ten minutes.<ref name="StudioMirRadioTalk1" /> In writing the film, Chung was particularly inspired by the [[Wikipedia:Tripitaka Koreana|''Tripiṭaka Koreana'']],<ref name="FilmmakerFocus" /> a collection of more than eighty thousand 13th-century [[Wikipedia:Woodblock printing|wooden blocks]] engraved by [[Wikipedia:Buddhist canons|Buddhist scriptures]] that is stored at the [[Wikipedia:Haeinsa|Haeinsa]] temple in South Korea,<ref name="TripitakaBritannica">{{WebCite|url=https://www.britannica.com/topic/Haein-Temple|text=Haein Temple &#124; South Korea, Buddhist, & Architecture|author=The Editors of Encyclopædia Britannica|date=2024-03-29|work=[[Wikipedia:Encyclopædia Britannica|''Encyclopædia Britannica'']]|archivedate=20250717073731}}</ref> with the [[Dolgarak temple|temple]] on the [[planet]] [[Dolgarak]] in the finished film also featuring a [[library]] of [[wood]]en blocks with [[writing]] on them.<ref name="JDH" /> Similarly to the film's characters, Chung gave Dolgarak a Korean name, with the [[Wikipedia:Hangul|Korean alphabet]] character [[Wiktionary:돌|돌]] in the planet's name (Korean: 돌가락)<ref name="StudioMirRadioTalk1" /> meaning "rock, stone."<ref name="CollinsRock">{{WebCite|url=https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/korean-english/%EB%8F%8C|text=English Translation of &ldquo;&#46028;&rdquo;|work=[https://www.collinsdictionary.com Collins Online Dictionary]|archiveurl=https://archive.fo/cWEya}}</ref>

[[File:Toul_Visions.png|thumb|right|250px|The writer developed the character Toul (pictured) based on reflection on other Jedi-in-training seen in ''Star Wars'' films.]]
In developing the character [[Toul]], Chung considered that the "nameless" [[Jedi]]-in-[[Jedi training|training]] seen in ''Star Wars'' films probably experienced upsetting events while they were still young enough that their own personality had yet to be fully developed. She wanted Toul to be a character overwhelmed with skepticism and fear. Even though all Jedi exhibited sensitivity to the [[Light side of the Force|light]] and [[Dark side of the Force|dark sides]] of [[the Force]], Chung characterized Toul as being particularly sensitive in that respect, with the two aspects of the mystical energy field being an agonizing presence for him. Furthermore, the writer expressed her belief that it was this heightened sensitivity that caused the character [[Bichan]] to have an interest in Toul. Overall, associate producer [[Lee Seung Wook]] noted that Chung presented a charming story about good and evil that nodded to [[Wikipedia:Yin and yang|yin and yang]]<ref name="FilmmakerFocus" />&mdash;an [[Wikipedia:Eastern philosophy|East Asian philosophical]] concept concerning opposite but complementary forces<ref name=YinYangBritannica">{{WebCite|url=https://www.britannica.com/topic/yinyang|text=Yinyang &#124; Definition, Meaning, & Facts|author=The Editors of Encyclopædia Britannica|date=2025-10-22|work=[[Wikipedia:Encyclopædia Britannica|''Encyclopædia Britannica'']]|archivedate=20250803025934}}</ref>&mdash;while also incorporating "all the basic elements" of ''Star Wars''.<ref name="FilmmakerFocus" />

===Praise===
{{Quote|For this story to be realized, so many people poured their love and their hearts into it to bring it to completion. Personally, every scene is meaningful and incredibly rich. I think this episode encompasses so many things. I hope you watch it twice.|Chung Se Rang|<ref name="FilmmakerFocus" />}}
[[File:ForceOracles-JourneyToTheDarkHead.png|thumb|left|300px|Chung Se Rang suggested the use of costumes in "Journey to the Dark Head" (depicted) based on historical Korean ones.]]
Studio Mir's art director [[Kang You Tae]] praised Chung for suggesting that "Journey to the Dark Head" utilize [[Clothing|outfits]] based on those of the historical era of [[Wikipedia:Goguryeo|Goguryeo]],<ref name="StudioMirRadioTalk1" /> a Korean kingdom that existed until the 7th century.<ref name=GoguryeoBritannica">{{WebCite|url=https://www.britannica.com/place/Goguryeo|text=Goguryeo &#124; Kingdom, Map, Korea, & History|author=The Editors of Encyclopædia Britannica|date=2025-03-07|work=[[Wikipedia:Encyclopædia Britannica|''Encyclopædia Britannica'']]|archivedate=20251004100602}}</ref> The screenwriter found the fact that Studio Mir was able to create an entirely new planet for the film "satisfying."<ref name="FilmmakerFocus" />

Chung also thought that the [[bomb]]s seen in "Journey to the Dark Head," the design of which was inspired by a common Korean [[Wikipedia:Larimichthys polyactis|fish product]], were adorable<ref name="StudioMirRadioTalk1" /> and looked great on screen. When asked to name [[Wikipedia:Hashtag|hashtags]] that would be the best-suited for "Journey to the Dark Head," she suggested <nowiki>#ComingOfAge</nowiki>, <nowiki>#KoreanStyle</nowiki>, and <nowiki>#NeverGiveUp</nowiki>.<ref name="Bloopers" /> The writer noted her view that every scene of "Journey to the Dark Head," which explored a variety of topics, was meaningful and rich, and she expressed the hope that the viewer would watch the film at least twice.<ref name="FilmmakerFocus" />
