{{Quote|We droids think in binary. One and zero, on and off, yes and no. This gives us clarity, makes us happy. Things are either real or not real, known or not known. A thinking individual is either an organic or a droid. Simple, right?|[[Zeta]]|<ref name="The Legends of Luke Skywalker" />}}
'''Binary''',<ref name="The Legends of Luke Skywalker" /> also known as '''droidspeak'''<ref name="TFA VD">''[[Star Wars: The Force Awakens: The Visual Dictionary]]''</ref> or '''astromech''',<ref name="Brotherhood">[[Brotherhood (novel)|''Brotherhood'']]</ref> was the [[language]] commonly spoken by [[droid]]s. It consisted of a set of beeps and whistles strung together into what resembled sentences.<ref name="AEYNK">''[[Star Wars: Absolutely Everything You Need to Know]]''</ref> The [[C1-series astromech droid]] utilized this form of communication,<ref name="TRB">''[[The Rebellion Begins]]''</ref> as did [[Industrial Automaton]]'s [[R2-series astromech droid]].<ref name="AEYNK" /> While difficult, some organics such as [[Anakin Skywalker]],<ref name="DotD">{{TCW|Duel of the Droids}}</ref> [[Hera Syndulla]],<ref name="DUME">{{Rebels|DUME}}</ref> [[Kazuda Xiono]],<ref name="Bucket's Quest">{{Resistance|Bucket's Quest}}</ref> [[Cal Kestis]],<ref name="FO">''[[Star Wars Jedi: Fallen Order]]''</ref> and [[Rey Skywalker|Rey]] were able to understand it.<ref name="TFA">{{Film|VII}}</ref> After [[Standard decade|decades]] of missions with [[R2-D2]], [[Luke Skywalker]] was reasonably fluent in droidspeak.<ref name="Expanded Edition">''[[Star Wars: The Last Jedi: Expanded Edition]]''</ref>

The most common way for binary droids to communicate with [[Species|organics]] was by writing, conveyed via another [[computer]] system. This is how [[astromech droid]] [[Pilot|copilots]] would speak with the pilot of a [[starfighter]].<ref name="TPM">{{Film|I}}</ref><ref name="ESB">{{Film|V}}</ref>

All droids could understand binary. Without a computer system to write their words when it was necessary for organics to hear what a binary-only droid had to say, another droid, such as [[protocol droid]]s or others capable of speaking a common language like [[Galactic Basic Standard]], could translate for them.<ref name="AEYNK" /> The astromech [[BB-8]] spoke in [[27th generation droidspeak]], a compressed variant of standard droidspeak.<ref name="TFA VD" /> [[Poe Dameron]], his long-time companion, was able to understand him without any need for translation.<ref name="Before the Awakening">''[[Before the Awakening]]''</ref>

Binary was also used as a programming language for devices that did not have the artificial intelligence of droids, such as binary loadlifters and the binary brain unit of [[moisture vaporator]]s.<ref name="ANH">{{Film|IV}}</ref><ref name="CL">[[Star Wars: Complete Locations (2016)|''Star Wars: Complete Locations'']]</ref>

==Behind the scenes==
{{Quote|I devised a means of playing the music synthesizer at the same time as I recorded my voice. In addition, I had my voice automatically trigger electronic sounds and simultaneously shape their envelope. When I vocalized and played the keyboard, I could get a close synchronization with my voice and the elctronic tones. If my voice rose in pitch, the electronic would shape itself to conform.|[[Ben Burtt]], sound designer of {{film|4}}|<ref name="guide">[[Galactic Phrase Book & Travel Guide (real-life book)|''Galactic Phrase Book & Travel Guide'']]</ref>}}
Droidspeak in the original films consisted of sound effects by sound designer [[Ben Burtt]], in order to give nonverbal speech to [[R2-D2]] in {{film|4}}. Initially, the screenplay indicated only where R2 would beep and whistle without any nuance of meaning. During filming, Artoo (operated by [[Kenny Baker]]) did not produce any sounds, but an assistant would say "beep-beep" to cue the actors.<ref name="guide" /> 

At first Burtt experimented with producing electronic sounds of different tones with [[Wikipedia:Moog synthesizer|Moog]] and [[Wikipedia:ARP Instruments|ARP]] synthesizers. In order to show Artoo's personality, intelligence and emotions, Burtt added some mechanical sounds which he performed himself; whistling through a plumbing tube, or interacting metal with frozen carbon dioxide, producing gas with various pitches as it melted.<ref name="guide" /> 

Inspired from Lucas, Burtt decided that "babytalk" communicates emotions and babies's needs by intonation even without words. Failing to catch useful sounds from actual babies, Burtt ended up recording himself delivering baby sounds (at that time he utilized Lucas's basement as an editing room). Those samples, sped up to higher pitch, were intercut with the electronic tones, but the final effect was achieved by timing the two accordingly and meld them into one voice.<ref name="guide" />

Ben Burtt wrote a mock script for Artoo; although these lines did not appear subtitled on screen, and is not [[canon]]ical, it aided Burtt to design and add sounds that fit the emotional and informational context of the scene and is consistent with the other characters's dialogue. For example, in the first scene of ''A New Hope'', Artoo is trying to calm down Threepio, while looking for a safe place; then trying to tell him about his mission.<ref name="guide" />

In 2024, Microsoft created a language called Droidspeak to streamline communication between artificial intelligence. The language was named after its ''Star Wars'' counterpart.<ref>{{BookCite|author=Sparkes, M.|title=AIs converse better in own language.|publisher=Scientific American|date=2023-11-23}}</ref>
