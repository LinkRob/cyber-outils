{{Quote|George Lucas' version of the future felt real. It felt worn out a little bit ''[&hellip;]'' Part of the idea for me with the erosions and the crystals is this notion that the works kind of float in time, right? We don't really know when exactly they're from. Part of this sense comes about through the feeling that the works are falling apart, but they also contain crystals which we associate with growth. So there's a question for the audience of whether they're falling apart or actually growing together. Could they be moving towards a kind of completion of the form?|Daniel Arsham|<ref name="SW.com">{{SW|url=news/daniel-arsham-sideshow-star-wars|text=Daniel Arsham Turns ''Star Wars'' Icons Into Future Relics}}</ref>}}
'''Daniel Arsham''' is a visual artist who designed crystallized relic sculptures of ''[[Star Wars]]'' pop culture icons, including [[C-3PO]], [[R2-D2]]&mdash;his favorite character&mdash;and [[Han Solo]] [[Carbon-freezing|frozen]] in [[carbonite]], in [[2023]]. The statues were produced as white cast resin collectibles in collaboration with [[Sideshow Collectibles|Sideshow]] and [[Lucasfilm]], and took reference from Sideshow's one-to-one scale models.<ref name="SW.com" /> Prior to the collectibles, Arsham had sculpted life-sized versions of ''Star Wars'' characters, including R2-D2<ref name="Instartoo">{{Instagram|username=danielarsham|url=p/CxDX739uhZL|name=Daniel Arsham|date=11-09-2023|archiveurl=https://archive.ph/faJgM}}</ref> C-3PO,<ref name="Perrotin">{{WebCite|url=https://leaflet.perrotin.com/view/577/20-ans|text=20 Ans|work=[https://perrotin.com Perrotin Gallery]|archivedate=20230920101143}}</ref> and a [[stormtrooper]]<ref name="Instorm">{{Instagram|username=danielarsham|url=p/Cxnxlo_O6KC|name=Daniel Arsham|date=25-09-2023|archiveurl=https://archive.ph/1kvyt}}</ref> that debuted at his Perrotin Gallery exhibitions in [[Wikipedia:New York City|New York]] and [[Wikipedia:Paris|Paris]] in [[September]], 2023 using minerals such as<ref name="Perrotin" /> amethyst,<ref name="Instorm" /> quartz, blue calcite, selenite, and hydrostone. The sculptor focused on crafting fictional archeological artifacts<ref name="Perrotin" /> to explore the concepts of erosion and growth.<ref name="SW.com" />
