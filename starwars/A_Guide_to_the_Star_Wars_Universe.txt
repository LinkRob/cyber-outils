'''''A Guide to the Star Wars Universe''''' is a reference book that was compiled by [[Raymond L. Velasco]] and published by [[Ballantine Books]] in [[December]] [[1984]]. It details the locations, inhabitants, and technology of [[The galaxy/Legends|the galaxy far, far away]] in approximately 951 alphabetized entries. It was later followed by [[A Guide to the Star Wars Universe, Second Edition, Revised and Expanded|second]] and [[A Guide to the Star Wars Universe, Third Edition, Revised and Expanded|third editions]].

==Contents==
The entries within ''A Guide to the Star Wars Universe'' are divided into a series of categorized lists to help readers find specific names or terms. The entries within the main body of the book are alphabetized with no further categorization. They can be short and to the point, as in the two-word entry for "[[Hydrospanner/Legends|hydrospanner]]." The longest entries are about a page and a half. The subjects of these long entries are usually main characters and battles.

Every entry is followed by one or more codes that indicate the source of the information contained in the entry. A list of source materials and their individual codes are provided at the front of the book. Where appropriate, an entry directs readers to other entries relevant to the subject that it covers. This is the sole function of approximately 67 entries within the book. Forty-three entries are accompanied by a picture of their subject, and 20 entries include a pronunciation guide. The entry for the "[[Behavioral circuitry matrix/Legends|Behavioral Circuitry Matrix]]" includes a diagram.

===Perspective===
''A Guide to the Star Wars Universe'' was written at a relatively early time in [[Star Wars#History|the history of ''Star Wars'']]. In contrast to more recent reference books that have been presented as [[in-universe]] works, certain entries within ''A Guide to the Star Wars Universe'' contain references to the real world.

For example, [[Orron III]] is described as "an [[Earth/Legends|Earthlike]] agricultural world." However, the most common examples of this occur in the entries for the [[Alliance to Restore the Republic/Legends|Rebel]] pilots at the [[Battle of Yavin/Legends|Battle of Yavin]], which point out differences between [[Star Wars: A New Hope (novelization)|the novelization]] and [[Star Wars: Episode IV A New Hope|the film]] in the pilots' [[Callsign/Legends|call signs]]. Additionally, [[Luke Skywalker/Legends|Luke Skywalker's]] entry names the three films of the [[original trilogy]] and mentions that he is their central character.

The book categorizes each entry into one or more of the eleven broad categories below. The one term that has an entry in the main body but is not categorized in the lists is [[Medpac/Legends|medipak]]. The synonymous term [[Medpac/Legends|medikit]] is in the category lists, followed by the parenthetical "(see ref.)" Although many entries in the main body are merely pointers to other entries, this is the only one so marked in the category lists.

==Development==
The illustrator of ''A Guide to the Star Wars Universe'' was uncredited in the book. Though [[June Brigman]] was credited in ''[[The Secrets of Star Wars: Shadows of the Empire]]'' for illustrations that had appeared in ''A Guide to the Star Wars Universe'' and she was acknowledged for her work in helping prepare the [[A Guide to the Star Wars Universe, Second Edition, Revised and Expanded|second edition of ''A Guide to the Star Wars Universe'']], there was no such acknowledgment in the first edition.

After Velasco turned in the manuscript of ''A Guide to the Star Wars Universe'', [[Wikipedia:Judy-Lynn del Rey|Judy-Lynn del Rey]] asked [[Brian Daley]] to review it and correct any errors that he found. Daley later claimed in an interview conducted by fan [[Alex Newborn]] that he had filled up two to three pages with errors that he had found in the manuscript relating to the movies, his novels and a number of other sources. Velasco had also related subjects that were never intended to be such. For example, Daley had never intended the character [[Squeak]] to be a [[Tintinna/Legends|Tin-Tin Dwarf]].<ref>{{WebCite|url=http://www.myuselessknowledge.com/swfa/daley.html|text=A STAR WARS Fan Interviews: Brian Daley|author=Newborn, Alex|archivedate=20190812065508}}</ref>

==Continuity==
Though he acknowledged that ''A Guide to the Star Wars Universe'' was not perfect, [[Abel G. Peña]] saluted Velasco for what he considered a "[[Corusca gem/Legends|corusca gem]] in the rough."<ref>{{JCFCite|url=posts/18192604|board=Authors and Artists|thread=The Official Wizards of the Coast RPG Designers Thread|user=Halagad_Ventor|date=2004-05-29|time=1:22 pm|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20150908120257/http://boards.theforce.net/threads/the-official-wizards-of-the-coast-rpg-designers-thread.15457767}}</ref> In an essay about the first edition, Peña cited the diagram of the Behavioral Circuitry Matrix and other examples in the book as reasons Velasco should be considered "the father of the [[Retroactive continuity|ret-con]] in the ''Star Wars'' Universe" and "''Star Wars''{{'}} very first fanboy author."<ref>{{WebCite|url=http://www.myuselessknowledge.com/swfa/rejects1.html|text=Rejects of the Star Wars Universe 1.0|author=Peña, Abel G|archivedate=20191026232214}}</ref>

It appears that the only continuity errors that made it into the published edition of ''A Guide to the Star Wars Universe'' had to do with the physical descriptions of the [[Bounty hunter/Legends|bounty hunters]] [[Bossk/Legends|Bossk]] and [[Zuckuss/Legends|Zuckuss]]. This may indicate a reliance upon the [[Star Wars: The Empire Strikes Back (novelization)|novelization of ''The Empire Strikes Back'']] for certain details. In addition, a few entries were misspelled (e.g., "Galandro" instead of "[[Gallandro/Legends|Gallandro]]," and "Sabador" instead of "[[Sabodor]]").

It is also questionable whether some entries should have been entries at all, as they were either standard English words with no different meaning in the ''Star Wars'' universe (e.g., "[[Wikipedia:erg|erg]]"), or phrases consisting of words that in combination carry no meaning beyond that implied by standard English construction (e.g., "external audio pickup").

The entry for [[T'iil]] describes it as a "flowering planet" instead of a plant. This typographical error is corrected in the [[A Guide to the Star Wars Universe, Second Edition, Revised and Expanded|Second Edition]].
