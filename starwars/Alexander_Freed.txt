'''Alexander Freed''' is a writer of ''[[Star Wars]]'' [[Star Wars comic books|comics]], novels and [[Star Wars video games|video game]]s.

==Biography==
===''The Old Republic''===
====Writing the Imperial Agent====
{{Quote|Alex was right about the direction that it needed to go, and he turned in one of the most amazing scripts I've ever seen.|Daniel Erickson, about Freed's take on the Imperial Agent storyline in ''Star Wars: The Old Republic''.|<ref name="TOR art and making">''[[The Art and Making of Star Wars: The Old Republic]]''</ref>}}
Alexander Freed started his writing career in [[2002]] as a published short fiction author,<ref name="Bibliography" /> and was a freelance tabletop [[roleplaying game]] designer until August [[2006]], when he joined<ref name="LinkedIn" /> [[BioWare]] Austin as a writer on the [[Star Wars video games|video game]] ''[[Star Wars: The Old Republic]]''.<ref name="SWTOR comics and novels" /> The game started pre-production a year earlier, and [[Daniel Erickson]], its lead writer, was gathering a team of writers to flesh out the setting and the story of the game, centered around the storylines of eight unique class of characters. Freed went on to be the main writer for the one of those class: the [[Cipher Nine|Imperial Agent]], after he refined the initial draft from a character meant to echo the notorious movie spy [[Wikipedia:James Bond|James Bond]], to one more serious and faced with difficult choices, such as the characters from the TV action series ''[[Wikipedia:24|24]]'' and the spy thriller [[Wikipedia:The Bourne Identity (2002 film)|''Bourne Identity'']], while still retaining some aspects from classic spy movies. It was also the only class not to be based on one of characters of the ''[[Star Wars]]'' movies.<ref name="TOR art and making" />

As a Senior Writer and Managing Editor,<ref name="Lost Suns Now" /> Freed also took part in the creation of large amounts of non-class specific content, and helped Erickson manage the team of writers.<ref name="Bibliography" /> Additionally, he wrote three articles for the game's official website, about how to write something within the ''Star Wars'' universe,<ref name="SWTOR SW universe" /> an introduction to the Imperial Agent,<ref name="Top Secret">{{TORweb|url=news/blog/20100101_001|text=Top Secret: Dossier on the Imperial Agent!}}</ref> and how the game was depicted in several comics and novels.<ref name="SWTOR comics and novels" /> When the game was released on [[December 20]], [[2011]],<ref name="Global Launch">{{TORweb|url=news/news-article/20111014-1|text=Global Launch for The Old Republic}}</ref> he was credited as Sub-Lead Writer.<ref name="SWTOR credits">''[[Star Wars: The Old Republic]]'' credits</ref>

====Foray into comics====
While working on the game, Freed also wrote two comics ''The Old Republic'' miniseries for [[Dark Horse Comics]]: ''[[Star Wars: The Old Republic, Blood of the Empire]]'' and ''[[Star Wars: The Old Republic—The Lost Suns]]''. ''Blood of the Empire'', with art by penciler [[Dave Ross]], inker [[Mark McKenna]] and colorist [[Michael Atiyeh]], was initially released in twelve parts, each seven pages,<ref name="Bibliography" /> as a free webcomic on the game official website, between [[April 23]]<ref name="BotE part 1">{{TORweb|url=news/news-article/20100423_001|text=Blood of the Empire Released!}}</ref> and [[September 24]], in [[2010]].<ref name="BotE part 12">{{TORweb|url=news/news-article/20100924-0|text=Blood of the Empire #12!}}</ref> Unfamiliar with the pacing of such a short serialized narrative, Freed prepared himself by looking into weekly comics such as [[Wikipedia:2000 AD (comics)|''2000 AD'']].<ref name="Bibliography" /> ''Blood of the Empire'' follows the adventures of [[Darth Thanaton|Tenel Keb]] and how he became Darth Thanaton.<ref name="BotE">''[[Star Wars: The Old Republic Volume 1: Blood of the Empire]]''</ref> The story is directly connected with the [[Darth Nox|Sith Inquisitor]] and [[The Barsen'thor|Jedi Consular]] storylines of the video game, while also exploring the culture of the [[Sith Empire (Post–Great Hyperspace War)|Sith Empire]].<ref name="SWTOR comics and novels" /> The comic was reedited in three physical issues during the last semester of 2010,<ref name="BotE issue 1">{{DarkHorse|url=Comics/17-273/Star-Wars-The-Old-Republic-4-Blood-of-the-Empire-part-1|text=Star Wars: The Old Republic #4&mdash;Blood of the Empire part 1}}</ref><ref name="BotE issue 2">{{DarkHorse|url=Comics/17-274/Star-Wars-The-Old-Republic-5-Blood-of-the-Empire-part-2|text=Star Wars: The Old Republic #5&mdash;Blood of the Empire part 2}}</ref><ref name="BotE issue 3">{{DarkHorse|url=Comics/17-275/Star-Wars-The-Old-Republic-6-Blood-of-the-Empire-part-3|text=Star Wars: The Old Republic #6&mdash;Blood of the Empire part 3}}</ref> and then in a trade paperback edition on [[February 2]], 2011.<ref name="BotE TPB">{{DarkHorse|url=Books/16-390/Star-Wars-The-Old-Republic-Volume-1----Blood-of-the-Empire-TPB|text=Star Wars: The Old Republic Volume 1 -- Blood of the Empire TPB}}</ref>

''The Lost Suns'', his second comic miniseries, with art by pencilers [[George Freeman]], Dave Ross and [[David Daza]], inker Mark McKenna and colorist Michael Atiyeh,<ref name="Bibliography" /> was released in five physical issues between [[June 3]],<ref name="Lost Suns Now" /> and [[October 12]], in 2011.<ref name="TLS 5">{{DarkHorse|url=Comics/17-777/Star-Wars-The-Old-Republic-The-Lost-Suns-5|text=Star Wars: The Old Republic—The Lost Suns #5}}</ref> They were later collected into a trade paperback edition released on [[March 21]], [[2012]].<ref name="TLS TPB">{{DarkHorse|url=Books/17-779/Star-Wars-The-Old-Republic-Volume-3-The-Lost-Suns-TPB|text=Star Wars: The Old Republic Volume 3: The Lost Suns TPB}}</ref> Contrary to previous ''The Old Republic'' comics, this story is set concurrently with the events of the game, and was described by Freed as a "ninth story" that run parallel to the eight class storylines. The comics follows [[Theron Shan]], a republic spy and a new character, notably absent from the base game.<ref name="SWTOR comics and novels" />

====Last days at BioWare====
As ''The Old Republic'' was releasing near the end of 2011, BioWare Austin kept working on the game, providing content updates,<ref name="SWTOR Rise">{{TORweb|url=info/news/press-release/20120201|text=The Old Republic™ Continues Meteoric Rise}}</ref> but also planing on an expansion: ''[[Star Wars: The Old Republic: Rise of the Hutt Cartel]]'', announced on [[December 12]], 2012.<ref name="RotHC Announced">{{TORweb|url=info/news/press-release/20121218|text=BioWare Announces Rise of the Hutt Cartel, the First Digital Expansion for ''Star Wars''™: The Old Republic™}}</ref> Freed was the Lead Writer for most of the development of the expansion, and was responsible for managing the writing team, but also heavily contributed to content, especially for the Imperial storyline on [[Makeb]].<ref name="Bibliography" /> While ''Rise of the Hutt Cartel'' was still in development, and after being a writer at Bioware for six years, Freed left the company of his own volition on [[October 5]], 2012, to pursue a freelancer career.<ref name="Twitter Freelance 1">{{Twitter|AlexanderMFreed|status/254249187422662656|Alexander Freed|date=2012-10-05|quote=Big news! As of today, I'm departing BioWare to go freelance. It's been a wonderful six years, but time to move on. More next tweet. #SWTOR|archivedate=20230118152710}}</ref><ref name="Twitter Freelance 2">{{Twitter|AlexanderMFreed|status/254249310655479808|Alexander Freed|date=2012-10-05|quote=This move is entirely my choice--I love BioWare and TOR, but it was time to face new challenges. I hope some of you will follow along.|archivedate=20191016095342}}</ref> On the next day, He participated in the "Star Wars Reads Day" event.<ref name="Twitter Read Day>{{Twitter|AlexanderMFreed|status/254414484477734912|Alexander Freed|date=2012-10-05|quote=Star Wars Reads Day is tomorrow! I'll be at Barnes & Noble with a ton of other folk [...] #SWTOR #StarWars|archivedate=20191016095337}}</ref>

Before leaving BioWare, Freed left another mark on ''The Old Republic'' with "[[The Last Battle of Colonel Jace Malcom]]," a short story published in [[Star Wars Insider 137|''Star Wars Insider'' 137]] on [[October]] [[2012]], focusing on [[Jace Malcom]].<ref name="Insider 137">{{TitanMagazines|url=t/star-wars-insider/us/137|text=Star Wars Insider Issue #137}}</ref> Set after the events of the game, it is meant as an introduction to [[Drew Karpyshyn]]'s ''[[The Old Republic: Annihilation]]'',<ref name="Bibliography" /> released on [[November 13]], [[2012]],<ref name="Amazon Annihilation">{{InsiderCite|137|Blaster}}</ref>, and features Theron Shan, as well as [[Teff'ith]],<ref name="Annihilation">''[[The Old Republic: Annihilation]]''</ref> characters first developed by Freed in ''The Lost Stars''.<ref name="Bibliography" />

When ''The Rise of the Hutt Cartel'' released on [[April 14]], [[2013]],<ref name="RotHC release date">{{TORweb|url=info/news/news-article/20130325|text=Digital Expansion: Rise of the Hutt Cartel Launches April 14}}</ref> Freed was credited as Principal Writer, with [[Charles Boyd]] and [[Hall Hood]] replacing him as co-Lead Writers.<ref name="RotHC credits">''[[Star Wars: The Old Republic: Rise of the Hutt Cartel]]'' credits</ref> Looking back at ''The Old Republic'', Freed as said to be immensely proud to have worked on such project along with a very talented team, and that he fells enormously gratified for having taken part in something that meant so much for so many fans. He also added to be proud for having been part in the addition of the some of the first LGBT characters in ''Star Wars'' through his work on ''Rise of the Hutt Cartel''.<ref name="Bibliography" />

===A freelancer life for me===
Freed expressed interest to write a third comic story during an interview conducted before the release of ''The Lost Suns''.<ref name="Lost Suns Now" /> While he wasn't offered the opportunity to write a third ''The Old Republic'' comics, Dark Horse Comics kept working with him on another franchise: [[Wikipedia:Dragon Age|''Dragon Age'']]. However, Freed soon returned to ''Star Wars'', this time with a two-part comic series: ''[[Star Wars: Purge: The Tyrant's Fist]]'', with art by pencilers/inkers [[Marco Castiello]] and [[Andrea Chella]], and colorist Michael Atiyeh,<ref name="Bibliography" /> and released on [[December 5]], 2012,<ref name="Purge 1">{{DarkHorse|url=Comics/22-231/Star-Wars-Purge-The-Tyrants-Fist-1|text=Star Wars: Purge—The Tyrant's Fist #1}}</ref> and [[January 2]], 2013.<ref name="Purge 2">{{DarkHorse|url=Comics/22-232/Star-Wars-Purge-The-Tyrants-Fist-2|text=Star Wars: Purge—The Tyrant's Fist #2}}</ref> Set in the continuance of previous ''Purge'' comics, one-shot stories all focused on the earliest days of the [[Galactic Empire/Legends|Galactic Empire]] while [[Anakin Skywalker/Legends|Darth Vader]] is on a quest to hunt down the remnant of the [[Jedi Order/Legends|Jedi Order]], Freed took this opportunity to explore how the cultural prevalence and the rich history of the Jedi disappeared from the galactic stage along with them.<ref name="Purge Blog">{{DarkHorse|url=Blog/1214/star-wars-friday-why-writing-darth-vader-different|text=Star Wars Friday: Why Writing Darth Vader is Different by Alexander Freed}}</ref> All ''Purge'' stories were later condensed into a single trade paperback edition, released on [[July 10]], 2013.<ref name="Purge TPB">{{DarkHorse|url=Books/22-245/Star-Wars-Purge-TPB|text=Star Wars: Purge TPB}}</ref>

Freed later went on to write the [[2015]] [[Del Rey]] novel ''[[Battlefront: Twilight Company]]'', a tie-in to the video game ''[[Star Wars Battlefront]]'' which released that same year.<ref name="BTCannouncement">{{SW|url=news/star-wars-battlefront-twilight-company-novel-coming-in-november|text=''Star Wars Battlefront: Twilight Company'' Novel Coming in November}}</ref> That same year also saw the release of the video game ''[[Star Wars: Uprising]]'', which he also wrote for. After that he wrote for the video game ''[[Star Wars Battlefront II]]''<ref name="Battlefront II Tweet">{{Twitter|@AlexanderMFreed|status/1590081121014149121|[[Alexander Freed]]|quote=My involvement in ME:A was pretty minor and didn't touch any of the real story points, though I had a lot of friends in the trenches at the studio. BF2 was all on the multiplayer side--thousands of wacky droid lines, mission briefings, shouting stormtroopers, etc.!|archiveurl=https://archive.md/o9Sza}} &mdash; [[Alexander Freed]] talks about his involvement with ''Battlefront II''.</ref>
as well as authoring the short stories "[[Contingency Plan]]"<ref name="Contingency">{{StoryCite|story=Contingency Plan|book=From a Certain Point of View}}</ref> and "[[The Man Who Built Cloud City]]"<ref name="TMWBCC">{{StoryCite|story=The Man Who Built Cloud City|book=From a Certain Point of View: The Empire Strikes Back}} {{1st}}</ref> for the respective short story collections ''[[From a Certain Point of View]]'', released in [[2017]],<ref name="Contingency" /> and ''[[From a Certain Point of View: The Empire Strikes Back]]'', released in [[2020]].<ref name="TMWBCC" /> Around the same time, he also wrote the ''[[Star Wars: Alphabet Squadron]]'' trilogy,<ref name="Polygon">{{WebCite|url=https://www.polygon.com/star-wars/2021/2/23/22294554/star-wars-victorys-price-alphabet-squadron-book-read-preview|text=A new Star Wars trilogy comes to an end in an exclusive preview of Victory's Price|author=[[Alexander Freed]]|date=2021-02-23|work=[[Wikipedia:Polygon (website)|Polygon]]|quote=Read an excerpt of the new and final Alphabet Squadron novel|archivedate=20211102144304}}</ref> the first volume, [[Alphabet Squadron (novel)|''Alphabet Squadron'']], releasing in [[2019]],<ref name="Alphabet Squadron">[[Alphabet Squadron (novel)|''Alphabet Squadron'']]</ref> the second, ''[[Shadow Fall: An Alphabet Squadron Novel]]'', released in 2020,<ref name="Shadow Fall">''[[Shadow Fall: An Alphabet Squadron Novel]]''</ref> and the third, ''[[Victory's Price: An Alphabet Squadron Novel]]'', which was released in [[2021]].<ref name="Victory's Price">''[[Victory's Price: An Alphabet Squadron Novel]]''</ref> He provided some minor contributions<ref name="Freed Comment 2">{{Bluesky|alexandermfreed.bsky.social|post/3ln74jf6evc2f|[[Alexander Freed]]|date=2025-04-19|quote=(Seriously, I do not want to overstate my contributions in any way. But it's a neat project and I love a good turn-based tactics game--and they've got a top-tier team of designers, writers, artists, cinematics folk, and so forth. I'm looking forward to playing it.)|archiveurl=https://archive.ph/aS8nK}}</ref> to ''[[Star Wars Zero Company]]'' early in its development.<ref name="Freed Comment 1">{{Bluesky|alexandermfreed.bsky.social|post/3ln73j6e7bk25|[[Alexander Freed]]|date=2025-04-19|quote=Pleased that Star Wars: Zero Company has been announced at last! I had the privilege of working a bit with Bit Reactor early in the project and am excited to see where it's all going. Wonderful team, full of talented and caring people... wishing them the best.|archiveurl=https://archive.ph/5FPkh}}</ref> He also wrote ''[[Reign of the Empire: The Mask of Fear]]'', the first book in the ''[[Star Wars: Reign of the Empire]]'' trilogy.<ref name="MOF">''[[Reign of the Empire: The Mask of Fear]]''</ref>
