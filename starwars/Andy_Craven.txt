'''Andy Craven''' is a former video game developer who founded [[Vektor Grafix]], a [[Wikipedia:United Kingdom|United Kingdom]]-based computer game development company. The company's first products were ports of ''[[Star Wars: The Arcade Game]]'' and [[The Empire Strikes Back (arcade game)|''The Empire Strikes Back'']] for home computers.

==Biography==
Before Craven designed video games, he was a rock musician, touring Europe with various bands. He often played on British and American military bases. Eventually he stopped touring and got married.<ref name="Crash">{{BookCite|author=|container=A Rock Star Ate My Computer|title=[[Wikipedia:Crash (magazine)|Crash]]|number=No. 64|publisher=[[Wikipedia:Newsfield|Newsfield]]|date=1989-05|link=https://web.archive.org/web/20240328191654/https://worldofspectrum.org/archive/magazines/pages/crash/64/53}}</ref>

Craven taught himself computer programming in the 1980s, as was the norm in that era. He eventually became an instructor in programming qualifications in [[Wikipedia:Leeds|Leeds]], where he met [[Daniel James Gallagher]]. Craven and Gallagher founded [[Vektor Grafix]] in [[June]] [[1987]], hiring [[Ciaran Gultnieks]] as the first employee. Within a month, the three men won a contract to port ''[[Star Wars: The Arcade Game]]'' to three period home computers: the [[Wikipedia:ZX Spectrum|ZX Spectrum]], [[Wikipedia:Commodore 64|Commodore 64]], and [[Wikipedia:BBC Micro|BBC Micro]].<ref name="Crash" />

The results were satisfactory, and Vektor was given the contract for the next game in the series, [[The Empire Strikes Back (arcade game)|''The Empire Strikes Back'']]. The company underwent a number of personnel changes at that point and Gallagher departed.<ref name="Crash" />
