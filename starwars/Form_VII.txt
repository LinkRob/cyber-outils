'''Form VII''', known by its two primary disciplines of '''Juyo''' and '''Vaapad'''; and also known as '''The Ferocity Form''', was the seventh form of [[lightsaber]] [[Lightsaber combat|combat]]. It was considered the most aggressive and unpredictable form. The Juyo variant was prevalent among [[dark side adept]]s such as [[Maul|Darth Maul]], a [[Sith Lord]], and [[The Grand Inquisitor]], an [[Inquisitorius]] member. Meanwhile, the Vaapad variant was established by [[Jedi Master]] [[Mace Windu]] with help from fellow [[Jedi]] [[Sora Bulq]].

==Description==
===Form VII: Juyo===
[[File:DowutinJedi-KoF.png|thumb|left|250px|A Dowutin Juyo Berserker]]
Although Juyo was designated Form VII, its development began [[Millennium|millennia]] before it was formalized. In fact, Juyo likely originated in techniques rejected from [[Form I|Shii-Cho]] in the earliest days of the [[Jedi Order]], techniques considered too aggressive and dangerous. In the long [[Standard century|centuries]] following, the methods that would come to constitute Juyo were sometimes practiced openly, sometimes in secret. Eventually, the Ferocity Form gained recognition by the [[Jedi High Council]], only to be banned little more than a century later.<ref name="KoF" />

Juyo was an intensely aggressive form, even more so than [[Form IV]]. What truly differentiated it from other [[lightsaber]] [[Lightsaber combat|forms]] was the emotional state it fostered and even required. Form VII was known as the Ferocity Form with good reason; not only did Juyo utilize a highly aggressive offense, but it required the practitioner to actively draw upon their anger and negative emotions to fuel the relentless assault.<ref name="KoF" />

Unsurprisingly, Form VII was controversial from its inception, and many saw its practice as a fundamental violation of the [[Jedi Code]]'s strictures against passion and chaos. It is likely that the Jedi Council recognized the form only because of the desperate nature of the time in which it arose, during a [[resurgence of the Sith]] approximately four [[Millennium|thousand]] [[Standard year|years]] before the [[Clone Wars]]. For decades, Juyo saw significant use among the Jedi and their ancient enemies as the two orders of [[Force-sensitive|Force users]] battled. Yet, by the end of this great war against the Sith, many Jedi who practiced Form VII had fallen to the dark side or come perilously close. In the aftermath of the conflict, with the reckoning clear, the Jedi Council forbade the study of Juyo. Over the following millennia, this prohibition gradually relaxed to an extent. Still, Form VII would never again be practiced by Jedi save those who received express permission from the council or who defied the council's will.<ref name="KoF" />

To maintain the onslaught of Form VII without exhausting oneself, giving the enemy an opening, or sacrificing accuracy, a practitioner had to channel [[the Force]] with every movement and strike. This focus was essentially what makes Juyo so dangerous to the enemy, but it also posed a danger to the warrior. Because Form VII drew on a negative emotional state, drawing so heavily and continuously on the Force brought the practitioner perilously close to the [[dark side of the Force]].<ref name="KoF" />

===Form VII: Vaapad===
[[File:MaceWindu-TCWForceWarriors.png|thumb|right|250px|Mace Windu, creator of Vaapad]]
The sole Form VII variant to have gained recognition by the Jedi Council, Vaapad, was only created in the final [[Standard decade|decades]] of the Jedi Order. The key architect of Vaapad was [[Jedi Master]] [[Mace Windu]],<ref name="KoF" /> who developed the form alongside his friend [[Sora Bulq]].<ref name="Abyss">''[[Mace Windu: The Glass Abyss]]''</ref> Windu created the form to address his weakness by controlling his inner darkness and channeling it into worthy ends. For this purpose, he refined advances from the preceding centuries and, in the minds of some, finally perfected Form VII as a true lightsaber form in line with the tenets of the Jedi Code.<ref name="KoF" />

An extremely difficult form of lightsaber combat,<ref name="Depa Card">{{ForceCollection|Depa Billaba|star=3}}</ref> only a handful of Jedi trained in Vaapad before the enactment of [[Order 66]] and the [[Great Jedi Purge|destruction]] of the Jedi Order. Practitioners of Vaapad drew on their anger and passion but never gave into them. Nonetheless, compared with the other lightsaber forms, which directed warriors to master their emotions, Vaapad's approach was dangerous. Well aware of the danger it posed, even Windu himself was wary of allowing others to study the form outside of his own [[Padawan]],<ref name="KoF" /> [[Depa Billaba]],<ref name="Depa Card" /> who herself would decide to mainly rely on [[Form III]] in close-quarters combat<ref name="Rise">{{Rebels|Rise of the Old Masters}}</ref> despite her training with<ref name="KoF" /> and mastery of Vaapad.<ref name="Depa Card" /> However, it was still not as reckless as Juyo's manner of employing unchecked aggression.<ref name="KoF" />

Despite its refinement over Juyo, Vaapad was Form VII at its core when seen in battle. The Form VII practitioner fought with controlled fury, laying about with a combination of fierce, rapid strikes and powerful blows. Both variants of the form were as demanding physically as they were emotionally, and in some ways harked back to the direct, kinetic simplicity of Shii-Cho.<ref name="KoF" /> While Billaba was a master of Vaapad, she preferred to bring an end to conflicts with diplomacy, using her words in her capacity as a [[Consular Jedi]] instead of relying on violence.<ref name="Depa Card" />
