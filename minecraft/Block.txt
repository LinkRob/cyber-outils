'''Blocks''' are the basic units of structure in ''[[Minecraft]]'' that make up the game's world. Many blocks can be collected and placed anywhere in the game's world, as well as be used as helpful resources, such as utility blocks like a [[Crafting Table|crafting table]], as well as materials like [[planks]].

== Behavior ==
[[File:Coral reef at night.png|thumb|right|A variety of different blocks occurring in an underwater coral reef; [[sand]] can be seen lining the ground, [[cut sandstone]] in underwater ruin structures, and various forms of coral.]]
Blocks are arranged into a [[wikipedia:Cubic honeycomb|3-dimensional grid]] of 1-cubic-meter cells, although some blocks appear to occupy a partial cell; these include [[slab]]s, [[snow]] layers, [[ladder]]s, [[vine]]s, [[stairs]], [[turtle egg]]s, [[sea pickle]]s, and others.

Together, blocks and [[fluids]] build up the in-game environment, and most can be harvested and utilized in various fashions. Some blocks, such as [[dirt]] and [[sandstone]], are [[Opacity|opaque]] and occupy their entire cubic meter, while other blocks, such as [[glass]] and [[flower]]s, are [[Opacity#Types of transparent blocks|transparent]] or non-solid. [[Explosion]]s destroy some blocks more easily than they destroy others. Some blocks are completely immune to explosions.

[[Air]] is a special block. It is an unbreakable transparent block, as a substitute for the absence of blocks. It has two variants: [[cave air]] and [[void air]]{{only|java}}.

Some blocks, such as [[sea lantern]] and [[glowstone]], emit [[light]]. The amount of light they emit varies widely; see [[Light#Blocks|this table of light values]] for further information. Opaque blocks completely block light, while transparent blocks can have no effect on light, block the light, or merely weaken it.

Almost all blocks ignore gravity, except for [[sand]], [[red sand]], [[gravel]], [[anvil]]s of all damage levels, [[dragon egg]]s, all colors of [[concrete powder]], [[scaffolding]], [[snow|snow layers]]{{only|bedrock|short=1}}, [[pointed dripstone]], and [[Suspicious Block|suspicious sand and gravel]], all of which [[Falling Block|turn into entities]] when their support is removed.

When broken, blocks emit [[sound]]s and [[particle]]s associated with themselves, except in the following cases:

* When the block is removed by a [[piston]].<ref>{{Cite bug|MC|203550|Blocks broken by pistons do not play breaking sounds|date=November 4, 2020}}</ref>
* If the block is affected by gravity and falls into an invalid space (If the block is gravity based and drops from a torch, for example).<ref>{{Cite bug|MC|135869|Gravity-affected blocks broken when falling into an invalid space do not make a sound or produce particles|date=August 10, 2018}}</ref>
* [[Anvil]]s that are destroyed by running out of durability or falling. Only particles are missing.<ref>{{Cite bug|MC|135868|Anvils broken by falling or overusage do not produce particles|date=August 10, 2018}}</ref>
* If the block can be washed away and is washed away by a flowing fluid.<ref>{{Cite bug|MC|25976|Blocks broken by flowing fluids do not produce particles or make breaking sounds|date=July 15, 2013}}</ref>
* If the block can be replaced by other blocks and is replaced.<ref>{{Cite bug|MC|136703|Blocks directly replaced by other blocks do not produce particles, make breaking sounds or drop items where appropiate|date=September 3, 2018}}</ref>
* If the block is one of a few blocks such as rails or redstone wire, supported by another block and its supporting block is removed.<ref>{{Cite bug|MC|123350|Rails and redstone components don't break / create particles when support block is broken|date=December 17, 2017}}</ref>
*If the block is [[leaves]] and decays.<ref>{{Cite bug|MC|136693|Decaying leaves do not produce block breaking particles/sounds|date=September 3, 2018}}</ref>

== Block height ==
{{main|Solid block#Height}}

Most solid blocks are 1 meter high (3.28084... feet or {{frac|1250|381}} feet), but several blocks have non-standard block heights, such as [[slab]]s.

A player can automatically step up from a lower to a higher height if the difference is at most 0.6 ({{frac|3|5}}) of a block or 1.9685... feet ({{frac|250|127}} feet).

A player can automatically (when auto-jump is on) or manually (by hitting the jump keybind) step up from a lower to a higher height if the difference is at most 1  block or 3.28084... feet ({{frac|1250|381}} feet).

== Textures ==
The textures on the faces of blocks are 16×16 pixels. Most blocks are proportionately one cubic meter by default, but their shape can be changed using [[model]]s.

Most blocks have static textures, but these blocks are [[animated]]: [[water]], [[lava]], [[Nether portal (block)|nether portal]], [[End portal (block)|end portal]], [[End gateway (block)|end gateway]], [[prismarine]] (slab; stairs; wall), [[sea lantern]], [[magma block]], [[seagrass]], [[kelp]], [[fire]],  [[lantern]], lit [[campfire]] and their soul variants, lit [[blast furnace]], [[heat block]]{{only|bedrock|education|short=1}}, [[stems]], [[hyphae]], lit [[smoker]], [[stonecutter]], [[sculk]], [[sculk shrieker]], [[sculk sensor]], [[calibrated sculk sensor]], [[sculk vein]], and [[command block]].

Using [[resource pack]]s, the player can change the textures and resolution of blocks, including whether their texture is animated. They can also change the shapes of blocks using [[model]]s and the size of blocks to any size with equal width and height, though sizes that are a power of two tend to work better.

Players can change their textures by changing their files in game.

==Trivia==
*Certain blocks may interpret positions they cannot be placed in by normal means as a valid position. For example, a [[sign]] placed in midair via {{command|setblock}} remains in midair even when updated, until either it is broken, or a block is placed below it and broken.
